Abstract

Universal Phase Theory (UPT) proposes that phase is a primitive structural entity from which geometry, dynamics, and physical excitations may emerge. A potentially distinctive consequence of this framework is the possibility that electromagnetic excitations could modify the emergent phase metric itself. If such a response were structurally unavoidable, photons could interact through a purely geometric channel even in otherwise empty vacuum, potentially producing nonlinear photon–photon scattering, collective states, or localized photonic bound configurations.

This paper performs a complete structural audit of that proposal.

The analysis begins with the UPT emergent-metric construction

[
g_{\mu\nu}^{\Phi}

T_{\mu a}\chi_\Phi^{ab}T_{\nu b},
\qquad
\chi_\Phi=\mathcal L_\Phi^{-1},
]

and derives the general variation of the metric under electromagnetic perturbations. The resulting expression shows that a nonzero electromagnetic metric response requires an explicit coupling between the electromagnetic field and either the phase-to-metric transfer map (T) or the phase stability operator (\mathcal L_\Phi).

No such coupling is presently forced by the existing UPT postulates.

A mathematically admissible zero-response branch therefore exists:

[
\frac{\delta\mathscr F}{\delta F}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta\Phi=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta g^\Phi=0.
]

Consequently, photon-induced phase-metric deformation is not currently a structural prediction of UPT.

A phenomenological nonlinear response can nevertheless be introduced and analyzed. Such a response can generate electromagnetic self-interaction and photon–photon interaction channels, but neither the sign of the interaction nor the existence of a stable localized bound state follows automatically. In particular, ideal co-propagating null electromagnetic waves can have vanishing Lorentz invariants, eliminating the simplest proposed quartic interaction channel.

The final result is therefore deliberately conservative:

[
\boxed{
\text{UPT currently permits phase-metric electromagnetic response but does not require it.}
}
]

The phase-metric binding hypothesis remains a conditional research program rather than a prediction. The decisive unresolved question is whether a non-arbitrary electromagnetic coupling can be derived from the universal phase equation itself.
