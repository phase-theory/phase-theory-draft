Abstract

We develop a geometric interpretation of electric charge in Φ-Wave Theory by identifying charge with the Hopf linking invariant of the phase field. While the first Chern class provides the cohomological quantization of charge, the Hopf invariant provides its underlying geometric realization as linked phase flux tubes, knotted field lines, or self-linked phase structures. We show that charged particles correspond to stable topological excitations whose linking number determines the electromagnetic sector. Charge conservation follows from topological preservation of linking, while positive and negative charge correspond to orientation of phase linking. This provides a direct geometric interpretation of electric charge as knotted phase topology.
