Abstract

We develop the computational framework of Φ-Wave Theory and introduce the phibit as the fundamental unit of phase computation. Unlike a classical bit, which occupies one of two discrete states, or a quantum qubit, which exists as a vector in Hilbert space, a phibit is a localized admissible phase structure whose computational state is represented by its position, topology, geometry, and coherence within the phase manifold. Computation is reinterpreted as controlled evolution through admissible phase configuration space. Classical computation, quantum computation, topological computation, and analog computation emerge as limiting cases of a more general phase-computational framework.
