Abstract

We develop the theory of bulk–boundary phase inflow in Φ-Wave Theory, showing how anomalies generated by chiral boundary or defect-localized matter are canceled by topological transport of phase current from the bulk phase manifold. Boundary-localized zero modes on domain walls, vortices, or higher codimension defects generally possess anomalous gauge variation. We show that this variation is exactly compensated by a bulk topological phase action whose boundary variation provides the missing current. This establishes anomaly cancellation as a consequence of global phase continuity, linking defect-localized matter to higher-dimensional topological transport. Bulk–boundary inflow provides the geometric mechanism by which phase reality preserves gauge consistency.
