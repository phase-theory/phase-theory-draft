## 1. The Standard Model Classification and Its Limits

### 1.1 The SM Particle Taxonomy

The Standard Model classifies particles through a rigid representational structure:

- **Spacetime symmetry:** Particles are irreducible representations of the Poincaré group, labeled by mass $m$ and spin $s$ (Wigner classification).

- **Ontology:** Every particle is a quantum of a fundamental field defined on a fixed 4-dimensional Lorentzian manifold.
- **Mass generation:** Masses arise through the Higgs mechanism (Yukawa couplings) or are set to zero by gauge symmetry.
- **Quantization:** The Born rule and Hilbert-space structure are imposed externally.

The SM classification rests on assumptions that UPT identifies as **insertions rather than derivations**:

| Point-particle ontology | Fundamental | Infrared approximation of extended phase orbit |
| Pre-assigned charge representations | Input data | Derived from topology and holonomy |
| Higgs mass mechanism | Fundamental | One possible phase-excess realization |

### 1.3 The Classification Gap

Any stable, observable entity that violates **one or more** of these structural assumptions cannot be classified within the SM. UPT demonstrates that the universal phase equation naturally produces such entities. They are not exotic additions to the SM; they belong to a **different ontological category** that the SM's representational framework is structurally incapable of describing.
