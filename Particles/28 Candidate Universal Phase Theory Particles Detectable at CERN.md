## Preamble

The following catalog identifies **28 candidate particles** that arise naturally from the Universal Phase Theory (UPT) framework and possess signatures potentially accessible at CERN's Large Hadron Collider (LHC) or its proposed successors (FCC-hh, HE-LHC, CLIC). Each particle is a **stable, finite-excess, localizable, transportable phase-sector orbit** $\mathfrak{P}_q = [\Phi_q]_{\mathscr{G}_\Phi}$ satisfying the seven conditions of Theorem 1, but falling outside the $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ representational classification of the Standard Model.

All quantum numbers listed are **derived UPT labels**, not SM assignments. Masses are expressed as phase-excess quantities $m_q = (\mathcal{E}[\Phi_q]-\mathcal{E}[\Phi_0])/c_\Phi^2$ and are structurally predicted, not fitted. Detection signatures are stated in terms of observable LHC final states.

## Detection Priority at CERN

### High Priority (HL-LHC accessible, $\sqrt{s} = 14$ TeV, $\mathcal{L} = 3000\text{ fb}^{-1}$)

- **PWB, PM, HCC:** HSCP and fractional charge searches (ATLAS/CMS/MoEDAL)
- **SGB, PES, PEV:** Resonance searches in dilepton, diphoton, diboson channels
- **HSE:** Angular distribution analyses in existing data

### Medium Priority (FCC-hh, $\sqrt{s} = 100$ TeV)

- **TPP, HPP, PKS, EPO:** High-mass extended structures requiring $> 14$ TeV
- **STP, TMP:** Tower spectroscopy requiring high statistics
- **SRP:** Threshold scans

### Low Priority (requires new detector concepts or beyond-collider experiments)

- **PGPR, NLPO:** Pre-geometric objects may not couple to SM sector at observable rates
- **CPSP, SQPO:** Quantum-incomplete objects require new measurement paradigms

## Falsifiability Statement

This catalog is **falsifiable**. It is excluded if:

1. No concrete $\mathscr{F}$ can be specified that produces any of the 28 phase-sector solutions satisfying Theorem 1.
2. The response metric $g^\Phi_{ij}$ fails to yield a Lorentzian stratum supporting any of these sectors.
3. All 28 candidates reduce to SM particles or known BSM extensions (SUSY, extra dimensions, etc.) upon explicit computation.
4. The predicted mass ranges and couplings are inconsistent with all existing LHC data at 95% CL.
5. No novel quantitative prediction (Criterion F) can be extracted from any specific $\mathscr{F}$.

The strongest validation would be: a single $\mathscr{F}$ producing multiple entries in this catalog with **computed** (not fitted) masses, spins, and charges, at least one of which is observed at CERN.
