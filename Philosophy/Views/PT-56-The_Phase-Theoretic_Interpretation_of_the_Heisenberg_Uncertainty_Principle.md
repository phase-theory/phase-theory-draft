Abstract

The conventional interpretation of the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle states that certain pairs of observables—most notably position and momentum—cannot simultaneously possess arbitrarily precise values.

This principle is often interpreted as a fundamental limitation imposed by nature itself.

Phase Theory proposes a different interpretation.

Rather than viewing uncertainty as a property of reality, Phase Theory treats uncertainty as a manifestation of admissibility constraints operating within a phase-governed universe.

In this framework:

* Reality remains fully defined.
* Phase structure remains deterministic.
* Indeterminacy emerges because incompatible observational projections cannot simultaneously preserve coherence.

The uncertainty relation therefore becomes a statement about admissible observation rather than incomplete reality.
