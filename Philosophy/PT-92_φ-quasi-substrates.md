Conclusion

The concept of Φ quasi-substrates resolves a recurring ambiguity in foundational physics: how a single ontological primitive can generate the immense diversity of observed phenomena without introducing additional fundamental substances.

Phase Theory answers this by distinguishing ontology from organization.

Ontology contains only Φ.

Everything else—topology, geometry, causality, gauge structure, quantum phenomena, gravity, matter, spacetime, information, life, and observation—arises as a hierarchy of quasi-substrates: stable organizational domains of admissible phase structure.

Thus the universe is not a collection of interacting substances. It is a nested hierarchy of emergent phase organizations, all reducible to the single underlying reality:

\boxed{\Phi}

the fundamental phase substrate from which all existence emerges.
