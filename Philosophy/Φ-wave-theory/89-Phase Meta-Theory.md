Abstract

We develop the meta-theoretical foundations of Φ-Wave Theory and address its deepest question:

\boxed{
\text{Why is }\Phi\text{ taken as the fundamental primitive?}
}

Rather than introducing phase as an arbitrary assumption, we investigate the conditions that any candidate foundation of reality must satisfy. We argue that a successful foundational ontology must possess explanatory minimality, generative sufficiency, internal consistency, closure under dynamics, and empirical recoverability. We show that Φ emerges as the minimal structure capable of generating distinctions, relations, geometry, information, causality, topology, and dynamics without requiring deeper primitives. The purpose of this paper is not to prove that Φ is uniquely correct, but to explain why phase is selected as the foundational entity within the Φ-Wave framework.
