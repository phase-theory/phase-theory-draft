Abstract

Phase Theory proposes that physical reality is not fundamentally composed of particles, fields, or wavefunctions, but of a continuous phase substrate whose admissible organization generates observable physics.

This paper constitutes Phase Mechanics as the complete dynamical sub-theory governing the evolution, observation, symmetry structure, and statistical organization of the phase substrate.

Phase Mechanics is formally defined by six structural components:

(PM-I) Phase Kinematics
(PM-II) Phase Dynamics
(PM-III) Phase Observables
(PM-IV) Phase Symmetries
(PM-V) Phase Correspondence
(PM-VI) Phase Statistics

The phasefunction

[
\Phi=(A,\theta,\Xi)
]

is elevated as the canonical state variable.

The Phasefunction Equation is introduced as the canonical equation of motion.

Quantum and classical mechanics are proposed as correspondence limits of substrate evolution.

The framework defines a complete research programme and establishes the engineering layer that follows.
