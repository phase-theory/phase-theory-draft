Relational Relativity: A Formulation of Spacetime in Which Photons Possess No Fundamental Motion

A Theoretical Framework for Reconstructing Light Propagation from Null Relations, Field Structure, and Causal Geometry

Research preprint — October 2026

Abstract

We investigate a reformulation of relativistic physics in which photon motion is removed from the primitive ontology of the theory. Rather than treating a photon as an individual object whose trajectory through space constitutes the fundamental explanation of light propagation, we formulate electromagnetic phenomena in terms of relations among spacetime events, the structure of electromagnetic fields, and the causal geometry that connects emission and detection.

The central proposal is that photon motion need not be a fundamental physical primitive even though light propagation remains an invariant observable. This distinction permits a rigorous reconstruction of much of relativistic kinematics without assigning ontological priority to the movement of individual photons.

We introduce a relational event structure ((\mathcal E,\mathcal N,\mathcal F)), where (\mathcal E) is a set of physical events, (\mathcal N) specifies null relations between events, and (\mathcal F) represents electromagnetic field structure. The spacetime metric is reconstructed, subject to appropriate regularity and compatibility assumptions, from causal and conformal data supplemented by a local scale structure. Electromagnetic propagation is then expressed through field equations and null-related emission and detection events rather than through a fundamental photon trajectory.

We derive the compatibility of this framework with Lorentz transformations, the invariant vacuum speed (c), the null condition (ds^2=0), electromagnetic wave propagation, and relativistic energy-momentum conservation. We distinguish the removal of photon trajectories from the stronger and physically problematic assertion that light signals never propagate or that photons have zero spatial velocity in every inertial frame.

The resulting framework, termed Relational Relativity without Fundamental Photon Motion (RR-NPM), is a candidate reformulation of relativistic ontology, not an experimentally established replacement for special or general relativity. Its distinctive contribution is a change in explanatory priority: spacetime relations and field structure are primitive, while photon trajectories are optional representations of certain interactions and detection records.

Keywords: special relativity; general relativity; photons; null geometry; relational ontology; electromagnetic field; causal structure; quantum field theory; spacetime reconstruction.

1. Introduction

1.1 The problem of photon motion

Einstein’s special theory of relativity establishes a spacetime structure in which inertial observers agree on the vacuum speed of light. General relativity extends this framework by allowing spacetime geometry to vary dynamically. In both theories, light is associated with null directions of spacetime, and geometrical optics represents idealized light propagation through null curves.

The conventional description is often expressed in the language of a photon traveling from an emission event to a detection event. That language is useful, but it can encourage a particular ontological interpretation: that a photon is a persisting individual object whose motion through a pre-existing space is the most fundamental explanation of the observed connection between emission and detection.

The present work investigates whether that interpretation is necessary.

Three statements must be distinguished:

1. Trajectory statement: A photon possesses a fundamental, continuously defined worldline.
2. Propagation statement: Electromagnetic disturbances and detection records exhibit invariant causal relations between events.
3. Stationarity statement: A photon has zero spatial velocity in every inertial reference frame.

The first statement is stronger than what is required for every quantum description of electromagnetic phenomena. The second is central to relativistic physics. The third is incompatible with the ordinary relativistic description of freely propagating light.

The theory developed here rejects the necessity of the first statement, preserves the second, and does not adopt the third as a physical claim.

The proposal is therefore not that a photon remains stationary while light somehow appears at a distant detector. It is that the physical connection between emission and detection may be fundamental, while the image of an individual photon traveling between them may be representational rather than primitive.

1.2 The explanatory reversal

In the conventional pedagogical account, one begins with a moving light signal and derives the geometry needed to preserve the invariance of its speed. In the relational formulation, one begins instead with the invariant structure of admissible event relations and derives the familiar kinematics of light from that structure.

The conceptual reversal is

[
\text{photon trajectory}
\longrightarrow
\text{observed propagation}
]

being replaced by

[
\text{event relations}
+
\text{field structure}
\longrightarrow
\text{observed propagation}.
]

The two formulations may produce identical experimental predictions. If they do, their difference is initially ontological and explanatory, not empirical. A genuinely new physical theory would require additional equations or constraints that produce testable differences.

1.3 Scope and objective

The objective is to construct a mathematically explicit framework satisfying four requirements:

* Recover the Lorentzian causal structure of special relativity.
* Describe electromagnetic propagation without requiring a photon worldline as a primitive entity.
* Remain compatible with the field equations and conservation laws of established electromagnetic theory.
* Identify the precise boundary between a consistent relational reinterpretation and a literal claim that light never propagates.

The framework will be called RR-NPM. Its equations will be labeled according to their logical status: established results, consequences of stated assumptions, proposed postulates, or conjectural extensions.

2. Ontological and Mathematical Foundations

2.1 The event domain

Let (\mathcal E) denote the set of physically distinguishable events. An event is specified operationally by a localized physical occurrence, such as an emission, scattering, or detection record.

An event is not itself a particle. It is a member of the domain over which physical relations are defined.

We introduce three structures:

[
\mathfrak R =
(\mathcal E,\mathcal N,\mathcal F),
]

where:

* (\mathcal E) is the event domain.
* (\mathcal N\subseteq\mathcal E\times\mathcal E) is the null-relation structure.
* (\mathcal F) is the electromagnetic field configuration or, in a quantum treatment, the relevant field state and correlation structure.

For events (p,q\in\mathcal E), write

[
p\sim_{\mathcal N}q
]

when the events are connected by a null relation in the relevant spacetime region.

This relation is intended to encode the causal structure associated with idealized vacuum light signals. It does not assert that the two events are identical, that their spatial coordinates are equal, or that no physical process occurs between them.

In particular,

[
p\sim_{\mathcal N}q
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
p=q.
]

Distinct emission and detection events may be null-related even though they occur at different spatial positions in a chosen coordinate system.

2.2 Removing trajectory primitivity

In standard geometrical optics, a light ray is represented by a curve

[
\gamma:I\rightarrow M,
]

where (M) is a spacetime manifold and (I) is a parameter interval. For a null geodesic with affine parameter (\lambda),

[
g_{\mu\nu}
\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}
\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}=0.
]

RR-NPM does not prohibit this representation. Instead, it denies that the curve must be interpreted as the fundamental physical substance of the photon.

The trajectory becomes a derived representation of an admissible sequence of events and field relations.

The distinction is important. A mathematical curve can encode a relation without requiring that the relation be constituted by a tiny material object occupying every intermediate point. The same distinction appears between a map of causal dependencies and the individual physical mechanisms that realize those dependencies.

Accordingly, the primitive structure of RR-NPM consists of events, relations, and field constraints. A photon trajectory may be introduced when it is mathematically convenient, but its ontological status is not assumed at the outset.

2.3 Three meanings of “no motion”

The phrase photons do not have motion admits several mathematically different interpretations.

Definition 1 — Trajectory-free ontology. Photon motion is not primitive; observable electromagnetic phenomena are represented by fields and event relations.

Definition 2 — No photon rest frame. A photon does not possess an inertial rest frame in which its spatial velocity vanishes.

Definition 3 — Zero spatial velocity. A photon has zero spatial coordinate velocity in a specified reference frame.

Definitions 1 and 2 can coexist. Definition 3, interpreted as a universal statement about all inertial frames, cannot be reconciled with standard special relativity for a freely propagating vacuum photon.

RR-NPM adopts Definition 1 and retains Definition 2. Definition 3 is not adopted.

This is the mathematically consistent interpretation under which the proposed framework can be developed without immediately contradicting the experimentally successful kinematics of light.

3. Reconstruction of Lorentzian Spacetime

3.1 Metric structure

Let (M) be a smooth four-dimensional manifold equipped with a Lorentzian metric (g_{\mu\nu}) of signature ((-+++)). In a local inertial coordinate system,

[
ds^2

-c^2dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2.
\tag{3.1}
]

For a vacuum light signal,

[
ds^2=0.
\tag{3.2}
]

Consequently,

[
c^2dt^2

dx^2+dy^2+dz^2,
]

and therefore, for a nontrivial future-directed light trajectory parametrized by coordinate time,

[
\left|\frac{d\mathbf x}{dt}\right|=c.
\tag{3.3}
]

Equation (3.3) is the ordinary coordinate-velocity description of light in an inertial frame. RR-NPM does not deny this result. It denies that the result, by itself, proves that an individual photon trajectory must be an ontological primitive.

3.2 Null relations as geometrical data

The metric determines which tangent directions are null. Conversely, under suitable conditions, knowledge of the full causal ordering of events determines the spacetime metric up to a positive conformal factor.

Write

[
g_{\mu\nu}

\Omega^2(x),\widehat g_{\mu\nu},
\qquad \Omega(x)>0.
\tag{3.4}
]

The conformal metric (\widehat g_{\mu\nu}) determines the null cones, since

[
g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu=0
\iff
\widehat g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu=0.
\tag{3.5}
]

Thus the causal structure alone does not generally determine the complete metric scale. Additional information, such as proper-time measurements, clock standards, or volume data, is required.

This gives the relational reconstruction principle:

[
\boxed{
\text{causal relations}
+
\text{scale information}
\longrightarrow
\text{spacetime metric}.
}
\tag{3.6}
]

The formula is a structural statement, not a claim that the metric can always be reconstructed from a bare list of emission and detection events. Sufficient coverage, causal information, and regularity assumptions are necessary.

3.3 Relational interpretation of the metric

In conventional notation, (g_{\mu\nu}) is a field defined on spacetime. In RR-NPM, its operational meaning is associated with the local structure of relations among events and the measurements made by clocks and rods.

The metric measures the interval between neighboring events:

[
ds^2=g_{\mu\nu},dx^\mu dx^\nu.
\tag{3.7}
]

The interval is invariant under coordinate transformations, while the components of (g_{\mu\nu}) and (dx^\mu) depend on the chosen coordinates.

The theory therefore does not replace the metric with an unstructured network. It proposes that the metric is a geometrical representation of a relational structure that can, in suitable circumstances, be specified independently of a photon-trajectory ontology.

3.4 Operational reconstruction

Suppose a network of observers records clock readings, emission events, reception events, and the local directions of electromagnetic signals. These data constrain the causal relations among events.

In a locally inertial region, the time of flight between stationary emitter and receiver separated by Euclidean distance (L) satisfies

[
\Delta t=\frac{L}{c}.
\tag{3.8}
]

A network of such measurements can constrain local light cones and, with additional measurements, the metric scale.

Equation (3.8) is not assumed to describe every global path in curved spacetime. Gravitational lensing, curvature, relative motion of endpoints, and the choice of synchronization convention must be accounted for in more general settings.

The relational program begins with operational records and reconstructs the geometrical structure consistent with them. It does not claim that a finite set of flight-time measurements uniquely determines an arbitrary spacetime geometry.

4. Special Relativity Without Primitive Photon Trajectories

4.1 Inertial coordinate transformations

Consider two inertial frames (S) and (S’), with their relative motion along the (x)-axis at speed (v). The Lorentz transformation is

[
x’=\gamma(x-vt),
]

[
t’=\gamma\left(t-\frac{vx}{c^2}\right),
]

[
y’=y,\qquad z’=z,
\tag{4.1}
]

where

[
\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.
\tag{4.2}
]

These transformations preserve the spacetime interval:

[
-c^2dt’^2+dx’^2+dy’^2+dz’^2

-c^2dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2.
\tag{4.3}
]

The null condition is therefore invariant.

If two events are null-related in one inertial frame, they remain null-related in every inertial frame.

[
p\sim_{\mathcal N}q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\sim_{\mathcal N’}q.
\tag{4.4}
]

This invariance is the geometrical core of the relativistic description of light. It does not depend on treating the photon as a material object with a rest frame.

4.2 Why the speed (c) remains invariant

For a coordinate trajectory, the one-dimensional velocity transformation is

[
u’=\frac{u-v}{1-\frac{uv}{c^2}}.
\tag{4.5}
]

Setting (u=c) gives

[
u’=\frac{c-v}{1-v/c}=c.
\tag{4.6}
]

Similarly, setting (u=-c) gives (u’=-c).

This is the standard velocity-transformation result. In RR-NPM, its interpretation is relational: every inertial coordinate system represents the same null structure, and the corresponding coordinate description assigns the same vacuum light speed (c).

The invariant speed is not explained by supposing that a photon travels at a special speed relative to a hidden universal rest frame. No such frame is introduced.

Instead,

[
\boxed{
c=\text{the invariant conversion scale between temporal and spatial units in the local null geometry}.
}
\tag{4.7}
]

This statement describes the role of (c) within Lorentzian geometry. It does not establish a new empirical mechanism for the numerical value of (c).

4.3 The impossibility of a universal stationary photon

Assume, for contradiction, that a freely propagating photon has zero spatial velocity in every inertial frame.

In frame (S), this would mean

[
u=0.
]

Under a Lorentz boost with relative velocity (v), Equation (4.5) gives

[
u’=-v.
\tag{4.8}
]

For arbitrary nonzero (v), the transformed velocity is not zero. Therefore, the hypothesis that the photon is stationary in every inertial frame contradicts Lorentz covariance.

There is a deeper obstruction. A photon in standard relativistic physics has null four-momentum:

[
p^\mu p_\mu=0,
\qquad p^\mu\neq0.
\tag{4.9}
]

A massive particle admits a rest frame in which its spatial momentum vanishes. A nonzero null four-vector does not. No proper Lorentz transformation can transform a nonzero null four-momentum into a purely temporal vector.

Thus the stronger claim that a photon is literally motionless in its own rest frame fails because a massless photon has no inertial rest frame.

RR-NPM avoids this contradiction by rejecting the requirement that a photon possess a fundamental trajectory, not by assigning it a forbidden rest frame.

5. Electromagnetic Fields as the Primary Physical Description

5.1 Field ontology

Let (F_{\mu\nu}) denote the electromagnetic field-strength tensor. In a region without electric currents, Maxwell’s equations can be written as

[
\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=0,
\tag{5.1}
]

[
\nabla_{[\alpha}F_{\beta\gamma]}=0.
\tag{5.2}
]

With sources, the first equation becomes

[
\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^\nu,
\tag{5.3}
]

in SI conventions.

The field tensor, rather than a collection of photon trajectories, supplies the classical description of electromagnetic interactions. It determines local field strengths, energy and momentum densities, and the forces experienced by charged matter.

RR-NPM therefore takes the electromagnetic field as part of its primary physical description. Photon language is retained for quantized excitations and detection phenomena, but the trajectory of an individual photon is not made fundamental.

5.2 Geometrical-optics limit

Consider a rapidly varying electromagnetic field represented locally by an eikonal ansatz,

[
F_{\mu\nu}(x)

\operatorname{Re}
\left[
f_{\mu\nu}(x)e^{iS(x)/\epsilon}
\right],
\qquad \epsilon\ll1.
\tag{5.4}
]

Define the wave covector

[
k_\mu=\nabla_\mu S.
\tag{5.5}
]

At leading order in the short-wavelength expansion, Maxwell’s equations yield the characteristic condition

[
g^{\mu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu=0.
\tag{5.6}
]

Thus the wave covector is null.

For a suitable affine parametrization of the associated characteristic curves, one obtains the null-geodesic equation

[
k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu=0.
\tag{5.7}
]

Equation (5.7) is the standard geometrical-optics result under the relevant assumptions. It does not establish that a photon is a miniature classical body moving along a uniquely defined path in every quantum situation.

In RR-NPM, Equation (5.6) is treated as a local propagation constraint on the electromagnetic field’s phase structure. Equation (5.7) is a useful derived representation of the corresponding characteristics.

The theory thus retains ray geometry without assigning it primitive ontological status.

5.3 Phase as a relational quantity

For a local plane wave in flat spacetime,

[
S(x)=k_\mu x^\mu,
\tag{5.8}
]

and the wave can be represented schematically by

[
\Psi(x)=A(x)e^{iS(x)}.
\tag{5.9}
]

The observable field is determined by the appropriate real components and polarization structure.

The phase difference between two events is

[
\Delta S=S(q)-S(p).
\tag{5.10}
]

This phase difference can enter interference and correlation measurements. It is a relation between field configurations evaluated at distinct events, not a statement that an individual photon must be continuously tracked between those events.

Care is required: phase is not universally an observable by itself, and a local plane-wave ansatz is not a complete description of arbitrary electromagnetic fields. Observable interference depends on relative phases and the experimental arrangement.

Nevertheless, the phase formulation provides a concrete mathematical setting in which relational field information can replace a primitive photon-trajectory description.

6. Energy, Momentum, and the Meaning of Propagation

6.1 Energy-momentum of electromagnetic fields

The electromagnetic stress-energy tensor in vacuum may be written, in a common covariant convention, as

[
T_{\mu\nu}

\frac{1}{\mu_0}
\left(
F_{\mu\alpha}F_\nu{}^\alpha
-\frac14g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}
\right),
\tag{6.1}
]

with index and sign conventions chosen consistently with the metric signature.

In source-free regions, Maxwell’s equations imply

[
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0.
\tag{6.2}
]

With matter present, the electromagnetic and matter stress-energy tensors exchange energy and momentum, while the total stress-energy tensor obeys the appropriate conservation law.

The field therefore carries measurable energy and momentum. Eliminating fundamental photon trajectories does not eliminate these quantities.

6.2 Quantum energy and momentum

For a photon in a momentum eigenstate in flat spacetime, the standard relations are

[
E=\hbar\omega,
\qquad
|\mathbf p|=\hbar|\mathbf k|,
\qquad
E=c|\mathbf p|.
\tag{6.3}
]

Equivalently,

[
E^2-c^2|\mathbf p|^2=0.
\tag{6.4}
]

These relations follow from the massless relativistic dispersion relation and the quantum relations between energy, momentum, frequency, and wave number.

RR-NPM preserves them.

The absence of a fundamental photon trajectory must not be confused with the absence of photon momentum. Momentum is a measurable and mathematically essential property of electromagnetic excitations, and it is responsible for radiation pressure and momentum transfer in photon-matter interactions.

6.3 Propagation without an ontologically fundamental traveler

Suppose an emitter undergoes a transition at event (p), and a detector registers an energy transfer at event (q). The detector record, the field state, and the causal geometry constrain the relationship between these events.

The conventional narrative says that a photon travels from (p) to (q). RR-NPM instead begins with:

[
\mathcal D(p,q)

{\text{field constraints, causal relations, detector records}},
\tag{6.5}
]

where (\mathcal D) denotes the complete relational data relevant to the experiment.

A trajectory may be inferred as an effective representation of these data when the geometrical-optics approximation is appropriate. In regimes dominated by diffraction, interference, entanglement, or strongly quantum interactions, a single classical trajectory may be insufficient.

The relational formulation is not a claim that the detector response is instantaneous. In flat spacetime, a causal electromagnetic influence between separated events remains constrained by the light cone. Nor does the formulation permit information to propagate outside the relativistic causal structure.

The proposed shift is narrower and more precise: the relation between the physical events is fundamental in the theory, whereas the image of an individual object traversing every intermediate point is not required as an additional primitive assumption.

6.4 A necessary limitation

The relational description cannot simply declare all intermediate physics nonexistent. Electromagnetic fields have local degrees of freedom, and local field interactions can be essential to the correct account of an experiment.

Consequently, RR-NPM removes the necessity of a photon trajectory as a fundamental object; it does not remove spacetime locality, field dynamics, or the causal restrictions that govern observable interactions.

A successful implementation must reproduce the relevant local field correlations and detector statistics. An endpoint-only theory that omits necessary intermediate field dynamics would require additional justification and may fail in experiments where those dynamics are observable.

7. Relativistic Time and the Absence of Photon Proper Time

7.1 Proper time for massive observers

For a timelike worldline (x^\mu(\lambda)), proper time satisfies

[
d\tau^2=-\frac{1}{c^2}g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu.
\tag{7.1}
]

For an inertial observer in flat spacetime,

[
d\tau=dt\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}.
\tag{7.2}
]

Time dilation follows directly:

[
\Delta\tau=\frac{\Delta t}{\gamma}
\tag{7.3}
]

for uniform motion over the corresponding interval.

These equations describe physical clocks and timelike histories. They remain unchanged in RR-NPM.

7.2 Why a photon clock is not defined

For a null relation,

[
ds^2=0.
\tag{7.4}
]

The timelike proper-time expression therefore does not define a nonzero elapsed proper time along a null curve. One cannot construct a photon rest frame by taking the ordinary massive-particle clock and setting its speed equal to (c).

The statement that a photon experiences zero proper time is sometimes used informally. It must not be interpreted as evidence that a photon has a valid rest frame, that the entire emission-to-detection process is instantaneous in every frame, or that a photon possesses a meaningful internal perspective.

RR-NPM avoids assigning a proper-time history to a photon as a fundamental object. The relevant temporal measurements are made by clocks associated with timelike observers and physical devices.

7.3 The invariant distinction

For a massive particle,

[
p^\mu p_\mu=-m^2c^2<0,
\tag{7.5}
]

whereas for a photon,

[
p^\mu p_\mu=0.
\tag{7.6}
]

The two cases occupy different Lorentz orbits. The photon cannot be transformed into a massive particle’s rest-frame configuration.

The relational interpretation therefore has a natural mathematical foundation: a null connection need not be equipped with a photon-centered clock or rest frame. Its temporal and spatial descriptions arise from the coordinate systems and physical clocks used to characterize the events.

8. Extending the Framework to General Relativity

8.1 Curved spacetime

Let ((M,g)) be a Lorentzian spacetime. The null condition becomes

[
g_{\mu\nu}(x),dx^\mu dx^\nu=0.
\tag{8.1}
]

For electromagnetic radiation in the geometrical-optics regime, the wave covector satisfies

[
g^{\mu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu=0,
\tag{8.2}
]

and, for an affine parametrization in vacuum,

[
k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu=0.
\tag{8.3}
]

Curvature affects the relationship between emission and detection. Light may be gravitationally lensed, gravitationally redshifted, or delayed relative to a reference path. These effects are properties of the spacetime geometry and the electromagnetic field’s interaction with that geometry.

RR-NPM interprets them relationally. The geometry constrains the admissible connections between events, and the field equations determine the local electromagnetic dynamics.

8.2 Einstein’s field equations

The standard Einstein field equations are

[
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}

\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}.
\tag{8.4}
]

In this framework, the metric is still a dynamical field governed by the gravitational equations unless a distinct gravitational theory is supplied.

RR-NPM alone does not derive Equation (8.4) from null relations. Nor does the relational interpretation establish a new gravitational field equation. It changes the proposed ontological starting point while retaining the established mathematical framework of general relativity.

A stronger theory would need to show how the metric and its dynamics emerge from more primitive relational data, and under what conditions the Einstein equations follow. That is a separate derivation problem, not a consequence of removing photon trajectories.

8.3 Gravitational redshift

Let (k^\mu) be the wave four-vector and (u^\mu) the four-velocity of an observer. The frequency measured by that observer is proportional to

[
\omega_{\mathrm{obs}}=-k_\mu u^\mu,
\tag{8.5}
]

with the proportionality convention fixed by the normalization of the wave covector and the observer’s time coordinate.

The frequency ratio between emission and reception is therefore determined by the field phase structure and the emitter and receiver four-velocities, evaluated within the spacetime geometry.

For a static spacetime with a timelike Killing vector (\xi^\mu), the conserved quantity along a vacuum null geodesic is

[
\mathcal E=-k_\mu\xi^\mu.
\tag{8.6}
]

The observed frequency depends on the local normalization of the observer’s four-velocity relative to this Killing field. Gravitational redshift follows from this geometrical structure.

The relational interpretation changes no prediction here. It changes what is taken to be primary: the observable frequency relation between physical events and observers, rather than a photon carrying a succession of observer-independent clock readings along a trajectory.

9. Quantum Field Theory and Photon Individuality

9.1 The photon in quantum electrodynamics

Quantum electrodynamics quantizes the electromagnetic field. Photons arise as quantum excitations associated with the field’s modes, and their observable properties depend on the state, interactions, and measurement context.

A one-photon state can be represented schematically as

[
|1_f\rangle

\int d\mu(k),
f(k),a^\dagger(k)|0\rangle,
\tag{9.1}
]

where (f(k)) is a mode amplitude, (a^\dagger(k)) creates a photon in the relevant mode, and (d\mu(k)) denotes the appropriate momentum-space measure.

This expression does not generally specify a single classical trajectory. A wave packet may involve a distribution of momenta, and the detection probability is determined by the quantum state and the detector’s coupling to the field.

The absence of a unique classical trajectory in such descriptions is compatible with the standard quantum-field-theoretic account. It does not, by itself, prove that photons are unreal or that propagation is absent.

9.2 Detection amplitudes

A simplified transition amplitude for a detector coupled to an electromagnetic field may be expressed schematically as

[
\mathcal A_{i\rightarrow f}
\propto
\langle f|
\mathcal T
\exp\left[
-\frac{i}{\hbar}
\int d^4x,\mathcal H_{\mathrm{int}}(x)
\right]
|i\rangle,
\tag{9.2}
]

where (\mathcal H_{\mathrm{int}}) is the interaction Hamiltonian density and (\mathcal T) denotes time ordering.

The actual expression depends on the interaction, state preparation, and detector model. Its significance here is structural: observable probabilities are computed from quantum amplitudes and field interactions, not by requiring a classical photon worldline as an input.

RR-NPM adopts this mathematical fact as motivation for a trajectory-nonfundamental ontology. It does not modify the quantum-field-theoretic amplitude unless a new dynamical postulate is introduced.

9.3 What the theory does not claim

The framework does not infer that a photon is simultaneously at every point, that it exists only at emission and detection, or that all possible paths are physically identical. Such claims require additional interpretive or dynamical assumptions.

Instead, the proposed ontology remains deliberately limited:

1. The electromagnetic field and its quantum state encode the relevant physical degrees of freedom.
2. Emission and detection are physical interactions.
3. Relativistic causal structure constrains the relations between events.
4. A photon trajectory is not required as a fundamental element of the ontology.

This position is compatible with more than one interpretation of quantum mechanics, provided that interpretation preserves the same empirical predictions and does not add contradictory claims.

10. The RR-NPM Postulates

We now state the proposed framework in compact form.

Postulate I — Event primacy. The primitive domain consists of physical events and the relations required to specify their possible causal and dynamical connections.

Postulate II — Relational null structure. In the relativistic vacuum regime, electromagnetic characteristics are associated with null relations defined by a Lorentzian metric or an equivalent local causal structure.

Postulate III — Field completeness for electromagnetic dynamics. The electromagnetic field, or its quantum-field-theoretic counterpart, supplies the local dynamical description needed to calculate observable electromagnetic phenomena.

Postulate IV — No fundamental photon trajectory. A photon worldline is not assumed to be a primitive physical entity. When useful, it may be introduced as an effective representation of field characteristics, momentum flow, or the geometrical-optics limit.

Postulate V — Relativistic covariance. The theory’s physical predictions are invariant under the appropriate Lorentz transformations in flat spacetime and are formulated covariantly in curved spacetime.

Postulate VI — Conservation and causal consistency. Energy-momentum transfer, detector probabilities, and field interactions must satisfy the relevant conservation laws and relativistic causal restrictions.

These postulates define a candidate interpretive framework. Postulates II, III, V, and VI substantially inherit established physical structure. Postulate IV is the distinctive ontological proposal. Postulate I specifies the relational emphasis but requires a precise operational account in any complete implementation.

The postulates do not yet constitute a new empirically distinguishable theory. Their primary task is to identify a coherent mathematical architecture within which the phrase “light without fundamental photon motion” has a defensible meaning.

11. A Relational Formulation of the Light-Signal Problem

11.1 The conventional initial-value formulation

In a classical field formulation, one specifies electromagnetic initial data on an appropriate hypersurface, subject to the Maxwell constraints, and evolves the field according to the field equations.

In flat spacetime, the source-free equations admit wave solutions whose characteristics lie on the null cone. For a localized source, the retarded field depends on the source history and the causal structure.

This is a field-evolution description, not a theory that requires individual photons to be tracked.

11.2 The relational boundary formulation

Consider instead an experimental configuration specified by:

* An emission interaction at event (p).
* A detection interaction at event (q).
* Boundary or initial data for the electromagnetic field.
* A spacetime metric and detector-response model.

The task is to calculate the conditional probability of a detection record at (q), given the preparation at (p) and the specified physical environment.

Symbolically,

[
P(q\mid p,\mathcal F,g,\mathcal I),
\tag{11.1}
]

where (\mathcal I) denotes the relevant interaction and measurement conditions.

Equation (11.1) is a notation for a conditional probability, not a proposed universal formula. Its value must be computed from the applicable classical or quantum dynamics.

The relational perspective treats this probability, together with the field and geometry constraints, as a primary object of explanatory interest. It does not require that a classical path be selected in advance.

11.3 The distinction between endpoints and intermediate dynamics

A purely endpoint-based theory would attempt to determine the detection probability using only the properties of (p) and (q). That is generally insufficient. Interference devices, scattering media, cavities, gravitational lenses, and time-dependent boundaries can alter detection outcomes through intermediate interactions.

The more defensible RR-NPM construction is therefore relational but dynamically complete: it permits descriptions in terms of boundary data and correlations while retaining the field degrees of freedom needed to predict outcomes.

This restriction is essential. Removing a trajectory from the ontology does not license removing experimentally relevant dynamics from the mathematical model.

13. Equivalence to Established Relativity and the Burden of Novelty

13.1 Empirical equivalence

If RR-NPM retains the standard Maxwell equations, Lorentzian geometry, and quantum electrodynamics, and merely changes the ontological interpretation of photon trajectories, then its predictions are the same as those of the corresponding established theory.

In that case, it is a reformulation rather than a new empirical theory.

This is not a defect in a conceptual investigation. A reformulation can clarify which mathematical structures are essential and which explanatory images are optional. But a claim of new physical discovery requires more.

13.2 What would constitute a genuine extension?

A physically distinct extension would need at least one of the following:

1. A new relational dynamical law that is not equivalent to the established field equations.
2. A principled derivation of the metric or the invariant scale (c) from a deeper structure.
3. A new theorem constraining admissible electromagnetic states or causal relations beyond existing theory.
4. A quantitatively testable deviation from a standard prediction, consistent with existing experimental bounds.
5. A proof that the proposed relational variables are sufficient to reconstruct the relevant field observables under stated conditions.

Without such a result, the framework should not claim to have replaced Einstein’s relativity.

13.3 The correct interpretation of “no motion”

The strongest defensible conclusion at this stage is that photon motion need not be fundamental to the ontology of a relativistic field theory.

The stronger claim that light has no propagation, that photons are stationary in every frame, or that emission and detection are connected instantaneously is not established by the relational construction and would conflict with standard relativistic results if interpreted literally.

This boundary determines the scientific status of the proposal.

14. Proposed Research Program

A complete research program for RR-NPM would proceed through the following mathematical problems.

14.1 Event-structure reconstruction

Define a class of event structures with sufficient causal information to reconstruct a smooth Lorentzian manifold, subject to explicit topological, differentiability, and causality assumptions.

Prove which metric information is uniquely recoverable and identify the remaining conformal or scale ambiguities.

14.2 Field reconstruction

Determine whether a relational data set containing suitable electromagnetic correlation functions and detector records is sufficient to reconstruct the field observables needed for prediction.

The problem is not simply to reconstruct a path. It is to reconstruct the dynamical quantities that govern measurable outcomes.

14.3 Covariant dynamics

Formulate the theory so that its fundamental variables and equations transform consistently under coordinate changes and local Lorentz transformations.

Any proposed replacement for the metric or field variables must preserve the relevant conservation laws or derive their appropriate generalization.

14.4 Quantum measurement

Specify the relation between preparation events, field states, detector couplings, and observed counts. Establish whether the trajectory-free ontology contributes any explanatory or mathematical structure beyond ordinary quantum field theory.

14.5 Empirical separation

If new dynamics are introduced, derive a quantitative prediction and identify an experiment capable of distinguishing it from standard relativity or quantum electrodynamics.

The test must be defined before examining the relevant experimental outcome, and its parameters must not be adjusted retrospectively to reproduce that outcome.

15. Conclusions

We have developed a candidate formulation of relativistic physics in which photon motion is not taken as a fundamental primitive. The framework begins with physical events, causal and null relations, electromagnetic fields, and the spacetime geometry that constrains their interactions.

The resulting formulation preserves the essential mathematical content of established relativity:

* The Lorentz-invariant spacetime interval.
* The null condition for vacuum electromagnetic characteristics.
* The invariant vacuum speed (c).
* Maxwellian field dynamics.
* The relativistic energy-momentum relations of photons.
* The absence of a photon rest frame.
* The geometrical-optics description of light in terms of null characteristics.
* The curved-spacetime structure of gravitational redshift and light propagation.

The principal change is ontological rather than dynamical. The framework treats photon trajectories as derived representations instead of primitive physical objects. It therefore allows one to speak precisely of light without fundamental photon motion, provided that “motion” refers to an assumed individual trajectory rather than to the observable propagation and causal influence of electromagnetic fields.

The investigation also establishes a strict limit. A photon cannot be assigned zero spatial velocity in every inertial frame while preserving ordinary Lorentzian relativity. Nor does the absence of a photon rest frame imply that light is instantaneous or that the interval between emission and detection vanishes for all observers.

The proposed formulation consequently leaves the empirical structure of relativity intact while changing the proposed order of explanation:

[
\boxed{
\text{relational structure}
\longrightarrow
\text{field dynamics}
\longrightarrow
\text{observable propagation}.
}
]

Whether this order of explanation yields a deeper physical theory depends on future work. A genuine extension must reconstruct the necessary field dynamics, demonstrate mathematical completeness, and provide a result that cannot be obtained merely by changing the interpretation of existing equations.

The central research question is thus no longer whether a photon can be made stationary without contradiction. It is whether the full relativistic structure of electromagnetic phenomena can be formulated and understood without making the motion of an individual photon a primitive feature of reality.

That is the precise and mathematically tractable version of the proposal.
