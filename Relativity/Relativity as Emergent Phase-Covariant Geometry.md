## 1. Relativity 0.0 as a phase-theoretic theorem

Relativity 0.0 states that no frame is absolute and that physical reality is the invariant content preserved under admissible transformations. In UPT, this is not an external methodological rule imposed upon a finished spacetime theory. It follows from the fact that a physical configuration is never an isolated phase representative \(\Phi\in\mathcal C_\Phi\), but an admissible orbit of such representatives. If \(g\in\mathscr G_\Phi\) is a phase transformation, then

\[
\Phi\sim g\cdot\Phi,
\qquad
\mathscr F[g\cdot\Phi;g\cdot\lambda]
 =g\cdot\mathscr F[\Phi;\lambda].
\tag{1}
\]

The physical state space is consequently

\[
\mathcal P_\Phi
:=\mathcal S_\Phi/\mathscr G_\Phi,
\qquad
\mathcal S_\Phi
:=\{\Phi\in\mathcal C_\Phi:\mathscr F[\Phi;\lambda]=0\}.
\tag{2}
\]

A frame is a local section of the quotient presentation: it is a choice of representative, local order-parameter coordinates, and phase-comparison rule. It is useful, but it is not primitive physical content. A physical observable is therefore a functional \(\mathcal O\) satisfying

\[
\mathcal O[g\cdot\Phi]=\mathcal O[\Phi],
\tag{3}
\]

or, more generally, a covariant section whose contractions, coincidence relations, spectra, holonomies, and transition probabilities are invariant. Equation (3) is the UPT realization of the Relativity 0.0 formula \(\mathrm{Phys}(D)=\mathrm{Inv}_{G}(D)\). It excludes both an absolute frame and indiscriminate relativism: only transformations that preserve the admissibility problem and operational comparison relation belong to \(\mathscr G_\Phi\).

> **Principle (phase-relational objectivity).** A statement has physical content precisely when it descends from a phase representative to a well-defined invariant or covariant object on \(\mathcal P_\Phi\).

The relativistic program is therefore contained in the UPT hierarchy

\[
\boxed{

\text{geometry}
\longrightarrow
\text{connections}

\text{fields}

This paper derives the geometric middle of (4) and identifies the conditions under which it has the special- and general-relativistic form. The target is not the mere covariant rewriting of an arbitrary system. Formal coordinate covariance can always be manufactured; substantive relativity requires an operationally nontrivial quotient, response cones, transport, and invariant coincidence structure. This is exactly the distinction emphasized by the Relativity 0.0 manuscript and by the historical discussion of general covariance.[3]

## 2. Primitive phase data and the stability hierarchy

### 2.1 Phase bundle and admissibility

Let \(\mathcal X\) be a pregeometric relation domain. It may be a smooth manifold, a stratified complex, a causal graph, or a groupoid of phase comparisons. It is **not** assumed to carry a Lorentzian metric. Let

\[
\Phi\in\Gamma(E_\Phi)=:\mathcal C_\Phi.
\tag{6}

\[
\boxed{\mathscr F[\Phi;\lambda]=0,}
\tag{7}

No spacetime volume form has been inserted in (8): \(d\mu_\Phi\) is a phase measure or relation measure. If it reduces in a stable branch to \(\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x\), that reduction is part of the result.

\boxed{

\tag{11}

The susceptibility is not merely a thermodynamic analogy. It is the operator that specifies how an admissible phase structure reacts to an infinitesimal source. Geometry emerges from its relational composition.

### 4.2 Derivation of the boost law

Let two homogeneous response frames differ by relative response velocity \(v\) in the \(x\)-direction. The most general linear map preserving (25), respecting reciprocity and reducing to identity at \(v=0\), is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}ct'\\x'\end{pmatrix}

\[
t'=\gamma\!\left(t-\frac{vx}{c_\Phi^2}\right),

\tag{34}

Equations (32)–(34) recover special-relativistic kinematics in the homogeneous phase. ## 6. From phase action to the Einstein sector

### 6.1 Coarse-grained phase action

Let \(\mathcal R_\ell\) coarse-grain phase modes below the response scale \(\ell\). The effective action on the Lorentzian phase branch is defined by

\[

\tag{50}
\]

Here \(\Psi\) collects stable defects and nonmetric phase modes. The definition expresses the ontological hierarchy (4) at the level of the action: geometry is a collective variable of the phase path integral or, in a nonquantum realization, of the coarse phase functional.

Assume the following effective closure conditions.

| Label | Phase-derivable closure condition | Consequence |

| C5 | Stable defect response defines a covariantly conserved \(T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}\) | Consistent metric sourcing |

Under C1–C5, derivative expansion gives

\[

=\int_{M_\mathrm{eff}}d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[

\tag{51}
\]

The constants \(\kappa_\Phi\), \(\Lambda_\Phi\), and \(\ell_\Phi\) are phase response coefficients: they are determined by the microscopic \(\mathscr F\), its phase measure, and the chosen stable branch. They are not inserted as fundamental metric couplings.

### 6.2 Metric variation and field equation

Define the effective stress tensor by

\[
T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}
:=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}
\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm m}^{\mathrm{eff}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\tag{52}
\]

Variation of the relevant terms in (51) yields

\[
\delta S_{\mathrm{eff}}
=\frac12\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}
\left[
\frac{1}{\kappa_\Phi}
\bigl(G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda_\Phi g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)
-T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}
-\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}
\right]\delta g^{\mu\nu},
\tag{53}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}
:=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}
\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
\int\sqrt{-g}\sum_{n\ge2}\ell_\Phi^{2n-2}\mathcal I_{2n}
\tag{54}
\]

is the residual phase correction tensor. Hence

\[
\boxed{
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda_\Phi g_{\mu\nu}
=\kappa_\Phi T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}
+\kappa_\Phi\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}.}
\tag{55}
\]

The contracted Bianchi identity, which follows geometrically from the curvature construction, implies

\[
\nabla^\mu\left(
T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}
\right)=0.
\tag{56}
\]

This is the phase-reduced expression of the variational symmetry identities treated by Noether.[6] In the infrared fixed phase for which higher phase invariants are irrelevant, \(\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}\to0\), and (55) becomes the Einstein equation with emergent constants:

\[
\boxed{
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda_\Phi g_{\mu\nu}
=\kappa_\Phi T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu}.}
\tag{57}
\]

Equation (57) is general relativity as an effective phase equation. Einstein’s geometric formulation identifies the metric with the gravitational field and places observable content in coordinate-independent coincidence relations.[5] UPT derives that identification from phase response and phase transport.
