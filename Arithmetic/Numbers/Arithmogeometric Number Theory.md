# Arithmogeometric Number Theory  
## A Tensorial Calculus of Valuation Manifolds, Arithmetic Curvature, and Factorization

**Abstract.**  
We introduce a new number-theoretic framework, *arithmogeometric number theory*, in which positive integers are treated as points of a discrete valuation manifold whose coordinate directions are indexed by primes. Multiplication becomes translation in valuation space, divisibility becomes a partial order, and prime factorization becomes a tensorial spectral decomposition. We define arithmetic metrics, discrete Levi-Civita connections, curvature tensors, Ricci tensors, scalar curvature, and an arithmetic Laplacian on divisor strata. The theory yields new arithmetic invariants: a tensorial divisor volume, a prime-interaction curvature index, a local zeta tensor, and an arithmetic Einstein-type field equation. We prove that the classical divisor function appears as the determinant of the identity plus the prime-exponent tensor, that the diagonal valuation metric is arithmetically flat, and that a rank-one interacting metric produces scalar curvature whose leading term is a pairwise prime-interaction sum. The paper develops the formal foundations, gives explicit computations, and proposes a research program for curvature-based analytic number theory.

**Keywords.** valuation manifold, arithmetic curvature, tensor factorization, divisor lattice, discrete Levi-Civita connection, arithmetic Laplacian, local zeta tensor.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical number theory usually regards a positive integer \(n\) as an element of the multiplicative monoid \(\mathbb N_{>0}\), with prime factorization

\[
n=\prod_{p}p^{v_p(n)}.
\]

The fundamental theorem of arithmetic asserts that the exponent vector

\[
v(n)=\bigl(v_p(n)\bigr)_p
\]

is finite-support and unique. In this paper we promote this observation into a geometric and tensorial theory. Instead of treating \(n\) merely as a scalar, we treat it as a point in a discrete manifold whose coordinates are prime valuations. This produces a new theory of numbers, which we call **arithmogeometric number theory**.

The central objects are:

1. **Valuation manifolds** \(M_S\), where \(S\) is a finite set of primes and points are exponent vectors \(a=(a^1,\dots,a^m)\in\mathbb N^m\).

2. **Arithmetic tensor fields**, defined on \(M_S\) using the commuting prime-shift operators \(E_i\).

3. **Arithmetic metrics** \(g_{ij}\), depending on valuations, which turn divisibility strata into weighted discrete geometries.

4. **Arithmetic curvature tensors** \(R^{\ell}{}_{kij}\), Ricci tensors \(R_{ij}\), scalar curvature \(R\), and Einstein-type tensors.

5. **Tensorial factorization invariants**, including the prime-exponent tensor \(F(n)\), whose trace is the total number of prime factors and whose determinant gives the divisor function.

The resulting theory is not a reformulation of classical analytic number theory but a new structural calculus. It allows one to attach to an integer geometric quantities such as curvature, volume, and Laplacian spectra. In particular, we shall see that prime factorization can be expressed as a tensor spectral theorem, that divisor functions become determinant and trace invariants, and that interactions among distinct prime factors generate arithmetic curvature.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 defines valuation manifolds and prime-shift operators. Section 3 introduces arithmetic metrics and computes their inverses and volume densities. Section 4 develops a discrete Levi-Civita connection and curvature tensor. Section 5 gives a tensorial formulation of factorization and divisor sums. Section 6 defines the arithmetic Laplacian and proposes arithmetic field equations. Section 7 contains explicit examples. Section 8 states conjectures and a future research program.

## 2. Valuation Manifolds and Arithmetic Frames

### 2.1 Finite prime frames

Fix a finite ordered set of primes

\[
S=\{p_1,\dots,p_m\}.
\]

Define the **valuation manifold**

\[
M_S=\mathbb N^m.
\]

A point \(a\in M_S\) has coordinates

\[
a=(a^1,\dots,a^m),\qquad a^i\in\mathbb N.
\]

To \(a\) we associate the \(S\)-integer

\[
n(a)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}p_i^{a^i}.
\]

Thus \(M_S\) is the exponent lattice of the multiplicative monoid generated by \(S\). Multiplication of \(S\)-integers corresponds to addition in \(M_S\):

\[
n(a)n(b)=n(a+b).
\]

Divisibility corresponds to the product order:

\[
n(b)\mid n(a)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b^i\le a^i\quad\text{for all }i.
\]

The full positive integers are recovered as the direct limit over finite prime sets \(S\). For local tensorial computations, it is preferable to work with finite \(S\), thereby avoiding infinite-dimensional convergence issues. Global invariants are then obtained by restriction to the active prime set

\[
S(n)=\{p:v_p(n)>0\}.
\]

### 2.2 Prime-shift operators

For each coordinate direction \(i\), define the shift operator

\[
\sigma_i:M_S\to M_S,\qquad \sigma_i(a)=a+e_i,
\]

where \(e_i\) is the \(i\)-th standard basis vector. The corresponding discrete directional difference operator acting on scalar functions \(f:M_S\to\mathbb R\) is

\[
E_i f(a)=f(a+e_i)-f(a).
\]

These operators commute:

\[
E_iE_j=E_jE_i.
\]

We interpret \(E_i\) as the arithmetic vector field corresponding to multiplication by the prime \(p_i\).

For higher differences,

\[
E_i^2 f(a)=f(a+2e_i)-2f(a+e_i)+f(a),
\]

and for mixed differences,

\[
E_iE_j f(a)=f(a+e_i+e_j)-f(a+e_i)-f(a+e_j)+f(a).
\]

### 2.3 Formal tangent and cotangent spaces

At each \(a\in M_S\), introduce a formal tangent space

\[
T_aM_S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\partial_1,\dots,\partial_m\},
\]

with dual cotangent space spanned by \(\{\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m\}\), satisfying

\[
\theta^i(\partial_j)=\delta^i_j.
\]

A tensor field of type \((r,s)\) is a map

\[
T:M_S\to V^{\otimes r}\otimes V^{*\otimes s},
\]

with components

\[
T^{i_1\cdots i_r}{}_{j_1\cdots j_s}(a).
\]

We use Einstein summation over repeated upper and lower indices unless otherwise stated.

### 2.4 Valuation and logarithm maps

For an \(S\)-integer \(n=n(a)\), define its **valuation vector**

\[
v(n)=a^i\partial_i.
\]

Define also the logarithmic embedding

\[
\lambda(n)=\log n=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^i\log p_i.
\]

The discrete gradient of \(\lambda\) is constant:

\[
E_i\lambda=\log p_i.
\]

This simple fact will be important in the definition of arithmetic energy tensors.

## 3. Arithmetic Metric Tensors

We now introduce metrics on valuation manifolds. These metrics encode how arithmetic distance changes as prime exponents increase.

### 3.1 Weighted valuation metric

Choose positive weights

\[
\alpha_i>0,\qquad i=1,\dots,m.
\]

Define

\[
h_i(a)=\frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}.
\]

Let \(\beta\in\mathbb R\) be an interaction parameter. The **arithmogeometric metric** is

\[
g_{ij}(a)=\delta_{ij}h_i(a)+\beta h_i(a)h_j(a).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
g=\left(\delta_{ij}\frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}\right)
+\beta
\left(\frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}\right)
\left(\frac{\alpha_j}{a^j+1}\right).
\]

The line element is

\[
ds^2
=
\sum_i \frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}(da^i)^2
+
\beta
\left(
\sum_i \frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}da^i
\right)^2.
\]

The first term is diagonal and measures independent prime-direction lengths. The second term couples all prime directions and is responsible for nontrivial curvature.

For positive definiteness, it suffices that \(\beta\ge0\). If \(\beta<0\), one must require

\[
1+\beta H(a)>0,
\]

where

\[
H(a)=\sum_i h_i(a).
\]

### 3.2 Inverse metric

Let

\[
H(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_i(a).
\]

**Proposition 3.1.**  
The inverse metric is

\[
g^{ij}(a)
=
\frac{\delta^{ij}}{h_i(a)}
-
\frac{\beta}{1+\beta H(a)}.
\]

**Proof.**  
Write \(g=D+\beta uu^{T}\), where

\[
D_{ij}=\delta_{ij}h_i,
\qquad
u_i=h_i.
\]

By the Sherman–Morrison formula,

\[
(D+\beta uu^T)^{-1}
=
D^{-1}
-
\frac{\beta D^{-1}uu^TD^{-1}}{1+\beta u^TD^{-1}u}.
\]

Now

\[
(D^{-1})^{ij}=\frac{\delta^{ij}}{h_i},
\]

and

\[
(D^{-1}u)^i=1,
\qquad
u^TD^{-1}u=\sum_i h_i=H.
\]

Therefore

\[
g^{ij}
=
\frac{\delta^{ij}}{h_i}
-
\frac{\beta}{1+\beta H}.
\]

∎

### 3.3 Volume density

By the matrix determinant lemma,

\[
\det g
=
\det(D)\left(1+\beta u^TD^{-1}u\right).
\]

Hence

\[
\det g
=
\left(\prod_i h_i\right)(1+\beta H).
\]

The Riemannian volume density is therefore

\[
\rho(a)=\sqrt{\det g(a)}
=
\sqrt{
\left(\prod_i \frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}\right)
\left(1+\beta\sum_j\frac{\alpha_j}{a^j+1}\right)
}.
\]

For \(\beta=0\), this reduces to

\[
\rho_0(a)=\prod_i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}}.
\]

This density will generate a geometric refinement of divisor sums.

## 4. Arithmetic Connection and Curvature

We now develop a discrete tensor calculus analogous to Riemannian geometry, but adapted to the arithmetic difference operators \(E_i\).

### 4.1 Discrete Levi-Civita connection

Define a torsion-free connection by

\[
\nabla_{E_i}E_j=\Gamma^k{}_{ij}E_k.
\]

We require the discrete Koszul formula

\[
2g_{k\ell}\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{ij}
=
E_i g_{jk}+E_j g_{ik}-E_k g_{ij}.
\]

Because the frame \(\{E_i\}\) is commutative, the torsion tensor

\[
T^k{}_{ij}=\Gamma^k{}_{ij}-\Gamma^k{}_{ji}
\]

vanishes automatically from the symmetry of the right-hand side in \(i,j\).

Metric compatibility is encoded by

\[
E_k g_{ij}
=
g_{\ell j}\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{ki}
+
g_{i\ell}\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{kj}.
\]

Thus \(\nabla\) is the discrete Levi-Civita connection of \(g\).

### 4.2 The diagonal metric and flatness

Set \(\beta=0\). Then

\[
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}h_i,
\qquad
g^{ij}=\frac{\delta^{ij}}{h_i}.
\]

We compute

\[
E_k h_i
=
\delta_{ki}
\left(
\frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+2}-\frac{\alpha_i}{a^i+1}
\right)
=
-\delta_{ki}
\frac{\alpha_i}{(a^i+1)(a^i+2)}.
\]

Introduce

\[
c_i(a)=\frac{1}{a^i+2},
\qquad
b_i(a)=h_i(a)c_i(a)
=
\frac{\alpha_i}{(a^i+1)(a^i+2)}.
\]

Then

\[
E_k h_i=-\delta_{ki}b_i.
\]

**Theorem 4.1.**  
For the diagonal valuation metric \(\beta=0\), the connection coefficients are

\[
\Gamma^k{}_{ij}
=
-\frac12\delta_{ij}\delta^k_i c_i.
\]

Equivalently, the only nonzero components are

\[
\Gamma^i{}_{ii}
=
-\frac{1}{2(a^i+2)}.
\]

**Proof.**  
The Koszul formula gives

\[
2h_\ell\Gamma^\ell{}_{ij}
=
E_i(\delta_{jk}h_j)
+
E_j(\delta_{ik}h_i)
-
E_k(\delta_{ij}h_i).
\]

If \(i\neq j\), all terms vanish. If \(i=j\), then only the direction \(k=i\) contributes, yielding

\[
2h_i\Gamma^i{}_{ii}
=
-b_i-b_i+b_i
=
-b_i.
\]

Thus

\[
\Gamma^i{}_{ii}
=
-\frac{b_i}{2h_i}
=
-\frac{c_i}{2}.
\]

∎

Define the curvature tensor by

\[
R(E_i,E_j)E_k
=
R^{\ell}{}_{kij}E_\ell,
\]

with

\[
R^{\ell}{}_{kij}
=
E_i\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{jk}
-
E_j\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{ik}
+
\Gamma^m{}_{jk}\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{im}
-
\Gamma^m{}_{ik}\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{jm}.
\]

**Theorem 4.2.**  
The diagonal valuation metric is arithmetically flat:

\[
R^{\ell}{}_{kij}=0
\]

for all indices.

**Proof.**  
For \(\beta=0\), the only nonzero connection coefficients are \(\Gamma^i{}_{ii}\). Hence \(\Gamma^{\ell}{}_{jk}\) is nonzero only when \(j=k=\ell\). If \(i\neq j\), the derivative terms vanish because \(E_i c_j=0\) unless \(i=j\). If \(i=j\), the two derivative terms cancel by antisymmetry. The quadratic terms are nonzero only when all three directions coincide, in which case the two products are identical and cancel. Therefore every curvature component vanishes. ∎

Thus the independent-prime valuation geometry carries no intrinsic curvature. Curvature arises only when distinct prime directions interact.

### 4.3 Interacting metric and first-order curvature

We now allow \(\beta\neq0\). Assume \(|\beta|\) is small and expand in powers of \(\beta\).

From Proposition 3.1,

\[
g^{ij}
=
\frac{\delta^{ij}}{h_i}
-
\beta
+
O(\beta^2).
\]

A direct expansion of the Koszul formula gives the following.

**Theorem 4.3.**  
To first order in \(\beta\),

\[
\Gamma^k{}_{ij}
=
-\frac12\delta_{ij}\delta^k_i c_i
-
\frac{\beta}{2}\delta_{ij}b_i
+
O(\beta^2).
\]

The first term is the flat diagonal connection. The second term couples all upper directions whenever the lower direction equals the vector direction.

Let

\[
C(a)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}c_r(a).
\]

The Ricci tensor is defined by contraction:

\[
R_{kj}=R^i{}_{kij}.
\]

The scalar curvature is

\[
R=g^{kj}R_{kj}.
\]

**Theorem 4.4.**  
For the interacting valuation metric, the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature satisfy

\[
R_{ij}
=
\frac{\beta}{4}\delta_{ij}b_i(C-c_i)
+
O(\beta^2),
\]

and

\[
R
=
\frac{\beta}{4}
\left(
C^2-\sum_i c_i^2
\right)
+
O(\beta^2).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
R
=
\frac{\beta}{2}
\sum_{i<j}c_i c_j
+
O(\beta^2).
\]

**Proof.**  
Write

\[
\Gamma=\Gamma^{(0)}+\beta\Gamma^{(1)}+O(\beta^2),
\]

where

\[
\Gamma^{(0)\,k}{}_{ij}
=
-\frac12\delta_{ij}\delta^k_i c_i,
\]

and

\[
\Gamma^{(1)\,k}{}_{ij}
=
-\frac12\delta_{ij}b_i.
\]

The linearized curvature is

\[
R^{(1)\,a}{}_{cbd}
=
E_b\Gamma^{(1)\,a}{}_{dc}
-
E_d\Gamma^{(1)\,a}{}_{bc}
+
\Gamma^{(0)\,m}{}_{dc}\Gamma^{(1)\,a}{}_{bm}
+
\Gamma^{(1)\,m}{}_{dc}\Gamma^{(0)\,a}{}_{bm}
-
\Gamma^{(0)\,m}{}_{bc}\Gamma^{(1)\,a}{}_{dm}
-
\Gamma^{(1)\,m}{}_{bc}\Gamma^{(0)\,a}{}_{dm}.
\]

Using

\[
\Gamma^{(1)\,a}{}_{bc}
=
-\frac12\delta_{bc}b_b,
\]

and

\[
E_i b_j=-2\delta_{ij}d_j,
\]

with

\[
d_j=\frac{\alpha_j}{(a^j+1)(a^j+2)(a^j+3)},
\]

the derivative terms cancel in the Ricci contraction, and the remaining algebraic terms yield

\[
R^{(1)\,a}{}_{cbd}
=
\frac14
\left(
\delta_{dc}\delta^a_b c_b b_d
-
\delta_{bc}\delta^a_d c_d b_b
\right).
\]

Contracting \(a\) with \(b\),

\[
R^{(1)}_{cd}
=
R^{(1)\,a}{}_{cad}
=
\frac14\delta_{cd}b_c(C-c_c).
\]

Finally, since the zeroth-order inverse metric is \(g_0^{ij}=\delta^{ij}/h_i\),

\[
R^{(1)}
=
g_0^{ij}R^{(1)}_{ij}
=
\frac14\sum_i\frac{b_i}{h_i}(C-c_i).
\]

But

\[
\frac{b_i}{h_i}=c_i,
\]

so

\[
R^{(1)}
=
\frac14\sum_i c_i(C-c_i)
=
\frac14
\left(
C^2-\sum_i c_i^2
\right).
\]

Since

\[
C^2-\sum_i c_i^2
=
2\sum_{i<j}c_i c_j,
\]

the result follows. ∎

### 4.4 Arithmetic curvature index

The leading scalar curvature is independent of the weights \(\alpha_i\). This motivates the following intrinsic arithmetic invariant.

For an integer \(n\) with active prime support \(S(n)\), define

\[
\mathcal K(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{p_i,p_j\mid n\\ i<j}}
\frac{1}{(v_{p_i}(n)+2)(v_{p_j}(n)+2)}.
\]

We call \(\mathcal K(n)\) the **prime-interaction curvature index**.

For small \(\beta\),

\[
R(n)
=
2\beta\mathcal K(n)+O(\beta^2).
\]

Thus scalar curvature detects the presence of at least two distinct prime factors.

**Corollary 4.5.**  
With respect to the active support,

\[
\mathcal K(n)=0
\]

if and only if \(n\) is a prime power.

**Proof.**  
If \(n\) is a prime power, the active support contains one prime, so the sum over pairs is empty. Conversely, if \(n\) has at least two distinct prime divisors, each term in the sum is positive, so \(\mathcal K(n)>0\). ∎

This gives a geometric distinction between prime powers and composite integers with multiple prime factors.

## 5. Tensorial Factorization and Divisor Invariants

We now express factorization, divisor functions, and divisor sums in tensorial language.

### 5.1 Prime-exponent tensor

For \(n\in\mathbb N_{>0}\), define the **prime-exponent tensor**

\[
F(n)^i{}_j=v_{p_i}(n)\delta^i{}_j.
\]

This is a diagonal endomorphism of the valuation tangent space. Its eigenvalues are the prime exponents of \(n\).

The trace is

\[
\operatorname{tr}F(n)
=
\sum_i v_{p_i}(n)
=
\Omega(n),
\]

where \(\Omega(n)\) is the total number of prime factors counted with multiplicity.

The rank of \(F(n)\) over the active support is

\[
\operatorname{rank}F(n)
=
\omega(n),
\]

where \(\omega(n)\) is the number of distinct prime divisors.

Most notably, the divisor function appears as a determinant.

**Theorem 5.1.**  
Let \(I\) denote the identity tensor \(\delta^i{}_j\). Then

\[
\tau(n)
=
\det(I+F(n)),
\]

where \(\tau(n)\) is the number of positive divisors of \(n\).

**Proof.**  
Since \(F(n)\) is diagonal,

\[
\det(I+F(n))
=
\prod_i(1+v_{p_i}(n)).
\]

By the classical divisor formula,

\[
\tau(n)=\prod_i(1+v_{p_i}(n)).
\]

∎

Thus the classical divisor function is the determinant of a tensorial object naturally attached to \(n\).

### 5.2 Spectral formulation of unique factorization

Let the primes be labeled. The labeled spectral data of \(F(n)\) are the pairs

\[
(p_i,v_{p_i}(n)).
\]

These determine \(n\) uniquely:

\[
n=\prod_i p_i^{v_{p_i}(n)}.
\]

Conversely, any integer gives such a diagonal tensor. Hence the fundamental theorem of arithmetic may be restated as follows.

**Theorem 5.2.**  
The map

\[
n\longmapsto F(n)
\]

embeds the multiplicative monoid of positive integers into the commutative monoid of diagonal prime-labeled endomorphisms. Unique factorization is equivalent to the uniqueness of the labeled spectral decomposition of \(F(n)\).

This is not merely a change of notation: it permits factorization to be studied through tensor rank, trace invariants, determinant invariants, and curvature couplings.

### 5.3 Arithmetic volume of a divisor lattice

For \(n=n(a)\), the divisor lattice is the box

\[
D(n)=\{k\in\mathbb N^m:0\le k^i\le a^i\}.
\]

Define the **arithmogeometric divisor volume**

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_g(n)
=
\sum_{d\mid n}\rho(v(d)),
\]

where \(\rho=\sqrt{\det g}\).

For the diagonal metric \(\beta=0\),

\[
\rho_0(k)=\prod_i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_i}{k^i+1}}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{0}(n)
=
\sum_{0\le k^i\le a^i}
\prod_i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_i}{k^i+1}}.
\]

Because the summand factorizes,

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{0}(n)
=
\prod_i
\left(
\sqrt{\alpha_i}
\sum_{r=0}^{a^i}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}}
\right).
\]

**Theorem 5.3.**  
For the diagonal valuation metric, \(\operatorname{Vol}_0\) is multiplicative on coprime integers. If \(\gcd(m,n)=1\), then

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_0(mn)
=
\operatorname{Vol}_0(m)\operatorname{Vol}_0(n).
\]

**Proof.**  
If \(m\) and \(n\) are coprime, their active prime sets are disjoint. The divisor lattice of \(mn\) is the Cartesian product of the divisor lattices of \(m\) and \(n\). The volume density factorizes over disjoint prime directions, hence the sum factorizes. ∎

Since

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_0(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\rho_0(d),
\]

Möbius inversion gives

\[
\rho_0(n)
=
\sum_{d\mid n}\mu\left(\frac nd\right)\operatorname{Vol}_0(d).
\]

Thus the geometric density \(\rho_0\) is recovered from divisor volume by the classical Möbius transform.

### 5.4 Local zeta tensor

Define the **local zeta generating tensor** of \(n\) by

\[
Z_n(s,t)
=
\sum_{d\mid n}
d^{-s}
\exp\left(t_i v^i(d)\right),
\]

where \(t=(t_1,\dots,t_m)\) are auxiliary covector parameters.

Since divisors correspond to independent choices \(0\le k^i\le a^i\),

\[
Z_n(s,t)
=
\prod_{i=1}^m
\sum_{k=0}^{a^i}
p_i^{-sk}e^{t_i k}.
\]

Thus

\[
Z_n(s,t)
=
\prod_{i=1}^m
\frac{
1-\left(p_i^{-s}e^{t_i}\right)^{a^i+1}
}{
1-p_i^{-s}e^{t_i}
}.
\]

Setting \(t=0\) recovers the finite Euler product

\[
Z_n(s,0)
=
\sum_{d\mid n}d^{-s}
=
\prod_{i=1}^m
\frac{1-p_i^{-s(a^i+1)}}{1-p_i^{-s}}.
\]

Moment tensors are obtained by differentiation:

\[
M_{i_1\cdots i_r}(s;n)
=
\left.
\frac{\partial^r Z_n}{\partial t_{i_1}\cdots\partial t_{i_r}}
\right|_{t=0}.
\]

In particular,

\[
M_i(s;n)
=
\sum_{d\mid n}d^{-s}v_i(d).
\]

The logarithmic Hessian

\[
\mathcal F_{ij}(s;n)
=
\left.
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t_i\partial t_j}
\log Z_n(s,t)
\right|_{t=0}
\]

defines a positive semidefinite covariance tensor on divisor valuations.

Let

\[
q_i=p_i^{-s}.
\]

Because \(Z_n\) factorizes,

\[
\mathcal F_{ij}(s;n)=\delta_{ij}\sigma_i^2(s;a^i),
\]

where

\[
\sigma_i^2(s;a^i)
=
\left(
q_i\frac{\partial}{\partial q_i}
\right)^2
\log
\left(
\frac{1-q_i^{a^i+1}}{1-q_i}
\right).
\]

Thus the local zeta function carries not only scalar divisor information but a full tensorial covariance structure.

## 6. Arithmetic Laplacian and Field Equations

### 6.1 Hessian and Laplacian

For a scalar function \(f:M_S\to\mathbb R\), define the arithmetic Hessian by

\[
\nabla_i\nabla_j f
=
E_iE_j f-\Gamma^k{}_{ij}E_k f.
\]

The arithmetic Laplacian is the trace

\[
\Delta f
=
g^{ij}\nabla_i\nabla_j f.
\]

For the diagonal metric \(\beta=0\), using

\[
g^{ii}=\frac{a^i+1}{\alpha_i},
\qquad
\Gamma^i{}_{ii}=-\frac{1}{2(a^i+2)},
\]

we obtain

\[
\Delta f
=
\sum_i
\frac{a^i+1}{\alpha_i}
\left[
E_i^2 f
+
\frac{1}{2(a^i+2)}E_i f
\right].
\]

This is a second-order arithmetic difference operator. It is the natural Laplace-type operator associated with the valuation metric.

### 6.2 Logarithmic energy tensor

Let

\[
\lambda(a)=\log n(a)=\sum_i a^i\log p_i.
\]

Then

\[
\nabla_i\lambda=E_i\lambda=\log p_i.
\]

Define the logarithmic energy-momentum tensor

\[
T_{ij}
=
\nabla_i\lambda\nabla_j\lambda
-
\frac12 g_{ij}g^{kl}\nabla_k\lambda\nabla_l\lambda.
\]

For \(\beta=0\),

\[
g^{kl}\nabla_k\lambda\nabla_l\lambda
=
\sum_r
\frac{(\log p_r)^2}{h_r}.
\]

Therefore

\[
T_{ij}
=
(\log p_i)(\log p_j)
-
\frac12 g_{ij}
\sum_r
\frac{(\log p_r)^2}{h_r}.
\]

This tensor measures the distribution of logarithmic prime weights across valuation directions.

### 6.3 Arithmetic Einstein equation

Define the arithmetic Einstein tensor

\[
G_{ij}=R_{ij}-\frac12Rg_{ij}.
\]

We propose the **arithmetic field equation**

\[
G_{ij}+\Lambda g_{ij}
=
\kappa T_{ij},
\]

where \(\Lambda\) is an arithmetic cosmological constant and \(\kappa>0\) is a coupling constant.

Taking the trace gives

\[
\left(1-\frac m2\right)R+m\Lambda
=
\kappa g^{ij}T_{ij}.
\]

But

\[
g^{ij}T_{ij}
=
\left(1-\frac m2\right)
g^{kl}\nabla_k\lambda\nabla_l\lambda.
\]

Hence

\[
\left(1-\frac m2\right)
\left(
R-\kappa|\nabla\lambda|^2
\right)
+
m\Lambda
=
0.
\]

In dimension \(m=2\), this reduces to

\[
2\Lambda=\kappa|\nabla\lambda|^2.
\]

For the flat diagonal metric, \(G_{ij}=0\), so the field equation forces \(T_{ij}\) to be proportional to \(g_{ij}\). In general this fails, showing that prime-logarithmic stress requires the interacting curvature terms generated by \(\beta\neq0\).

## 7. Examples

### 7.1 The integer \(6\)

Let

\[
6=2^1 3^1.
\]

Take \(S=\{2,3\}\), so \(a=(1,1)\). For simplicity set \(\alpha_1=\alpha_2=1\).

Then

\[
h_1=h_2=\frac12,
\]

and

\[
g_{ij}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac12 & 0\\
0 & \frac12
\end{pmatrix}
+
\frac{\beta}{4}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The prime-exponent tensor is

\[
F(6)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{tr}F(6)=2,
\qquad
\det(I+F(6))=4,
\]

recovering \(\Omega(6)=2\) and \(\tau(6)=4\).

The curvature coefficients are

\[
c_1=c_2=\frac13.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal K(6)=\frac{1}{3\cdot3}=\frac19,
\]

and

\[
R(6)=\frac{\beta}{18}+O(\beta^2).
\]

The diagonal divisor volume is

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_0(6)
=
\left(1+\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)^2.
\]

The local zeta function is

\[
Z_6(s,0)=(1+2^{-s})(1+3^{-s}).
\]

### 7.2 The integer \(12\)

Let

\[
12=2^2 3^1.
\]

Thus \(a=(2,1)\). Again take \(\alpha_1=\alpha_2=1\).

Then

\[
h_1=\frac13,
\qquad
h_2=\frac12.
\]

The prime-exponent tensor is

\[
F(12)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{tr}F(12)=3,
\]

and

\[
\det(I+F(12))=(1+2)(1+1)=6,
\]

which equals \(\tau(12)=6\).

The curvature coefficients are

\[
c_1=\frac14,
\qquad
c_2=\frac13.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal K(12)=\frac{1}{4\cdot3}=\frac{1}{12},
\]

and

\[
R(12)=\frac{\beta}{24}+O(\beta^2).
\]

The diagonal divisor volume is

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_0(12)
=
\left(
1+\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac1{\sqrt3}
\right)
\left(
1+\frac1{\sqrt2}
\right).
\]

The local zeta function is

\[
Z_{12}(s,0)
=
(1+2^{-s}+2^{-2s})(1+3^{-s}).
\]

### 7.3 Prime powers

Let

\[
n=p^a.
\]

The active support contains one prime. Hence

\[
\mathcal K(p^a)=0.
\]

Thus, to first order,

\[
R(p^a)=0.
\]

Prime powers are arithmetically flat in the interacting curvature sense. This gives a geometric characterization of numbers with only one prime direction.

## 8. Conjectures and Research Program

The preceding constructions suggest several directions for further development.

### Conjecture 8.1: Curvature growth of typical integers

Let

\[
\mathcal K(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{p_i,p_j\mid n\\ i<j}}
\frac{1}{(v_{p_i}(n)+2)(v_{p_j}(n)+2)}.
\]

For typical large integers, the number of distinct prime factors satisfies

\[
\omega(n)\sim\log\log n.
\]

If most active valuations contributing to typical curvature are small, then each active factor contributes approximately \(1/3\). Therefore one expects the heuristic average

\[
\frac1x\sum_{n\le x}\mathcal K(n)
\sim
\frac{1}{18}(\log\log x)^2
\]

up to lower-order terms.

A precise formulation requires a probabilistic model on valuation manifolds and a renormalization of inactive prime directions.

### Conjecture 8.2: Arithmetic Ricci flow

Consider a one-parameter family of arithmetic metrics \(g_{ij}(t)\) satisfying

\[
\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}
=
-2R_{ij}.
\]

This defines an **arithmetic Ricci flow** on valuation space. We conjecture that under this flow, prime-interaction curvature concentrates on strata corresponding to integers with many small prime factors, while prime-power strata remain flat.

### Conjecture 8.3: Spectral arithmetic

The arithmetic Laplacian

\[
\Delta
=
g^{ij}\nabla_i\nabla_j
\]

on finite divisor boxes should possess a discrete spectrum. We conjecture that spectral invariants of \(\Delta\) encode refined factorization data beyond \(\tau(n)\) and \(\Omega(n)\), including exponent distributions and prime-support geometry.

### Conjecture 8.4: Zeta-curvature correspondence

The local zeta tensor \(Z_n(s,t)\) and the arithmetic curvature tensor \(R^{\ell}{}_{kij}\) both arise from the valuation structure of \(n\). We conjecture that there exists a compactified valuation orbifold on which averaged zeta tensors can be represented as spectral traces of arithmetic Laplacians. Such a correspondence may provide a geometric framework for studying families of divisor zeta functions.

## 9. Conclusion

We have introduced arithmogeometric number theory, a tensorial and geometric framework for the study of integers. In this theory:

- integers are points in valuation manifolds;
- multiplication is translation;
- divisibility is a partial order;
- prime factorization is a tensor spectral decomposition;
- the divisor function is a determinant invariant;
- divisor lattices carry natural volume densities;
- prime interactions generate arithmetic curvature;
- integers admit Laplacians, energy tensors, and Einstein-type equations.

The theory transforms elementary arithmetic data into geometric objects. It suggests that integers possess an intrinsic discrete geometry whose curvature, volume, and spectral invariants merit systematic study.
