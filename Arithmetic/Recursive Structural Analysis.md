# Recursive Structural Analysis

**Preprint**

---

## Abstract

This paper develops **Recursive Structural Analysis (RSA)**, a framework in which the basic objects of analysis are not primitive limiting operations such as difference quotients and Riemann sums, but **recursively evolving structural operators** acting on spaces of functions, sections, distributions, and tensors. Within this framework, differentiation appears as the infinitesimal generator of a recursive structural flow, while integration appears as the corresponding accumulation, resolvent, or inverse structural operation. Classical calculus is recovered as a limiting case of more general recursive analytical processes, including nonlocal, fractional, geometric, and curvature-coupled operators.

The paper establishes the foundational definitions of RSA, proves generator and resolvent theorems for recursive structural systems, and develops applications to partial differential equations, functional analysis, harmonic analysis, and numerical computation. On manifolds and vector bundles, RSA naturally incorporates tensorial structure through parallel transport and structural kernels. The resulting formalism provides a unified analytical language for continuous and discrete analysis, for local and nonlocal operators, and for theoretical and computational methods.

**Keywords:** recursive structural analysis, structural operators, semigroups, generators, resolvents, partial differential equations, harmonic analysis, tensor analysis, numerical analysis.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical analysis is usually founded on two limiting constructions: the derivative as a limit of difference quotients, and the integral as a limit of accumulations. These constructions are extraordinarily successful, but they treat the limit as analytically prior to structure. In many modern problems — geometric PDEs, nonlocal operators, multiscale harmonic analysis, structure-preserving numerical methods — the more primitive object is not a bare limit but a **structural transformation** acting on a space of states.

The central thesis of this paper is the following.

> **RSA Principle.** Analytical operations are generated by recursively evolving structural operators. Differentiation and integration are limiting shadows of more general recursive structural processes.

In Recursive Structural Analysis, one begins with a family or sequence of operators
\[
\mathcal R_h : X \to X,
\]
where \(X\) is a Banach space, Hilbert space, Fréchet space, or space of sections of a vector bundle. The parameter \(h\) is a structural scale, not necessarily a spatial or temporal increment. The operators \(\mathcal R_h\) encode the admissible structural transformations of the underlying space: translations, diffusions, parallel transports, averaging kernels, multiscale projections, or nonlinear renormalizing maps.

When the operators are recursively composed,
\[
\mathcal R_{t+s} \approx \mathcal R_t \mathcal R_s,
\]
or more generally when they obey a recursion
\[
\mathcal R_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_n + \tau_n \mathfrak F(\mathcal R_n),
\]
they generate an analytical dynamics. The infinitesimal form of this dynamics is differentiation; its accumulated form is integration.

This viewpoint has several advantages.

1. **Unification.** Classical derivatives, covariant derivatives, fractional operators, elliptic generators, and nonlocal operators arise from the same recursive mechanism.

2. **Geometric naturality.** On manifolds, structural kernels may act through parallel transport, so tensor type and covariance are preserved.

3. **Operator-theoretic clarity.** Differentiation becomes a generator; integration becomes a resolvent, semigroup integral, or structural primitive.

4. **Numerical compatibility.** Recursive structural operators provide canonical discretizations. Consistency, stability, and convergence acquire intrinsic operator-theoretic formulations.

The purpose of this paper is to develop RSA as a rigorous analytical framework and to demonstrate its usefulness in PDEs, functional analysis, harmonic analysis, and numerical computation.

## 2. Foundations of Recursive Structural Analysis

### 2.1 Structured state spaces

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, possibly with boundary, equipped with a Riemannian metric \(g\). Let
\[
\pi : E \to M
\]
be a finite-rank vector bundle. Typical examples include the tangent bundle \(TM\), cotangent bundle \(T^*M\), tensor bundles
\[
T^r_s M
=
TM^{\otimes r} \otimes T^*M^{\otimes s},
\]
exterior form bundles \(\Lambda^p T^*M\), and spinor bundles.

A structured state is a section
\[
u \in \Gamma(E).
\]
In local coordinates and a local frame, we write
\[
u = u^A e_A,
\]
where \(A\) is a multi-index encoding the tensor, form, or spinor type. Depending on context, \(X\) will denote a Banach space of sections, for example
\[
X = L^p(M;E), \quad H^s(M;E), \quad C^k(M;E).
\]

To compare fibers at different points, we use parallel transport. Let
\[
\Pi_{y \to x} : E_y \to E_x
\]
denote parallel transport along a chosen geodesic or connection path. In components,
\[
(\Pi_{y \to x} v)^A
=
\Pi^A{}_B(y \to x) v^B.
\]

---

### 2.2 Structural operators and structural kernels

A **structural operator** of scale \(h>0\) is a map
\[
\mathcal R_h : X \to X.
\]
In the linear case, we assume that \(\mathcal R_h\) admits a structural kernel representation
\[
(\mathcal R_h u)^A(x)
=
\int_M
K_h{}^A{}_B(x,y)
\,
\Pi^B{}_C(y \to x)
\,
u^C(y)
\,
d\operatorname{vol}_g(y).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Here
\[
K_h{}^A{}_B(x,y)
\]
is a tensorial kernel acting on transported fibers. The kernel is assumed to concentrate near the diagonal as \(h \to 0\), so that \(\mathcal R_h\) becomes increasingly local.

The kernel formulation is flexible enough to include:

- translation operators,
- averaging operators,
- heat semigroups,
- fractional diffusion semigroups,
- wave propagators,
- multiscale projections,
- geometric smoothing operators,
- discretization operators on meshes or point clouds.

For nonlinear structural operators, one writes instead
\[
(\mathcal R_h u)(x)
=
\mathcal N_h[u](x),
\]
where \(\mathcal N_h\) may depend on \(u\), its local averages, its gradients, or recursively generated quantities. The linear kernel case is the model from which the nonlinear theory is built.

---

### 2.3 Recursive structural systems

A **recursive structural system** is a family or sequence of structural operators satisfying a recursion. There are three closely related forms.

#### 2.3.1 Autonomous recursive structure

A family \(\{\mathcal R_t\}_{t \ge 0}\) is an autonomous recursive structural system if
\[
\mathcal R_0 = I,
\tag{2.3a}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal R_{t+s} = \mathcal R_t \mathcal R_s.
\tag{2.3b}
\]
This is the operator-theoretic expression of recursive composition. In kernel form, the composition law becomes a Chapman–Kolmogorov-type identity:
\[
K_{t+s}(x,z)
=
\int_M
K_t(x,y) K_s(y,z)
\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(y),
\tag{2.4}
\]
with parallel transport understood.

#### 2.3.2 Nonautonomous recursive structure

For time-dependent or scale-dependent structure, one uses propagators
\[
U(t,s) : X \to X,
\]
satisfying
\[
U(s,s)=I,
\qquad
U(t,s)U(s,r)=U(t,r).
\]
A discrete recursion is then
\[
u_{n+1}
=
U(t_{n+1},t_n) u_n.
\tag{2.5}
\]

#### 2.3.3 Operator-level recursion

More generally, the structural operators themselves may evolve. Let \(\mathfrak F\) be a structural vector field on an operator algebra \(\mathcal A\). A recursive structural evolution may be written as
\[
\mathcal R_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_n
+
\tau_n \mathfrak F(\mathcal R_n),
\qquad
\mathcal R_0 = I.
\tag{2.6}
\]
In the continuum limit, this becomes
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal R(t)
=
\mathfrak F(\mathcal R(t)),
\qquad
\mathcal R(0)=I.
\tag{2.7}
\]
When
\[
\mathfrak F(\mathcal R)=L\mathcal R,
\]
one recovers the linear semigroup equation
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal R(t)=L\mathcal R(t).
\]

Equation (2.6) is fundamental in RSA: it treats analytical structure itself as a dynamical object.

---

### 2.4 RSA derivative

Let \(X\) be a Banach space and let \(\mathcal R_h\) be a recursive structural family. The **RSA derivative** is defined as the infinitesimal generator of the structural recursion.

**Definition 2.1.**  
For \(u \in X\), define
\[
D_{\mathcal R} u
=
\lim_{h \downarrow 0}
\frac{\mathcal R_h u - u}{h},
\tag{2.8}
\]
whenever the limit exists in \(X\). The domain of \(D_{\mathcal R}\) is
\[
\mathcal D(D_{\mathcal R})
=
\left\{
u \in X :
\lim_{h \downarrow 0}
\frac{\mathcal R_h u - u}{h}
\text{ exists in } X
\right\}.
\]

For a discrete recursion
\[
u_{n+1}=\mathcal R_{\tau_n}u_n,
\]
the discrete RSA derivative at step \(n\) is
\[
D_n u_n
=
\frac{u_{n+1}-u_n}{\tau_n}.
\tag{2.9}
\]
If the mesh \(\max_n \tau_n\) tends to zero and the recursion is consistent, then \(D_n u_n\) converges to \(D_{\mathcal R}u\).

---

### 2.5 RSA integral

The dual operation is accumulation.

**Definition 2.2.**  
For \(f \in X\) and \(T>0\), the finite RSA integral is
\[
\mathcal J_T f
=
\int_0^T \mathcal R_t f \, dt.
\tag{2.10}
\]
The resolvent RSA integral is
\[
\mathcal J_\lambda f
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal R_t f \, dt,
\qquad
\lambda>0.
\tag{2.11}
\]
A discrete approximation is
\[
\mathcal J_{T,N} f
=
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} w_n \mathcal R_{t_n} f,
\tag{2.12}
\]
where \(w_n\) are quadrature weights.

If \(D_{\mathcal R}\) is invertible and \(\mathcal R_t\) is stable as \(t\to\infty\), then
\[
(-D_{\mathcal R})^{-1} f
=
\int_0^\infty \mathcal R_t f \, dt.
\tag{2.13}
\]
Thus integration is the inverse or accumulated structural flow.

## 3. Classical Calculus as a Limiting Case

We now show that classical differentiation and integration are special cases of RSA.

---

### 3.1 Classical derivative from translation recursion

Let \(X=C^1_b(\mathbb R^d)\). Fix \(v \in \mathbb R^d\) and define
\[
(\mathcal T_h f)(x)=f(x+h v).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal T_{h+k}=\mathcal T_h \mathcal T_k,
\]
so \(\{\mathcal T_h\}_{h\ge0}\) is a recursive structural family. Moreover,
\[
\frac{\mathcal T_h f(x)-f(x)}{h}
=
\frac{f(x+h v)-f(x)}{h}
\to
v^i \partial_i f(x).
\tag{3.2}
\]
Thus
\[
D_{\mathcal T} f = v^i \partial_i f.
\]

On a Riemannian manifold, let \(\gamma_x(s)\) be the geodesic with
\[
\gamma_x(0)=x,
\qquad
\gamma_x'(0)=v_x.
\]
Define the structural transport of a tensor field \(T\) by
\[
(\mathcal T_h^v T)^A(x)
=
\Pi^A{}_B(\gamma_x(h)\to x)
\,
T^B(\gamma_x(h)).
\tag{3.3}
\]
Using covariant Taylor expansion,
\[
\Pi_{\gamma_x(h)\to x} T(\gamma_x(h))
=
T(x)
+
h v^c \nabla_c T(x)
+
O(h^2),
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{\mathcal T_h^v T - T}{h}
\to
v^c \nabla_c T.
\tag{3.4}
\]
In components,
\[
D_{\mathcal T} T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}
=
v^c
\nabla_c
T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence the covariant directional derivative is an RSA derivative generated by recursive parallel transport.

---

### 3.2 Classical integral from recursive accumulation

Let \(f \in C([0,T])\). Define the translation structural operator
\[
(\mathcal T_s f)(x)=f(x+s).
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal J_T f(x)
=
\int_0^T \mathcal T_s f(x)\,ds
=
\int_0^T f(x+s)\,ds.
\tag{3.6}
\]
A discrete RSA integral with uniform weights \(h=T/N\) is
\[
\mathcal J_{T,N} f(x)
=
h \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} f(x+n h).
\tag{3.7}
\]
As \(N\to\infty\),
\[
\mathcal J_{T,N} f(x)
\to
\int_x^{x+T} f(y)\,dy.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Thus the Riemann integral is the accumulation of recursively translated structural states.

For a manifold-valued tensor field, the accumulated integral includes parallel transport:
\[
\mathcal J_T^{v} T(x)
=
\int_0^T
\Pi_{\gamma_x(s)\to x}
T(\gamma_x(s))
\,ds.
\tag{3.9}
\]
This gives a covariant line-integral primitive.

---

### 3.3 Higher derivatives from recursive finite differences

Let \(\mathcal R_h=e^{hL}\), where \(L\) is a closed operator. Define the \(m\)-th recursive difference
\[
\Delta_h^m
=
\sum_{k=0}^m
(-1)^{m-k}
\binom{m}{k}
\mathcal R_{kh}.
\tag{3.10}
\]
Since
\[
\mathcal R_{kh}=e^{khL},
\]
we have
\[
\Delta_h^m
=
(e^{hL}-I)^m.
\tag{3.11}
\]
Expanding,
\[
(e^{hL}-I)^m
=
h^m L^m + O(h^{m+1}).
\]
Therefore, for \(u \in \mathcal D(L^m)\),
\[
L^m u
=
\lim_{h\downarrow 0}
\frac{1}{h^m}
\sum_{k=0}^m
(-1)^{m-k}
\binom{m}{k}
\mathcal R_{kh}u.
\tag{3.12}
\]

This identity shows that higher-order differential structure is encoded recursively in the structural semigroup.

## 4. Moment Expansion and Local Generators

We now derive the local differential operator generated by a structural kernel.

Let \(M\) be \(d\)-dimensional and let \(u\) be a smooth section. In normal coordinates at \(x\), write
\[
y=\exp_x(hz).
\]
Assume the structural kernel has the asymptotic form
\[
K_h(x,\exp_x(hz))
=
\kappa(z)
+
h a(x,z)
+
O(h^2),
\tag{4.1}
\]
where \(\kappa\) is integrable and sufficiently rapidly decaying.

Using parallel transport and covariant Taylor expansion,
\[
\Pi_{\exp_x(hz)\to x} u(\exp_x(hz))
=
u(x)
+
h z^i \nabla_i u(x)
+
\frac{h^2}{2}z^i z^j \nabla_i\nabla_j u(x)
+
O(h^3).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Substituting into (2.1) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathcal R_h u)(x)
&=
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\left[\kappa(z)+h a(x,z)+O(h^2)\right]
\\
&\quad \times
\left[
u(x)
+
h z^i \nabla_i u(x)
+
\frac{h^2}{2}z^i z^j \nabla_i\nabla_j u(x)
+
O(h^3)
\right]
dz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Assume
\[
\int \kappa(z)\,dz = 1.
\]
Define the first moment
\[
b^i
=
\int_{\mathbb R^d} z^i \kappa(z)\,dz,
\tag{4.4}
\]
and the zeroth moment of the first correction
\[
c(x)
=
\int_{\mathbb R^d} a(x,z)\,dz.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal R_h u
=
u
+
h b^i \nabla_i u
+
h c(x)u
+
O(h^2).
\tag{4.6}
\]
Therefore,
\[
\boxed{
D_{\mathcal R}u
=
b^i \nabla_i u
+
c(x)u.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

If the first moment vanishes,
\[
b^i=0,
\]
and the kernel is rescaled diffusively, then the leading term is second order. Define the second moment
\[
a^{ij}
=
\frac12
\int_{\mathbb R^d} z^i z^j \kappa(z)\,dz.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathcal R}u
=
a^{ij}\nabla_i\nabla_j u
+
\text{lower-order terms}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Thus first-order derivatives, drift-diffusion operators, and Laplace-type operators all arise as leading generators of recursive structural kernels.

## 5. Operator-Theoretic Core of RSA

### 5.1 Convergence of operator-level recursion

Let \(\mathcal A\) be a Banach algebra of bounded operators on \(X\). Let
\[
\mathfrak F : \mathcal A \to \mathcal A
\]
be locally Lipschitz. Consider the recursion
\[
\mathcal R_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_n
+
\tau_n \mathfrak F(\mathcal R_n),
\qquad
\mathcal R_0=I.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Define \(t_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\tau_k\), and let \(\mathcal R(t)\) be the piecewise linear interpolation of \(\mathcal R_n\).

**Theorem 5.1.**  
Suppose \(\mathfrak F\) is Lipschitz on bounded subsets of \(\mathcal A\), and \(T>0\). As
\[
|\tau|:=\max_n \tau_n \to 0,
\]
the interpolated sequence \(\mathcal R(t)\) converges in \(\mathcal A\), uniformly on \([0,T]\), to the unique solution of
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal R(t)
=
\mathfrak F(\mathcal R(t)),
\qquad
\mathcal R(0)=I.
\tag{5.2}
\]

*Proof sketch.* This is the standard convergence theorem for the explicit Euler method in Banach spaces, applied to the Banach algebra \(\mathcal A\). The Lipschitz condition gives a Gronwall estimate for the difference between the discrete recursion and the exact flow. \(\square\)

This theorem justifies treating recursive structural operators as discrete approximations to a continuum structural dynamics.

---

### 5.2 Semigroup generators as RSA derivatives

Let \(\{\mathcal R_t\}_{t\ge0}\) be a strongly continuous semigroup on a Banach space \(X\). Its generator is
\[
L u
=
\lim_{t\downarrow0}
\frac{\mathcal R_t u-u}{t},
\tag{5.3}
\]
with domain \(\mathcal D(L)\). In RSA notation,
\[
D_{\mathcal R}=L.
\]

Thus every \(C_0\)-semigroup defines a recursive structural derivative. Conversely, under the Hille–Yosida conditions, a closed operator \(L\) generates a recursive structural flow.

---

### 5.3 Resolvent as RSA integral

Let \(L\) generate a \(C_0\)-semigroup \(\mathcal R_t\) satisfying
\[
\|\mathcal R_t\| \le M e^{\omega t}.
\]
For \(\lambda>\omega\), define
\[
\mathcal J_\lambda f
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal R_t f\,dt.
\tag{5.4}
\]

**Theorem 5.2.**  
For \(\lambda>\omega\),
\[
\mathcal J_\lambda
=
(\lambda I - L)^{-1}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

*Proof.* For \(f\in X\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\lambda I-L)\mathcal J_\lambda f
&=
\int_0^\infty
e^{-\lambda t}
(\lambda I-L)\mathcal R_t f
\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal R_t f
=
L\mathcal R_t f,
\]
one obtains
\[
(\lambda I-L)\mathcal R_t f
=
\lambda \mathcal R_t f - \frac{d}{dt}\mathcal R_t f.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\lambda I-L)\mathcal J_\lambda f
&=
\int_0^\infty
\lambda e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal R_t f\,dt
-
\int_0^\infty
e^{-\lambda t}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal R_t f\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
Integration by parts gives
\[
-\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal R_t f\,dt
=
f
-
\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal R_t f
+
\int_0^\infty \lambda e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal R_t f\,dt.
\]
The boundary term at infinity vanishes for \(\lambda>\omega\). Therefore,
\[
(\lambda I-L)\mathcal J_\lambda f=f.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathcal J_\lambda=(\lambda I-L)^{-1}.
\]
\(\square\)

A discrete version follows immediately. Let \(\tau>0\), \(t_n=n\tau\), and define
\[
\mathcal J_{\lambda,N}^{(\tau)} f
=
\tau
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
e^{-\lambda n\tau}
\mathcal R_{n\tau}f.
\tag{5.6}
\]
If \(\tau\to0\) and \(N\tau\to\infty\), then
\[
\mathcal J_{\lambda,N}^{(\tau)} f
\to
(\lambda I-L)^{-1}f.
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is the RSA formulation of integration as resolvent accumulation.

---

### 5.4 Chernoff-type structural approximation

In numerical and geometric applications, one often does not have the exact semigroup \(\mathcal R_t=e^{tL}\), but only an approximate structural operator \(S(h)\).

**Theorem 5.3.**  
Suppose
\[
S(h)=I+hL+o(h)
\tag{5.8}
\]
strongly on \(\mathcal D(L)\), and suppose \(S(h)\) is uniformly bounded. If \(L\) generates a \(C_0\)-semigroup \(\mathcal R_t\), then for each \(t\ge0\),
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
S\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)^n
=
\mathcal R_t
\tag{5.9}
\]
strongly on \(X\).

This is a Chernoff-type product formula. It shows that recursive composition of first-order consistent structural operators recovers the exact analytical flow.

## 6. Applications to Partial Differential Equations

RSA provides a natural language for PDEs because many PDEs are evolution equations generated by structural operators.

---

### 6.1 Linear evolution equations

Let \(L\) be a differential operator acting on sections of \(E\). Consider
\[
\partial_t u = L u,
\qquad
u(0)=u_0.
\tag{6.1}
\]
If \(L\) generates a \(C_0\)-semigroup \(\mathcal R_t\), then
\[
u(t)=\mathcal R_t u_0.
\tag{6.2}
\]
The PDE is the infinitesimal expression of the recursive structural law
\[
u(t+s)=\mathcal R_t u(s).
\]

In kernel form,
\[
u^A(t,x)
=
\int_M
K_t{}^A{}_B(x,y)
\Pi^B{}_C(y\to x)
u_0^C(y)
\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(y).
\tag{6.3}
\]

---

### 6.2 Tensorial heat-type equations

Let \(T\) be a tensor field of type \((r,s)\):
\[
T \in \Gamma(T^r_s M).
\]
A geometric heat-type equation has the form
\[
\partial_t
T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}
=
g^{ij}
\nabla_i\nabla_j
T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}
+
\mathcal B^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}(T),
\tag{6.4}
\]
where \(\mathcal B\) is a curvature-coupled zeroth-order term. A typical expression is
\[
\mathcal B^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}
=
\sum_{\alpha=1}^r
R^{a_\alpha}{}_{cij}
T^{a_1\cdots c\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}
-
\sum_{\beta=1}^s
R^c{}_{b_\beta ij}
T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots c\cdots b_s},
\tag{6.5}
\]
up to sign conventions for the Riemann curvature tensor.

The associated structural heat kernel satisfies
\[
(T(t))^A(x)
=
\int_M
H_t{}^A{}_B(x,y)
\Pi^B{}_C(y\to x)
T_0^C(y)
\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(y).
\tag{6.6}
\]
The generator is a Laplace-type operator
\[
L = \nabla^*\nabla + \mathcal B.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Thus RSA treats tensor PDEs as recursive structural flows preserving tensor type through parallel transport.

---

### 6.3 Nonlinear PDEs and structural splitting

Consider a semilinear PDE
\[
\partial_t u = L u + N(u),
\qquad
u(0)=u_0.
\tag{6.8}
\]
Let \(\mathcal R_h^L=e^{hL}\) be the linear structural flow, and let \(\mathcal R_h^N\) be the nonlinear flow generated by \(N\).

A first-order RSA splitting is
\[
u_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^L
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^N
u_n.
\tag{6.9}
\]
A Lie-type variant is
\[
u_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^L u_n
+
\tau_n
N(\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^L u_n).
\tag{6.10}
\]
A second-order Strang-type RSA splitting is
\[
u_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n/2}^L
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^N
\mathcal R_{\tau_n/2}^L
u_n.
\tag{6.11}
\]

**Theorem 6.1.**  
Assume:

1. \(L\) generates a stable \(C_0\)-semigroup.
2. \(N\) is locally Lipschitz on the relevant Banach space.
3. The structural splitting is locally consistent of order \(p\):
   \[
   \| \Phi_{\tau} u - \Phi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{exact}}u \|
   \le C \tau^{p+1}.
   \]
4. The numerical recursion is stable on the time interval \([0,T]\).

Then the RSA approximation satisfies
\[
\|u(t_n)-u_n\|
\le
C_T \tau^p.
\tag{6.12}
\]

*Proof sketch.* The local error is \(O(\tau^{p+1})\). Stability propagates the error through \(O(1/\tau)\) steps, yielding \(O(\tau^p)\) global error by a discrete Gronwall argument. \(\square\)

This is the RSA version of the Lax equivalence principle: consistency plus stability implies convergence.

---

### 6.4 Elliptic and stationary PDEs

For a stationary equation
\[
(\lambda I - L)u=f,
\tag{6.13}
\]
the RSA integral gives the solution directly:
\[
u
=
(\lambda I-L)^{-1}f
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal R_t f\,dt.
\tag{6.14}
\]
If \(L=\Delta\) with suitable boundary conditions and \(\lambda=0\), then formally
\[
u
=
(-\Delta)^{-1}f
=
\int_0^\infty e^{t\Delta}f\,dt,
\tag{6.15}
\]
provided the compatibility and decay conditions hold.

Thus elliptic inversion is RSA integration over structural time.

## 7. Functional Analysis in RSA

RSA naturally produces function spaces, resolvent approximations, and spectral constructions.

---

### 7.1 RSA smoothness spaces

Let \(\mathcal R_t\) be a structural semigroup. For \(s>0\), \(1\le p,q\le\infty\), and \(m>s\), define
\[
\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathcal R)}
=
\|f\|_{L^p}
+
\left(
\int_0^1
\left[
t^{-s}
\|(I-\mathcal R_t)^m f\|_{L^p}
\right]^q
\frac{dt}{t}
\right)^{1/q},
\tag{7.1}
\]
with the usual modification when \(q=\infty\).

When \(\mathcal R_t=e^{t\Delta}\) is the heat semigroup, these spaces coincide with classical Besov spaces. In the Hilbert case \(p=q=2\), one obtains Sobolev-type norms through square functions.

Define the RSA square function
\[
S_{\mathcal R}f
=
\left(
\int_0^\infty
\left|
t^{-s}(I-\mathcal R_t)^m f
\right|^2
\frac{dt}{t}
\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Then
\[
\|f\|_{\dot H^s}
\sim
\|S_{\mathcal R}f\|_{L^2}
\]
under standard ellipticity assumptions.

---

### 7.2 Spectral approximation

Suppose \(L\) has an isolated eigenvalue \(\mu\) with eigenfunction \(\phi\):
\[
L\phi=\mu\phi.
\tag{7.3}
\]
If \(\mathcal R_h\) is a consistent structural approximation to \(e^{hL}\), then
\[
\mathcal R_h \phi
\approx
e^{h\mu}\phi.
\tag{7.4}
\]
If \(\nu_h\) is the corresponding eigenvalue of \(\mathcal R_h\), then
\[
\mu
\approx
\frac{1}{h}\log \nu_h.
\tag{7.5}
\]
For nonnormal operators or clusters of eigenvalues, one uses RSA resolvent contour integrals. Let \(\Gamma\) enclose an isolated part of the spectrum. Define
\[
P_\Gamma
=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_\Gamma
(zI-L)^{-1}\,dz.
\tag{7.6}
\]
The discrete RSA approximation is
\[
P_{\Gamma,h}
=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\int_\Gamma
(zI-\mathcal R_h)^{-1}
\frac{dz}{?}
\tag{7.7}
\]
more carefully, after mapping the spectral parameter through \(z\mapsto e^{h z}\), one obtains stable approximations of spectral projections. The essential point is that recursive structural operators approximate spectral data through their generators.

## 8. Harmonic Analysis and Recursive Decompositions

RSA is especially natural in harmonic analysis, where multiscale operators are already fundamental.

---

### 8.1 Fourier multiplier representation

On \(\mathbb R^d\), suppose
\[
\widehat{\mathcal R_t f}(\xi)
=
m(t,\xi)\widehat f(\xi).
\tag{8.1}
\]
If the limit
\[
\ell(\xi)
=
\lim_{t\downarrow0}
\frac{m(t,\xi)-1}{t}
\tag{8.2}
\]
exists, then the RSA derivative is the Fourier multiplier
\[
\widehat{D_{\mathcal R}f}(\xi)
=
\ell(\xi)\widehat f(\xi).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Examples:

1. **Translation.**  
   \[
   m(h,\xi)=e^{i h v\cdot \xi},
   \qquad
   \ell(\xi)=i v\cdot \xi.
   \]

2. **Heat semigroup.**  
   \[
   m(t,\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^2},
   \qquad
   \ell(\xi)=-|\xi|^2.
   \]

3. **Fractional diffusion.**  
   \[
   m(t,\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^\alpha},
   \qquad
   \ell(\xi)=-|\xi|^\alpha.
   \]

4. **Schrödinger flow.**  
   \[
   m(t,\xi)=e^{i t|\xi|^2},
   \qquad
   \ell(\xi)=i|\xi|^2.
   \]

Thus many pseudodifferential operators arise as RSA generators.

---

### 8.2 Recursive Littlewood–Paley theory

Let \(\mathcal R_t\) be a smoothing semigroup. Choose dyadic scales
\[
t_j=2^{-j/\alpha}.
\]
Define recursive difference operators
\[
\Delta_j
=
\mathcal R_{t_{j+1}}
-
\mathcal R_{t_j}.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Then formally
\[
f
=
\lim_{J\to\infty}
\mathcal R_{t_J}f
+
\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}
\Delta_j f.
\tag{8.5}
\]
If \(\mathcal R_t f\to0\) as \(t\to\infty\) and \(\mathcal R_t f\to f\) as \(t\to0\), this becomes a multiscale decomposition:
\[
f
=
\sum_{j=0}^\infty
\Delta_j f.
\tag{8.6}
\]

The associated RSA square function is
\[
S_{\mathrm{RSA}}f(x)
=
\left(
\sum_{j=0}^\infty
|\Delta_j f(x)|^2
\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{8.7}
\]
Under standard kernel assumptions,
\[
\|f\|_{L^p}
\sim
\|S_{\mathrm{RSA}}f\|_{L^p},
\qquad
1<p<\infty.
\tag{8.8}
\]
This gives a recursive reconstruction of Littlewood–Paley theory.

---

### 8.3 Hodge theory and differential forms

Let \(\Omega^p(M)\) be the space of smooth \(p\)-forms. The Hodge Laplacian is
\[
\Delta_H = d\delta + \delta d.
\]
The heat semigroup
\[
\mathcal R_t = e^{-t\Delta_H}
\]
is a recursive structural operator on forms. Its generator is
\[
D_{\mathcal R}=-\Delta_H.
\]

For a \(p\)-form \(\omega\), the structural heat kernel is tensorial:
\[
(\mathcal R_t\omega)_{i_1\cdots i_p}(x)
=
\int_M
H_t{}^{j_1\cdots j_p}_{i_1\cdots i_p}(x,y)
\omega_{j_1\cdots j_p}(y)
\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(y).
\tag{8.9}
\]

The Hodge Green operator may be written as an RSA integral:
\[
G
=
\int_0^\infty
\left(
e^{-t\Delta_H}
-
P_{\mathcal H}
\right)
dt,
\tag{8.10}
\]
where \(P_{\mathcal H}\) is the projection onto harmonic forms. Since
\[
G = \Delta_H^{-1}(I-P_{\mathcal H}),
\]
RSA integration recovers the Hodge decomposition:
\[
\Omega^p(M)
=
\mathcal H^p(M)
\oplus
d\Omega^{p-1}(M)
\oplus
\delta\Omega^{p+1}(M).
\tag{8.11}
\]

## 9. Numerical Computation and RSA Algorithms

RSA is naturally compatible with numerical analysis because recursive structural operators are already discrete or discretizable objects.

---

### 9.1 Discrete RSA derivative and integral

Given a structural operator \(\mathcal R_h\), define the discrete derivative
\[
D_h
=
\frac{\mathcal R_h-I}{h}.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Higher-order symmetric forms may be used to improve accuracy. For example,
\[
D_h^{(2)}
=
\frac{\mathcal R_h-\mathcal R_{-h}}{2h}
\tag{9.2}
\]
when backward structural operators exist.

The discrete RSA integral is
\[
J_h f
=
h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
\mathcal R_{nh} f.
\tag{9.3}
\]
If \(D_h\) approximates a generator \(L\), then \(J_h\) approximates a resolvent or primitive.

---

### 9.2 RSA time stepping

For an evolution equation
\[
\partial_t u = F(u),
\]
an RSA method selects structural maps \(\mathcal R_{\tau_n}\) and defines
\[
u_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n} u_n.
\tag{9.4}
\]
If \(F\) is split as \(F=L+N\), one uses structural splitting:
\[
u_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^L
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^N
u_n,
\tag{9.5}
\]
or a higher-order composition.

The method is structure-preserving if the discrete operators preserve the relevant geometric data: tensor type, metric compatibility, divergence constraints, symplectic form, mass, or energy.

---

### 9.3 Residual correction

Suppose one seeks \(u\) satisfying
\[
D u = f.
\]
Given an approximation \(u_n\), define the residual
\[
r_n
=
f-D_h u_n.
\tag{9.6}
\]
A recursive correction is
\[
e_n
=
J_h r_n,
\qquad
u_{n+1}
=
u_n+e_n.
\tag{9.7}
\]
Since \(J_h\) approximates \(D^{-1}\), this is a structural preconditioning step.

**Theorem 9.1.**  
Assume:

1. \(D_h\) is consistent:
   \[
   \|D_h u - D u\| \le C h^p.
   \]
2. The discrete inverse \(J_h\) is uniformly bounded:
   \[
   \|J_h\|\le C.
   \]
3. The continuous problem is well-posed:
   \[
   \|D^{-1}\|\le C.
   \]

Then the RSA correction scheme converges with order \(p\):
\[
\|u-u_h\|\le C h^p.
\tag{9.8}
\]

*Proof sketch.* The error satisfies
\[
D e = f-Du_n,
\]
while the discrete correction solves
\[
D_h e_h = f-D_hu_n.
\]
Subtracting and applying boundedness of \(J_h\) gives the estimate. \(\square\)

---

### 9.4 Adaptive RSA

Local error can be estimated by step doubling. Let
\[
u_{n+1}^{(1)}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}u_n
\]
be a full-step structural update, and let
\[
u_{n+1}^{(2)}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n/2}
\mathcal R_{\tau_n/2}
u_n
\]
be the two half-step update. Define
\[
\eta_n
=
\|u_{n+1}^{(1)}-u_{n+1}^{(2)}\|.
\tag{9.9}
\]
If the method has order \(p\), then
\[
\eta_n
\approx
C\tau_n^{p+1}.
\]
An adaptive step choice is
\[
\tau_{n+1}
=
\theta
\tau_n
\left(
\frac{\mathrm{tol}}{\eta_n}
\right)^{1/(p+1)},
\tag{9.10}
\]
with safety factor \(0<\theta<1\).

---

### 9.5 Tensorial structure preservation

For tensor fields, naive componentwise discretizations may destroy covariance. RSA avoids this by updating through discrete parallel transport.

Let \(x_i\) be mesh or point-cloud nodes. A tensorial RSA update has the form
\[
T_{n+1}^{A}(x_i)
=
\Pi^A{}_B(x_j\to x_i)
\left[
T_n^B(x_j)
+
\tau_n
F_n^B(x_j)
\right],
\tag{9.11}
\]
summed or integrated over structural neighbors \(j\). This preserves tensor type and respects the underlying geometry.

For metric-dependent PDEs, one may also evolve the metric structurally:
\[
g_{n+1}
=
\mathcal R_{\tau_n}^g g_n,
\]
while tensor fields are transported using the evolving connection.

## 10. Illustrative Examples

### 10.1 Translation and the ordinary derivative

Let
\[
(\mathcal R_h f)(x)=f(x+h).
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathcal R}f=f'.
\]
The RSA integral is
\[
\mathcal J_T f(x)
=
\int_0^T f(x+s)\,ds.
\]
Classical one-dimensional calculus is recovered.

---

### 10.2 Gaussian recursion and the Laplacian

Let
\[
(\mathcal R_t f)(x)
=
(G_t*f)(x),
\]
where
\[
G_t(x)
=
\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}}
e^{-|x|^2/(4t)}.
\]
The kernels satisfy
\[
G_t*G_s=G_{t+s},
\]
so the recursion is exact. Taylor expansion gives
\[
\mathcal R_t f
=
f
+
t\Delta f
+
\frac{t^2}{2}\Delta^2 f
+
O(t^3).
\]
Thus
\[
D_{\mathcal R}=\Delta.
\]
The Poisson equation
\[
-\Delta u=f
\]
has RSA solution
\[
u
=
\int_0^\infty \mathcal R_t f\,dt,
\]
under suitable decay and compatibility conditions.

---

### 10.3 Fractional RSA

Let
\[
\mathcal R_t
=
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}},
\qquad
0<\alpha<2.
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathcal R}
=
-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}.
\]
The associated RSA integral of order \(s>0\) is
\[
(-\Delta)^{-s/2}f
=
\frac{1}{\Gamma(s/2)}
\int_0^\infty
t^{s/2-1}
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}}
f\,dt.
\]
This recovers Riesz potentials as recursive structural accumulations.

---

### 10.4 Covariant derivative along a vector field

Let \(v\) be a vector field and define
\[
(\mathcal R_h T)(x)
=
\Pi_{\exp_x(hv)\to x}
T(\exp_x(hv)).
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathcal R}T
=
\nabla_v T.
\]
In components,
\[
(D_{\mathcal R}T)^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}
=
v^c
\nabla_c
T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}.
\]
This example shows how RSA incorporates geometric transport directly into differentiation.

## 11. Discussion

Recursive Structural Analysis reorganizes the foundations of analysis around three primitive notions:

1. **Structure:** encoded by operators, kernels, and transport maps.
2. **Recursion:** encoded by composition, semigroup laws, or operator-level dynamics.
3. **Limit:** differentiation and integration appear as infinitesimal and accumulative limits of recursive structure.

This perspective has several conceptual consequences.

First, the derivative is no longer tied to a coordinate difference quotient. It is the generator of a structural transformation. On manifolds, this makes covariance automatic.

Second, integration is not merely an antiderivative or area limit. It is the resolvent, accumulation, or inverse of a recursive structural flow.

Third, numerical methods are not external approximations of analysis. They are finite recursions of the same structural operators that define the continuum theory. Consistency, stability, and convergence become intrinsic properties of recursive structural systems.

Finally, RSA provides a common language for local and nonlocal analysis. A local derivative, a fractional operator, a heat semigroup, and a multiscale projection are all structural generators. Their differences lie in the recursion, the kernel, and the scaling limit.

---

## 12. Conclusion

This paper has introduced Recursive Structural Analysis as a unified framework for reconstructing analysis from recursively evolving structural operators. The main results are:

1. A definition of structural operators via kernels and parallel transport.
2. A recursive operator formalism in which differentiation is the infinitesimal generator.
3. A resolvent and accumulation theory in which integration is the inverse or structural primitive.
4. Moment expansions showing how local differential operators arise from structural kernels.
5. Applications to linear and nonlinear PDEs, including tensorial geometric flows.
6. Functional-analytic constructions of RSA smoothness spaces and spectral approximations.
7. Harmonic-analytic decompositions based on recursive multiscale operators.
8. Numerical schemes with consistency, stability, adaptivity, and structure preservation.

Classical calculus is therefore not replaced but generalized: it is the first-order, local, commutative limit of a much broader recursive structural theory.
