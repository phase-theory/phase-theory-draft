# Recursive Measure Dynamics  
## A Formal Theory of Evolving Measure Fields

**Preprint**

---

## Abstract

We develop a systematic theory of **Recursive Measure Dynamics** (RMD), the study of measure-valued sequences generated by recursive transformations
\[
\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}(\mu_n),
\]
where \(\mu_n\) is a measure on a state space \(X\) and \(\mathcal{T}\) is a linear or nonlinear operator acting on a space of measures. The central conceptual shift is to treat measures not as fixed background objects with respect to which integration is performed, but as dynamical fields whose evolution determines the integration functional itself. We formulate RMD on Polish spaces and Riemannian manifolds, establish existence and stability results for fixed points in weak and Wasserstein topologies, derive Perron–Frobenius and transfer-operator representations, obtain exact and approximate tensorial moment hierarchies, and construct continuum limits as measure-valued evolution equations. We then extend the framework to geometric measure dynamics, including rectifiable measures, varifolds, and currents. Applications are developed in probability, dynamical systems, statistical mechanics, and geometric measure theory. The resulting framework provides a unified analytical language for invariant measures, recursive distributional equations, renormalization dynamics, transport-generated densities, and evolving geometric measures.

**Keywords:** recursive measure dynamics, transfer operators, Perron–Frobenius operator, Wasserstein contraction, moment tensors, invariant measures, geometric measure theory, renormalization, Fokker–Planck dynamics.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical measure theory is predominantly static. One fixes a measure \(\mu\) on a measurable space \(X\), and integration is then the linear evaluation
\[
f\longmapsto \int_X f\,d\mu.
\]
This paradigm is powerful, but it is insufficient for many systems in which the measure itself is generated iteratively. In probability, distributions arise as fixed points of recursive distributional equations. In dynamical systems, invariant and physical measures are limits of pushforwards under iterated maps. In statistical mechanics, renormalization transformations act repeatedly on Gibbs measures. In geometric measure theory, sets and currents evolve under mappings, producing evolving Hausdorff or varifold measures.

We propose that these phenomena are instances of a single structural principle:

> **Measures evolve through recursive transformations rather than remaining fixed.**

The elementary object of Recursive Measure Dynamics is a sequence \((\mu_n)_{n\ge0}\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}(\mu_n)}
\]
for a map \(\mathcal{T}\) acting on a suitable space of measures. Integration is then no longer evaluation against a fixed background measure; it becomes the study of a **dynamic measure field**
\[
n\longmapsto \mu_n
\]
and its associated sequence of integration functionals
\[
I_n(f):=\int_X f\,d\mu_n.
\]

The aim of this paper is to develop RMD as a rigorous analytical framework. We treat four foundational questions:

1. **Existence and structure of recursive measure orbits.**  
   Under what conditions is the recursion well posed, and when do invariant measures exist?

2. **Stability and convergence.**  
   When does \(\mu_n\) converge to a fixed point, periodic orbit, or attractor in the space of measures?

3. **Representation of dynamic integration.**  
   How do observables, densities, moments, and tensorial statistics evolve under the recursion?

4. **Geometric and continuum limits.**  
   How does recursive measure dynamics interact with smooth transport, Riemannian volume, currents, and continuous-time measure-valued flows?

The resulting theory is not merely a reformulation of known facts about Markov operators or transfer operators. Rather, RMD treats the measure itself as the primary dynamical variable and provides a unified setting for linear, nonlinear, probabilistic, and geometric measure evolution.

## 2. Functional Setting and Notation

Let \((X,d)\) be a Polish space, and let \(\mathcal{B}(X)\) denote its Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra. We write:

- \(\mathcal{M}(X)\): finite signed Radon measures on \(X\);
- \(\mathcal{M}_+(X)\): finite nonnegative Radon measures;
- \(\mathcal{P}(X)\): Borel probability measures on \(X\);
- \(\mathcal{P}_p(X)\): probability measures with finite \(p\)-th moment.

For \(\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)\) and bounded measurable \(f:X\to\mathbb{R}\), we write
\[
\langle f,\mu\rangle := \int_X f(x)\,d\mu(x).
\]

If \(F:X\to Y\) is measurable, the **pushforward measure** \(F_\#\mu\) is defined by
\[
F_\#\mu(A):=\mu(F^{-1}(A)),\qquad A\in\mathcal{B}(Y).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\int_Y f(y)\,dF_\#\mu(y)=\int_X f(F(x))\,d\mu(x).
\]

The topology of weak convergence on \(\mathcal{P}(X)\) is defined by
\[
\mu_n\rightharpoonup \mu
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\int f\,d\mu_n\to\int f\,d\mu
\]
for every bounded continuous \(f\). For \(p\ge1\), the \(p\)-Wasserstein distance on \(\mathcal{P}_p(X)\) is
\[
W_p(\mu,\nu)
=
\left(
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}
\int_{X\times X} d(x,y)^p\,d\pi(x,y)
\right)^{1/p},
\]
where \(\Pi(\mu,\nu)\) is the set of couplings of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\).

When \(X=\mathbb{R}^d\), we use Einstein summation over repeated upper and lower indices. For \(x\in\mathbb{R}^d\), the \(k\)-fold tensor power is
\[
x^{\otimes k}
=
x^{i_1}\cdots x^{i_k}\,e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_k}.
\]
For a linear map \(A:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d\), the induced action on \(k\)-tensors is
\[
A^{\otimes k}: ( \mathbb{R}^d)^{\otimes k}\to(\mathbb{R}^d)^{\otimes k},
\]
with components
\[
\bigl(A^{\otimes k}T\bigr)^{i_1\cdots i_k}
=
A^{i_1}{}_{j_1}\cdots A^{i_k}{}_{j_k}
T^{j_1\cdots j_k}.
\]

If \(M\) is a \(d\)-dimensional Riemannian manifold with metric \(g\), we denote by \(d\operatorname{vol}_g\) the Riemannian volume measure. In local coordinates,
\[
d\operatorname{vol}_g
=
\sqrt{\det g(x)}\,dx^1\cdots dx^d.
\]
For a \(C^1\) map \(F:M\to M\), the Jacobian determinant relative to \(g\) is
\[
J_F(x)
=
\left|\det DF(x)\right|
\]
when interpreted in coordinate volume, with metric corrections made explicit below.

## 3. Recursive Measure Fields and Dynamic Integration

### 3.1 Basic definition

Let \(\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)\). A **Recursive Measure Dynamics** is a pair \((\mu_0,\mathcal{T})\), where \(\mu_0\in\mathcal{D}\) and
\[
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{P}(X),
\]
such that the recursively defined sequence
\[
\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}(\mu_n),\qquad n\ge0,
\]
is well defined.

The sequence
\[
\mu:\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{P}(X),\qquad n\mapsto \mu_n,
\]
is called a **measure field** or **measure orbit**.

More generally, if \(B\) is an index space with a map \(\sigma:B\to B\), and \(\mathcal{T}_b\) is a family of measure transformations, a recursive measure field satisfies
\[
\mu_{\sigma(b)}=\mathcal{T}_b(\mu_b).
\]
The discrete-time case corresponds to \(B=\mathbb{N}\) and \(\sigma(n)=n+1\).

---

### 3.2 Dynamic integration

Given a measure field \((\mu_n)\), the associated **dynamic integral** of an observable \(f\) is the scalar sequence
\[
I_n(f):=\int_X f\,d\mu_n.
\]
Thus integration becomes a dynamical object:
\[
I_n(f)=\langle f,\mu_n\rangle.
\]

If \(\mathcal{T}\) is linear and has a predual operator \(L\) acting on observables, then
\[
\int f\,d\mathcal{T}\mu
=
\int Lf\,d\mu.
\]
Consequently,
\[
I_n(f)
=
\int f\,d\mu_n
=
\int L^n f\,d\mu_0.
\]
In this case, recursive integration is equivalent to the iteration of a linear observable evolution operator.

For nonlinear \(\mathcal{T}\), there is generally no linear operator \(L\) on observables. Nevertheless, one may define a nonlinear observable transform
\[
(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}f)(\mu)
:=
\int f\,d\mathcal{T}(\mu),
\]
so that
\[
I_{n+1}(f)
=
(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}f)(\mu_n).
\]
Thus, in the nonlinear setting, observables become functions on the space of measures.

---

### 3.3 Invariant, periodic, and attracting measures

A measure \(\mu_\ast\in\mathcal{D}\) is an **invariant measure** for the recursion if
\[
\mathcal{T}(\mu_\ast)=\mu_\ast.
\]
A measure \(\mu\) is **periodic of period \(p\)** if
\[
\mathcal{T}^p(\mu)=\mu
\]
and \(p\) is the smallest positive integer for which this holds.

The **omega-limit set** of \(\mu_0\) is
\[
\omega(\mu_0)
:=
\bigcap_{N\ge0}
\overline{\{\mu_n:n\ge N\}},
\]
where the closure is taken in the relevant topology, usually weak convergence or Wasserstein topology.

A compact set \(\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)\) is a **measure attractor** if it is invariant and attracts a nonempty set of initial measures:
\[
\operatorname{dist}(\mu_n,\mathcal{A})\to0,
\]
where \(\operatorname{dist}\) is a chosen metric on measures.

## 4. Canonical Classes of Recursive Transformations

### 4.1 Linear Markov recursions

Let \(K(x,dy)\) be a Markov kernel on \(X\). Define
\[
\mathcal{T}_K\mu(A)
:=
\int_X K(x,A)\,d\mu(x).
\]
Then \(\mathcal{T}_K:\mathcal{P}(X)\to\mathcal{P}(X)\) is linear. The associated observable operator is
\[
(L_K f)(x)
:=
\int_X f(y)\,K(x,dy),
\]
and
\[
\int f\,d\mathcal{T}_K\mu
=
\int L_K f\,d\mu.
\]

If \(K\) is Feller, meaning \(L_K f\in C_b(X)\) whenever \(f\in C_b(X)\), then \(\mathcal{T}_K\) is continuous for the weak topology on \(\mathcal{P}(X)\).

---

### 4.2 Deterministic pushforward recursions

Let \(F:X\to X\) be measurable. Define
\[
\mathcal{T}_F\mu:=F_\#\mu.
\]
Then
\[
\int f\,d\mathcal{T}_F\mu
=
\int f\circ F\,d\mu.
\]
The corresponding observable operator is the Koopman operator
\[
L_F f=f\circ F.
\]

If \(F\) is continuous and \(X\) is compact, then \(\mathcal{T}_F\) is weakly continuous.

---

### 4.3 Iterated function systems

Let \(F_i:X\to X\), \(i=1,\dots,m\), and let \(p_i>0\) with \(\sum_i p_i=1\). Define
\[
\mathcal{T}\mu
:=
\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\,F_{i\#}\mu.
\]
This defines a linear recursive measure dynamics. It is the measure-theoretic form of an iterated function system with fixed probabilities.

If the maps \(F_i\) are contractions on average in Wasserstein distance, the recursion admits a unique invariant measure. This is one of the simplest and most important examples of recursive measure generation.

---

### 4.4 Nonlinear mean-field recursions

A large class of nonlinear recursions has the form
\[
\mathcal{T}(\mu)
=
(F_\mu)_\#\mu,
\]
where the map \(F_\mu:X\to X\) depends on \(\mu\). More generally, one may write
\[
\mathcal{T}(\mu)(A)
=
\int_X K_\mu(x,A)\,d\mu(x),
\]
where the kernel \(K_\mu\) depends on \(\mu\). Such dynamics arise in mean-field models, interacting particle systems, nonlinear filtering, and self-consistent transport.

A representative example is
\[
F_\mu(x)
=
x-\eta\nabla V(x)-\eta\nabla W\ast\mu(x),
\]
which produces a discrete nonlinear aggregation-diffusion recursion.

## 5. Existence, Uniqueness, and Stability

### 5.1 Existence of invariant measures on compact spaces

**Theorem 5.1.**  
Let \(X\) be a compact metric space, and let \(K\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)\) be nonempty, compact, and convex in the weak topology. If
\[
\mathcal{T}:K\to K
\]
is weakly continuous, then \(\mathcal{T}\) admits a fixed point:
\[
\exists\,\mu_\ast\in K
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\mathcal{T}(\mu_\ast)=\mu_\ast.
\]

**Proof.**  
The space \(\mathcal{M}(X)\) of finite signed measures embeds as the dual of \(C(X)\). The set \(\mathcal{P}(X)\) is weak-\(*\) closed in the unit ball of \(\mathcal{M}(X)\), and by Banach–Alaoglu it is weak-\(*\) compact when \(X\) is compact. Hence \(K\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of a locally convex topological vector space. The Schauder–Tychonoff fixed-point theorem applies to the continuous map \(\mathcal{T}:K\to K\), yielding a fixed point. \(\square\)

This theorem is particularly useful for deterministic pushforwards and Feller Markov kernels on compact spaces.

### 5.2 Wasserstein contraction

**Theorem 5.2.**  
Let \((X,d)\) be a complete separable metric space, and let \(p\ge1\). Suppose
\[
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{P}_p(X)\to\mathcal{P}_p(X)
\]

\[
W_p(\mathcal{T}\mu,\mathcal{T}\nu)
\le
\lambda W_p(\mu,\nu)
\]

1. \(\mathcal{T}\) has a unique fixed point \(\mu_\ast\in\mathcal{P}_p(X)\).
2. For every initial \(\mu_0\in\mathcal{P}_p(X)\), the recursive sequence \(\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}\mu_n\) converges to \(\mu_\ast\) in \(W_p\).
3. The convergence satisfies the estimate
   \[
   W_p(\mu_n,\mu_\ast)
   \le
   \frac{\lambda^n}{1-\lambda}

   \]

**Proof.**  
Since \(\mathcal{P}_p(X)\) is complete under \(W_p\), the sequence \((\mu_n)\) is Cauchy:
\[
W_p(\mu_{n+m},\mu_n)
\le
\sum_{j=n}^{n+m-1}
W_p(\mu_{j+1},\mu_j)
\le
\sum_{j=n}^{n+m-1}
\lambda^j W_p(\mu_1,\mu_0).
\]
Because \(\lambda<1\), the right-hand side tends to zero. Hence \(\mu_n\to\mu_\ast\) for some \(\mu_\ast\in\mathcal{P}_p(X)\). Continuity of \(\mathcal{T}\) in \(W_p\) gives
\[
\mathcal{T}\mu_\ast
=
\mathcal{T}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_n\right)
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{T}\mu_n
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n+1}
=
\mu_\ast.
\]
Uniqueness follows because if \(\nu_\ast\) is another fixed point, then
\[

=

\le
\lambda W_p(\mu_\ast,\nu_\ast),
\]
forcing \(W_p(\mu_\ast,\nu_\ast)=0\). \(\square\)

### 4.4 Nonlinear mean-field recursions

A large class of nonlinear recursions has the form
\[
\mathcal{T}(\mu)
=
(F_\mu)_\#\mu,
\]
where the map \(F_\mu:X\to X\) depends on \(\mu\). More generally, one may write
\[
\mathcal{T}(\mu)(A)
=
\int_X K_\mu(x,A)\,d\mu(x),
\]
where the kernel \(K_\mu\) depends on \(\mu\). Such dynamics arise in mean-field models, interacting particle systems, nonlinear filtering, and self-consistent transport.

A representative example is
\[
F_\mu(x)
=
x-\eta\nabla V(x)-\eta\nabla W\ast\mu(x),
\]
which produces a discrete nonlinear aggregation-diffusion recursion.

---

## 5. Existence, Uniqueness, and Stability

### 5.1 Existence of invariant measures on compact spaces

**Theorem 5.1.**  
Let \(X\) be a compact metric space, and let \(K\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)\) be nonempty, compact, and convex in the weak topology. If
\[
\mathcal{T}:K\to K
\]
is weakly continuous, then \(\mathcal{T}\) admits a fixed point:
\[
\exists\,\mu_\ast\in K
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\mathcal{T}(\mu_\ast)=\mu_\ast.
\]

**Proof.**  
The space \(\mathcal{M}(X)\) of finite signed measures embeds as the dual of \(C(X)\). The set \(\mathcal{P}(X)\) is weak-\(*\) closed in the unit ball of \(\mathcal{M}(X)\), and by Banach–Alaoglu it is weak-\(*\) compact when \(X\) is compact. Hence \(K\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of a locally convex topological vector space. The Schauder–Tychonoff fixed-point theorem applies to the continuous map \(\mathcal{T}:K\to K\), yielding a fixed point. \(\square\)

This theorem is particularly useful for deterministic pushforwards and Feller Markov kernels on compact spaces.

---

### 5.2 Wasserstein contraction

**Theorem 5.2.**  
Let \((X,d)\) be a complete separable metric space, and let \(p\ge1\). Suppose
\[
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{P}_p(X)\to\mathcal{P}_p(X)
\]
satisfies
\[
W_p(\mathcal{T}\mu,\mathcal{T}\nu)
\le
\lambda W_p(\mu,\nu)
\]
for all \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_p(X)\), with \(0\le\lambda<1\). Then:

1. \(\mathcal{T}\) has a unique fixed point \(\mu_\ast\in\mathcal{P}_p(X)\).
2. For every initial \(\mu_0\in\mathcal{P}_p(X)\), the recursive sequence \(\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}\mu_n\) converges to \(\mu_\ast\) in \(W_p\).
3. The convergence satisfies the estimate
   \[
   W_p(\mu_n,\mu_\ast)
   \le
   \frac{\lambda^n}{1-\lambda}
   W_p(\mu_1,\mu_0).
   \]

**Proof.**  
Since \(\mathcal{P}_p(X)\) is complete under \(W_p\), the sequence \((\mu_n)\) is Cauchy:
\[
W_p(\mu_{n+m},\mu_n)
\le
\sum_{j=n}^{n+m-1}
W_p(\mu_{j+1},\mu_j)
\le
\sum_{j=n}^{n+m-1}
\lambda^j W_p(\mu_1,\mu_0).
\]
Because \(\lambda<1\), the right-hand side tends to zero. Hence \(\mu_n\to\mu_\ast\) for some \(\mu_\ast\in\mathcal{P}_p(X)\). Continuity of \(\mathcal{T}\) in \(W_p\) gives
\[
\mathcal{T}\mu_\ast
=
\mathcal{T}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_n\right)
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{T}\mu_n
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n+1}
=
\mu_\ast.
\]
Uniqueness follows because if \(\nu_\ast\) is another fixed point, then
\[
W_p(\mu_\ast,\nu_\ast)
=
W_p(\mathcal{T}\mu_\ast,\mathcal{T}\nu_\ast)
\le
\lambda W_p(\mu_\ast,\nu_\ast),
\]
forcing \(W_p(\mu_\ast,\nu_\ast)=0\). \(\square\)

---

### 5.3 Contractive iterated function systems

**Corollary 5.3.**  
Let \(F_i:X\to X\) be Lipschitz with constants \(\lambda_i\), and let \(p_i>0\) with \(\sum_i p_i=1\). Define
\[
\mathcal{T}\mu
=
\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\,F_{i\#}\mu.
\]
Then for \(p\ge1\),
\[
W_p(\mathcal{T}\mu,\mathcal{T}\nu)^p
\le
\left(\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\lambda_i^p\right)
W_p(\mu,\nu)^p.
\]
In particular, if
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\lambda_i^p<1,
\]
then \(\mathcal{T}\) has a unique invariant probability measure in \(\mathcal{P}_p(X)\), and every recursive orbit converges to it in \(W_p\).

**Proof.**  
Let \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\) be an optimal coupling. Choose an index \(i\) with probability \(p_i\), and apply the same map \(F_i\) to both coordinates. This yields a coupling of \(\mathcal{T}\mu\) and \(\mathcal{T}\nu\). Hence
\[
W_p(\mathcal{T}\mu,\mathcal{T}\nu)^p
\le
\sum_{i=1}^m p_i
\int d(F_i x,F_i y)^p\,d\pi(x,y).
\]
Using Lipschitz continuity,
\[
d(F_i x,F_i y)^p
\le
\lambda_i^p d(x,y)^p,
\]
so
\[
W_p(\mathcal{T}\mu,\mathcal{T}\nu)^p
\le
\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\lambda_i^p
\int d(x,y)^p\,d\pi(x,y).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\pi\) gives the result. \(\square\)

---

### 5.4 Lyapunov stability in measure space

**Theorem 5.4.**  
Let \(K\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)\) be compact, and let \(\mathcal{T}:K\to K\) be weakly continuous. Suppose there exists a continuous functional
\[
V:K\to\mathbb{R}
\]
bounded below such that
\[
V(\mathcal{T}\mu)\le V(\mu)
\]
for all \(\mu\in K\). Then for every orbit \(\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}\mu_n\):

1. The sequence \(V(\mu_n)\) converges.
2. Every weak limit point \(\mu_\infty\) of the orbit satisfies
   \[
   V(\mathcal{T}\mu_\infty)=V(\mu_\infty).
   \]
3. If \(V(\mathcal{T}\mu)=V(\mu)\) implies \(\mathcal{T}\mu=\mu\), then every weak limit point is an invariant measure.

**Proof.**  
The sequence \(V(\mu_n)\) is monotone nonincreasing and bounded below, hence convergent. Let \(\mu_{n_j}\rightharpoonup\mu_\infty\). By continuity of \(\mathcal{T}\),
\[
\mathcal{T}\mu_{n_j}\rightharpoonup \mathcal{T}\mu_\infty.
\]
Since \(V\) is continuous,
\[
V(\mu_\infty)
=
\lim_{j\to\infty}V(\mu_{n_j})
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}V(\mu_n),
\]
and similarly
\[
V(\mathcal{T}\mu_\infty)
=
\lim_{j\to\infty}V(\mathcal{T}\mu_{n_j})
=
\lim_{j\to\infty}V(\mu_{n_j+1})
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}V(\mu_n).
\]
Thus \(V(\mathcal{T}\mu_\infty)=V(\mu_\infty)\). The final claim follows immediately. \(\square\)

This theorem provides a general mechanism for proving convergence to invariant measures using measure-space Lyapunov functionals such as entropy, energy, free energy, or relative entropy.

---

### 5.5 Entropy dissipation for Markov recursions

Let \(K(x,dy)\) be a Markov kernel, and let \(\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X)\) be invariant:
\[
\mathcal{T}_K\nu=\nu.
\]
Define the relative entropy
\[
D(\mu\|\nu)
:=
\int_X \log\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\right)\,d\mu
\]
when \(\mu\ll\nu\), and \(+\infty\) otherwise.

**Proposition 5.5.**  
For every \(\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)\),
\[
D(\mathcal{T}_K\mu\|\nu)
\le
D(\mu\|\nu).
\]

This is the data-processing inequality for relative entropy. It shows that relative entropy with respect to an invariant reference measure is a Lyapunov functional for a large class of linear recursive measure dynamics.

---

### 5.6 Linearization near a fixed measure

Suppose \(\mu_\ast\) is a fixed point and \(\mathcal{T}\) is differentiable in a suitable sense. For a perturbation
\[
\mu_\varepsilon=\mu_\ast+\varepsilon\eta+o(\varepsilon),
\]
one obtains
\[
\mathcal{T}(\mu_\varepsilon)
=
\mu_\ast+\varepsilon\,D\mathcal{T}_{\mu_\ast}[\eta]+o(\varepsilon).
\]
The linearized recursion is
\[
\eta_{n+1}=D\mathcal{T}_{\mu_\ast}[\eta_n].
\]
Spectral properties of \(D\mathcal{T}_{\mu_\ast}\) determine local stability. In transfer-operator settings, this linearized operator is often a Perron–Frobenius or Markov operator acting on densities or signed measures.

---

## 6. Density Recursions and Perron–Frobenius Operators

Assume now that \(M\) is a \(d\)-dimensional Riemannian manifold with metric \(g\), and that
\[
\mu_n=\rho_n\,d\operatorname{vol}_g.
\]
Let \(F:M\to M\) be a \(C^1\) diffeomorphism, and define
\[
\mu_{n+1}=F_\#\mu_n.
\]
For a test function \(\varphi\),
\[
\int_M \varphi(y)\,\rho_{n+1}(y)\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(y)
=
\int_M \varphi(F(x))\,\rho_n(x)\,d\operatorname{vol}_g(x).
\]

In local coordinates, using
\[
d\operatorname{vol}_g(x)=\sqrt{\det g(x)}\,dx,
\]
the change-of-variables formula gives
\[
\rho_{n+1}(F(x))
\sqrt{\det g(F(x))}
\left|\det DF(x)\right|
=
\rho_n(x)\sqrt{\det g(x)}.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{n+1}(y)
=
\rho_n(F^{-1}(y))
\frac{\sqrt{\det g(F^{-1}(y))}}
{\sqrt{\det g(y)}\,
\left|\det DF(F^{-1}(y))\right|}.
}
\]

If \(F\) is finite-to-one rather than injective, the formula becomes
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{n+1}(y)
=
\sum_{x\in F^{-1}(y)}
\rho_n(x)
\frac{\sqrt{\det g(x)}}
{\sqrt{\det g(y)}\,
\left|\det DF(x)\right|}.
}
\]

Thus the density recursion is governed by the Perron–Frobenius operator
\[
\mathcal{L}_F\rho(y)
=
\sum_{x\in F^{-1}(y)}
\rho(x)
\frac{\sqrt{\det g(x)}}
{\sqrt{\det g(y)}\,
\left|\det DF(x)\right|}.
\]
The measure recursion
\[
\mu_{n+1}=F_\#\mu_n
\]
is equivalent to
\[
\rho_{n+1}=\mathcal{L}_F\rho_n.
\]

In Euclidean space with Lebesgue measure, this reduces to the classical formula
\[
\rho_{n+1}(y)
=
\sum_{x\in F^{-1}(y)}
\frac{\rho_n(x)}{|\det DF(x)|}.
\]

## 7. Moment Tensor Dynamics

Let \(X=\mathbb{R}^d\). For \(k\ge1\), define the \(k\)-th raw moment tensor of \(\mu\) by
\[
M^{(k)}(\mu)
:=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} x^{\otimes k}\,d\mu(x).
\]
In coordinates,
\[
M^{i_1\cdots i_k}(\mu)
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}
x^{i_1}\cdots x^{i_k}\,d\mu(x).
\]

For a deterministic recursive measure dynamics
\[
\mu_{n+1}=F_\#\mu_n,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
M^{i_1\cdots i_k}_{n+1}
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}
F^{i_1}(x)\cdots F^{i_k}(x)\,d\mu_n(x).
}
\]
This is the fundamental moment evolution law.

---

### 7.1 Polynomial maps and exact hierarchies

Let \(F:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d\) be polynomial of degree \(q\), with components
\[
F^i(x)
=
\sum_{|\alpha|\le q}
a^i_\alpha x^\alpha,
\]
where \(\alpha=(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_d)\) is a multi-index,
\[
x^\alpha
=
(x^1)^{\alpha_1}\cdots(x^d)^{\alpha_d},
\qquad
|\alpha|=\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_d.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
M^{i_1\cdots i_k}_{n+1}
=
\sum_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k}
a^{i_1}_{\alpha_1}\cdots a^{i_k}_{\alpha_k}
\,
M^{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_k}_n.
}
\]
Here
\[
M^{\beta}_n
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} x^\beta\,d\mu_n.
\]

This gives an exact tensorial recurrence. If \(q=1\), the hierarchy for moments up to order \(K\) is closed. If \(q>1\), the \(k\)-th moment at time \(n+1\) depends on moments of order up to \(qk\) at time \(n\), producing a hierarchy that generally requires closure or truncation.

---

### 7.2 Affine dynamics

Let
\[
F(x)=Ax+b,
\]
where \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) and \(b\in\mathbb{R}^d\). Then the first moment satisfies
\[
m_{n+1}
=
A m_n+b,
\]
where
\[
m_n:=M^{(1)}_n=\int x\,d\mu_n.
\]

The second raw moment satisfies
\[
M^{(2)}_{n+1}
=
A M^{(2)}_n A^\top
+
A m_n b^\top
+
b m_n^\top A^\top
+
b b^\top.
\]

Let
\[
C_n
=
M^{(2)}_n-m_nm_n^\top
\]
be the covariance tensor. Then
\[
\boxed{
C_{n+1}
=
A C_n A^\top.
}
\]
Thus affine measure dynamics induces a tensorial linear action on central moments.

More generally, for the \(k\)-th central moment tensor
\[
\kappa^{(k)}_n
=
\int (x-m_n)^{\otimes k}\,d\mu_n,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\kappa^{(k)}_{n+1}
=
A^{\otimes k}\kappa^{(k)}_n.
}
\]

If the recursion includes additive noise,
\[
\mu_{n+1}
=
\operatorname{Law}(AX_n+b+\xi_n),
\]
with \(\xi_n\) independent of \(X_n\) and covariance tensor \(\Sigma\), then
\[
m_{n+1}=Am_n+b+\mathbb{E}\xi_n,
\]
and
\[
C_{n+1}=AC_nA^\top+\Sigma.
\]

---

### 7.3 Nonlinear moment closure by Taylor expansion

Suppose
\[
\mu_{n+1}=F_\#\mu_n
\]
with smooth nonlinear \(F\). Let \(m_n=\int x\,d\mu_n\) and write
\[
x=m_n+\delta x.
\]
Expanding \(F\) about \(m_n\),
\[
F^i(x)
=
F^i(m_n)
+
J^i{}_j(m_n)\delta x^j
+
\frac12 H^i{}_{jk}(m_n)\delta x^j\delta x^k
+
O(|\delta x|^3),
\]
where
\[
J^i{}_j=\frac{\partial F^i}{\partial x^j},
\qquad
H^i{}_{jk}=\frac{\partial^2 F^i}{\partial x^j\partial x^k}.
\]

Then the mean evolves as
\[
m_{n+1}^i
=
F^i(m_n)
+
\frac12 H^i{}_{jk}(m_n)C_n^{jk}
+
O(\mathbb{E}|\delta x|^3).
\]

The covariance evolves as
\[
C_{n+1}^{ij}
=
J^i{}_p(m_n)J^j{}_q(m_n)C_n^{pq}
+
\text{higher-order corrections}.
\]

This provides a systematic tensorial closure scheme: the evolution of low-order moments is expressed in terms of higher-order moments, and truncation yields approximate finite-dimensional recursions.

---

## 8. Continuum Limits and Measure-Valued Evolution Equations

Recursive measure dynamics is discrete in its natural form. However, many applications require a continuous-time limit. Suppose the recursion depends on a small parameter \(h>0\):
\[
\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}_h(\mu_n).
\]
We seek a limit
\[
\mu_{\lfloor t/h\rfloor}\to \mu_t
\]
as \(h\to0\).

A basic example is
\[
X_{n+1}
=
X_n+h b(X_n,\mu_n)
+
\sqrt{h}\,\sigma(X_n,\mu_n)\xi_n,
\]
where \(\xi_n\) are independent standard Gaussian random variables. Then
\[
\mu_{n+1}=\operatorname{Law}(X_{n+1}).
\]

For a smooth test function \(\varphi\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int \varphi\,d\mu_{n+1}
-
\int \varphi\,d\mu_n
&=
\int
\left[
\varphi(x+h b(x,\mu_n))
+
\frac{h}{2}
a^{ij}(x,\mu_n)
\partial_i\partial_j\varphi(x)
-
\varphi(x)
\right]
d\mu_n(x)
+
o(h),
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
a(x,\mu)=\sigma(x,\mu)\sigma(x,\mu)^\top.
\]
Expanding,
\[
\varphi(x+h b)-\varphi(x)
=
h b^i\partial_i\varphi
+
o(h).
\]
Therefore,
\[
\frac{1}{h}
\left(
\int \varphi\,d\mu_{n+1}
-
\int \varphi\,d\mu_n
\right)
\to
\int
\left(
b^i\partial_i\varphi
+
\frac12 a^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j\varphi
\right)
d\mu_t.
\]

In density form, if \(\mu_t=\rho_t\,dx\), the limiting equation is the Fokker–Planck equation
\[
\boxed{
\partial_t\rho_t
=
-\partial_i(b^i\rho_t)
+
\frac12\partial_i\partial_j(a^{ij}\rho_t).
}
\]
Using tensor double-contraction notation,
\[
\partial_t\rho
=
-\nabla\cdot(b\rho)
+
\frac12 \nabla\nabla:(a\rho).
\]

Thus many continuous measure-valued evolution equations arise as scaling limits of recursive measure dynamics.

---

### 8.1 Gradient-flow recursions

If the vector field has variational form
\[
b=-\nabla\frac{\delta \mathcal{F}}{\delta\rho},
\]
then the continuum limit becomes
\[
\partial_t\rho
=
\nabla\cdot
\left(
\rho\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\rho}
\right),
\]
which is the Wasserstein gradient flow of the functional \(\mathcal{F}\). Discrete recursive measure dynamics may therefore be interpreted as implicit or explicit schemes for variational measure flows.

For example, with entropy
\[
\mathcal{S}(\rho)=\int\rho\log\rho\,dx,
\]
one obtains diffusion; with energy
\[
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\int V\rho\,dx+\frac12\int W(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,dx\,dy,
\]
one obtains aggregation-diffusion dynamics.

---

## 9. Geometric Measure Dynamics

Recursive measure dynamics extends naturally from probability measures to general Radon measures, Hausdorff measures, varifolds, and currents. This is especially relevant in geometric measure theory.

---

### 9.1 Rectifiable measures

Let \(E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^D\) be countably \(m\)-rectifiable, and let
\[
\mu=\theta\,\mathcal{H}^m\!\llcorner E
\]
be a weighted Hausdorff measure, where \(\theta\) is a nonnegative density.

Let \(F:\mathbb{R}^D\to\mathbb{R}^D\) be Lipschitz and injective on \(E\). Define
\[
\mu_{n+1}=F_\#\mu_n.
\]
If
\[
\mu_n=\theta_n\,\mathcal{H}^m\!\llcorner E_n,
\]
then
\[
E_{n+1}=F(E_n),
\]
and for \(y\in E_{n+1}\),
\[
\theta_{n+1}(y)
=
\theta_n(F^{-1}(y))\,J_mF^{-1}(y),
\]
where \(J_mF^{-1}\) is the \(m\)-dimensional Jacobian of \(F^{-1}\) restricted to the approximate tangent plane.

Equivalently, if \(x=F^{-1}(y)\), then
\[
\theta_{n+1}(F(x))
=
\frac{\theta_n(x)}{J_mF(x)}.
\]

The \(m\)-Jacobian is defined intrinsically by
\[
J_mF(x)
=
\sqrt{
\det\left(
(DF|_{\tau_x E})^\ast DF|_{\tau_x E}
\right)
},
\]
where \(\tau_x E\) is the approximate tangent \(m\)-plane to \(E\) at \(x\).

This yields a recursive dynamics for weighted rectifiable sets:
\[
(E_n,\theta_n)
\longmapsto
(F(E_n),\theta_{n+1}).
\]

---

### 9.2 Varifold recursions

An \(m\)-varifold in \(\mathbb{R}^D\) may be viewed as a measure on \(\mathbb{R}^D\times G(D,m)\), where \(G(D,m)\) is the Grassmannian of \(m\)-planes. A rectifiable varifold has the form
\[
V=\mu\otimes\delta_{\tau_x}.
\]

Given a map \(F\), the pushforward varifold \(F_\#V\) records both the transported mass and the transformed tangent planes:
\[
\tau_y F(E)
=
DF(x)\tau_x E,
\qquad
y=F(x).
\]
Thus recursive measure dynamics generates geometric flows of generalized surfaces:
\[
V_{n+1}=F_{n\#}V_n.
\]

If \(F_n\) depends on geometric quantities such as curvature, the recursion becomes a discrete geometric evolution law.

---

### 9.3 Current recursions

Let \(T\) be an \(m\)-dimensional current. For a proper Lipschitz map \(F\), the pushforward current \(F_\#T\) is defined by
\[
(F_\#T)(\omega)
=
T(F^\ast\omega),
\]
where \(\omega\) is a compactly supported smooth \(m\)-form.

A recursive current dynamics is
\[
T_{n+1}=F_{n\#}T_n.
\]
The boundary operator commutes with pushforward:
\[
\partial(F_\#T)=F_\#(\partial T).
\]
If \(F\) has Lipschitz constant \(L\), then the mass satisfies
\[
\mathbf{M}(F_\#T)
\le
L^m\mathbf{M}(T).
\]

Thus RMD provides a natural framework for discrete evolution of oriented geometric measures, including conservation or controlled growth of boundary structure and mass.

---

## 10. Applications

### 10.1 Probability theory

In probability, RMD appears whenever a distribution is defined recursively. A canonical form is the distributional fixed-point equation
\[
X\stackrel{d}{=} \Phi(X,Z),
\]
where \(Z\) is an auxiliary random element. If \(\mu=\operatorname{Law}(X)\), this becomes
\[
\mu=\mathcal{T}\mu,
\]
with
\[
\mathcal{T}\mu
=
\operatorname{Law}(\Phi(X,Z)),
\qquad X\sim\mu.
\]

The recursive sequence
\[
\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}\mu_n
\]
is a Picard iteration in the space of probability laws. If \(\mathcal{T}\) is a contraction in Wasserstein distance, Theorem 5.2 gives existence, uniqueness, and convergence.

Important special cases include:

- smoothing transforms;
- branching random walks;
- recursive stochastic equations;
- random affine equations;
- iterative random algorithms;
- Markov chain stationary measures.

For Markov kernels, RMD is precisely the forward evolution of the law:
\[
\mu_{n+1}=\mu_n K.
\]
Invariant measures are stationary distributions, and Lyapunov functionals such as relative entropy provide convergence information.

---

### 10.2 Dynamical systems

Let \(F:X\to X\) be a measurable dynamical map. The recursive measure dynamics
\[
\mu_{n+1}=F_\#\mu_n
\]
describes the evolution of initial uncertainty under deterministic dynamics. Invariant measures satisfy
\[
F_\#\mu_\ast=\mu_\ast.
\]

For nonsingular smooth maps, the density evolution is governed by the Perron–Frobenius operator:
\[
\rho_{n+1}=\mathcal{L}_F\rho_n.
\]
Fixed densities solve
\[
\mathcal{L}_F\rho_\ast=\rho_\ast.
\]

In chaotic systems, physically relevant measures such as SRB measures may be obtained as limits or statistical attractors of recursive measure dynamics. For expanding or hyperbolic systems, transfer operators acting on suitable Banach spaces yield spectral information about mixing, decay of correlations, and linear response.

RMD also clarifies the relationship between three notions:

1. **Pushforward dynamics** \(\mu_{n+1}=F_\#\mu_n\);
2. **Observable dynamics** \(f\mapsto f\circ F\);
3. **Density dynamics** \(\rho_{n+1}=\mathcal{L}_F\rho_n\).

The measure-theoretic recursion is the common foundation.

---

### 10.3 Statistical mechanics and renormalization

In statistical mechanics, states are often represented by probability measures on configuration spaces. A renormalization transformation \(R\) maps a microscopic measure to an effective coarse-grained measure:
\[
\mu_{n+1}=R(\mu_n).
\]
This is intrinsically a recursive measure dynamics.

For spin systems, a block-spin transformation may be written as
\[
(R\mu)(d\sigma')
=
\int \mu(d\sigma)\,K(\sigma,d\sigma'),
\]
where \(K\) encodes coarse-graining. More generally, \(R\) may be nonlinear due to normalization, constraint enforcement, or feedback from effective parameters.

Fixed points
\[
R\mu_\ast=\mu_\ast
\]
correspond to scale-invariant phases. Linearization about \(\mu_\ast\),
\[
\eta_{n+1}=DR_{\mu_\ast}[\eta_n],
\]
identifies relevant, marginal, and irrelevant directions in the space of measures. In this sense, RMD gives a measure-theoretic formulation of the renormalization group.

Free-energy functionals often serve as Lyapunov functionals under coarse-graining. When a renormalization map is entropy-decreasing or relative-entropy-decreasing with respect to a reference Gibbs measure, Theorem 5.4 and Proposition 5.5 provide a rigorous stability mechanism.

### 10.4 Geometric measure theory

RMD is particularly natural in geometric measure theory because many geometric objects are themselves measures.

Examples include:

- iterated function systems generating self-similar measures;
- recursive construction of Cantor-type measures;
- pushforwards of Hausdorff measures under Lipschitz maps;
- discrete varifold flows;
- current evolution under Lipschitz or smooth maps;
- geometric approximation schemes for curvature-driven flows.

For self-similar sets, the invariant measure satisfies
\[
\mu
=
\sum_{i=1}^m p_i F_{i\#}\mu.
\]
This is both a probabilistic object and a geometric one: it encodes the distribution of mass on a fractal set.

For currents and varifolds, RMD permits discrete evolution while preserving structural operations such as boundary and pushforward:
\[
\partial F_\#T=F_\#\partial T.
\]
This makes RMD suitable for discrete geometric analysis and variational approximation schemes.

## 11. Structural Interpretation

Recursive Measure Dynamics can be interpreted as a dynamical system on an infinite-dimensional space of measures. The essential structural features are the following.

### 11.1 Measure space as phase space

The phase space is not \(X\), but a space of measures:
\[
\mathcal{P}(X),\quad \mathcal{M}_+(X),\quad\text{or a space of geometric measures}.
\]
The transformation \(\mathcal{T}\) is the vector field or update rule on this phase space.

### 11.2 Integration as evaluation of a moving state

The integral
\[
\int f\,d\mu_n
\]
is no longer a static pairing. It is the evaluation of an observable against a moving state in measure space.

### 11.3 Moments as tensorial coordinates

Moment tensors provide coordinates on measure space:
\[
\mu
\longmapsto
\left(
M^{(1)}(\mu),M^{(2)}(\mu),M^{(3)}(\mu),\dots
\right).
\]
The recursive measure dynamics induces a tensor recurrence. For affine dynamics this recurrence is linear; for polynomial dynamics it is algebraic and hierarchical.

### 11.4 Transfer operators as observable shadows

When \(\mathcal{T}\) is linear, its action on observables is a transfer or Koopman operator. In nonlinear RMD, the observable evolution becomes a nonlinear functional on measure space.

### 11.5 Geometry through Jacobians

When measures are absolutely continuous or rectifiable, recursion is mediated by Jacobians. The geometric content of RMD is encoded in determinant and area factors:
\[
\rho_{n+1}
\sim
\frac{\rho_n}{|\det DF|},
\qquad
\theta_{n+1}
\sim
\frac{\theta_n}{J_mF}.
\]

## 12. Conclusion

We have formulated Recursive Measure Dynamics as a general theory of measure evolution. The central principle is that measures may be treated as dynamical fields satisfying
\[
\mu_{n+1}=\mathcal{T}(\mu_n),
\]
and that integration, moments, densities, and geometric mass distributions must then be studied as evolving objects.

The framework unifies several classical theories:

- Markov chains and stochastic kernels;
- Perron–Frobenius and transfer-operator theory;
- invariant measures in dynamical systems;
- recursive distributional equations in probability;
- renormalization transformations in statistical mechanics;
- pushforwards of Hausdorff measures, varifolds, and currents in geometric measure theory.

The main analytical mechanisms are:

1. fixed-point theory in weak topologies;
2. contraction in Wasserstein spaces;
3. Lyapunov functionals such as entropy and free energy;
4. tensorial moment recurrences;
5. Jacobian-based density transformations;
6. continuum limits as Fokker–Planck and gradient-flow equations;
7. geometric pushforward laws for rectifiable measures and currents.

Recursive Measure Dynamics thus provides a coherent language for problems in which the measure is not a background structure but the primary evolving quantity. Future development may include stochastic recursive measure equations, measure-space differential geometry, infinite-dimensional spectral theory of nonlinear transfer maps, and numerical schemes preserving geometric and variational structure.
