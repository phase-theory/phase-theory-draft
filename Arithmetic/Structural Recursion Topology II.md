# Structural Recursion Topology II  
## Recursive Neighborhood Systems and Self-Refining Spaces

**Preprint / White Paper**

---

## Abstract

Structural Recursion Topology II (SRT-II) develops a topology in which open sets are not primitive static objects but are derived from recursively generated neighborhood systems. The central evolution law is

\[
\tau_{n+1}=\mathcal{G}(\tau_n),
\]

where \(\tau_n\) is the topology, or more fundamentally the neighborhood structure, available at recursion depth \(n\), and \(\mathcal{G}\) is a structural refinement operator. In SRT-II, the basic ontological object is not the open set but the finite-depth neighborhood proof: a point is regarded as being inside a region only insofar as that region can be certified by successive local refinements.

This paper gives an axiomatic formulation of SRT-II, proves fixed-point and convergence theorems for recursive topological evolution, develops a tensorial calculus for finite recursive refinement systems, and applies the framework to four domains: general topology, fractal analysis, dynamical systems, and complex networks. In particular, we show that SRT-II naturally yields least fixed-point topologies, coarsest continuous topologies for iterated maps, symbolic/fractal cylinder topologies, and graph color-refinement topologies analogous to Weisfeiler–Leman refinement.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical topology treats a topology \(\tau\) on a set \(X\) as a completed family of open sets satisfying axioms of arbitrary union and finite intersection. This static conception is powerful but less natural in contexts where spatial structure is observed, computed, or generated recursively:

1. **Fractals** are often given by iterative refinement of cells.
2. **Dynamical systems** generate observable topology by repeated pullback under a map.
3. **Complex networks** acquire topology through iterative neighborhood aggregation.
4. **Constructive and computational topology** only accesses open sets through finite proofs or finite approximations.

SRT-II proposes that topology should be understood as an evolving recursive system:

\[
\tau_0 \longrightarrow \tau_1 \longrightarrow \tau_2 \longrightarrow \cdots,
\qquad
\tau_{n+1}=\mathcal{G}(\tau_n).
\]

The operator \(\mathcal{G}\) is not an arbitrary map; it encodes a structural refinement rule. It may arise from inverse images of maps, modal neighborhood operators, subdivision rules, graph adjacencies, or tensorial refinement kernels.

The essential shift is:

\[
\text{open sets as primitives}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\text{recursively generated neighborhood systems as primitives}.
\]

An open set at depth \(n\) is then a set whose membership can be certified by \(n\)-fold recursive neighborhood data.

## 2. Lattice-Theoretic Preliminaries

Let \(X\) be a set. Denote by \(\mathcal{P}(X)\) its power set and by \(\operatorname{Top}(X)\) the collection of all topologies on \(X\).

For a family \(\mathcal{S}\subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)\), let

\[
\langle \mathcal{S}\rangle
\]

denote the topology generated by \(\mathcal{S}\) as a subbasis. If \(\tau,\sigma\in \operatorname{Top}(X)\), define their join by

\[
\tau\vee\sigma := \langle \tau\cup\sigma\rangle.
\]

The partial order is inclusion:

\[
\tau\leq\sigma
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tau\subseteq\sigma,
\]

where \(\sigma\) is finer than \(\tau\).

### Proposition 2.1

\(\operatorname{Top}(X)\) is a complete lattice.

**Proof.**  
The meet of a family \(\{\tau_i\}_{i\in I}\) is the intersection

\[
\bigwedge_{i\in I}\tau_i=\bigcap_{i\in I}\tau_i,
\]

which is again a topology. The join is

\[
\bigvee_{i\in I}\tau_i
=
\left\langle \bigcup_{i\in I}\tau_i\right\rangle.
\]

Thus every subset of \(\operatorname{Top}(X)\) has a meet and a join. \(\square\)

This lattice structure is the natural ambient space for recursive topological evolution.

## 3. Core Framework of SRT-II

### 3.1 Structural operators

Let \(\Phi\) be a set of monotone operators

\[
\varphi:\mathcal{P}(X)\to \mathcal{P}(X).
\]

We call \(\Phi\) a **structural signature**. Typical examples include:

1. **Inverse-image operators** for maps \(f:X\to X\):

   \[
   \varphi_f(U)=f^{-1}(U).
   \]

2. **Modal neighborhood operators** for relations \(R\subseteq X\times X\):

   \[
   \Box_R(U)
   =
   \{x\in X : \{x\}\cup R[x]\subseteq U\},
   \]

   where

   \[
   R[x]=\{y\in X:(x,y)\in R\}.
   \]

3. **Refinement operators** induced by subdivision or cell systems.

For a topology \(\tau\), define

\[
\Phi(\tau)
=
\{\varphi(U):\varphi\in \Phi,\ U\in\tau\}.
\]

The SRT-II structural recursion operator is

\[
\mathcal{G}_\Phi(\tau)
=
\left\langle \tau\cup \Phi(\tau)\right\rangle.
\]

Thus an SRT-II evolution is a sequence

\[
\tau_{n+1}
=
\mathcal{G}_\Phi(\tau_n),
\qquad
n=0,1,2,\dots.
\]

This is the precise form of the core equation

\[
\tau_{n+1}=\mathcal{G}(\tau_n).
\]

---

### 3.2 Recursive neighborhood systems

Although \(\tau_n\) is a topology, SRT-II regards the topology as derived from a recursive neighborhood system.

Let \(\mathcal{S}_0\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)\) be an initial subbasis generating \(\tau_0\):

\[
\tau_0=\langle \mathcal{S}_0\rangle.
\]

Define recursively

\[
\mathcal{S}_{n+1}
=
\mathcal{S}_n
\cup
\{\varphi(U):\varphi\in\Phi,\ U\in\tau_n\}.
\]

Then

\[
\tau_n=\langle \mathcal{S}_n\rangle.
\]

For each \(x\in X\), define the depth-\(n\) neighborhood basis

\[
\mathscr{N}_n(x)
=
\left\{
\bigcap_{k=1}^m S_k
:
m\geq 1,\ S_k\in \mathcal{S}_n,\ x\in \bigcap_{k=1}^m S_k
\right\}.
\]

Then a set \(U\subseteq X\) is \(\tau_n\)-open precisely when

\[
\forall x\in U,\ \exists N\in \mathscr{N}_n(x)
\quad\text{such that}\quad
N\subseteq U.
\]

Thus the primitive object is not \(\tau_n\) but the recursively generated family

\[
\{\mathscr{N}_n(x)\}_{x\in X}.
\]

The topology is its induced observable open structure.

---

### 3.3 Basic properties

#### Proposition 3.1

The operator \(\mathcal{G}_\Phi\) is monotone and inflationary.

**Proof.**  
If \(\tau\subseteq\sigma\), then

\[
\Phi(\tau)\subseteq \Phi(\sigma),
\]

so

\[
\tau\cup\Phi(\tau)
\subseteq
\sigma\cup\Phi(\sigma).
\]

Applying topology generation gives

\[
\mathcal{G}_\Phi(\tau)
\subseteq
\mathcal{G}_\Phi(\sigma).
\]

Also,

\[
\tau\subseteq \tau\cup\Phi(\tau),
\]

hence

\[
\tau\subseteq \mathcal{G}_\Phi(\tau).
\]

Thus \(\mathcal{G}_\Phi\) is monotone and inflationary. \(\square\)

#### Corollary 3.2

The SRT-II sequence is increasing:

\[
\tau_0\subseteq \tau_1\subseteq \tau_2\subseteq\cdots.
\]

Equivalently, the neighborhood systems refine:

\[
\mathscr{N}_n(x)\subseteq \mathscr{N}_{n+1}(x)
\]

for every \(x\in X\).

## 4. Fixed Points, Closure, and Convergence

Because \(\operatorname{Top}(X)\) is a complete lattice and \(\mathcal{G}\) is monotone, SRT-II evolution admits fixed-point semantics.

### 4.1 Transfinite fixed-point theorem

Given \(\tau_0\), define recursively

\[
\tau_{n+1}=\mathcal{G}(\tau_n)
\]

for finite \(n\). More generally, define a transfinite sequence:

\[
\tau_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal{G}(\tau_\alpha),
\]

and for a limit ordinal \(\lambda\),

\[
\tau_\lambda
=
\bigvee_{\alpha<\lambda}\tau_\alpha.
\]

#### Theorem 4.1

Let \(\mathcal{G}:\operatorname{Top}(X)\to \operatorname{Top}(X)\) be monotone and inflationary. Then there exists an ordinal \(\kappa\) such that

\[
\tau_{\kappa+1}=\tau_\kappa.
\]

Moreover, \(\tau_\kappa\) is the least fixed point of \(\mathcal{G}\) above \(\tau_0\).

**Proof.**  
Since \(\mathcal{G}\) is inflationary,

\[
\tau_\alpha\subseteq \tau_{\alpha+1}
\]

for all \(\alpha\). Hence \(\{\tau_\alpha\}\) is an increasing chain in the complete lattice \(\operatorname{Top}(X)\).

Because \(\operatorname{Top}(X)\) is a set, no strictly increasing chain can have length greater than the cardinality of \(\operatorname{Top}(X)\) plus one. Therefore there exists an ordinal \(\kappa\) such that

\[
\tau_{\kappa+1}=\tau_\kappa.
\]

Thus \(\tau_\kappa\) is a fixed point.

To show minimality, let \(\sigma\) be any fixed point with \(\tau_0\subseteq\sigma\). By monotonicity,

\[
\tau_\alpha\subseteq\sigma
\]

for all \(\alpha\) by transfinite induction. Therefore

\[
\tau_\kappa\subseteq\sigma.
\]

Thus \(\tau_\kappa\) is the least fixed point above \(\tau_0\). \(\square\)

---

### 4.2 \(\omega\)-continuity

In many applications, one wants the fixed point to be reached at the countable stage

\[
\tau_\omega=\bigvee_{n<\omega}\tau_n.
\]

This holds under a continuity condition.

#### Theorem 4.2

If \(\mathcal{G}\) preserves directed joins, i.e.

\[
\mathcal{G}\left(\bigvee_i \tau_i\right)
=
\bigvee_i \mathcal{G}(\tau_i)
\]

for every directed family \(\{\tau_i\}\), then

\[
\mathcal{G}(\tau_\omega)=\tau_\omega.
\]

**Proof.**  
Since

\[
\tau_\omega=\bigvee_{n<\omega}\tau_n,
\]

we have

\[
\mathcal{G}(\tau_\omega)
=
\mathcal{G}\left(\bigvee_{n<\omega}\tau_n\right)
=
\bigvee_{n<\omega}\mathcal{G}(\tau_n)
=
\bigvee_{n<\omega}\tau_{n+1}
=
\tau_\omega.
\]

Thus \(\tau_\omega\) is a fixed point. \(\square\)

When \(\mathcal{G}\) is not \(\omega\)-continuous, the SRT-II fixed point may require transfinite recursion.

---

### 4.3 Finite stabilization

If \(X\) is finite, \(\operatorname{Top}(X)\) is finite. Hence any increasing sequence

\[
\tau_0\subseteq \tau_1\subseteq \tau_2\subseteq\cdots
\]

must stabilize after finitely many steps.

#### Corollary 4.3

For finite \(X\), every SRT-II evolution generated by a monotone inflationary operator stabilizes at some finite stage \(N\):

\[
\tau_N=\tau_{N+1}=\cdots.
\]

This is especially important in finite network and symbolic applications.

## 5. Tensorial Calculus of Recursive Refinement

In finite or discretized settings, SRT-II admits a tensorial formulation. This is useful for computational topology, fractals, and networks.

Let \(C_n\) be a finite set of cells at level \(n\). Each cell \(\alpha\in C_n\) has a support

\[
\sigma_n(\alpha)\subseteq X.
\]

Define the incidence tensor

\[
I^{(n)}_{x\alpha}
=
\begin{cases}
1, & x\in \sigma_n(\alpha),\\
0, & x\notin \sigma_n(\alpha).
\end{cases}
\]

Let

\[
\mathsf{R}^{(n)}{}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
\in
\{0,1\}
\]

be the refinement tensor, where

\[
\mathsf{R}^{(n)}{}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=1
\]

means that cell \(\beta\in C_{n+1}\) refines cell \(\alpha\in C_n\).

The refinement condition is

\[
\sigma_{n+1}(\beta)
\subseteq
\sigma_n(\alpha)
\quad
\text{whenever}
\quad
\mathsf{R}^{(n)}{}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=1.
\]

The covering condition can be written tensorially as

\[
I^{(n)}_{x\alpha}
=
\bigvee_{\beta}
\mathsf{R}^{(n)}{}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
\wedge
I^{(n+1)}_{x\beta},
\]

where \(\vee,\wedge\) denote Boolean join and meet.

If an open set at level \(n\) is represented by a selector vector

\[
o^{(n)\alpha}\in\{0,1\},
\]

then its pullback to level \(n+1\) is

\[
o^{(n+1)\beta}
=
\bigvee_{\alpha}
\mathsf{R}^{(n)}{}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
\wedge
o^{(n)\alpha}.
\]

The full recursive operator may be written schematically as

\[
o^{(n+1)}
=
\mathcal{G}^{(n)}(o^{(n)}),
\]

where \(\mathcal{G}^{(n)}\) is a Boolean tensor polynomial encoding refinement, intersection, and local closure rules.

For higher-order structures such as hypergraphs or simplicial complexes, one uses higher-order adjacency tensors

\[
A_{i_0}{}^{i_1\cdots i_r}.
\]

A recursive neighborhood aggregation may then be written as

\[
h^{(n+1)}_{i_0}
=
\sigma\left(
h^{(n)}_{i_0},
\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_r}
A_{i_0}{}^{i_1\cdots i_r}
h^{(n)}_{i_1}
\otimes\cdots\otimes
h^{(n)}_{i_r}
\right),
\]

where \(\sigma\) is a nonlinear activation or Boolean threshold. The topology at level \(n\) is generated by the level sets of \(h^{(n)}\).

## 6. Applications

# 6.1 General Topology

SRT-II gives a natural framework for topologies generated by successive approximations.

### Dyadic refinement

Let

\[
X=[0,1].
\]

Let \(\mathcal{B}_n\) be the family of dyadic intervals

\[
\left(\frac{k}{2^n},\frac{k+1}{2^n}\right),
\]

together with appropriate endpoint neighborhoods. Define \(\tau_n\) as the topology generated by \(\mathcal{B}_n\). The refinement operator \(\mathcal{G}\) bisects each interval:

\[
\mathcal{G}(\tau_n)=\tau_{n+1}.
\]

Then

\[
\tau_0\subseteq\tau_1\subseteq\tau_2\subseteq\cdots,
\]

and the limit topology

\[
\tau_\infty=\bigvee_{n<\omega}\tau_n
\]

is the usual topology on \([0,1]\).

More generally, suppose \((X,d)\) is a metric space and \(\{\mathscr{N}_n\}\) is an SRT-II neighborhood system satisfying:

1. Every \(N\in\mathscr{N}_n(x)\) is \(d\)-open.
2. For every \(x\in X\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exist \(n\) and \(N\in\mathscr{N}_n(x)\) such that

   \[
   N\subseteq B_d(x,\varepsilon).
   \]

Then the SRT-II limit topology equals the metric topology.

#### Theorem 6.1

Under the two conditions above,

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{SRT}}=\tau_d.
\]

**Proof.**  
Since every generator of \(\tau_n\) is \(d\)-open,

\[
\tau_n\subseteq\tau_d
\]

for all \(n\). Hence

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{SRT}}
=
\bigvee_n\tau_n
\subseteq
\tau_d.
\]

Conversely, let \(U\in\tau_d\) and \(x\in U\). Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) such that

\[
B_d(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U.
\]

By assumption, there exist \(n\) and \(N\in\mathscr{N}_n(x)\) with

\[
N\subseteq B_d(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq U.
\]

Therefore \(U\) is \(\tau_{\mathrm{SRT}}\)-open. Hence

\[
\tau_d\subseteq \tau_{\mathrm{SRT}}.
\]

Thus equality holds. \(\square\)

SRT-II also gives a clean language for non-Archimedean and ultrametric topologies arising from nested partitions.

# 6.2 Fractal Analysis

SRT-II is particularly natural for fractals generated by iterated function systems.

Let \((Y,d)\) be a complete metric space and let

\[
S_i:Y\to Y,
\qquad
i=1,\dots,m,
\]

be contracting similarities with ratios \(0<r_i<1\). The attractor \(K\) satisfies

\[
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^m S_i(K).
\]

Let

\[
\Sigma=\{1,\dots,m\}^{\mathbb{N}}
\]

be the shift space. For a finite word

\[
w=i_1i_2\cdots i_n,
\]

define

\[
S_w=S_{i_1}\circ S_{i_2}\circ\cdots\circ S_{i_n}
\]

and

\[
K_w=S_w(K).
\]

The sets \(K_w\) are the level-\(n\) fractal cells.

The refinement tensor is

\[
\mathsf{R}_{w,wi}=1,
\]

meaning that the child cell \(K_{wi}\) refines the parent cell \(K_w\).

Define the SRT-II topology at level \(n\) by

\[
\tau_n
=
\left\{
U\subseteq K:
\pi^{-1}(U)
\text{ is a union of length-}n\text{ cylinders}
\right\},
\]

where

\[
\pi:\Sigma\to K
\]

is the coding map.

Then

\[
\tau_{n+1}=\mathcal{G}(\tau_n),
\]

where \(\mathcal{G}\) splits each length-\(n\) cylinder into its \(m\) children.

The SRT-II limit topology is

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{SRT}}
=
\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}\tau_n.
\]

When the coding map is injective, or under strong separation, this topology agrees with the natural symbolic topology on the attractor.

---

### Fractal dimension from recursive refinement

Let

\[
r_w=r_{i_1}r_{i_2}\cdots r_{i_n}.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{diam}(K_w)
\leq
r_w\operatorname{diam}(K).
\]

The level-\(n\) SRT cover sum is

\[
S_n(s)
=
\sum_{|w|=n}
\operatorname{diam}(K_w)^s.
\]

For similarities,

\[
S_n(s)
\leq
\operatorname{diam}(K)^s
\left(
\sum_{i=1}^m r_i^s
\right)^n.
\]

The critical exponent \(s\) is determined by

\[
\sum_{i=1}^m r_i^s=1.
\]

This is the usual similarity dimension. In the SRT-II framework, it arises as the exponent at which the recursive refinement measure is scale-invariant.

If all contractions have the same ratio \(r\), then

\[
m r^s=1,
\]

so

\[
s=\frac{\log m}{-\log r}.
\]

Thus SRT-II recovers the standard box/similarity dimension through recursive topological refinement.

More generally, define the pressure function

\[
P(s)
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{1}{n}
\log
\sum_{|w|=n}
r_w^s.
\]

The SRT-II fractal dimension is the unique \(s\) such that

\[
P(s)=0,
\]

assuming standard separation conditions.

# 6.3 Dynamical Systems

Let

\[
F:X\to X
\]

be a map. Let \(\tau_0\) be an initial topology representing primitive observations.

Define

\[
\mathcal{G}_F(\tau)
=
\left\langle
\tau\cup F^{-1}(\tau)
\right\rangle,
\]

where

\[
F^{-1}(\tau)
=
\{F^{-1}(U):U\in\tau\}.
\]

Then the SRT-II evolution is

\[
\tau_{n+1}
=
\mathcal{G}_F(\tau_n).
\]

By induction,

\[
\tau_n
=
\bigvee_{k=0}^n F^{-k}(\tau_0).
\]

The limit topology is

\[
\tau_\infty
=
\bigvee_{k=0}^{\infty} F^{-k}(\tau_0).
\]

#### Theorem 6.2

The topology \(\tau_\infty\) is the coarsest topology finer than \(\tau_0\) for which \(F\) is continuous.

**Proof.**  
For any generator \(F^{-k}(U)\) with \(U\in\tau_0\),

\[
F^{-1}(F^{-k}(U))
=
F^{-(k+1)}(U),
\]

which belongs to \(\tau_\infty\). Therefore

\[
F^{-1}(\tau_\infty)\subseteq\tau_\infty,
\]

so \(F\) is continuous with respect to \(\tau_\infty\).

Now let \(\sigma\) be any topology such that

\[
\tau_0\subseteq\sigma
\]

and \(F\) is continuous with respect to \(\sigma\). Then

\[
F^{-1}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma.
\]

By induction,

\[
F^{-k}(\tau_0)\subseteq\sigma
\]

for all \(k\). Hence

\[
\tau_\infty
=
\bigvee_{k=0}^{\infty}F^{-k}(\tau_0)
\subseteq
\sigma.
\]

Thus \(\tau_\infty\) is the coarsest such topology. \(\square\)

For a family of maps \(\{f_a\}_{a\in A}\), define

\[
\mathcal{G}(\tau)
=
\left\langle
\tau
\cup
\bigcup_{a\in A}f_a^{-1}(\tau)
\right\rangle.
\]

Then the fixed point is the coarsest topology making all \(f_a\) continuous.

### Symbolic dynamics and entropy

Suppose \(\tau_0\) is generated by a finite partition \(\mathcal{P}\). Then \(\tau_n\) is generated by the join

\[
\mathcal{P}^{(n)}
=
\bigvee_{k=0}^n F^{-k}\mathcal{P}.
\]

The atoms of \(\tau_n\) are sets of the form

\[
P_{a_0}
\cap
F^{-1}(P_{a_1})
\cap
\cdots
\cap
F^{-n}(P_{a_n}).
\]

If \(\mathcal{P}\) is a generating partition, the topological entropy can be expressed as

\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(F)
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{1}{n}
\log
\left|
\operatorname{Atoms}(\tau_n)
\right|.
\]

Thus SRT-II interprets entropy as the exponential growth rate of recursive topological complexity.

# 6.4 Complex Networks

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite graph. Let

\[
A^i{}_j
\]

be the adjacency tensor, which in the simple graph case is the adjacency matrix.

Define the closed neighborhood of a vertex \(v\) by

\[
N[v]=\{v\}\cup\{u:(v,u)\in E\}.
\]

For \(U\subseteq V\), define the graph box operator

\[
\Box_A U
=
\{v\in V:N[v]\subseteq U\}.
\]

Given an initial topology \(\tau_0\) generated by vertex features or labels, define

\[
\mathcal{G}_A(\tau)
=
\left\langle
\tau
\cup
\{\Box_A U:U\in\tau\}
\right\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\tau_{n+1}
=
\mathcal{G}_A(\tau_n).
\]

Because \(V\) is finite, the sequence stabilizes:

\[
\tau_0
\subseteq
\tau_1
\subseteq
\cdots
\subseteq
\tau_N
=
\tau_{N+1}.
\]

The atoms of \(\tau_n\) define a recursive coloring of the graph.

At step \(n\), let \(P_n(v)\) be the atom of \(\tau_n\) containing \(v\). The next refinement is determined by the local neighborhood signature

\[
\Sigma_n(v)
=
\left(
P_n(v),
\{P_n(u):u\in N(v)\}
\right).
\]

With counting modalities, this becomes

\[
\Sigma_n(v)
=
\left(
P_n(v),
\left(
\#\{u\in N(v):P_n(u)=c\}
\right)_{c\in \operatorname{Atoms}(\tau_n)}
\right).
\]

This is precisely the color-refinement mechanism underlying the Weisfeiler–Leman algorithm.

In tensorial form, one may write a feature refinement equation

\[
h^{(n+1)}_i
=
\sigma\left(
h^{(n)}_i,
\sum_j A_i{}^j h^{(n)}_j
\right),
\]

where \(h^{(n)}_i\) is the feature vector of vertex \(i\) at recursion depth \(n\), and \(\sigma\) is a nonlinear aggregation map. The SRT-II topology \(\tau_n\) is generated by the level sets of \(h^{(n)}\).

For hypergraphs or simplicial complexes, replace \(A_i{}^j\) by a higher-order tensor

\[
A_{i_0}{}^{i_1\cdots i_r},
\]

yielding

\[
h^{(n+1)}_{i_0}
=
\sigma\left(
h^{(n)}_{i_0},
\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_r}
A_{i_0}{}^{i_1\cdots i_r}
h^{(n)}_{i_1}
\otimes
\cdots
\otimes
h^{(n)}_{i_r}
\right).
\]

Thus SRT-II provides a topological semantics for recursive network message passing, community refinement, and graph neural architectures.

## 7. SRT-II as a Conceptual Shift

SRT-II replaces the classical question

\[
\text{“Which sets are open?”}
\]

with the recursive question

\[
\text{“Which neighborhoods are certifiable at depth }n\text{?”}
\]

A topology is no longer a static collection but the limit of an operational refinement process.

The hierarchy

\[
\tau_0\subseteq\tau_1\subseteq\tau_2\subseteq\cdots
\]

should be interpreted as increasing observational or constructive resolution. The fixed point

\[
\tau_\infty
\]

is the structural closure of the initial data under the chosen recursion rules.

This perspective unifies several phenomena:

1. **Topological fixed points** as closures under recursive neighborhood rules.
2. **Fractal topology** as cylinder refinement under iterated function systems.
3. **Dynamical topology** as the coarsest topology making evolution continuous.
4. **Network topology** as iterative neighborhood aggregation and color refinement.

---

## 8. Conclusion

Structural Recursion Topology II provides a rigorous framework for topologies generated by recursive self-refinement. Its central equation,

\[
\tau_{n+1}=\mathcal{G}(\tau_n),
\]

encodes a broad class of topological evolutions arising from maps, relations, refinement tensors, and network adjacencies.

The main results established here are:

1. SRT-II may be formulated as a monotone inflationary operator on the complete lattice of topologies.
2. Recursive topological evolution has a least fixed point, obtainable by transfinite iteration.
3. Under directed-join continuity, the fixed point is reached at the countable stage.
4. Finite systems stabilize in finite time.
5. Tensorial refinement systems give a computational and geometric calculus for SRT-II.
6. The framework naturally applies to fractals, dynamical systems, and complex networks.

SRT-II suggests that topology should be understood not merely as the study of open sets, but as the study of recursively generated neighborhood structures and their limiting fixed points.
