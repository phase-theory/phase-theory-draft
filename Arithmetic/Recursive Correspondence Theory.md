# Recursive Correspondence Theory

**A preprint**

---

## Abstract

Recursive Correspondence Theory (RCT) develops a unified formalism in which correspondences are not static relations, spans, or morphisms, but evolving structural objects generated by recursive transformation. The primitive equation is

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n),
\]

where \(\mathcal C_n\) is a correspondence and \(\Psi\) is a structural operator acting on correspondences. This generalizes functions, set-valued maps, binary relations, categorical spans, profunctors, algebraic cycles, and optimization kernels. The present paper develops the foundational tensor calculus of correspondences, a categorical semantics, fixed-point and convergence theorems, and a series of applications to algebraic geometry, category theory, optimization, and economics. The central claim is that many structural phenomena in mathematics and mathematical economics are best modeled not by a single morphism \(f:A\to B\), but by a recursively generated family of multi-valued structural mappings whose limiting, periodic, or invariant behavior encodes the object of interest.

**Keywords:** correspondences, recursive systems, profunctors, spans, algebraic cycles, set-valued maps, fixed points, tropical mathematics, dynamic programming, general equilibrium.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical mathematics begins with functions

\[
f:A\to B.
\]

Relations generalize functions by allowing multiple outputs:

\[
R\subseteq A\times B.
\]

Correspondences generalize relations still further. In category theory they appear as spans,

\[
A \xleftarrow{p} R \xrightarrow{q} B,
\]

in algebraic geometry as algebraic cycles on \(X\times Y\), and in economics and optimization as set-valued maps,

\[
F:X\rightrightarrows Y.
\]

In most treatments, a correspondence is a static object. Recursive Correspondence Theory begins from a different premise: the correspondence itself should be allowed to evolve.

The basic recursive law is

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n),
\]

where \(\mathcal C_n\) is a correspondence and \(\Psi\) is a structural operator acting on correspondences. The object of study is the orbit

\[
\mathcal C_0,\quad \mathcal C_1=\Psi(\mathcal C_0),\quad
\mathcal C_2=\Psi(\mathcal C_1),\quad \ldots
\]

and its fixed points, periodic orbits, closures, invariants, and limiting structures.

This simple change of perspective has several consequences.

1. **Functions become degenerate recursive correspondences.**  
   If \(\mathcal C_n\) is single-valued for every \(n\), the theory reduces to ordinary discrete dynamics.

2. **Relations become dynamic structural pathways.**  
   If \(\mathcal C_n\) is a Boolean relation, iteration may generate transitive closures, reachability structures, or network evolutions.

3. **Algebraic correspondences become recursive operators on Chow groups and cohomology.**  
   Recursive correspondences give a natural language for iterated kernels, correspondences generated by intersection operations, and dynamical degrees.

4. **Optimization and economics become theories of recursively generated feasible, optimal, or equilibrium correspondences.**  
   Best-response maps, demand correspondences, policy correspondences, and equilibrium relations are naturally recursive rather than static.

The purpose of this paper is to provide a systematic foundation for this point of view.

## 2. Correspondences and Their Tensor Calculus

### 2.1 Correspondences as spans

Let \(\mathcal E\) be a category with pullbacks. A correspondence from \(A\) to \(B\) in \(\mathcal E\) is a span

\[
A \xleftarrow{p} R \xrightarrow{q} B.
\]

We denote this correspondence by

\[
\mathcal C:A\dashrightarrow B.
\]

When \(\mathcal E=\mathbf{Set}\), a span is equivalent to a relation \(R\subseteq A\times B\) together with the two coordinate projections. A function \(f:A\to B\) is the special case in which the left leg is the identity and the right leg is the graph of \(f\):

\[
A \xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}} A \xrightarrow{f} B.
\]

Given correspondences

\[
A \xleftarrow{p} R \xrightarrow{q} B,
\qquad
B \xleftarrow{r} S \xrightarrow{s} C,
\]

their composite is defined by pullback:

\[
R\times_B S
=
\{(r,s)\in R\times S \mid q(r)=r(s)\},
\]

with projections to \(A\) and \(C\). Thus

\[
\mathcal D\circ \mathcal C:
A \xleftarrow{p\circ \pi_1} R\times_B S
\xrightarrow{s\circ \pi_2} C.
\]

This defines the bicategory of correspondences, often denoted \(\mathbf{Corr}(\mathcal E)\).

### 2.2 Weighted correspondences

For many applications it is useful to linearize or weight correspondences. Let \(K\) be a commutative semiring. For finite sets \(A,B\), a \(K\)-correspondence from \(A\) to \(B\) is a matrix

\[
\mathcal C=(\mathcal C^b{}_a)_{a\in A,\,b\in B},
\qquad
\mathcal C^b{}_a\in K.
\]

We use the convention that lower indices refer to the source and upper indices to the target. Thus \(\mathcal C^b{}_a\) is the weight of the correspondence from \(a\) to \(b\).

Important examples are:

| Semiring \(K\) | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| \(\mathbb B=\{0,1\}\), with \(\lor,\land\) | Boolean relations |
| \(\mathbb R_{\ge 0}\), with \(+,\cdot\) | weighted relations, Markov kernels |
| \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\), with \(\min,+\) | cost correspondences |
| \(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C\) | linear correspondences, cohomological kernels |

Given \(\mathcal C:A\dashrightarrow B\) and \(\mathcal D:B\dashrightarrow C\), their composite is

\[
(\mathcal D\circ \mathcal C)^c{}_a
=
\sum_{b\in B}
\mathcal D^c{}_b\,\mathcal C^b{}_a,
\]

where the summation and multiplication are those of the semiring \(K\). In Einstein notation,

\[
(\mathcal D\circ \mathcal C)^c{}_a
=
\mathcal D^c{}_b \mathcal C^b{}_a.
\]

The identity correspondence on \(A\) has components

\[
\delta^b{}_a=
\begin{cases}
1_K, & a=b,\\
0_K, & a\neq b.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus weighted correspondences form a category enriched over \(K\)-modules or semimodules, depending on the semiring.

### 2.3 Set-valued maps

In analysis, optimization, and economics one often works with correspondences as set-valued maps

\[
F:X\rightrightarrows Y,
\]

where for each \(x\in X\),

\[
F(x)\subseteq Y.
\]

Such a map is equivalent to the relation

\[
R_F=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y \mid y\in F(x)\}.
\]

When \(X,Y\) are topological spaces, important regularity conditions include:

- upper hemicontinuity,
- lower hemicontinuity,
- compact values,
- convex values,
- closed graph.

These regularity conditions are central for fixed-point theorems and equilibrium theory.

## 3. Recursive Correspondence Systems

### 3.1 Basic definition

A **recursive correspondence system** consists of:

1. source and target objects \(A,B\);
2. an initial correspondence \(\mathcal C_0:A\dashrightarrow B\);
3. an operator
   \[
   \Psi:\mathrm{Corr}(A,B)\to \mathrm{Corr}(A,B);
   \]
4. the recursively defined sequence
   \[
   \mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n).
   \]

If \(A=B\), we speak of an endocorrespondence system. If the type of the correspondence changes with \(n\), one instead has a graded or typed recursive system

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}:\! A_{n+1}\dashrightarrow B_{n+1},
\qquad
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi_n(\mathcal C_n).
\]

For simplicity, the untyped or fixed-type case will be the primary object of study.

### 3.2 Orbits, fixed points, and closures

Given a recursive correspondence system \((\mathcal C_0,\Psi)\), define the orbit

\[
\mathcal O(\mathcal C_0)
=
\{\Psi^n(\mathcal C_0)\mid n\ge 0\}.
\]

A correspondence \(\mathcal C_\ast\) is a **fixed correspondence** if

\[
\Psi(\mathcal C_\ast)=\mathcal C_\ast.
\]

It is a **periodic correspondence of period \(p\)** if

\[
\Psi^p(\mathcal C_\ast)=\mathcal C_\ast,
\]

and \(p\) is minimal with this property.

If the space of correspondences admits joins, one defines the **recursive closure** of \(\mathcal C_0\) by

\[
\overline{\mathcal C}_0
=
\bigvee_{n\ge 0}\Psi^n(\mathcal C_0).
\]

In Boolean settings, this is often the reachable or generated correspondence.

### 3.3 Tensorial recursive operators

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a weighted correspondence with components \(\mathcal C^b{}_a\). A very general recursive operator may be written in tensorial form as

\[
\Psi(\mathcal C)^b{}_a
=
\Lambda^b{}_a
+
L^{b d}{}_{a c}\,\mathcal C^c{}_d
+
Q^{b d f}{}_{a c e}\,
\mathcal C^c{}_d \mathcal C^e{}_f
+
\cdots.
\]

Here:

- \(\Lambda\) is an exogenous seed correspondence;
- \(L\) is a linear operator on correspondences;
- \(Q\) is a bilinear interaction tensor;
- higher-order tensors encode nonlinear structural transformations.

Using Einstein summation,

\[
\Psi(\mathcal C)^b{}_a
=
\Lambda^b{}_a
+
L^{b d}{}_{a c}\mathcal C^c{}_d
+
Q^{b d f}{}_{a c e}
\mathcal C^c{}_d \mathcal C^e{}_f
+\cdots.
\]

This representation shows that RCT is naturally a tensor theory of structural evolution.

### 3.4 Linear recursive correspondences

The linear case is particularly important. Suppose

\[
\Psi(\mathcal C)=\Lambda+L(\mathcal C),
\]

with

\[
L(\mathcal C)^b{}_a
=
L^{b d}{}_{a c}\mathcal C^c{}_d.
\]

Then the recursion is

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Lambda+L(\mathcal C_n).
\]

Iterating gives

\[
\mathcal C_n
=
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^k(\Lambda)
+
L^n(\mathcal C_0).
\]

If \(L^n(\mathcal C_0)\to 0\), then

\[
\mathcal C_\infty
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}L^k(\Lambda).
\]

This is the correspondence-theoretic analogue of the Neumann series.

In semiring notation, if \(L\) admits a Kleene star, one writes

\[
\mathcal C_\infty=L^\ast \Lambda,
\qquad
L^\ast=\sum_{k\ge 0}L^k.
\]

### 3.5 Recursive rank, degree, and entropy

Recursive correspondence systems admit several numerical invariants.

#### Recursive rank

For weighted correspondences over a field, define

\[
r_n=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C_n).
\]

For linear operators,

\[
r_{n+1}\le \min\{\operatorname{rank}(L),r_n\}
\]

under suitable composition conventions. Over semirings, one may use semiring rank, tropical rank, or Boolean rank.

#### Recursive degree

In algebraic settings, one may define the degree

\[
d_n=\deg(\mathcal C_n).
\]

The asymptotic degree growth rate is

\[
\lambda
=
\lim_{n\to\infty} d_n^{1/n},
\]

when the limit exists. This is closely related to dynamical degrees in algebraic dynamics.

#### Recursive entropy

For stochastic correspondences, define

\[
H(\mathcal C_n)
=
-\sum_{a,b}\pi(a)\mathcal C_n^b{}_a
\log \mathcal C_n^b{}_a.
\]

The entropy rate is

\[
h
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H(\mathcal C_n),
\]

if the limit exists.

These invariants provide quantitative measures of the complexity generated by recursive correspondence dynamics.

## 4. Fixed Points and Convergence Theorems

This section establishes the main general existence and convergence results for recursive correspondences.

### 4.1 Order-theoretic fixed points

Let \(\mathrm{Corr}(A,B)\) be a complete lattice under pointwise order. For Boolean correspondences, this order is inclusion of relations. For weighted correspondences over suitable semirings, it is the pointwise order.

**Theorem 4.1 (Kleene–Tarski for recursive correspondences).**  
Let \(\Psi:\mathrm{Corr}(A,B)\to \mathrm{Corr}(A,B)\) be monotone. Then \(\Psi\) has a least fixed point and a greatest fixed point. If \(\Psi\) preserves directed suprema, then the least fixed point is

\[
\mu\Psi
=
\bigvee_{n\ge 0}\Psi^n(\bot),
\]

where \(\bot\) is the least correspondence.

*Proof.* Define

\[
c_0=\bot,\qquad c_{n+1}=\Psi(c_n).
\]

Monotonicity of \(\Psi\) gives

\[
c_0\le c_1\le c_2\le \cdots.
\]

Let

\[
c_\infty=\bigvee_{n\ge 0}c_n.
\]

If \(\Psi\) preserves directed suprema, then

\[
\Psi(c_\infty)
=
\Psi\left(\bigvee_n c_n\right)
=
\bigvee_n \Psi(c_n)
=
\bigvee_n c_{n+1}
=
c_\infty.
\]

Thus \(c_\infty\) is a fixed point. If \(x\) is any fixed point, then \(\bot\le x\). By induction, \(c_n\le x\) for all \(n\), hence \(c_\infty\le x\). Therefore \(c_\infty\) is the least fixed point. The greatest fixed point is obtained dually by iterating from \(\top\). \(\square\)

This theorem is fundamental for Boolean recursive correspondences, transitive closures, reachability relations, and inductive definitions.

### 4.2 Metric convergence

Let \(X,Y\) be compact metric spaces. Let \(\mathcal H(X\times Y)\) be the space of nonempty closed subsets of \(X\times Y\), equipped with the Hausdorff metric \(d_H\). A closed correspondence \(F:X\rightrightarrows Y\) may be identified with its graph,

\[
\Gamma_F\subseteq X\times Y.
\]

**Theorem 4.2 (Contraction principle for recursive correspondences).**  
Let \(\Psi:\mathcal H(X\times Y)\to \mathcal H(X\times Y)\) be a contraction with constant \(0\le \lambda<1\). Then there exists a unique closed correspondence \(\mathcal C_\ast\) such that

\[
\Psi(\mathcal C_\ast)=\mathcal C_\ast.
\]

Moreover, for every initial closed correspondence \(\mathcal C_0\),

\[
\mathcal C_n\to \mathcal C_\ast
\]

in the Hausdorff metric, and

\[
d_H(\mathcal C_n,\mathcal C_\ast)
\le
\frac{\lambda^n}{1-\lambda}
d_H(\mathcal C_1,\mathcal C_0).
\]

*Proof.* The space \(\mathcal H(X\times Y)\) is complete when \(X\times Y\) is compact. The map \(\Psi\) is a contraction on this complete metric space. Banach’s fixed-point theorem gives a unique fixed point and the stated rate of convergence. \(\square\)

This result applies to recursive set-valued maps in optimization, control, and economics whenever the correspondence update is contractive.

### 4.3 Linear spectral convergence

Let \(\mathcal V\) be a Banach space of weighted correspondences. Suppose

\[
\Psi(\mathcal C)=\Lambda+L(\mathcal C),
\]

where \(L:\mathcal V\to\mathcal V\) is a bounded linear operator with

\[
\|L\|<1.
\]

**Theorem 4.3 (Neumann convergence).**  
The recursion

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Lambda+L(\mathcal C_n)
\]

has a unique fixed point

\[
\mathcal C_\ast
=
(I-L)^{-1}\Lambda
=
\sum_{k=0}^\infty L^k(\Lambda).
\]

Moreover,

\[
\|\mathcal C_n-\mathcal C_\ast\|
\le
\frac{\|L\|^n}{1-\|L\|}
\|\mathcal C_1-\mathcal C_0\|.
\]

*Proof.* The fixed-point equation is

\[
\mathcal C=\Lambda+L(\mathcal C),
\]

so

\[
(I-L)\mathcal C=\Lambda.
\]

Since \(\|L\|<1\), the Neumann series

\[
(I-L)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty L^k
\]

converges. Iteration gives

\[
\mathcal C_n
=
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L^k(\Lambda)+L^n(\mathcal C_0),
\]

and the error estimate follows from the geometric bound. \(\square\)

A local nonlinear analogue follows by linearizing \(\Psi\) near a fixed point.

**Corollary 4.4 (Local stability).**  
Let \(\mathcal C_\ast\) be a fixed point of a Fréchet-differentiable operator \(\Psi\). If the spectral radius of the derivative

\[
D\Psi_{\mathcal C_\ast}
\]

is strictly less than one, then \(\mathcal C_\ast\) is locally asymptotically stable under the recursion \(\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n)\).

## 5. Categorical Semantics

Recursive Correspondence Theory admits several categorical formulations. The two most useful are:

1. correspondences as spans in a category \(\mathcal E\);
2. correspondences as profunctors between categories.

### 5.1 The bicategory of spans

Let \(\mathcal E\) have pullbacks. The bicategory \(\mathbf{Span}(\mathcal E)\) has:

- objects: objects of \(\mathcal E\);
- 1-morphisms: spans
  \[
  A\xleftarrow{p} R\xrightarrow{q} B;
  \]
- 2-morphisms: maps of spans;
- composition: pullback.

A recursive correspondence system in this setting is an endomorphism of a hom-category:

\[
\Psi:\mathbf{Span}(\mathcal E)(A,B)
\to
\mathbf{Span}(\mathcal E)(A,B),
\]

together with an initial span \(\mathcal C_0\).

If \(A=B\), one obtains a discrete dynamical system internal to the bicategory of spans.

### 5.2 Profunctors

Let \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\) be small categories. A profunctor

\[
\mathcal C:\mathcal A \nrightarrow \mathcal B
\]

is a functor

\[
\mathcal C:\mathcal B^{\mathrm{op}}\times \mathcal A\to \mathbf{Set}.
\]

In index-like notation one writes

\[
\mathcal C^b{}_a
=
\mathcal C(a,b).
\]

Given profunctors

\[
\mathcal C:\mathcal A\nrightarrow \mathcal B,
\qquad
\mathcal D:\mathcal B\nrightarrow \mathcal C,
\]

their composite is the coend

\[
(\mathcal D\circ \mathcal C)^c{}_a
=
\int^{b\in \mathcal B}
\mathcal C^b{}_a\times \mathcal D^c{}_b.
\]

This is the categorical analogue of tensor contraction.

A recursive profunctor system is a sequence

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n),
\]

where

\[
\Psi:\mathbf{Prof}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
\to
\mathbf{Prof}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B).
\]

### 5.3 Recursive closure as a categorical fixed point

Suppose \(\Psi\) is monotone and preserves directed colimits in an appropriate profunctor category. Define

\[
R(\mathcal C)
=
\bigvee_{n\ge 0}\Psi^n(\mathcal C).
\]

Then \(R\) is a closure operator:

1. \(\mathcal C\le R(\mathcal C)\);
2. \(\mathcal C\le \mathcal D \Rightarrow R(\mathcal C)\le R(\mathcal D)\);
3. \(R(R(\mathcal C))=R(\mathcal C)\).

Moreover,

\[
R(\Psi(\mathcal C))=R(\mathcal C).
\]

Thus recursive closure produces the least \(\Psi\)-invariant correspondence above \(\mathcal C\).

In a more algebraic formulation, one may define a free recursive correspondence monad by

\[
T(\mathcal C)
=
\mu X.\,(\mathcal C+\Psi(X)),
\]

where \(\mu X\) denotes the initial algebra. This captures the idea that recursive correspondences are freely generated by applying \(\Psi\) indefinitely to an initial structural seed.

## 6. Recursive Correspondences in Algebraic Geometry

Algebraic geometry provides one of the most natural homes for correspondence theory. In this section we develop recursive correspondences for algebraic cycles.

### 6.1 Algebraic correspondences

Let \(X,Y\) be smooth projective varieties over an algebraically closed field. An algebraic correspondence from \(X\) to \(Y\) is a cycle

\[
\Gamma\in \mathrm{CH}^{\dim X}(X\times Y).
\]

Equivalently, \(\Gamma\) is a formal linear combination of subvarieties of \(X\times Y\) of dimension \(\dim Y\).

Given correspondences

\[
\Gamma\in \mathrm{CH}^{\dim X}(X\times Y),
\qquad
\Delta\in \mathrm{CH}^{\dim Y}(Y\times Z),
\]

their composition is

\[
\Delta\circ\Gamma
=
p_{XZ\ast}
\left(
p_{XY}^\ast\Gamma\cdot p_{YZ}^\ast\Delta
\right),
\]

where

\[
p_{XY}:X\times Y\times Z\to X\times Y,
\]

\[
p_{YZ}:X\times Y\times Z\to Y\times Z,
\]

\[
p_{XZ}:X\times Y\times Z\to X\times Z,
\]

and \(\cdot\) denotes the intersection product.

This gives a category of algebraic correspondences, closely related to the category of pure motives.

### 6.2 Cohomological tensor representation

Fix a Weil cohomology theory \(H^\ast\). A correspondence \(\Gamma:X\dashrightarrow X\) determines a cohomology class

\[
[\Gamma]\in H^\ast(X\times X).
\]

By the Künneth decomposition, one may write

\[
[\Gamma]
=
\Gamma^i{}_j\, e_i\otimes e^j,
\]

where \(\{e_i\}\) is a basis of \(H^\ast(X)\) and \(\{e^j\}\) is the dual basis. The action of \(\Gamma\) on cohomology is

\[
\Gamma_\ast(\alpha)
=
p_{2\ast}\left([\Gamma]\cup p_1^\ast\alpha\right).
\]

In components,

\[
(\Gamma_\ast \alpha)^i
=
\Gamma^i{}_j \alpha^j.
\]

Composition of correspondences becomes matrix multiplication:

\[
(\Delta\circ\Gamma)^i{}_k
=
\Delta^i{}_j \Gamma^j{}_k.
\]

Thus algebraic correspondences act as tensorial kernels on cohomology.

### 6.3 Linear recursive algebraic correspondences

Let \(T:X\dashrightarrow X\) be a fixed algebraic correspondence. Define a recursive correspondence system by

\[
\Gamma_{n+1}=T\circ\Gamma_n.
\]

Then

\[
\Gamma_n=T^{\circ n}\circ \Gamma_0.
\]

On cohomology,

\[
[\Gamma_n]_\ast
=
[T]_\ast^n\,[\Gamma_0]_\ast.
\]

In components,

\[
(\Gamma_n)^i{}_k
=
(T^n)^i{}_j(\Gamma_0)^j{}_k.
\]

If \(T_\ast\) has eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\), then the asymptotic growth of cohomological action is governed by the spectral radius

\[
\rho(T_\ast)=\max_i |\lambda_i|.
\]

When \(T_\ast\) is not diagonalizable, Jordan blocks produce polynomial corrections:

\[
\|T_\ast^n\|
\sim
C n^r \rho(T_\ast)^n.
\]

Thus recursive algebraic correspondences give a natural framework for dynamical degrees and cohomological growth.

### 6.4 Nonlinear recursive algebraic correspondences

A nonlinear recursive correspondence may be defined by

\[
\Gamma_{n+1}
=
F_\ast\left(
G^\ast\Gamma_n\cdot \Gamma_n
\right),
\]

where \(F,G\) are morphisms or projections and the product is the intersection product.

A particularly important special case is recursive self-composition:

\[
\Gamma_{n+1}=\Gamma_n\circ\Gamma_n.
\]

Suppose \(\Gamma_0=\Gamma_f\) is the graph of a finite morphism

\[
f:X\to X
\]

of topological degree \(d\). Then

\[
\Gamma_1=\Gamma_f\circ\Gamma_f=\Gamma_{f^2},
\]

\[
\Gamma_2=\Gamma_{f^2}\circ\Gamma_{f^2}=\Gamma_{f^4},
\]

and inductively,

\[
\Gamma_n=\Gamma_{f^{2^n}}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\deg(\Gamma_n)=d^{2^n}.
\]

This exhibits doubly exponential degree growth under quadratic recursive self-composition.

By contrast, linear recursion

\[
\Gamma_{n+1}=\Gamma_f\circ\Gamma_n
\]

gives

\[
\Gamma_n=\Gamma_{f^n},
\qquad
\deg(\Gamma_n)=d^n.
\]

Thus the recursion law itself determines the asymptotic geometry.

### 6.5 Example: projective line

Let \(X=\mathbb P^1\), and let

\[
f:\mathbb P^1\to \mathbb P^1,
\qquad
z\mapsto z^m,
\]

with \(m\ge 2\). The graph \(\Gamma_f\subset \mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1\) is a correspondence of degree \(m\).

For the linear recursion

\[
\Gamma_{n+1}=\Gamma_f\circ\Gamma_n,
\qquad
\Gamma_0=\Gamma_f,
\]

we obtain

\[
\Gamma_n=\Gamma_{f^n},
\]

and

\[
\deg(\Gamma_n)=m^n.
\]

For the quadratic recursion

\[
\Gamma_{n+1}=\Gamma_n\circ\Gamma_n,
\]

we obtain

\[
\Gamma_n=\Gamma_{f^{2^n}},
\]

and

\[
\deg(\Gamma_n)=m^{2^n}.
\]

This elementary example illustrates the central principle of RCT: the recursive operator encodes the growth law of the correspondence.

## 7. Recursive Correspondences in Optimization

Recursive correspondences arise naturally in optimization whenever feasible sets, optimal solution sets, or policy sets evolve with information, iteration, or time.

### 7.1 Tropical correspondences

Let \(K=\mathbb T=(\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\},\min,+)\). A tropical correspondence from \(A\) to \(B\) is a cost matrix

\[
\mathcal C^b{}_a\in \mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}.
\]

Composition is min-plus matrix multiplication:

\[
(\mathcal D\circ\mathcal C)^c{}_a
=
\min_{b\in B}
\left(
\mathcal C^b{}_a+\mathcal D^c{}_b
\right).
\]

The identity correspondence is

\[
\delta^b{}_a=
\begin{cases}
0, & a=b,\\
+\infty, & a\neq b.
\end{cases}
\]

This is the natural algebra of shortest-path problems and deterministic optimal control.

### 7.2 Recursive shortest paths

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a cost correspondence on a finite set of states \(S\). Define \(D_n\) recursively by

\[
D_0=\delta,
\]

\[
D_{n+1}
=
D_n\oplus(\mathcal C\circ D_n),
\]

where \(\oplus\) denotes pointwise minimum. In components,

\[
(D_{n+1})^k{}_i
=
\min
\left\{
(D_n)^k{}_i,
\min_{j\in S}
\left(
\mathcal C^j{}_i+(D_n)^k{}_j
\right)
\right\}.
\]

Then \((D_n)^k{}_i\) is the minimum cost of a path from \(i\) to \(k\) using at most \(n\) edges.

The limiting correspondence

\[
D_\ast
=
\bigoplus_{n\ge 0}\mathcal C^{\circ n}
\]

satisfies the Bellman equation

\[
D_\ast
=
\delta\oplus(\mathcal C\circ D_\ast).
\]

This is the fixed-point equation of shortest-path theory.

### 7.3 Policy correspondences

The optimal successor correspondence is

\[
\Pi^j{}_i=
\begin{cases}
1, & j\in \arg\min_{k}
\left(
\mathcal C^k{}_i+(D_\ast)^k{}_? 
\right),\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

More invariantly, for a value function \(V\), define

\[
\Pi(i)
=
\arg\min_{j\in S}
\left\{
\mathcal C^j{}_i+V(j)
\right\}.
\]

When the minimizer is not unique, \(\Pi\) is genuinely multi-valued. Recursive policy iteration can then be written as

\[
\Pi_{n+1}
=
\operatorname{ArgMin}
\left(
\mathcal C+V_n
\right),
\]

with \(V_{n+1}\) updated from \(\Pi_n\). This yields a coupled recursive correspondence system.

### 7.4 Dynamic programming

Let \(S\) be a finite state space and \(A\) a finite action set. Let

\[
R^s{}_a
\]

be the reward for taking action \(a\) in state \(s\), and let

\[
T^{s'}{}_{s a}
\]

be the transition tensor. The value iteration recursion is

\[
V_{n+1}^s
=
\max_{a\in A}
\left(
R^s{}_a
+
\beta T^{s'}{}_{s a}V_n^{s'}
\right),
\]

with \(0<\beta<1\).

The associated policy correspondence is

\[
\Pi_{n+1}^a{}_s
=
\begin{cases}
1, & a\in \arg\max_{a'}
\left(
R^s{}_{a'}
+
\beta T^{s'}{}_{s a'}V_n^{s'}
\right),\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

At a fixed point,

\[
V^s
=
\max_a
\left(
R^s{}_a+\beta T^{s'}{}_{s a}V^{s'}
\right),
\]

and the policy correspondence satisfies the Bellman optimality condition. Thus dynamic programming is naturally a recursive correspondence theory in which policies and value functions co-evolve.

### 7.5 Variational and KKT correspondences

In convex optimization, one often associates to a parameter \(p\) the solution correspondence

\[
\mathcal S(p)
=
\arg\min_x f(x,p).
\]

If constraints or regularizers are updated recursively, one obtains

\[
\mathcal S_{n+1}(p)
=
\arg\min_x
\left\{
f(x,p)+\phi_n(x)
\right\}.
\]

Similarly, the KKT correspondence

\[
\mathcal K(p)
=
\{(x,\lambda)\mid \text{KKT conditions hold at parameter }p\}
\]

may be recursively regularized or perturbed:

\[
\mathcal K_{n+1}
=
\Psi(\mathcal K_n).
\]

Fixed points of such recursive KKT correspondences correspond to stable optimality structures.

## 8. Recursive Correspondences in Economics

Economics is inherently correspondence-theoretic. Demand, supply, budget sets, best responses, and equilibrium allocations are generally multi-valued.

### 8.1 Best-response correspondences

Let \(I=\{1,\dots,N\}\) be a set of players. Player \(i\) has strategy set \(S_i\) and payoff function

\[
u_i:S_i\times S_{-i}\to \mathbb R.
\]

The best-response correspondence of player \(i\) is

\[
B_i(s_{-i})
=
\arg\max_{s_i\in S_i} u_i(s_i,s_{-i}).
\]

The joint best-response correspondence is

\[
B:S\rightrightarrows S,
\qquad
B(s)=\prod_{i=1}^N B_i(s_{-i}).
\]

A Nash equilibrium is a fixed point

\[
s^\ast\in B(s^\ast).
\]

If each \(S_i\) is nonempty, compact, and convex, and each \(u_i\) is continuous and quasi-concave in \(s_i\), Kakutani’s fixed-point theorem guarantees the existence of a Nash equilibrium.

In RCT, one does not merely study the static fixed point but a recursive evolution of strategic correspondences:

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n),
\]

where \(\mathcal C_n\) may encode admissible responses, beliefs, learning rules, or institutional constraints.

### 8.2 Recursive equilibrium correspondences

A recursive equilibrium correspondence is a correspondence \(\mathcal C_\ast\) satisfying

\[
\mathcal C_\ast=\Psi(\mathcal C_\ast),
\]

where \(\Psi\) incorporates optimization, feasibility, expectations, and adjustment rules.

For example, let \(S\) be a state space of economic variables. Define

\[
\Psi(\mathcal C)(s)
=
\left\{
s'\in S
\mid
\exists a\in A(s)
\text{ such that }
s'\in F(s,a)
\text{ and }
a \text{ is optimal given } \mathcal C
\right\}.
\]

Here:

- \(A(s)\) is the feasible action correspondence;
- \(F(s,a)\) is the transition correspondence;
- optimality is evaluated with respect to preferences and expectations encoded by \(\mathcal C\).

A recursive equilibrium is a fixed correspondence

\[
\mathcal C_\ast=\Psi(\mathcal C_\ast).
\]

This generalizes recursive equilibrium in dynamic economics by allowing equilibrium relations to be intrinsically multi-valued and recursively generated.

### 8.3 Market correspondences and price adjustment

Let there be \(G\) goods. The price space is the simplex

\[
\Delta^{G-1}
=
\left\{
p\in \mathbb R_+^G
\mid
\sum_{g=1}^G p^g=1
\right\}.
\]

For each agent \(i\), let the demand correspondence be

\[
D_i:\Delta^{G-1}\rightrightarrows \mathbb R_+^G.
\]

Aggregate demand is the Minkowski sum

\[
D(p)=\sum_{i=1}^N D_i(p).
\]

Given endowments \(\omega_i\), the excess demand correspondence is

\[
Z(p)=D(p)-\sum_{i=1}^N \omega_i.
\]

In components,

\[
z^g(p)
=
\sum_{i=1}^N D_i^g(p)
-
\sum_{i=1}^N \omega_i^g.
\]

A standard price-adjustment recursion is

\[
p_{n+1}
=
\Pi_\Delta
\left(
p_n+\eta z(p_n)
\right),
\]

where \(\Pi_\Delta\) denotes projection onto the price simplex and \(\eta>0\) is an adjustment parameter.

When demand is multi-valued, this becomes a recursive correspondence

\[
\mathcal P_{n+1}
=
\Psi(\mathcal P_n),
\]

where \(\mathcal P_n\) records the relation between prices, allocations, and excess demands at stage \(n\). A fixed point of this recursive market correspondence corresponds to a competitive equilibrium.

### 8.4 Stability of economic correspondences

Let \(S\) be a compact convex state space and let

\[
\Phi:S\rightrightarrows S
\]

be an upper hemicontinuous correspondence with nonempty compact convex values. By Kakutani’s theorem, there exists \(s^\ast\in S\) such that

\[
s^\ast\in \Phi(s^\ast).
\]

If, in addition, \(\Phi\) is a contraction in the Hausdorff metric,

\[
d_H(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))
\le
\lambda d(x,y),
\qquad
0\le \lambda<1,
\]

then the fixed point is unique, and recursive correspondences generated by \(\Phi\) converge to it.

Thus RCT distinguishes two regimes:

1. **Existence regime:** convexity and continuity yield fixed correspondences.
2. **Stability regime:** contraction or monotonicity yields convergence of recursive correspondences.

This distinction is essential in economic dynamics, where existence of equilibrium is generally easier to establish than convergence of learning or adjustment processes.

## 9. Computational Aspects

Recursive correspondence systems are often computable when the underlying correspondence space is finite or discretized.

### 9.1 Basic iteration algorithm

Given \(\mathcal C_0\) and \(\Psi\), the elementary algorithm is:

1. Set \(n=0\).
2. Compute
   \[
   \mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n).
   \]
3. Test a stopping condition:
   \[
   d(\mathcal C_{n+1},\mathcal C_n)<\varepsilon,
   \]
   or
   \[
   \mathcal C_{n+1}=\mathcal C_n.
   \]
4. If not converged, increment \(n\) and repeat.

For finite Boolean correspondences, equality testing is exact. For weighted correspondences, one uses a norm or metric.

### 9.2 Tensor contraction complexity

If \(\mathcal C\) is an \(m\times m\) matrix correspondence and composition is semiring matrix multiplication, then one iteration of

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\mathcal C_n\circ \mathcal C_n
\]

costs

\[
O(m^3)
\]

with naive multiplication, or faster using optimized tensor contraction schemes.

For sparse correspondences, the cost is proportional to the number of nonzero structural pathways generated during contraction.

### 9.3 Acceleration methods

Several acceleration techniques are available:

- **Kleene acceleration:** use joins to accumulate generated structure.
- **Anderson acceleration:** accelerate fixed-point iteration in vector spaces.
- **Policy iteration:** in dynamic programming, replace value iteration by policy-correspondence iteration.
- **Newton-type methods:** for differentiable recursive operators.
- **Homotopy methods:** track fixed correspondences as parameters vary.

In Boolean settings, recursive squaring can compute transitive closures in logarithmically many composition steps relative to path length.

## 10. Examples

### 10.1 Transitive closure

Let \(R\subseteq A\times A\) be a relation. Define

\[
\mathcal C_0=R,
\]

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}
=
\mathcal C_n\cup(\mathcal C_n\circ\mathcal C_n).
\]

Then \(\mathcal C_n\) contains pairs connected by paths of length at most \(2^n\). The limit

\[
\mathcal C_\infty
=
\bigcup_{n\ge 0}\mathcal C_n
\]

is the transitive closure of \(R\).

In Boolean matrix notation,

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}
=
\mathcal C_n\lor(\mathcal C_n\odot \mathcal C_n),
\]

where \(\lor\) is Boolean addition and \(\odot\) is Boolean matrix multiplication.

### 10.2 Weighted reachability

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a nonnegative weighted relation. Define

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}
=
\mathcal C_0+\mathcal C_n\circ \mathcal C_0.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal C_n
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\mathcal C_0^{\circ k}.
\]

If the spectral radius of \(\mathcal C_0\) is less than one, the infinite sum converges:

\[
\mathcal C_\infty
=
\sum_{k\ge 1}\mathcal C_0^{\circ k}.
\]

This is the weighted reachability correspondence.

### 10.3 Graph dynamical systems

Let \(G\) be a directed graph with adjacency matrix \(A\). Define a recursive graph correspondence by

\[
A_{n+1}
=
\sigma(\alpha A_n+\beta A_n\circ A_n),
\]

where \(\sigma\) is a thresholding or activation function. This models network evolution in which new edges are created by two-step structural interaction.

### 10.4 Cournot best-response recursion

Let \(q_i\) be the output of firm \(i\). Let profit be

\[
\pi_i(q_i,q_{-i})
=
p(Q)q_i-c_i(q_i),
\qquad
Q=\sum_j q_j.
\]

The best-response correspondence is

\[
B_i(q_{-i})
=
\arg\max_{q_i\ge 0}\pi_i(q_i,q_{-i}).
\]

Joint recursion:

\[
q_{n+1}\in B(q_n).
\]

In RCT one may instead evolve the whole correspondence of admissible responses:

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}
=
\Psi(\mathcal C_n),
\]

where \(\Psi\) incorporates updated beliefs about competitors’ strategies. Fixed points correspond to equilibrium conventions or stable strategic structures.

## 11. Conceptual Interpretation

Recursive Correspondence Theory proposes a shift in the ontology of mappings.

A function

\[
f:A\to B
\]

is a static assignment.

A relation

\[
R\subseteq A\times B
\]

is a static multi-assignment.

A recursive correspondence

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n)
\]

is a structural process whose state at stage \(n\) encodes the accumulated transformations, constraints, or pathways generated by the system.

Thus the object of study is not merely the correspondence itself, but the operator \(\Psi\) and the structure of its orbits.

In this sense, RCT is a theory of **structural time**: the iteration index \(n\) measures the depth of generated structure, while \(\Psi\) encodes the law by which structure reproduces, refines, or transforms itself.

## 12. Open Problems

The theory opens several directions for further research.

### 12.1 Moduli of recursive correspondences

For algebraic varieties, one may ask whether the set of fixed correspondences

\[
\{\Gamma\mid \Psi(\Gamma)=\Gamma\}
\]

has the structure of an algebraic variety, stack, or motivic moduli space.

### 12.2 Higher categorical RCT

Profunctors and spans admit higher-categorical and \(\infty\)-categorical analogues. A natural problem is to develop recursive correspondences in:

- \((\infty,1)\)-categories;
- bicategories of spans;
- enriched profunctor categories;
- derived algebraic geometry.

### 12.3 Recursive motivic correspondences

Since algebraic correspondences are closely related to motives, recursive correspondences suggest a theory of **recursive motives**, in which motivic kernels evolve under structural operators.

### 12.4 Stability beyond contraction

Many economically and geometrically natural operators are not contractions. A major challenge is to develop general stability criteria for monotone, expansive, or intermittently contracting recursive correspondence systems.

### 12.5 Tensor decomposition and complexity

Recursive operators are often high-order tensors. Understanding their low-rank structure, sparsity, and spectral properties is essential for computation.

### 12.6 Learning and equilibrium selection

In economics, recursive correspondences naturally model learning, belief formation, and institutional evolution. A key question is how recursive equilibrium correspondences select among multiple equilibria.

---

## 13. Conclusion

Recursive Correspondence Theory provides a unified formalism for dynamically generated multi-valued structural mappings. The basic recursion

\[
\mathcal C_{n+1}=\Psi(\mathcal C_n)
\]

generalizes ordinary functions, relations, span composition, algebraic correspondence composition, optimization kernels, and economic best-response dynamics.

The theory has three central features.

1. **Structural recursion.**  
   The correspondence itself evolves under an operator \(\Psi\).

2. **Tensorial composition.**  
   Correspondences compose by contraction, making the theory naturally compatible with algebraic and computational formalisms.

3. **Fixed-point centrality.**  
   Equilibria, closures, optimal policies, and invariant geometric structures arise as fixed or limiting correspondences.

The formalism is intentionally broad. Its value lies in revealing that many apparently different phenomena—transitive closure, motive-like correspondences, dynamic programming, and equilibrium recursion—are manifestations of a single structural principle: correspondences evolve recursively, and their invariant structures encode the mathematics of complex systems.
