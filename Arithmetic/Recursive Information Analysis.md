# Recursive Information Analysis

**Preprint**

---

## Abstract

We develop **Recursive Information Analysis (RIA)**, a framework in which the basic objects of analysis are not limits taken as primitives, but recursively evolving information operators  
\[
\mathcal I_n .
\]
An analytic quantity is represented as an information state acted upon by a sequence of refinement, transport, or conditioning operators. Differentiation appears as the infinitesimal generator of recursive refinement; integration appears as the invariant, accumulative, or resolvent functional associated with the recursion. We formulate RIA on smooth manifolds and measure spaces using kernel operators, moment tensors, carré-du-champ forms, and dual transport of measures. We prove that classical derivatives, higher-order differential operators, and integrals arise as asymptotic or algebraic special cases of recursive information dynamics. We then develop applications to information theory, partial differential equations, signal analysis, and machine learning. The resulting framework unifies finite-difference calculus, Markov semigroups, entropy dissipation, multiscale signal decompositions, and recursive learning dynamics under a single operator-theoretic theory.

**Keywords:** recursive operators, information theory, differential calculus, integral calculus, Markov kernels, PDEs, multiscale analysis, machine learning.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical analysis is conventionally founded on limits. The derivative is a limit of difference quotients, the integral is a limit of sums, and continuity is expressed through limiting processes. While this foundation is powerful, it obscures a structural fact: many analytic operations are more naturally understood as consequences of **recursive transformations of information**.

In physical, statistical, and computational settings, one rarely observes or manipulates an object directly. Instead, one applies successive transformations:

\[
f_0 \longmapsto f_1 \longmapsto f_2 \longmapsto \cdots,
\]

where each step refines, coarsens, transports, filters, or conditions the information contained in the previous state. The central claim of this paper is that such recursive transformations can be taken as the primary analytic objects. Limits are not discarded, but they are demoted from primitive status: they arise as asymptotic regimes of recursive information dynamics.

We introduce a family of **recursive information operators**

\[
\mathcal I_n ,
\]

acting on appropriate information states. The operator \(\mathcal I_n\) maps information at level \(n\) to information at level \(n+1\). The recursion

\[
f_{n+1}=\mathcal I_n f_n
\]

generates an analytic process. Derivatives arise as normalized first-order deviations of \(\mathcal I_n\) from the identity. Integrals arise as invariant mass functionals, accumulation operators, or resolvents of the recursive difference.

The guiding principles of RIA are:

1. **Recursion before limits.**  
   Analytic structure is generated by iterated information transformations.

2. **Operators before points.**  
   Functions are studied through the operators that refine, transport, or condition them.

3. **Duality between forward and backward information flow.**  
   The adjoint operators \(\mathcal I_n^*\) govern the evolution of measures, probabilities, and densities.

4. **Derivatives as generators.**  
   Differential operators are infinitesimal generators of recursive information refinement.

5. **Integrals as invariants.**  
   Integration is the conserved total information functional, or the inverse of recursive difference.

This paper provides a formal foundation for this viewpoint. We define recursive information systems, prove emergence theorems for differentiation and integration, and show how the framework applies to entropy, PDEs, signal processing, and machine learning.

## 2. Recursive Information Systems

### 2.1 Basic setting

Let \(M\) be a smooth \(d\)-dimensional manifold, possibly equipped with a Riemannian metric \(g\), a volume form \(\omega\), and a measure \(\mu\). Let \(\mathscr F(M)\) denote a suitable space of functions, sections, or distributions on \(M\). In applications, \(M\) may be Euclidean space, a graph, a probability manifold, a spacetime domain, or a latent space.

A **recursive information system** is a sequence of operators

\[
\mathcal I_n : \mathscr F_n(M) \to \mathscr F_{n+1}(M),
\]

where \(\mathscr F_n(M)\) denotes the space of information states at level \(n\). For simplicity, we often take \(\mathscr F_n(M)=\mathscr F(M)\), but the formalism allows changing state spaces.

Given an initial information state \(f_0\), the recursive evolution is

\[
f_{n+1} = \mathcal I_n f_n.
\]

The composite operator from level \(m\) to level \(n\) is denoted

\[
\mathcal I_{m,n}
=
\mathcal I_{n-1}\circ \mathcal I_{n-2}\circ \cdots \circ \mathcal I_m,
\qquad m<n,
\]

and

\[
\mathcal I_{n,n}=\mathrm{Id}.
\]

Thus

\[
f_n = \mathcal I_{0,n} f_0.
\]

---

### 2.2 Kernel representation

A large class of information operators can be represented by transition kernels. Let \(K_n(x,dy)\) be a family of probability measures on \(M\), indexed by \(x\in M\). Define

\[
(\mathcal I_n f)(x)
=
\int_M f(y)\,K_n(x,dy).
\]

Equivalently, using the exponential map to express local increments, one may write

\[
(\mathcal I_n f)(x)
=
\int_{T_xM}
f(\exp_x \xi)\,
K_n(x,d\xi),
\]

where \(\xi\in T_xM\) represents a local information displacement.

The kernel \(K_n\) is interpreted as a **conditional information channel**: given the current state \(x\), it specifies how information is propagated, refined, or averaged at the next recursive level.

The adjoint operator acts on measures:

\[
(\mathcal I_n^*\nu)(A)
=
\int_M K_n(x,A)\,\nu(dx),
\]

for measurable sets \(A\subseteq M\). If \(p_n\) is a density with respect to \(\mu\), its recursive evolution is

\[
p_{n+1} = \mathcal I_n^* p_n.
\]

This duality between functions and measures is fundamental in RIA.

---

### 2.3 Moment tensors of recursive information

The local behavior of \(\mathcal I_n\) is encoded by its **recursive information moment tensors**. Define

\[
m_n^{i_1\cdots i_k}(x)
=
\int_{T_xM}
\xi^{i_1}\cdots \xi^{i_k}
\,K_n(x,d\xi).
\]

The first moment \(m_n^i\) encodes directed information drift. The second moment \(m_n^{ij}\) encodes information diffusion or covariance. Higher moments encode higher-order recursive structure.

We assume the existence of positive step sizes \(\varepsilon_n>0\) such that, locally uniformly in \(x\),

\[
m_n^i(x)
=
\varepsilon_n b_n^i(x)+o(\varepsilon_n),
\]

\[
m_n^{ij}(x)
=
\varepsilon_n a_n^{ij}(x)+o(\varepsilon_n),
\]

and, for \(k\geq 3\),

\[
m_n^{i_1\cdots i_k}(x)
=
O(\varepsilon_n^{1+\alpha})
\]

for some \(\alpha>0\), or under diffusive scaling,

\[
m_n^{i_1\cdots i_k}(x)
=
O(\varepsilon_n^{k/2}).
\]

The tensors \(b_n^i\) and \(a_n^{ij}\) are the **recursive drift** and **recursive information covariance**. They will generate the differential operators of the continuum limit.

---

### 2.4 Axioms for recursive information operators

We impose the following structural axioms.

#### Axiom 1: Positivity and normalization

For every \(n\),

\[
\mathcal I_n f \geq 0 \quad \text{if } f\geq 0,
\]

and

\[
\mathcal I_n 1 = 1.
\]

This means that \(\mathcal I_n\) preserves total certainty and maps probability kernels to conditional expectations.

#### Axiom 2: Locality of recursive refinement

The kernels \(K_n(x,\cdot)\) concentrate near \(x\) as \(n\to\infty\). In local coordinates,

\[
\int_{T_xM} |\xi|^2 K_n(x,d\xi) = O(\varepsilon_n),
\]

with \(\varepsilon_n\to 0\) in the continuum regime.

#### Axiom 3: Stability

There exists a constant \(C_T\) such that for all \(0\leq n\leq N_T\),

\[
\|\mathcal I_{0,n}\| \leq C_T
\]

on the relevant function space. This prevents recursive amplification of unbounded noise.

#### Axiom 4: Consistency

The normalized first-order recursive difference

\[
\mathscr D_n f
=
\frac{\mathcal I_n f - f}{\varepsilon_n}
\]

admits a limit on a dense domain \(\mathcal D\subset \mathscr F(M)\):

\[
\mathscr D_n f \to L f,
\]

where \(L\) is a differential operator.

These axioms formalize the idea that recursive information evolution is stable, local, and asymptotically differentiable.

## 3. Emergence of Derivatives

### 3.1 Recursive difference and recursive derivative

Define the **recursive information difference**

\[
\delta_n = \mathcal I_n - \mathrm{Id}.
\]

The finite-level recursive derivative is

\[
\mathscr D_n
=
\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n}
=
\frac{\mathcal I_n-\mathrm{Id}}{\varepsilon_n}.
\]

This operator is well-defined before any limit is taken. Classical derivatives arise when \(\mathscr D_n\) becomes asymptotically local.

---

### 3.2 Taylor expansion on a manifold

Let \(f\in C^3(M)\). Using the exponential map,

\[
f(\exp_x\xi)
=
f(x)
+
\xi^i \nabla_i f(x)
+
\frac12 \xi^i \xi^j \nabla_i\nabla_j f(x)
+
O(|\xi|^3).
\]

Here \(\nabla_i\) denotes covariant differentiation, and Einstein summation is used.

Integrating against \(K_n(x,d\xi)\), we obtain

\[
(\mathcal I_n f)(x)
=
f(x)
+
m_n^i(x)\nabla_i f(x)
+
\frac12 m_n^{ij}(x)\nabla_i\nabla_j f(x)
+
O\!\left(\int |\xi|^3 K_n(x,d\xi)\right).
\]

Using the moment assumptions,

\[
m_n^i(x)=\varepsilon_n b_n^i(x)+o(\varepsilon_n),
\]

\[
m_n^{ij}(x)=\varepsilon_n a_n^{ij}(x)+o(\varepsilon_n),
\]

and

\[
\int |\xi|^3 K_n(x,d\xi)=o(\varepsilon_n),
\]

we find

\[
(\mathcal I_n f)(x)
=
f(x)
+
\varepsilon_n
\left[
b_n^i(x)\nabla_i f(x)
+
\frac12 a_n^{ij}(x)\nabla_i\nabla_j f(x)
\right]
+
o(\varepsilon_n).
\]

Therefore,

\[
\mathscr D_n f(x)
=
b_n^i(x)\nabla_i f(x)
+
\frac12 a_n^{ij}(x)\nabla_i\nabla_j f(x)
+
o(1).
\]

If \(b_n\to b\) and \(a_n\to a\), then

\[
\mathscr D_n f \to Lf,
\]

where

\[
L
=
b^i\nabla_i
+
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i\nabla_j.
\]

Thus the derivative emerges as the infinitesimal generator of recursive information refinement.

---

### 3.3 Directional derivatives as special cases

Let \(v\) be a vector field on \(M\). Define the deterministic recursive shift

\[
(\mathcal S_{n,v} f)(x)
=
f(\exp_x(\varepsilon_n v(x))).
\]

Then

\[
\mathscr D_{n,v}f
=
\frac{\mathcal S_{n,v}f-f}{\varepsilon_n}.
\]

By Taylor expansion,

\[
\mathscr D_{n,v}f
=
v^i\nabla_i f
+
\frac{\varepsilon_n}{2}
v^i v^j \nabla_i\nabla_j f
+
O(\varepsilon_n^2).
\]

Hence,

\[
\lim_{\varepsilon_n\to 0}
\mathscr D_{n,v}f
=
\nabla_v f.
\]

The usual directional derivative is therefore a first-order recursive information increment.

---

### 3.4 Higher-order recursive derivatives

Higher derivatives arise from iterated recursive differences. Let

\[
\delta_{n,v}
=
\mathcal S_{n,v}-\mathrm{Id}.
\]

Define

\[
\mathscr D_{n,v}^{(k)} f
=
\frac{\delta_{n,v}^k f}{\varepsilon_n^k}.
\]

For deterministic shifts,

\[
\delta_{n,v}^k f(x)
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^k
(-1)^{k-\ell}
\binom{k}{\ell}
f(\exp_x(\ell\varepsilon_n v)).
\]

A Taylor expansion gives

\[
\mathscr D_{n,v}^{(k)} f
\to
\nabla_v^k f
\]

under suitable regularity and compatibility assumptions. On curved manifolds, curvature corrections appear in the higher-order terms.

Thus the full jet of a function can be recovered from recursive information differences.

---

### 3.5 Finite recursive Leibniz rule

Let \(\mathcal S_h\) be the shift operator along a vector field:

\[
(\mathcal S_h f)(x)=f(\exp_x(hv)).
\]

Define

\[
D_h f
=
\frac{\mathcal S_h f-f}{h}.
\]

Then

\[
D_h(fg)
=
f D_h g
+
g D_h f
+
h(D_h f)(D_h g).
\]

As \(h\to 0\), the correction term vanishes, yielding the classical Leibniz rule:

\[
\nabla_v(fg)
=
f\nabla_v g
+
g\nabla_v f.
\]

This demonstrates that the product rule is the infinitesimal shadow of an exact recursive identity.

---

### 3.6 Carré du champ and information metric

For a general Markov-type information operator, define the finite-level carré du champ:

\[
\Gamma_n(f,g)
=
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_n}
\left[
\mathcal I_n(fg)
-
(\mathcal I_n f)(\mathcal I_n g)
\right].
\]

If \(\mathscr D_n\to L\), then

\[
\Gamma_n(f,g)\to \Gamma(f,g),
\]

where

\[
\Gamma(f,g)
=
L(fg)-fLg-gLf.
\]

For the generator

\[
L
=
b^i\nabla_i
+
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i\nabla_j,
\]

one obtains

\[
\Gamma(f,g)
=
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i f\,\nabla_j g.
\]

If \(a^{ij}\) is positive semidefinite, \(\Gamma\) defines a local quadratic information form. In information geometry, this is closely related to the Fisher metric.

The associated chain rule is

\[
L\phi(f)
=
\phi'(f)Lf
+
\frac12 \phi''(f)\Gamma(f,f),
\]

for suitable smooth \(\phi\). This is the analytic expression of nonlinear information propagation.

## 4. Emergence of Integrals

### 4.1 Integration as invariant information mass

Let \(\mu_n\) be a measure at level \(n\). Define its recursive evolution by

\[
\mu_{n+1} = \mathcal I_n^*\mu_n.
\]

Then for every bounded measurable \(f\),

\[
\int_M f\,d\mu_{n+1}
=
\int_M (\mathcal I_n f)\,d\mu_n.
\]

In particular, since \(\mathcal I_n 1=1\),

\[
\mu_{n+1}(M)
=
\mu_n(M).
\]

Thus the total mass is a recursive invariant.

If there exists a measure \(\mu\) such that

\[
\mathcal I_n^*\mu=\mu,
\]

then

\[
\int_M \mathcal I_n f\,d\mu
=
\int_M f\,d\mu.
\]

The integral is the invariant zero-mode of the recursive information operator.

---

### 4.2 Recursive summation as inverse difference

Differentiation arises from the recursive difference

\[
\delta_n = \mathcal I_n-\mathrm{Id}.
\]

Integration arises as its inverse, subject to boundary conditions.

Define the recursive Green operator

\[
\mathcal G_n
=
\varepsilon_n(\mathrm{Id}-\mathcal I_n)^{-1},
\]

whenever the inverse exists. Given \(f\), set

\[
u = \mathcal G_n f.
\]

Then

\[
u-\mathcal I_n u
=
\varepsilon_n f.
\]

This is a discrete integral equation.

For example, let \(M=\mathbb R\) and define the translation operator

\[
(\mathcal S_h f)(x)=f(x+h).
\]

Then

\[
u(x)-u(x+h)=h f(x).
\]

Assuming \(u(x)\to 0\) as \(x\to+\infty\), we obtain

\[
u(x)
=
h\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} f(x+kh).
\]

As \(h\to 0\),

\[
u(x)
\to
\int_x^\infty f(s)\,ds.
\]

Similarly, with a backward shift and a boundary condition at \(a\),

\[
u(x)
=
h\sum_{k=0}^{(x-a)/h-1} f(a+kh)
\to
\int_a^x f(s)\,ds.
\]

Thus the Riemann integral emerges as the continuum limit of recursive accumulation.

---

### 4.3 Volume forms and tensorial integration

Let \(\omega\) be a volume form on \(M\). In local coordinates,

\[
\omega
=
\rho(x)\,dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^d.
\]

Integration of a scalar function is

\[
\int_M f\,\omega.
\]

If the recursive step is induced by a local map

\[
\Phi_n:M\to M,
\]

then the transported volume form satisfies

\[
\Phi_n^*\omega_{n+1}
=
J_n\,\omega_n,
\]

where

\[
J_n
=
\left|\det D\Phi_n\right|
\]

is the Jacobian determinant. In tensor notation,

\[
(\Phi_n^*\omega_{n+1})_{i_1\cdots i_d}
=
\frac{\partial y^{j_1}}{\partial x^{i_1}}
\cdots
\frac{\partial y^{j_d}}{\partial x^{i_d}}
(\omega_{n+1})_{j_1\cdots j_d}.
\]

The change-of-variables formula is therefore a recursive transformation of volume forms.

The adjoint operator \(\mathcal I_n^*\) is precisely the mechanism by which integration is transported across recursive levels.

---

### 4.4 Integration by parts from recursive duality

Suppose \(\mathcal I_n\) has generator \(L\) and invariant measure \(\mu\). Then

\[
\int_M Lf\,d\mu=0.
\]

If \(L\) is symmetric or reversible with respect to \(\mu\), then

\[
\int_M g\,Lf\,d\mu
=
-\int_M \Gamma(f,g)\,d\mu.
\]

This identity is the recursive origin of integration by parts. In the diffusion case,

\[
\Gamma(f,g)
=
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i f\nabla_j g,
\]

so that

\[
\int_M g\,Lf\,d\mu
=
-\frac12
\int_M a^{ij}\nabla_i f\nabla_j g\,d\mu.
\]

Thus the pairing between differentiation and integration is encoded in the duality of recursive information operators.

## 5. Recursive-to-Continuum Convergence

We now state a general convergence theorem showing how continuous analysis arises from recursive information evolution.

Let

\[
t_n
=
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_k
\]

be the recursive time. Define a piecewise-constant interpolation

\[
F(t)=f_n,
\qquad
t\in[t_n,t_{n+1}).
\]

Assume that

\[
\frac{\mathcal I_n f-f}{\varepsilon_n}
=
L_{t_n}f
+
r_n(f),
\]

with consistency error

\[
\|r_n(f)\| \leq \omega(\varepsilon_n)\|f\|_{\mathcal D},
\]

where \(\omega(\varepsilon)\to 0\) as \(\varepsilon\to 0\). Assume also stability:

\[
\|\mathcal I_{m,n}\|\leq C_T
\]

for \(0\leq m\leq n\leq N_T\).

### Theorem 5.1: Recursive convergence to a continuum evolution

Under the above assumptions, the interpolated recursive states \(F(t)\) converge, uniformly on compact time intervals, to the solution \(u(t)\) of the evolution equation

\[
\partial_t u(t)=L_t u(t),
\qquad
u(0)=f_0.
\]

Moreover, if \(\mu_n\) are recursively transported measures and \(\mu_n\to\mu_t\) weakly, then

\[
\int_M F(t)\,d\mu_{N(t)}
\to
\int_M u(t)\,d\mu_t.
\]

#### Proof sketch

From the recursion,

\[
f_{n+1}-f_n
=
\varepsilon_n L_{t_n}f_n
+
\varepsilon_n r_n(f_n).
\]

Summing from \(0\) to \(n-1\),

\[
f_n
=
f_0
+
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\varepsilon_k L_{t_k}f_k
+
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\varepsilon_k r_k(f_k).
\]

The first sum converges to

\[
\int_0^t L_s u(s)\,ds,
\]

while the error sum is bounded by

\[
C_T\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_k\omega(\varepsilon_k),
\]

which vanishes as the mesh size tends to zero. Stability and a Gronwall argument yield convergence.

The measure convergence follows from duality:

\[
\int f\,d\mu_{n+1}
=
\int \mathcal I_n f\,d\mu_n.
\]

Taking limits yields the weak formulation of the adjoint evolution.

∎

This theorem formalizes the central RIA principle: continuum differential and integral analysis are asymptotic invariants of recursive information operators.

## 6. Nonlinear Recursive Information Operators

Linear recursive operators already generate differential and integral calculus. However, many information processes are nonlinear. A fundamental construction is the logarithmic transform.

Given a positive linear information operator \(\mathcal I_n\), define

\[
\mathcal T_n^\varepsilon u
=
\varepsilon
\log
\left(
\mathcal I_n e^{u/\varepsilon}
\right).
\]

This is a nonlinear recursive information operator. It appears naturally in large deviations, risk-sensitive control, entropy regularization, and softmax-type mechanisms.

Suppose \(\mathcal I_n\) corresponds to a small-step diffusion with drift \(b^i\) and covariance \(a^{ij}\). Then, formally,

\[
\mathcal T_n^\varepsilon u
=
u
+
\varepsilon_n
\left[
b^i\nabla_i u
+
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i u\nabla_j u
\right]
+
o(\varepsilon_n),
\]

in the regime where recursive step size and noise scale compatibly.

Therefore the recursion

\[
u_{n+1}
=
\mathcal T_n^\varepsilon u_n
\]

generates the Hamilton–Jacobi-type equation

\[
\partial_t u
=
b^i\nabla_i u
+
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i u\nabla_j u.
\]

More generally, one obtains equations of the form

\[
\partial_t u + H(x,\nabla u)=0,
\]

where the Hamiltonian \(H\) is the infinitesimal logarithmic generator of the recursive information operator.

This shows that nonlinear analysis, viscosity solutions, and optimal control equations can also be interpreted as recursive information dynamics.

## 7. Applications to Information Theory

### 7.1 Entropy and recursive channels

Let \(p_n\) be a probability density evolving by

\[
p_{n+1}=\mathcal I_n^*p_n.
\]

The Shannon entropy is

\[
H(p_n)
=
-\int_M p_n\log p_n\,d\mu.
\]

The relative entropy between two densities \(p_n\) and \(q_n\) is

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_n\|q_n)
=
\int_M p_n\log\frac{p_n}{q_n}\,d\mu.
\]

Because \(\mathcal I_n\) is a Markov kernel, the data-processing inequality holds:

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal I_n^*p_n\|\mathcal I_n^*q_n)
\leq
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_n\|q_n).
\]

Thus recursive information operators are entropy-contracting channels.

---

### 7.2 Fisher information as recursive carré du champ

Let \(L\) be the generator of a reversible recursive information process with invariant measure \(\mu\). Define the Fisher information of a positive density \(p\) by

\[
\mathcal F(p)
=
\int_M
\Gamma(\log p,\log p)\,p\,d\mu.
\]

For a diffusion generator,

\[
\Gamma(\log p,\log p)
=
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i\log p\,\nabla_j\log p.
\]

Thus Fisher information is the quadratic recursive information form of the log-density.

---

### 7.3 Entropy dissipation

Suppose \(p_t\) solves the continuum adjoint equation

\[
\partial_t p_t = L^*p_t,
\]

and let \(\pi\) be an invariant density. Define

\[
h_t=\frac{p_t}{\pi}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{d}{dt}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t\|\pi)
=
-\int_M
\Gamma(\log h_t,\log h_t)\,p_t\,d\mu
\leq 0.
\]

This identity shows that entropy dissipation is the rate at which recursive information loses distinguishability under the action of the operator.

In RIA, entropy, Fisher information, and relative entropy are not external statistical quantities; they are structural invariants of recursive information operators.

## 8. Applications to Partial Differential Equations

### 8.1 Parabolic equations

Let

\[
\mathcal I_h f
=
f
+
h
\left(
b^i\nabla_i f
+
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i\nabla_j f
\right)
+
o(h).
\]

The recursion

\[
f_{n+1}=\mathcal I_h f_n
\]

approximates

\[
\partial_t u
=
b^i\nabla_i u
+
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i\nabla_j u.
\]

This includes the heat equation, Fokker–Planck equation, and Kolmogorov forward equations as special cases.

For example, if

\[
\mathcal I_h f(x)
=
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{d/2}}
\exp\left(
-\frac{|y-x|^2}{2h}
\right)
f(y)\,dy,
\]

then

\[
\mathcal I_h f
=
f
+
\frac{h}{2}\Delta f
+
O(h^2).
\]

The recursive evolution converges to

\[
\partial_t u
=
\frac12 \Delta u.
\]

---

### 8.2 Hamilton–Jacobi equations

Using the logarithmic recursion

\[
u_{n+1}
=
\varepsilon
\log
\left(
\mathcal I_n e^{u_n/\varepsilon}
\right),
\]

one obtains

\[
\partial_t u
=
H(x,\nabla u),
\]

where

\[
H(x,p)
=
b^i(x)p_i
+
\frac12 a^{ij}(x)p_i p_j.
\]

This gives a recursive construction of viscosity solutions and connects RIA with dynamic programming and optimal control.

---

### 8.3 Weak solutions and recursive test functions

Let \(u_n\) be a recursive solution and \(\phi_n\) a recursive test function. Define

\[
\phi_{n+1}=\mathcal I_n^*\phi_n.
\]

The discrete weak formulation is

\[
\int u_{n+1}\phi_{n+1}\,d\mu_{n+1}
-
\int u_n\phi_n\,d\mu_n
=
0.
\]

Passing to the limit yields

\[
\int_0^T
\int_M
\left(
u\,\partial_t\phi
+
u\,L^*\phi
\right)
d\mu\,dt
=
0,
\]

which is the standard weak formulation of a PDE. Thus weak solutions are invariant pairings under recursive information evolution.

## 9. Applications to Signal Analysis

### 9.1 Recursive filtering

A signal \(s\) is processed by a sequence of information operators:

\[
s_{n+1}
=
\mathcal I_n s_n.
\]

If \(\mathcal I_n\) is a local averaging or conditional expectation operator, the recursion acts as a multiscale low-pass filter.

The recursive detail coefficients are

\[
d_n
=
s_n-\mathcal I_n s_n.
\]

They represent the information lost when passing from level \(n\) to level \(n+1\). Under appropriate moment conditions, these coefficients behave like wavelet or multiscale coefficients.

---

### 9.2 Vanishing moments and sparse representations

If the kernel \(K_n\) satisfies

\[
m_n^{i_1\cdots i_k}=0
\]

for all multi-indices of order \(1\leq k<r\), then the recursive difference annihilates polynomials of degree less than \(r\). This is precisely the vanishing-moment property underlying sparse signal representations.

Thus wavelet-like decompositions arise naturally from recursive information differences.

---

### 9.3 Instantaneous frequency and recursive derivatives

For a complex signal \(z(t)\), the recursive derivative

\[
\mathscr D_n z(t)
=
\frac{z(t+\varepsilon_n)-z(t)}{\varepsilon_n}
\]

approximates the time derivative. The instantaneous frequency may be estimated by

\[
\omega(t)
=
\operatorname{Im}
\left(
\frac{\partial_t z(t)}{z(t)}
\right).
\]

In RIA, the finite-level estimator is

\[
\omega_n(t)
=
\operatorname{Im}
\left(
\frac{\mathscr D_n z(t)}{z(t)}
\right).
\]

This provides a recursive, pre-limit notion of instantaneous frequency.

---

### 9.4 Information bandwidth

The carré du champ

\[
\Gamma(f,f)
\]

defines a local measure of signal variation. For diffusion-type operators,

\[
\Gamma(f,f)
=
\frac12 a^{ij}\nabla_i f\nabla_j f.
\]

The global quantity

\[
\int_M \Gamma(f,f)\,d\mu
\]

is a recursive bandwidth functional. It generalizes Dirichlet energy and appears in denoising, regularization, and uncertainty principles.

## 10. Applications to Machine Learning

### 10.1 Layers as recursive information operators

A deep network may be written as

\[
h_{n+1}
=
\mathcal I_n h_n,
\]

where \(h_n\) is a latent representation and \(\mathcal I_n\) is a learned information channel. In residual networks,

\[
h_{n+1}
=
h_n
+
\varepsilon_n F_n(h_n).
\]

This is exactly a recursive information system. Its continuum limit is a neural ordinary differential equation:

\[
\frac{dh}{dt}
=
F(t,h).
\]

RIA provides a principled interpretation of depth as recursive information refinement.

---

### 10.2 Attention as data-dependent information kernels

An attention operator has the form

\[
(\mathcal A f)(x)
=
\int
K(x,y) f(y)\,dy,
\]

with

\[
K(x,y)
=
\frac{
\exp(q(x)\cdot k(y)/\sqrt{d})
}{
\int \exp(q(x)\cdot k(z)/\sqrt{d})\,dz
}.
\]

This is a recursive information operator whose kernel depends on the information state itself. Multi-head attention corresponds to multiple recursive information channels acting in parallel.

---

### 10.3 Generalization through information contraction

Let \(p_\theta\) be a model distribution and \(q\) a reference distribution. If learning proceeds through recursive channels \(\mathcal I_n\), then

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal I_n^*p_\theta\|\mathcal I_n^*q)
\leq
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\theta\|q).
\]

Thus recursive processing cannot increase distinguishability. Generalization can be understood as the selection of recursive channels that retain task-relevant information while discarding noise.

---

### 10.4 Natural gradient as recursive information descent

Let \(R(\theta)\) be a risk functional. The Fisher information matrix is

\[
G_{\alpha\beta}(\theta)
=
\mathbb E
\left[
\partial_\alpha \log p_\theta
\,
\partial_\beta \log p_\theta
\right].
\]

Natural gradient descent is

\[
\theta_{n+1}
=
\theta_n
-
\eta_n G^{-1}(\theta_n)\nabla_\theta R(\theta_n).
\]

In RIA, \(G\) is the discrete or continuum carré du champ of the statistical information channel. Natural gradient descent is therefore recursive information descent along the geometry induced by \(\Gamma\).

## 11. Examples

### Example 1: Translation and the ordinary derivative

Let

\[
(\mathcal S_h f)(x)=f(x+h).
\]

Then

\[
\mathscr D_h f(x)
=
\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}.
\]

As \(h\to 0\),

\[
\mathscr D_h f(x)\to f'(x).
\]

The recursive integral is

\[
\mathcal G_h f(x)
=
h\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} f(x+kh),
\]

which converges to

\[
\int_x^\infty f(s)\,ds.
\]

Thus one-dimensional calculus is a recursive information system.

---

### Example 2: Gaussian smoothing and the Laplacian

Let

\[
(\mathcal I_h f)(x)
=
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{d/2}}
\exp\left(
-\frac{|y-x|^2}{2h}
\right)
f(y)\,dy.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal I_h f
=
f
+
\frac{h}{2}\Delta f
+
O(h^2).
\]

The recursive derivative is

\[
\mathscr D_h f
=
\frac{\mathcal I_h f-f}{h}
\to
\frac12\Delta f.
\]

The recursion

\[
f_{n+1}=\mathcal I_h f_n
\]

converges to the heat equation.

---

### Example 3: Graph random walks

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a graph. Define a random-walk information operator

\[
(\mathcal I f)(x)
=
\sum_{y\sim x}
P(x,y)f(y),
\]

where \(P(x,y)\) is a transition probability.

The recursive difference

\[
\delta f(x)
=
\mathcal I f(x)-f(x)
\]

is a graph Laplacian-type operator. The invariant measure is the stationary distribution of the random walk, and the integral is summation with respect to that measure.

Thus discrete geometry, graph neural networks, and discrete PDEs fit naturally into RIA.

## 13. Discussion and Future Directions

The framework presented here suggests several directions for further development.

### 13.1 Optimal recursive information operators

Given a target analytic task, one may seek kernels \(K_n\) minimizing information loss while preserving stability. This leads to variational problems over recursive channels.

### 13.2 Noncommutative recursive information analysis

When the underlying information states are noncommutative, as in quantum information or operator algebras, the operators \(\mathcal I_n\) become completely positive maps. RIA then extends naturally to quantum stochastic calculus and noncommutative geometry.

### 13.3 Recursive information curvature

The failure of recursive directional derivatives to commute defines a curvature tensor. A systematic theory of recursive information curvature may provide new tools for geometric analysis and learning theory.

### 13.4 Adaptive recursive calculi

In machine learning and signal processing, the operators \(\mathcal I_n\) should depend on data. A full theory of adaptive recursive information operators would unify numerical analysis, statistical estimation, and deep learning.

### 13.5 Foundations of analysis without limits as primitives

RIA suggests that much of analysis can be reconstructed from algebraic and probabilistic recursion. Limits remain useful, but they appear as emergent fixed points, continuum approximations, or invariant regimes of recursive information systems.
