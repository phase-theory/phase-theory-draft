Abstract

Large-scale information systems require mechanisms that preserve communication across distance, complexity, and loss.

Classical repeaters regenerate signals. Quantum repeater research studies methods for extending communication using entanglement distribution, purification, storage, and coordinated state transfer under physical constraints.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ routing

→ optimization

→ distributed networks

→ quantum-geometric information organization.

A further question emerges:

If networks become accessibility across geometry—

what becomes of repeaters?

This paper develops Geometric Repeaters (GR): a theoretical framework in which repeater behavior is represented as preservation and extension of accessibility across distributed computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting repeaters as intermediate relay stations, the framework interprets repeaters as regions that maintain continuity of computational accessibility.

The central proposal:

Repeaters do not forward information.

Repeaters preserve reachable structure.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an experimentally validated repeater architecture.
