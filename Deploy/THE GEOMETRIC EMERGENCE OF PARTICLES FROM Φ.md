Abstract

We propose a mathematical program for determining the ontology of particles without assuming in advance that particles are knots, point objects, solitons, field quanta, defects, or any other predetermined structure.

The starting postulate is deliberately minimal:

\boxed{\Phi\rightarrow\text{Geometry}\rightarrow\text{Spacetime}\rightarrow\text{Everything Else}}

where \Phi denotes a proposed primitive phase structure and geometry denotes the first emergent mathematical organization of that structure.

The central thesis of this work is methodological rather than ontological:

The nature of particles should be determined by the stable mathematical structures that emerge from the underlying theory, rather than imposed upon the theory beforehand.

Accordingly, particle ontology is treated as an inverse problem. Given a primitive phase structure \Phi, one seeks the admissible geometric configurations generated by it, determines whether a Lorentzian spacetime emerges, constructs the effective fields supported by that spacetime, and identifies localized, stable, quantized, or representation-theoretically distinguished solutions. Only structures satisfying appropriate existence, stability, covariance, compositional, and phenomenological criteria are candidates for particles.

This framework deliberately suspends the earlier hypothesis that particles are knots of a phase substrate. Knot theory is retained only as one possible mathematical diagnostic among many. A particle may ultimately prove to be a geometric excitation, a field configuration, a topological sector, a nonlinear localized solution, a representation of an emergent symmetry, a defect, a composite structure, or something not presently anticipated.

The framework therefore seeks to replace particle assignment with particle derivation.

Part I — Foundational Position

1. Introduction

Modern physics contains two extraordinarily successful but conceptually distinct descriptions of nature.

General Relativity describes gravitation through spacetime geometry:

(M,g_{\mu\nu}),

while quantum field theory describes elementary particles through quantum fields and their excitations.

The Standard Model consequently provides a highly successful particle spectrum without establishing that its particles are the ultimate constituents of reality.

Phase Theory begins at a deeper hypothetical level.

Its provisional postulate is:

\boxed{\Phi\text{ is more fundamental than geometry.}}

The theory does not, at present, establish what \Phi physically is, nor does it establish the exact mechanism through which geometry emerges.

\boxed{

The crucial point is that the arrows are hypotheses to be derived, not assumptions that have already been demonstrated.
