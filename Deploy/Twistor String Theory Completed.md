# Abstract

Twistor string theory, introduced by Edward Witten in 2003, represented one of the most elegant reformulations of quantum field theory ever discovered. By mapping perturbative gauge theory into the geometry of twistor space, it transformed scattering amplitudes into algebraic-geometric structures and reproduced tree-level amplitudes of maximally supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory with unprecedented simplicity.

Yet the program stalled.

The fundamental obstacle was the unavoidable appearance of conformal supergravity in the closed-string sector. Because conformal supergravity contains ghost degrees of freedom and lacks a satisfactory ultraviolet completion, the twistor-string framework was largely abandoned as a candidate for fundamental physics. Subsequent developments—including BCFW recursion, Grassmannian formulations, positive geometries, and the amplituhedron—preserved many computational successes while discarding the original string-theoretic picture.

This paper proposes a completion of twistor string theory.

The central hypothesis is that the closed-string sector was incorrectly identified. Instead of conformal supergravity, closed-string excitations correspond to global topological holonomies of twistor space itself. Gravity emerges not as an independent field propagating alongside gauge theory but as the curvature induced by collective deformations of twistor incidence structures.

In this framework:

* Open strings generate gauge sectors.
* Closed strings generate twistor-space curvature modes.
* Einstein gravity emerges as an effective infrared limit.
* Conformal gravity appears only as a high-energy gauge redundancy.
* Amplituhedron geometry arises naturally from twistor-string moduli spaces.
* Spacetime itself becomes an emergent projection of twistor holonomy.

The result is a unified geometric theory in which gauge interactions, gravity, scattering amplitudes, and spacetime structure arise from a common twistor-string origin.
