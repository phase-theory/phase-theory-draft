Abstract

Conventional quantum information theory assumes that qubits exist on a classical geometric background in which spatial coordinates commute. However, multiple approaches to quantum gravity, non-commutative geometry, and generalized uncertainty principles suggest that spacetime itself may possess a fundamentally non-commuting structure at sufficiently small scales. In such theories, coordinates become operators satisfying nontrivial commutation relations, and geometry acquires intrinsically quantum properties.

This paper develops the theory of Non-Commutative Geometric Qubits (NCGQs), a framework in which quantum information is encoded directly into non-commutative geometric degrees of freedom. Building upon the mathematical foundations of the Moyal plane, deformation quantization, operator algebras, spectral geometry, and non-commutative manifolds, we define non-commutative geometric Hilbert spaces, coordinate qubits, geometric operator states, non-commutative entanglement, geometric logic gates, error correction in non-commutative spaces, and candidate physical implementations.

The resulting framework establishes a bridge between quantum information theory, non-commutative geometry, generalized uncertainty principles, and geometry-native quantum computation.

Conclusions

Non-Commutative Geometric Qubits extend geometry-based quantum information theory into regimes where coordinates themselves become quantum operators. By encoding information into coordinate algebras, spectral structures, deformation sectors, and non-commutative manifolds, quantum information becomes inseparable from geometric structure.

The framework integrates concepts from non-commutative geometry, generalized uncertainty principles, deformation quantization, spectral triples, matrix geometry, and topological quantum information. It introduces coordinate qubits, spectral qubits, non-commutative channels, algebraic logic gates, spectral stabilizers, and quantum-gravity-compatible information architectures.

Within the Structural Sector, Non-Commutative Geometric Qubits represent the first explicit merger of geometric quantum information theory with non-commutative spacetime physics. They provide a possible pathway toward computation on quantum geometry itself and establish a foundation for future investigations into Planck-scale information processing, emergent spacetime computation, and geometry-native quantum technologies.
