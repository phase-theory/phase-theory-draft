Abstract

Quantum computation has traditionally focused on physical implementations where information is stored in material degrees of freedom such as superconducting circuits, trapped ions, semiconductor spins, or atomic systems. Photonic quantum systems provide a distinct approach: information is carried by quantum states of light, enabling high-speed propagation, low thermal interaction, and compatibility with optical communication.

However, conventional photonic qubits primarily encode information in local properties:

* Polarization
* Time bins
* Frequency modes
* Path modes
* Orbital angular momentum

This paper develops a new architecture: Silicon Photonic Geometric Qubits (SPGQ).

In this framework, the qubit is not defined by a single photon attribute but by engineered geometric structure within an integrated photonic substrate. Information is encoded into geometric degrees of freedom including:

* Synthetic gauge fields
* Photonic band geometry
* Berry curvature
* Topological modes
* Resonator manifold states
* Holonomic transformations

The silicon photonic platform becomes a geometric information processor where optical paths, phase structures, topology, and curvature act as computational resources.

This paper defines the theoretical model, device architecture, engineering specifications, error mechanisms, control systems, and comparison with existing quantum computing approaches.
