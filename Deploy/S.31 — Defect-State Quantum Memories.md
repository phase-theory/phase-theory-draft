Abstract

Quantum computation requires not only processing but reliable storage of quantum information. Existing quantum memory approaches—including atomic ensembles, trapped ions, superconducting resonators, spins, and photonic delay systems—face tradeoffs among coherence, scalability, environmental sensitivity, and integration.

Across condensed matter physics, photonics, and topological systems, defects often create localized states that differ fundamentally from bulk behavior. Rather than being imperfections to eliminate, defects can become functional information structures.

This paper develops Defect-State Quantum Memories (DSQM): a geometry-native memory framework in which quantum information is stored in engineered localized defect states embedded inside larger physical manifolds.

The central principle:

Information persists because geometry prevents escape.

Defect states become quantum memory cells, geometric traps, and protected information reservoirs.

This document presents a theoretical architecture and research program rather than an experimentally validated universal memory technology.
