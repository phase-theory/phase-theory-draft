## Abstract

Causal-Informational Relativity is the most radical extension of the Einstein program. It proposes that spacetime, geometry, matter, and gravitational dynamics are not fundamental. They emerge from a deeper structure consisting of elementary events, causal order, and local quantum-informational degrees of freedom. The fundamental object is not a manifold \(M\), not a metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\), and not even a quantum state on a fixed Hilbert space. It is a causal-informational structure,

\[
\mathcal{C}
=
\{e_i,\prec,\mathcal{H}_i\},
\]

where \(e_i\) are elementary events, \(\prec\) is a locally finite partial causal order, and \(\mathcal{H}_i\) are finite quantum-informational systems associated with events or elementary causal processes. Spacetime volume emerges from counting causal events,

\[
N \sim \frac{V}{\ell_{\text{P}}^4},
\]

while spatial geometry emerges from patterns of entanglement and causal connectivity. The Einstein equation appears not as a fundamental law but as a hydrodynamic limit of causal-informational dynamics,

\[
\text{causal information dynamics}
\quad
\Longrightarrow
\quad
G_{\mu\nu}
+
\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=
8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}.
\]

The deepest principle is not coordinate invariance alone. It is causal invariance:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical law must be expressible without reference to background time, background space, or an external observer.}
}
\]

Relativity 13.0 is the ultimate generalization of relativity: not merely the relativity of coordinates, frames, horizons, or locality, but the relativity of spacetime itself as an emergent causal-informational order.

## 1. Introduction

Every previous stage of relativity removed an absolute structure.

Special relativity removed absolute simultaneity. General relativity removed absolute geometry. Quantum reference frames removed absolute observers. Holography removed absolute bulk locality. Thermodynamic relativity removed absolute gravitational dynamics. Celestial relativity removed absolute asymptotic scattering. Precision multi-messenger relativity made the entire structure empirically testable.

Causal-Informational Relativity removes the final absolute structure:

\[
\boxed{
\text{spacetime itself.}
}
\]

It does not begin with a manifold. It does not begin with a metric. It does not begin with a global Hilbert space evolving in time. It begins with causal relations among elementary events and with quantum-informational degrees of freedom carried by those events.

The central hypothesis is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Causal order plus quantum information generates geometry.}
}
\]

This hypothesis is motivated by several independent lines of evidence.

First, general relativity teaches that causal structure is almost geometry. In well-behaved spacetimes, the causal order determines the metric up to a conformal factor. The remaining conformal factor is equivalent to volume. If volume is supplied by counting elementary events, the full metric is recovered.

Second, black-hole thermodynamics teaches that geometry carries entropy. Horizon entropy is proportional to area, suggesting that the degrees of freedom of spacetime are finite, informational, and boundary-organized.

Third, holography teaches that bulk geometry can be reconstructed from entanglement in a lower-dimensional quantum system.

Fourth, quantum information theory teaches that causal order itself can be quantum, indefinite, or relational.

Fifth, causal-set theory teaches that a discrete causal order, together with a number-volume correspondence, can recover Lorentzian geometry without a preferred frame.

Causal-Informational Relativity unifies these lessons.

It is not merely another quantum-gravity proposal. It is a meta-framework for understanding what relativity becomes when causality and information are taken as primitive.

## 2. Axioms of Causal-Informational Relativity

The theory may be organized around seven axioms.

### Axiom 1: Events Are Primitive

The fundamental entities are elementary events

\[
e_i.
\]

They are not points embedded in a pre-existing spacetime. They are the constituents from which spacetime emerges.

### Axiom 2: Causal Order Is Primitive

Events are related by a partial order,

\[
e_i \prec e_j,
\]

meaning that event \(i\) can causally influence event \(j\).

The order is locally finite: for any two events \(e_i\prec e_j\), the causal interval

\[
I(e_i,e_j)
=
\{e_k \mid e_i\prec e_k\prec e_j\}
\]

contains finitely many events.

### Axiom 3: Information Is Local

Each event or elementary causal link carries quantum-informational degrees of freedom,

\[
\mathcal{H}_i,
\]

or more generally a local algebra \(\mathcal{A}_i\).

### Axiom 4: Dynamics Is Causal

Physical dynamics consists of quantum-informational transformations respecting causal order. In the most general quantum regime, causal order itself may be indefinite.

### Axiom 5: Volume Is Counting

Continuum spacetime volume emerges from the number of elementary events:

\[
N
\sim
\frac{V}{\ell_{\text{P}}^4}.
\]

### Axiom 6: Geometry Is Entanglement plus Causality

Metric geometry emerges from the joint structure of causal connectivity and quantum entanglement.

### Axiom 7: Einstein Gravity Is Hydrodynamic

The Einstein equation is a coarse-grained equation of state of the underlying causal-informational system.

These axioms define Relativity 13.0.

## 3. Causal Sets as the Skeletal Structure

A causal set is a pair

\[
(C,\prec),
\]

where \(C\) is a set of elementary events and \(\prec\) is a partial order satisfying:

1. Reflexivity:

\[
e_i \prec e_i.
\]

2. Antisymmetry:

\[
e_i\prec e_j,\; e_j\prec e_i
\Rightarrow
e_i=e_j.
\]

3. Transitivity:

\[
e_i\prec e_j,\; e_j\prec e_k
\Rightarrow
e_i\prec e_k.
\]

4. Local finiteness:

\[
|I(e_i,e_j)|<\infty.
\]

The slogan of causal-set theory is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{order plus number equals geometry.}
}
\]

The order gives causal structure. The number gives volume.

Together they determine Lorentzian geometry in the continuum approximation.

## 4. Causal Order Determines Conformal Geometry

A classical theorem due to Malament shows that, for a broad class of spacetimes, the causal order determines the metric up to a conformal factor.

If two spacetimes \((M,g_{\mu\nu})\) and \((M',g'_{\mu\nu})\) have isomorphic causal structures, then under suitable conditions their metrics are conformally related:

\[
g'_{\mu\nu}
=
\Omega^2(x)
g_{\mu\nu}.
\]

The conformal factor \(\Omega(x)\) encodes local volume.

Thus, if one knows:

1. the causal order,
2. the volume measure,

one knows the full metric.

In causal-set theory, the volume measure is supplied by counting events. If the density is

\[
\rho
=
\ell_{\text{P}}^{-4},
\]

then a spacetime region of volume \(V\) contains approximately

\[
N
=
\rho V
=
\frac{V}{\ell_{\text{P}}^4}
\]

events.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{causal order} + \text{event counting}
\quad
\Longrightarrow
\quad
\text{Lorentzian metric}.
}
\]

This is the geometric foundation of Causal-Informational Relativity.

## 5. Lorentz Invariance from Random Sprinkling

A discrete spacetime usually threatens Lorentz invariance. A regular lattice selects preferred directions.

Causal sets avoid this by using a Poisson sprinkling of events into a Lorentzian manifold.

The probability of finding \(N\) events in a spacetime volume \(V\) is

\[
P(N)
=
\frac{(\rho V)^N}{N!}
e^{-\rho V}.
\]

A Poisson process is Lorentz invariant in distribution. No preferred frame is introduced.

Thus discreteness and relativity can coexist:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The fundamental structure is discrete but statistically Lorentz invariant.}
}
\]

This is one of the deepest insights behind causal-set theory.

## 6. Dimension, Distance, and Proper Time

In a continuum Lorentzian spacetime, the number of events in a causal interval depends on the spacetime dimension.

For a small causal interval of proper time \(\tau\), the expected number of events scales as

\[
N
\sim
\rho\, \tau^d,
\]

up to dimension-dependent numerical factors.

By measuring the relation between interval cardinality and order-theoretic height, one can define an order-theoretic dimension estimator. The Myrheim–Meyer dimension uses the cardinality of causal intervals to infer the effective continuum dimension.

Proper time is approximated by the length of the longest chain between causally related events. If \(L_{\max}\) is the maximal number of links in a chain, then

\[
\tau
\sim
\ell_{\text{P}}\,L_{\max},
\]

up to dimension-dependent constants.

Spatial distance is more subtle, but it can be reconstructed from common past and common future volumes, causal overlap, and entanglement structure.

Thus the familiar coordinates \(x^\mu\) are not fundamental. They are coarse-grained parameters summarizing causal-informational relations.

\[

\[

\[
\mathcal{A}_i

e_i \not\prec e_j

\[
[\mathcal{A}_i,\mathcal{A}_j]=0.
\]

If \(e_i\prec e_j\), information may flow from \(i\) to \(j\) through quantum channels.

Thus causality becomes a constraint on quantum-informational transformations.

## 9. Indefinite Causal Order

At the Planck scale, causal order itself may become quantum.

Quantum information theory already provides examples of indefinite causal order. The quantum switch places two operations \(A\) and \(B\) in a superposition of orders:

\[
\ket{0}\ket{\psi}
\rightarrow
\ket{0}\,B A\ket{\psi},
\]

\[
\ket{1}\ket{\psi}
\rightarrow
\ket{1}\,A B\ket{\psi}.
\]

For a control qubit in a superposition,

\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\left(
\ket{0}+\ket{1}
\right),
\]

the resulting process has no definite global causal order between \(A\) and \(B\).

More generally, process-matrix theory describes correlations without assuming a fixed causal background.

A process matrix \(W\) assigns probabilities to local operations by

\[
p(a,b,\ldots)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\left[
W
\left(
M_A^a
\otimes
M_B^b
\otimes
\cdots
\right)
\right],
\]

where \(M_A^a,M_B^b,\ldots\) are local instruments.

The matrix \(W\) must satisfy positivity and linear constraints ensuring valid probabilities:

\[
W\geq 0,
\qquad
\mathcal{L}(W)=0.
\]

In Causal-Informational Relativity, such structures are not exotic laboratory curiosities. They are finite-dimensional precursors of Planck-scale quantum geometry.

At low energies, causal order becomes effectively definite. At high energies, it may be quantum, fluctuating, or indefinite.

\[
S_A
=
\frac{\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4G\hbar}.

Mutual information between two regions,

\[
I(A:B)
=
S_A+S_B-S_{A\cup B},

When mutual information is large, the corresponding bulk regions are geometrically connected. When it vanishes at leading order, the bulk geometry factorizes.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Entanglement builds spatial connectivity.}

In tensor-network models, geometry is literally the network pattern of entanglement. Minimal cuts through the network compute entanglement entropy, mimicking the Ryu–Takayanagi formula.

For holographic states,

\delta S_A
=
\frac{\delta\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4G\hbar}.

Thus:

\[
\text{entanglement equilibrium}
\quad
\Longrightarrow

### 14.3 Causal-Set Route

Thus the same hydrodynamic limit appears from three directions:

1. horizon thermodynamics,

This convergence is the central evidence for Causal-Informational Relativity.
