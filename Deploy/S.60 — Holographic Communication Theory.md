Abstract

Communication systems traditionally describe how information propagates between distributed systems.

Classical communication theory models transmission using channels, encoding, capacity, and error correction. Quantum communication extends these ideas using quantum states, distributed correlations, and information-theoretic constraints.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ routing

→ networks

→ repeaters

→ channels

→ topological communication.

A further question emerges:

If communication becomes preservation of accessibility—

must information travel through the entire system?

This paper develops Holographic Communication Theory (HCT): a speculative theoretical framework in which communication is represented through structured relationships between boundary-accessible descriptions and higher-dimensional computational organization.

The inspiration comes from holographic reasoning used in parts of modern theoretical physics and information theory, where lower-dimensional descriptions may encode aspects of higher-dimensional structure. This paper uses those ideas as computational metaphors and does not claim physical realization or applicability of any specific holographic duality.

The central proposal:

Communication is not modeled as moving complete internal structure.

Communication is modeled as reconstructing accessible structure from constrained descriptions.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an experimentally validated communication architecture.
