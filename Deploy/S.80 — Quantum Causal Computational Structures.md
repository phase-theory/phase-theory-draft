Abstract

Computation is commonly represented through ordered execution, state transition, communication, and controlled dependency.

Classical systems impose execution order through clocks, schedulers, and dependency graphs. Quantum information introduces nonclassical correlations while preserving operational constraints on information flow.

Across the Structural Sector, information evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ computation

→ security

→ memory

→ preservation

→ informational geometry

→ relational organization.

A further question emerges:

If computation becomes organized geometry—

can execution itself be represented as structured causality?

This paper develops Quantum Causal Computational Structures (QCCS): a speculative theoretical framework in which information processing is represented through organized dependency relations and accessibility structure.

This framework does not propose a physical theory of causality, replacement of quantum mechanics, or derivation of spacetime.

Instead, it studies computation as organization constrained by permissible informational relationships.

The central proposal:

Execution is not only ordered transition.

Execution may be represented as structured dependency.

This document presents speculative conceptual research rather than a realizable computational architecture.
