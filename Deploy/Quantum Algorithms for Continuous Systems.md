Quantum Algorithms for Continuous Systems

Toward a Native Complexity Theory and Algorithmic Calculus for Infinite-Dimensional Quantum Computation

Author: Anonymous
Date: July 2026

Abstract

Quantum algorithm theory has developed almost entirely within finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Canonical complexity classes, universal gate constructions, and algorithmic speedup proofs rely on qubit registers, finite tensor products, and discrete computational primitives. Yet the overwhelming majority of physical systems—quantum fields, wave equations, Hamiltonian flows, continuous symmetries, and partial differential equations—are naturally formulated over infinite-dimensional state spaces.

Current continuous-variable (CV) quantum computing inherits discrete computational assumptions: finite truncation, lattice discretization, and qubit embedding. These procedures often destroy geometric invariants, introduce ultraviolet artifacts, and obscure algorithmic advantages available in native continuous dynamics.

This paper develops a unified theoretical foundation for continuous quantum algorithmics.

Four principal results are introduced.

1. Continuous Quantum Polynomial Time (CQP): a complexity class for algorithms defined directly over separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces with bounded physical resources.
2. A Continuous Solovay–Kitaev Theorem (CSK): establishing approximate universality for physically admissible continuous gate algebras generated by finite Gaussian and nonlinear primitive sets.
3. Infinite-Dimensional Quantum Algorithmic Calculus (IQAC): a native framework for quantum algorithms acting on operator semigroups, PDE generators, and continuous spectra.
4. Symplectic Quantum Algorithms (SQA): algorithms exploiting Hamiltonian geometry directly through quantum canonical transformations.

Together these results propose an algorithmic theory of quantum computation beyond discretization.
