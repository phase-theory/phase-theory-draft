# Abstract

Renormalization Group (RG) theory is one of the deepest principles in modern physics. It explains universality, critical phenomena, effective field theories, and the emergence of macroscopic behavior from microscopic laws. Conventional RG analyzes how physical systems change under variations of momentum scale.

Finite-density quantum field theory introduces additional scales beyond momentum. Temperature, chemical potentials, entropy density, baryon density, and information geometry all influence physical behavior. Traditional RG frameworks treat these variables as external parameters rather than dynamical flow coordinates.

Unified Finite-Density Quantum Field Theory (UFD-QFT) generalizes renormalization theory by promoting density variables to components of an extended renormalization manifold. Couplings evolve not only with energy scale but also with thermodynamic position. The resulting Density Renormalization Group (DRG) framework unifies scale evolution, thermodynamic geometry, topology change, and dense matter phase structure.

Within this theory, the QCD phase diagram emerges as a landscape of renormalization trajectories flowing across density space.
