Abstract

Stabilizer codes form the mathematical foundation of modern quantum error correction. By defining protected subspaces through commuting operator constraints, stabilizer codes enable the detection and correction of errors without directly measuring encoded quantum information. They underpin many of the most successful fault-tolerant architectures, including surface codes, color codes, subsystem codes, and topological quantum memories.

Within the Geometric Qubit framework, information is encoded in geometric degrees of freedom such as curvature states, topological sectors, defect configurations, boundary structures, and connectivity networks. This motivates a generalized stabilizer formalism capable of protecting geometry-encoded information against geometric noise and decoherence.

This paper develops the theory of Geometric Stabilizer Codes (GSC), extending conventional stabilizer theory into geometric Hilbert spaces. We define geometric stabilizer groups, geometric code spaces, geometric logical operators, geometric syndrome structures, curvature stabilizers, topological stabilizers, defect stabilizers, boundary stabilizers, holographic stabilizer architectures, and fault-tolerant geometric computation. The resulting framework provides a rigorous algebraic foundation for scalable geometric quantum information processing.
