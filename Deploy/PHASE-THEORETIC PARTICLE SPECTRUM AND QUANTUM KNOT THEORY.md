Abstract

This paper develops a Phase-Theory-based particle ontology in which particles are understood as stable, metastable, or dynamically persistent configurations of an underlying phase structure. Quantum Knot Theory (QKT) is introduced as the mathematical language for representing the topology of those configurations.

The central proposal is that the experimentally established Standard Model spectrum represents a finite, low-complexity sector of a broader spectrum of admissible phase configurations. In the QKT representation, particle states are characterized by topological quantities including crossing complexity, twist/chirality, braid structure, and vibrational mode.

The framework establishes a unified correspondence

[

\rightarrow
\text{stable phase configuration}

\rightarrow

The paper reconstructs the Standard Model correspondence in QKT language and develops a 28-state extended spectrum consisting of predicted configurations beyond the Standard Model. The predicted spectrum includes neutral self-conjugate knot states, gluonic configurations, dark-sector solitons, exotic hadronic topologies, hidden-sector states, phase defects, and higher-complexity stable configurations.

The three principal predictions inherited directly from the canonical QKT construction are the figure-eight Majorana state, the Borromean scalar glueball, and the Gordian dark-matter soliton. Additional members of the 28-state spectrum are treated as Phase-Theory candidates whose existence requires dynamical derivation and experimental validation.

The framework does not claim that the proposed QKT identifications are established particle-physics facts. Instead, it defines a mathematically organized hypothesis in which experimentally observed particles and predicted states occupy a common topological state space.
