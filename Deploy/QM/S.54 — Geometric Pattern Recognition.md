Abstract

Pattern recognition is one of the most fundamental operations in computation.

Classical systems recognize patterns through feature extraction, statistical inference, classification, clustering, and similarity metrics. Contemporary learning systems construct representations that support recognition across complex and high-dimensional domains.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ optimization

→ learning

→ neural representation

→ semantic organization

→ adaptive intelligence.

A further question emerges:

If intelligence becomes organization of computational geometry—

what becomes of recognition?

This paper develops Geometric Pattern Recognition (GPR): a theoretical framework in which recognition is represented as structured detection of organization across computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting recognition as assigning labels to observations, the framework interprets recognition as identifying stable geometric correspondences.

The central proposal:

Recognition is not matching symbols.

Recognition is detecting persistent structure.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an experimentally validated recognition architecture.
