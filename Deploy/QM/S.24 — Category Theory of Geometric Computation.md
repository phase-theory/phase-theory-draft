Abstract

Category theory provides one of the most powerful unifying frameworks in modern mathematics. Originally developed by Samuel Eilenberg and Saunders Mac Lane, category theory abstracts mathematical structures into collections of objects and morphisms, revealing deep relationships between seemingly unrelated fields.

Quantum computation, topology, gauge theory, fiber bundles, differential geometry, and information theory all possess rich categorical structures. Yet existing approaches typically employ category theory as a descriptive language rather than as a computational foundation. In particular, the emerging framework of geometric quantum information lacks a unified categorical formulation capable of describing curvature states, topological operators, gauge structures, holonomies, geometric channels, and geometry-native computation within a single mathematical architecture.

This paper develops the Category Theory of Geometric Computation (CTGC), establishing a categorical framework in which information, geometry, computation, communication, and physical evolution are represented by objects, morphisms, functors, higher categories, and geometric transformations. Computation becomes composition, communication becomes functorial transport, error correction becomes categorical stabilization, and physical law emerges as structure-preserving transformation.

The resulting framework provides the highest-level mathematical language developed thus far within the Structural Sector.
