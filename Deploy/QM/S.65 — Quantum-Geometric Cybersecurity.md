Abstract

Cybersecurity determines whether computation remains trustworthy under observation, failure, misuse, and adversarial interaction.

Classical cybersecurity organizes defense through identity, access control, monitoring, cryptography, segmentation, recovery, and operational resilience. Quantum information introduces additional concerns including state preparation, measurement effects, distributed coordination, and post-quantum assumptions.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ distribution

→ consensus

→ cryptography.

A further question emerges:

If information and security become geometry—

what becomes of defense?

This paper develops Quantum-Geometric Cybersecurity (QGC): a speculative theoretical framework in which cybersecurity is represented as preservation of admissible accessibility across computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting cybersecurity as perimeter protection around isolated assets, the framework interprets defense as maintenance of allowable computational organization.

The central proposal:

Security is not protection of locations.

Security is preservation of admissible accessibility.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework and not a real-world cybersecurity standard, operational guidance document, or replacement for established security practices.
