Abstract

Topological quantum computation seeks computational primitives whose logical states derive protection from global structure rather than local control precision. One influential direction studies Majorana zero modes—special quasiparticle excitations with nonlocal encoding and non-Abelian exchange properties. These ideas are actively explored in condensed matter systems and remain an area of ongoing experimental and theoretical development.

Separately, topological photonics has shown that optical systems can realize protected transport, synthetic gauge fields, defect-localized modes, and engineered band structures.

This paper develops a speculative but mathematically motivated framework called Photonic Majorana Geometric Qubits (PMGQ).

The central idea is not that literal fermionic Majorana particles exist in photonic circuits. Instead, photonic structures are engineered to exhibit Majorana-analog geometric modes—optical states designed to reproduce selected mathematical properties associated with protected zero-mode architectures:

* nonlocal encoding,
* degeneracy protection,
* geometric transport,
* holonomic evolution,
* and topological state manipulation.

Information is encoded into distributed optical mode structure and manipulated through geometry rather than localized particle operations.

This document presents a conceptual architecture and research program rather than an experimentally validated qubit platform.
