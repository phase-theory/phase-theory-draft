Abstract

Traditional quantum computation models logic as sequences of externally specified unitary operations acting on qubits. Geometric approaches reinterpret computation as motion through structured state spaces.

This paper develops Curvature-Based Logic Operations (CBLO): a theoretical framework in which logical transformations emerge from engineered curvature of computational state manifolds.

Rather than defining logic through explicit gate primitives, the framework proposes that logic may arise from how geometry redirects state evolution.

Curvature acts as the computational resource.

The central proposal:

Information evolves because geometry bends trajectories.

Logical outcomes are generated by controlled curvature fields imposed on admissible state motion.

This document presents a theoretical architecture and mathematical research direction rather than an experimentally established computing model.
