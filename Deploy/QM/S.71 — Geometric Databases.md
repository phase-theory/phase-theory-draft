Abstract

Databases determine how information is organized, retrieved, maintained, and evolved.

Classical database systems represent information through records, tables, indexes, graphs, transactions, and query execution. Distributed systems extend these concepts through replication, consistency, partitioning, and fault tolerance. Emerging information systems increasingly emphasize relationships, semantics, and adaptive organization.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ computation

→ security

→ memory

→ archives

→ distributed reconstruction.

A further question emerges:

If storage becomes recoverable organization—

what becomes of databases?

This paper develops Geometric Databases (GDB): a speculative theoretical framework in which databases are represented as navigable computational geometry whose structure governs accessibility and retrieval.

Rather than interpreting databases as collections of discrete records, the framework interprets databases as organized accessibility landscapes.

The central proposal:

Databases are not containers of records.

Databases are structured accessibility.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than a replacement for relational, graph, vector, or distributed database systems.
