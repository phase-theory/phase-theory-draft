Abstract

Topological phases of matter revealed that physical systems may possess global structures that remain invariant under continuous deformation. In photonics, this insight led to topological photonic systems in which light propagation is governed not only by local refractive index profiles but also by global geometric and topological properties of optical media.

Topological photonics has demonstrated robust transport, protected edge states, defect-localized modes, and synthetic gauge structures. Yet existing approaches generally treat topology as a transport enhancement rather than a complete computational substrate.

This paper develops Topological Photonic Geometries (TPG) as a geometry-native computational and information framework. In this architecture, information is encoded directly into photonic topological sectors, transformed through geometric transport, manipulated by holonomies, protected by topology, and executed through optical manifold evolution.

Photonic geometry becomes not merely a communication medium but a programmable computational space.

This paper defines the mathematical structure, device architecture, information encoding schemes, processor concepts, fault tolerance mechanisms, and scaling principles for topological photonic computation.
