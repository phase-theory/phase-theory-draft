Abstract

Conventional information systems rely on fixed materials whose computational behavior emerges from intrinsic electronic, photonic, or atomic properties. Metamaterials introduced a different paradigm: physical behavior can be engineered through structure rather than composition.

By designing subwavelength architecture, metamaterials may exhibit effective responses not naturally found in bulk materials, enabling engineered transport, wave control, dispersion shaping, and synthetic field behavior.

This paper develops Metamaterial Geometric Information Systems (MGIS): a theoretical framework in which information is represented, transformed, stored, and communicated through engineered geometric structure.

The central proposal:

Information is not encoded primarily in matter.

Information is encoded in the geometry that matter imposes on allowable transformations.

Within this framework, metamaterials become programmable computational media capable of implementing geometric states, topological transport, defect memories, synthetic gauge structures, and geometry-native processing.

This document presents a theoretical architecture and research program rather than an experimentally established universal computing platform.
