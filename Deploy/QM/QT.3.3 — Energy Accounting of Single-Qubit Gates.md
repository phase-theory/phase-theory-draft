The Thermodynamic Cost of Quantum Computation

Part III — Thermodynamics of Quantum Gates

Section 15. Energy Accounting of Single-Qubit Gates

⸻

15.1 Introduction

The single-qubit gate is the fundamental computational primitive of quantum information processing. Every quantum algorithm, irrespective of architecture, is ultimately composed of elementary one-qubit rotations combined with entangling operations. While Section 14 established a general framework for physical gate implementations, the present section develops the first systematic thermodynamic accounting for individual single-qubit operations.

Traditional analyses characterize single-qubit gates through fidelity, gate time, and control error. These metrics do not quantify the energetic resources consumed during gate execution. Quantum Computational Thermodynamics (QCT) instead assigns every elementary gate a complete thermodynamic budget consisting of

* supplied work,
* stored energy,
* dissipated heat,
* entropy production,
* free-energy variation,
* exergy consumption,
* implementation efficiency.

The resulting framework provides the microscopic foundation upon which algorithm-level thermodynamic complexity will later be constructed.

15.19 Universal Energy Accounting Principle

The preceding theorem establishes a fundamental distinction between logical and physical computation.

The logical operation specifies only the target unitary transformation,

whereas the physical implementation determines the total energetic cost.

Consequently,

identical logical gates executed on different quantum hardware may exhibit identical algorithmic complexity but different thermodynamic complexity.

Energy accounting therefore becomes an intrinsic component of quantum algorithm analysis rather than a secondary engineering consideration.

15.20 Summary

This section develops the first comprehensive thermodynamic accounting framework for elementary single-qubit operations. Every gate is characterized not only by its logical action on the Bloch sphere but also by its complete physical resource budget, including work, heat, entropy generation, free-energy variation, exergy loss, and implementation efficiency.

The principal mathematical results include:

* formulation of single-qubit gate energetics from time-dependent control Hamiltonians;
* derivation of gate work, heat, free-energy, and exergy accounting equations;
* proof of the Rotation-Angle Energy Law linking implementation work to rotation angle;
* definition of the Single-Qubit Energy Functional, Thermodynamic Gate Vector, and Single-Qubit Thermodynamic Tensor;
* establishment of the Universal Single-Qubit Cost Bound;
* and proof of the Single-Qubit Energy Accounting Theorem, providing a complete decomposition of gate work into useful computational free energy, irreversible entropy production, and parasitic implementation losses.

These results establish the microscopic thermodynamic foundation for quantum computation. The next section extends this framework to multi-qubit operations, where interaction Hamiltonians, correlated resource flows, and entangling dynamics introduce fundamentally new sources of energetic and entropic cost that have no analogue in isolated single-qubit gates.
