Abstract

Gauge symmetry is one of the deepest organizing principles in modern physics. Electromagnetism, the weak interaction, quantum chromodynamics, and numerous condensed-matter systems are fundamentally described by gauge structures. In mathematics, gauge theories arise naturally through fiber bundles, connections, holonomies, and curvature forms, providing a geometric language for describing local symmetries and their dynamics.

Within the Geometric Qubit framework, gauge structure acquires an additional role: it becomes a carrier and processor of information. Information can be encoded not only in geometric states, curvature excitations, or topological sectors, but also in the gauge relationships that connect them. Gauge transformations become computational operations, holonomies become logical gates, and gauge invariants become protected logical observables.

This paper develops the theory of Geometric Gauge Structures (GGS), establishing gauge Hilbert spaces, gauge operators, connection-state encoding, holonomy computation, gauge-entangled states, gauge quantum channels, gauge stabilizers, fault-tolerant gauge computation, and silicon-photonic analog implementations. The resulting framework elevates gauge symmetry from a physical law to a geometry-native information-processing architecture.
