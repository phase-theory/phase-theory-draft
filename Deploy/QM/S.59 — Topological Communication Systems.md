Abstract

Communication systems determine how information remains transferable despite noise, distance, and system evolution.

Classical communication organizes transmission through channels, protocols, addressing, and redundancy. Quantum communication introduces coherence preservation, distributed state preparation, measurement constraints, and entanglement-assisted transfer.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ routing

→ networks

→ repeaters

→ communication channels.

A further question emerges:

If communication becomes organized accessibility—

what remains invariant under transformation?

This paper develops Topological Communication Systems (TCS): a theoretical framework in which communication is represented through preservation of global computational organization rather than dependence on local transmission structure.

Rather than interpreting communication as signal movement through channels, the framework interprets communication as maintenance of persistent connectivity across computational geometry.

The central proposal:

Communication is not movement of signals.

Communication is preservation of reachable organization.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an experimentally validated communication architecture.
