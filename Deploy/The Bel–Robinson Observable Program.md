# Abstract

The Bel–Robinson tensor occupies a unique position within General Relativity. Constructed quadratically from the Weyl curvature tensor, it possesses nearly every mathematical property one would desire from a gravitational energy-momentum tensor: complete symmetry, tracelessness, positivity, causal propagation, and conservation in Einstein spacetimes. Yet despite these remarkable features, it has remained physically uninterpreted for almost seventy years.

This paper develops a new framework—the **Bel–Robinson Observable Program (BROP)**—which promotes the Bel–Robinson tensor from a purely mathematical object to an operationally measurable quantity.

The central proposal is that Bel–Robinson superenergy is not energy itself but the **curvature-information density** carried by spacetime geometry. Gravitational-wave detectors measure this quantity indirectly through second-order tidal observables. We derive:

1. An operational definition of Bel–Robinson superenergy density.
2. A nonlinear gravitational super-Poynting theorem.
3. A physically dimensioned conservation law connecting curvature transport to detector observables.
4. A second-order geodesic-deviation coupling law.
5. An explicit detector response functional.
6. A gravitational-wave flux law measurable by future precision interferometers.

The result provides a direct experimental interpretation of the Bel–Robinson tensor and identifies observables capable of measuring its flux.

# Conclusion

The Bel–Robinson tensor has long been regarded as one of General Relativity's most elegant but physically orphaned structures. Its positivity, symmetry, tracelessness, and vacuum conservation have no parallel among gravitational constructs. Yet it lacked a measurable interpretation.

This white paper proposes that the tensor measures **curvature-information density and flux**, not energy. By deriving a nonlinear Bel–Robinson Poynting theorem, introducing a physically dimensioned conservation law, and demonstrating the coupling of the super-Poynting vector to second-order geodesic deviation, the framework converts superenergy from a mathematical abstraction into an operational observable.
