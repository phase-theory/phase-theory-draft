Information Transport Through Geometry-Encoded Quantum Systems

A Theory of Communication, Evolution, and Information Flow in Geometric Hilbert Spaces

Abstract

Quantum channels provide the operational framework through which quantum states evolve, interact with environments, transmit information, and undergo measurement. In conventional quantum information theory, channels describe the transformation of density matrices through unitary evolution, noise processes, communication media, and open-system dynamics.

Within the Geometric Qubit framework, information is encoded in geometric degrees of freedom including curvature configurations, topological sectors, defect structures, boundary states, connectivity networks, and geometric excitations. Consequently, a generalized theory of quantum channels is required to describe how geometric information propagates, transforms, degrades, and is recovered.

This paper develops the theory of Geometric Quantum Channels (GQC), establishing the mathematical framework governing geometric information transport. We define geometric channel operators, geometric communication pathways, curvature channels, topological channels, defect channels, holographic channels, geometric channel capacities, geometric information flow, and fault-tolerant geometric communication. Geometric Quantum Channels provide the dynamic infrastructure underlying geometric computation, geometric networking, geometric memory systems, and quantum-geometric information processing.

Conclusions

Geometric Quantum Channels generalize quantum-channel theory to systems in which information is encoded within geometric degrees of freedom. Curvature states, topological sectors, defect structures, boundary configurations, and connectivity networks become information carriers transformed by geometric channels.

The framework introduces geometric channel operators, geometric communication pathways, channel capacities, geometric routing, holographic channels, geometric teleportation, and fault-tolerant geometric communication. It establishes the mathematical infrastructure required for geometric networking, geometric computation, geometric memories, and distributed geometric information processing.

Within the Geometric Qubit corpus, Geometric Quantum Channels provide the dynamical layer connecting geometric state theory to practical information transport. Together with Geometric Density Matrices, they complete the operational description of how geometric information evolves, propagates, and is utilized within geometry-based quantum systems.
