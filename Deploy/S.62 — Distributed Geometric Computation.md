Abstract

Computation at scale increasingly depends on distribution.

Classical distributed computing decomposes problems across processors, coordinates execution through messaging and synchronization, and reconstructs results from partial computation. Quantum computing introduces distributed state preparation, communication limits, coherence management, and non-classical correlations.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ circuits

→ routing

→ networks

→ communication

→ internetworking.

A further question emerges:

If information and communication become geometry—

what becomes of computation itself?

This paper develops Distributed Geometric Computation (DGC): a speculative theoretical framework in which computation is represented as coordinated evolution across distributed computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting computation as independent execution across discrete machines, the framework interprets computation as structured transformation of accessibility distributed across computational manifolds.

The central proposal:

Distributed systems do not exchange completed results.

They co-evolve accessible computational structure.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an experimentally validated distributed computing architecture.
