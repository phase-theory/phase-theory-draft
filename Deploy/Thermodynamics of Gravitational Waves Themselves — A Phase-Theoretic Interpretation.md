# Thermodynamics of Gravitational Waves Themselves: A Phase-Theoretic Interpretation

## Abstract

This white paper develops a phase-theoretic foundation for the thermodynamics of gravitational waves as a field, not merely as a carrier of energy. In Phase Theory, the gravitational-wave field is a coherent phase-transport excitation of the underlying substrate, and its thermodynamic description emerges from phase coherence, topological consistency, and coarse-graining over inaccessible microconfigurations. In the Isaacson regime, the wave field admits an effective radiation fluid with \(p=\rho/3\), while its entropy is zero for a pure coherent state and positive only when the state is mixed, decohered, or thermally populated. The result is a closed formalism in which gravitational-wave thermodynamics is an emergent description of phase disorder, not an intrinsic property of a single deterministic waveform.

## 1. Introduction

Phase Theory posits that phase is the fundamental substrate from which spacetime, matter, and fields emerge through global consistency and topological organization. Within that ontology, a gravitational wave is not a thing traveling through spacetime in the usual sense; it is a coherent update of the phase substrate whose long-wavelength, low-strain limit is captured by an emergent metric description. The thermodynamic question is therefore: when does such a phase excitation behave like a gas, carry entropy, or obey an equation of state? The answer is that it does so only after coarse-graining over phase microstates, exactly as in ordinary radiation thermodynamics.

The main conceptual advance here is to treat the GW field as a phase-topological medium with a state space, not as a mere classical waveform. That move makes entropy precise, because entropy counts the number of phase-consistent microconfigurations compatible with a macroscopic wave description. It also clarifies why classical, deterministic waves are low-entropy objects: they are coherent phase configurations, not thermally randomized ensembles.

## 2. Phase Ontology of Gravitational Waves

In Phase Theory, the base ontology is a continuous phase substrate with an emergent metric derived from phase correlators and coherence-limited averaging. A gravitational wave corresponds to a propagating strain in the emergent metric, but at the substrate level it is a coherent phase mode whose dynamics minimize the phase-inconsistency functional. This is important because the GW field is then treated as a dynamical phase medium with an underlying information geometry, rather than as a perturbation on a pre-given spacetime.

The thermodynamic variables are therefore not assigned by fiat. Instead, the energy content is identified with the effective stress-energy of the wave field, while entropy is identified with the logarithm of the number of phase configurations consistent with the macroscopic state. In this picture, thermodynamics is a statement about restricted phase access under finite coherence, which is exactly the kind of restriction that Phase Theory elevates to a foundational axiom.

## 3. Effective Energy Density

The energy of a short-wavelength GW is captured by the Isaacson tensor, which is the standard effective stress-energy in the high-frequency limit. In the phase-theoretic reading, this tensor is the coarse-grained energy flux of the phase mode after averaging over scales smaller than the background curvature scale. The resulting energy density is quadratic in the wave amplitude, so it behaves like the energy density of any radiation field.

Define
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{GW}} = \langle T^{\mathrm{GW}}_{00}\rangle,
\]
with \(T^{\mathrm{GW}}_{\mu\nu}\) the Isaacson effective tensor in the TT gauge. Then the total GW energy in a closed volume \(V\) is
\[
U_{\mathrm{GW}} = \int_V \rho_{\mathrm{GW}}\, d^3x.
\]
In Phase Theory, this quantity is interpreted as the phase-inconsistency energy stored in coherent propagation across the emergent geometry. The same object becomes thermodynamic energy once the wave is treated as a member of an ensemble rather than as an isolated coherent excitation.

## 4. Temperature from Phase Fluctuations

Temperature in Phase Theory is not attached to a single waveform in isolation; it is a measure of the intensity of phase fluctuations in a subsystem. For a gravitational-wave bath, temperature becomes meaningful when the field is described by a density operator over many graviton-like modes, or when the phase coherence is limited by environmental coupling. In that case, the standard canonical relation[7][1]
\[
\rho = Z^{-1} e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{GW}}}
\]
becomes appropriate, with \(H_{\mathrm{GW}}\) the effective Hamiltonian constructed from the coarse-grained wave energy.

The phase-theoretic meaning of \(T\) is therefore operational: it measures the spread of accessible phase configurations. High temperature corresponds to large occupation of incoherent or weakly correlated phase modes; low temperature corresponds to near-pure coherent propagation. This is compatible with the usual radiation thermodynamics of massless bosons, but it is more fundamental in the present framework because it connects temperature directly to phase disorder.

## 5. Entropy of a GW State

Entropy is the key quantity that Phase Theory makes genuinely state-dependent. The entropy of a gravitational-wave subsystem \(A\) is
\[
S_A = k_B \ln \Omega_A,
\]
where \(\Omega_A\) is the number of phase configurations compatible with the macroscopic constraints on \(A\). Equivalently, for a quantum state \(\rho\),[1]
\[
S = -k_B \mathrm{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho).
\]
These are the same entropy in different limits: the first is the phase-topological counting form, the second is the density-matrix form.

For a pure coherent GW state, \(S=0\) in the von Neumann sense, which is exactly the statement used in the gravitational-wave literature for classical merger waveforms modeled as coherent states. This does not mean the wave has no physical significance; it means it has maximal phase order. A coherent waveform is energetic, structured, and causal, but not thermodynamically disordered. Nonzero GW entropy arises only when the field is mixed, decohered, or statistically populated across many phase sectors.

## 6. Equation of State

For an isotropic bath of massless gravitational excitations, the equation of state is
\[
p_{\mathrm{GW}} = \frac{1}{3}\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}.
\]
This follows from the tracelessness of the effective radiation stress-energy and from directional averaging of null propagation. In phase-theoretic terms, the GW bath behaves like a massless phase-radiation fluid in the emergent geometry. The equation of state is therefore identical to that of blackbody radiation at leading order.

A useful thermodynamic identity follows immediately:
\[
U = 3pV.
\]
Combined with the first law,
\[
dU = T\,dS - p\,dV,
\]
one obtains the standard scaling laws for a massless bosonic gas, namely \(\rho \propto T^4\), \(p \propto T^4\), and \(S \propto VT^3\) in equilibrium. Phase Theory interprets these as macroscopic consequences of phase-state counting under finite coherence.

## 7. Phase-Theoretic Derivation

The derivation begins by identifying the gravitational-wave field with a coherent phase mode of the substrate, and then writing the coarse-grained Hamiltonian \(H_{\mathrm{GW}}\) from the Isaacson energy. The canonical partition function is
\[
Z = \mathrm{Tr}\, e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{GW}}},
\]
so the free energy is
\[
F = -k_B T \ln Z.
\]
From this, the entropy and pressure follow by standard thermodynamic differentiation. Because the field quanta are massless and propagate isotropically in the averaged limit, the thermodynamics reproduces the radiation result \(p=\rho/3\).

Phase Theory adds one conceptual layer: the partition function is not merely counting graviton occupation numbers, but counting allowed phase configurations consistent with global coherence and topological constraints. That is the key interpretive upgrade over ordinary bosonic thermodynamics. The “microstates” are phase-topological states, and the macroscopic observables are their coarse-grained projections.

## 8. Coherent and Mixed States

The cleanest physical distinction is between a coherent GW and a thermal or stochastic GW background. A coherent state is a pure state, and therefore has zero entropy unless one traces over degrees of freedom. This is why a deterministic inspiral waveform is not itself a thermal object, even though it carries energy through spacetime.

A mixed background, by contrast, is what one expects from a stochastic gravitational-wave background, unresolved source populations, or environmental decoherence. In that case the density matrix is not pure, and the entropy measures the number of accessible phase sectors after coarse-graining. Phase Theory interprets this as loss of coherence in the substrate, not as a property of a waveform abstracted from all context.

## 9. Relation to Phase Theory

This gravitational-wave thermodynamics is not an add-on to Phase Theory; it is a direct corollary of its axioms. The coherence limitation axiom supplies the finite entropy bound, the metric-emergence axiom supplies the effective spacetime description, and the global consistency principle supplies the phase-energy functional. Together they imply that any coherent GW state must be representable as an emergent low-entropy phase configuration, while any thermal GW bath must be a mixed state in the same phase substrate.

This also explains why gravitational-wave thermodynamics is structurally compatible with black-hole thermodynamics but not reducible to it. Black-hole entropy is boundary-based and holographic in Phase Theory, whereas GW entropy is field-state-based and volumetric after coarse-graining. They are both manifestations of phase-state counting, but in different physical regimes and with different effective observables.

## 10. Limits and Predictions

The formalism is valid in the weak-field, short-wavelength, or coarse-grained regime where the Isaacson approximation applies. It should not be overextended into strongly nonlinear or fully nonperturbative spacetime regions without additional dynamical input. Within its domain, the framework predicts that a truly coherent astrophysical GW signal is entropy-poor, while a sufficiently mixed or stochastic background carries a large bosonic entropy.

A second prediction concerns deviations from exact thermality in non-equilibrium graviton ensembles. If phase coherence remains partially preserved, the observed spectrum should deviate from the ideal Planck form in ways that reflect residual phase correlations. In Phase Theory language, the departure from equilibrium is literally a departure from perfect global phase consistency.

## 11. Conclusion

The phase-theoretic interpretation of gravitational-wave thermodynamics yields a coherent and formal picture: gravitational waves are phase-excitations of the emergent geometry, their effective energy is encoded by the Isaacson tensor, their equation of state is radiation-like, and their entropy is state-dependent rather than waveform-intrinsic. A pure GW coherent state has zero thermodynamic entropy in the standard sense, while mixed or thermal graviton ensembles have positive entropy given by the usual Bose statistics or density-matrix entropy. The crucial conceptual point is that the wave does not “carry entropy” just because it carries energy; entropy arises when phase access is restricted by coarse-graining, decoherence, or thermalization.’
