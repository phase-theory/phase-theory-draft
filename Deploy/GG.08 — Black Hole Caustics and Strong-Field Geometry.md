Abstract

Parts I–VII developed a Lorentzian catastrophe theory for geodesic congruences and established that generic spacetime caustics belong to a finite Einstein–Thom spectrum.

This paper applies the framework to strong-field General Relativity.

Black holes provide the extreme regime in which geodesic focusing, curvature anisotropy, and causal compression become dominant. We construct a catastrophe theory of strong-field optical and timelike transport and classify caustic structures generated by photon regions and near-horizon geometry.

We derive:

1. strong-field exponential geometry,
2. catastrophe transport in stationary black holes,
3. Kerr caustic structure,
4. horizon-adjacent singular transitions,
5. universality classes of black-hole caustics.

The principal result is the Horizon Catastrophe Theorem: generic horizon-scale geodesic singularities remain restricted to the Lorentzian ADE spectrum but organize into characteristic strong-field sequences.
