Abstract

Quantum computation traditionally represents information through states evolving over continuous mathematical spaces.

Many approaches to fundamental physics, however, explore whether continuity itself may emerge from more primitive discrete organization. Examples in existing research include lattice approaches, network descriptions, causal structures, and discretized geometrical models.

Across the Structural Sector, information evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ computation

→ security

→ memory

→ preservation

→ scalable reconstruction

→ informational geometry.

A further question emerges:

If information becomes geometric—

how should qubits be represented when the underlying organizational substrate is discrete?

This paper develops Geometric Qubits on Discrete Spacetime (GQDS): a speculative theoretical framework in which qubit organization is represented over discrete computational geometry rather than continuous accessibility manifolds.

This framework does not claim that physical spacetime is discrete.

It instead studies a conceptual computational setting in which discrete geometric organization provides the language for information representation.

The central proposal:

A qubit need not be represented only as a point in continuous state space.

It may be represented as structured accessibility across discrete geometry.

This document presents speculative conceptual research rather than a physical model of spacetime.
