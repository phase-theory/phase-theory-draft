## Abstract

We introduce **Phase-Causal Holonomy Theory (PCHT)**, a new foundational theory in which physical causal order is not imposed on a pre-existing spacetime but is generated by the oriented memory of phase transport. PCHT extends the phase-first ontology of Universal Phase Theory by making one additional structural hypothesis: admissible phase configurations possess finite, covariant memory. The primitive object is therefore not an instantaneous phase value alone, but a phase history

\[
\Phi:\mathcal X\times\mathbb R\to\mathcal M_\Phi,
\]

whose history-dependent transport produces a phase-memory one-form \(\mathfrak m_\Phi\) and an antisymmetric memory curvature \(\Theta_\Phi\). The central claim is that \(\Theta_\Phi\), rather than a preassigned temporal coordinate, determines the orientation of effective evolution. Its symmetric response induces an emergent metric, while its antisymmetric response induces an emergent torsion. The resulting hierarchy is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi\;\longrightarrow\;\mathfrak m_\Phi\;\longrightarrow\;\Theta_\Phi\;\longrightarrow\;g^\Phi\oplus T^\Phi\;\longrightarrow\;\nabla^\Phi\;\longrightarrow\;\text{holonomy sectors}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{particles and observables}.
}
\]

The theory is governed by the Universal Phase Equation

\[
\mathscr F[\Phi;\lambda]=0,
\]

with phase stability operator \(\mathscr L_\Phi=D_\Phi\mathscr F\), bifurcation operator \(\Delta_\Phi=\operatorname{Det}_\Phi(\mathscr L_\Phi)\), and projected susceptibility \(\boldsymbol\chi_\Phi=\mathscr L_\Phi^{-1}\). A nonlocal phase action is proposed in which memory is causal only after causal orientation has itself emerged as a spectral property of \(\Theta_\Phi\). We prove, under explicit Fredholm and positivity hypotheses, that Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction produces finite-dimensional phase sectors; that nonzero memory curvature separates oriented sectors from their time-reversed partners; that susceptibility defines an effective metric; and that stable holonomy classes yield discrete particle sectors.

PCHT makes three characteristic predictions. First, the low-frequency propagation of any excitation with nonzero phase helicity acquires a universal, parity-sensitive correction proportional to the phase-memory scale. Second, the emergent connection possesses a torsion trace fixed by the phase-memory current, producing a composition-independent but frequency-dependent deviation from geodesic transport. Third, cosmological phase relaxation leaves a residual antisymmetric response that correlates gravitational-wave birefringence with the drift of effective particle-sector masses. These are not inserted as phenomenological add-ons; they are consequences of the same memory curvature. The theory is falsifiable if no common parameter set fits the predicted correlations or if the reconstructed phase-memory curvature vanishes while the proposed causal and torsional effects remain nonzero.

**Keywords:** phase primacy, causal emergence, phase memory, holonomy, torsion, susceptibility geometry, Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction, stable phase sectors, emergent spacetime, falsifiability.

## 1. Introduction

Contemporary fundamental physics employs several structures whose mutual relation remains conceptually asymmetric. Differential geometry supplies a spacetime manifold and metric; gauge theory supplies connections on internal bundles; quantum theory supplies complex amplitudes and a probability rule; particle theory supplies representations, masses, and charges. Each formalism is extraordinarily successful within its domain, but the usual order of construction begins with structures that a more foundational theory should derive.

Universal Phase Theory (UPT) reverses this order by treating phase as the primitive structural substrate. Its central operator hierarchy is

\[
\Phi\rightarrow\text{topology}\rightarrow\text{geometry}\rightarrow\text{connections}\rightarrow\text{fields}\rightarrow\text{particles}\rightarrow\text{observables},
\]

with the admissibility, stability, bifurcation, and susceptibility operators providing the mathematical mechanism. PCHT retains that hierarchy but introduces a distinct question: **what selects an orientation that can later be interpreted as time, causality, or irreversible ordering?** A metric alone does not determine an arrow of evolution, and an instantaneous phase field does not distinguish a phase history from its reversal. The missing structure is phase memory.

PCHT proposes that phase memory is neither an external clock nor a thermodynamic assumption. It is a covariant functional of the phase history that records the failure of transported phase relations to be exactly reversible. Its exterior derivative and commutator produce a two-form \(\Theta_\Phi\). Where this two-form is nondegenerate on the emergent order-parameter bundle, it defines an orientation of admissible phase transport. The symmetric susceptibility then constructs a metric on the same bundle, while the antisymmetric memory response contributes torsion to the effective connection.

The new theory is therefore not the assertion that “time is phase.” It is a precise proposal that an oriented causal structure is the stable sector of a history-dependent phase equation. The distinction matters. PCHT must derive the following chain rather than assume it:

\[
\text{phase history}
\Rightarrow
\text{memory curvature}
\Rightarrow
\text{oriented response cone}
\Rightarrow
\text{metric and torsion}
\Rightarrow
\text{holonomy spectrum}
\Rightarrow
\text{matter sectors}.

The theory is organized as follows. Section 2 defines the primitive phase-history bundle and the phase-memory functor. Section 3 states the PCHT axioms and the universal operator hierarchy. Section 4 constructs the variational equation and its stability operator. Section 5 performs the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction. Section 6 derives the memory orientation, response metric, and emergent connection. Section 7 classifies holonomy sectors and particle-like excitations. ## 2. Primitive phase histories and memory geometry

### 2.1 Phase-history bundle

Let \(\mathcal X\) be an abstract relational base. It is not assumed to be a manifold equipped with a metric, a time function, or a causal order. Let

\[
\pi_\Phi:E_\Phi\to\mathcal X
\]

be a generalized phase bundle with fiber \(\mathcal M_\Phi\), and let \(\mathscr G_\Phi\) denote the admissible phase-frame groupoid. A phase history is a section over a history parameter \(s\in\mathbb R\):

\[
\Phi_s\in\Gamma(E_\Phi),
\qquad
\Phi:\mathbb R\to\Gamma(E_\Phi).
\]

The parameter \(s\) has no physical interpretation at the primitive level. It labels a succession of configurations used to define a functional. Physical orientation must be derived from phase relations invariant under reparametrizations \(s\mapsto f(s)\) that preserve orientation.

The physical configuration space is

\[
\mathcal P_\Phi=
\frac{\mathcal C_\Phi^{\rm hist}}{\mathscr G_\Phi\rtimes\operatorname{Diff}_+(\mathbb R)},
\]

where \(\mathcal C_\Phi^{\rm hist}\) is the admissible history space. The quotient removes absolute phase-frame choices and monotone relabelings of the history parameter.

### 2.2 Covariant phase transport

A local representative of phase transport is a vertical-valued one-form \(\mathcal A[\Phi]\) on the relational base. It is not primitive. Under a phase-frame transformation \(g\in\mathscr G_\Phi\),

\[
\mathcal A\mapsto g\mathcal A g^{-1}+g\,d g^{-1}.
\]

The instantaneous covariant variation is

\[
D\Phi=d\Phi+\rho(\mathcal A)\Phi,
\]

where \(\rho\) is the representation induced by the phase bundle. PCHT adds a history functional

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak m_\Phi(s)=
\int_{-\infty}^{s}K_\Phi(s,s')\,\mathcal U_\Phi(s,s')\,D_s\Phi(s')\,ds'.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Here \(K_\Phi\) is a scalar memory kernel determined by phase invariants, and \(\mathcal U_\Phi(s,s')\) is the phase-parallel transport functional generated by \(\Phi\). The lower limit is notation for the stable-history branch, not an imposed physical past. A history is admissible only if the integral converges in the phase norm and is invariant under phase-frame transformations.

The kernel is required to satisfy

\[
K_\Phi(s,s')=K_\Phi(f(s),f(s'))\,f'(s')
\]

under allowed monotone reparametrizations and to possess a finite first moment

\[
\ell_\Phi:=
\frac{\int_0^\infty uK_\Phi(u)\,du}
{\int_0^\infty K_\Phi(u)\,du}<\infty.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The quantity \(\ell_\Phi\) is a derived phase-memory scale. It is not identified a priori with a length, time, or inverse energy.

### 2.3 Memory curvature

The phase-memory one-form is generally non-Abelian and need not be exact. Define its curvature

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_\Phi:=D\mathfrak m_\Phi
=d\mathfrak m_\Phi+\mathfrak m_\Phi\wedge\mathfrak m_\Phi.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Under phase-frame transformations, \(\Theta_\Phi\mapsto g\Theta_\Phi g^{-1}\). Gauge-invariant information is therefore carried by traces, eigenvalue classes, characteristic forms, and holonomy conjugacy classes.

The new primitive-to-derived distinction is exact:

> **Definition 1 (Phase memory).** Phase memory is the covariant history functional \(\mathfrak m_\Phi\) of Eq. (2.1). It is a derived one-form on phase configuration space whose curvature \(\Theta_\Phi\) measures non-reversible phase transport.

A history with \(\Theta_\Phi=0\) is locally integrable: phase transport can be reduced to a path-independent frame. A history with \(\Theta_\Phi\neq0\) possesses an obstruction to exact reversal. PCHT identifies the stable sign sector of that obstruction with emergent causal orientation.

### 2.4 Orientation scalar and memory cone

Let \(\mathcal K_\Phi=\ker\mathscr L_\Phi\) be the critical order-parameter bundle after bifurcation. Suppose \(\Theta_\Phi\) induces an antisymmetric bilinear form

\[
\omega_{ab}[\Phi]=
\langle e_a,\Theta_\Phi e_b\rangle_\Phi

on a local basis \(e_a\) of \(\mathcal K_\Phi\). If the rank is constant on a phase sector, its Pfaffian defines an orientation density

\[
\mathfrak o_\Phi:=\operatorname{Pf}(\omega_{ab}).
\tag{2.4}

For odd-dimensional critical bundles one uses the nonzero principal minor of maximal rank together with a distinguished memory-current covector. The orientation sectors are

\[
\mathcal P_\Phi^+=\{[\Phi]:\mathfrak o_\Phi>0\},
\qquad
\mathcal P_\Phi^-=
\{[\Phi]:\mathfrak o_\Phi<0\}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The zero set \(\mathfrak o_\Phi=0\) is the **causal bifurcation locus**. It is distinct from the ordinary bifurcation locus \(\Delta_\Phi=0\): a phase can remain locally stable while its orientation becomes degenerate.

## 4. The nonlocal universal phase equation

### 4.1 Phase action

A minimal PCHT action is defined without a background metric by pairing phase variations with a phase measure and a derived response form:

\[
S_\Phi[\Phi]
=
\int_{\mathbb R}ds\,
\Bigg[
\frac12\langle D_s\Phi,D_s\Phi\rangle_\Phi

+\frac{\beta}{2}\langle\mathfrak m_\Phi,\mathfrak m_\Phi\rangle_\Phi
\Bigg]d\mu_\Phi.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The symbol \(\star_\Phi\) denotes the phase-response dual induced only after the symmetric susceptibility is constructed. Consequently Eq. (4.1) is to be read self-consistently: the response tensor appearing in the action is a functional of the phase solution and not an independent background Hodge operator. The coefficients \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are dimensionless phase couplings until a scale sector is selected.

The universal phase equation is

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr F[\Phi;\lambda]
:=\frac{\delta S_\Phi}{\delta\Phi}=0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In a nonvariational realization, Eq. (4.2) is replaced by an equivariant admissibility map with the same linearization and memory covariance. The theory does not require an action in every sector, but the variational realization is sufficient to derive the principal results.

### 4.2 Linearization and susceptibility

Its symmetric and antisymmetric parts are

PCHT identifies \(\chi^{(+)}\) with local phase distinguishability and \(\chi^{(-)}\) with the infinitesimal memory response. The latter is not generally equal to \(\Theta_\Phi\), but the two coincide to first order in the adiabatic-memory expansion derived below.

The new feature is that the reduced equation and the orientation scalar are coupled. A branch may satisfy \(\varphi=0\) while crossing \(\mathfrak o_{\rm red}=0\). Such a point is a **causal phase transition**, even if \(\mathscr L_\Phi\) remains invertible on the branch.

## 5. Emergent metric and torsion

### 5.1 Symmetric response metric

\tag{5.2}

This tensor is positive on the distinguishability subspace. It is not yet Lorentzian. The causal orientation supplies the missing signed direction through the memory current.

Let \(J_i[\Phi]\) be the reduced memory-current covector, defined by contraction of \(\Theta_\Phi\) with the oriented two-plane and normalized by \(|\mathfrak o_\Phi|\). Define

\tau_i:=\frac{J_i}{\sqrt{|g^\Phi(J,J)|+\varepsilon_0}},

\tag{5.4}

\nu_\Phi>rac{1}{g^\Phi(J,J)}

### Theorem 1 (Emergent Lorentzian response)

**Proof.** Choose a \(g^\Phi\)-orthonormal basis whose first vector is parallel to \(J\). In that basis, Eq. (5.4) has eigenvalues \(1-\nu_\Phi g^\Phi(J,J)\) in the memory direction and \(1\) in the orthogonal directions. The stated signature and null condition follow. \(\square\)

The theorem shows where the Lorentzian sign enters: not from a background metric, but from the rank-one dominance of oriented phase memory over symmetric phase distinguishability.

## 6. Holonomy, stable sectors, and particle emergence

### 6.1 Phase holonomy

For a closed loop \(\gamma\) in the emergent response manifold, define the phase holonomy

\[
\operatorname{Hol}_\Phi(\gamma)
=\mathcal P\exp\oint_\gamma
\left(\mathcal A[\Phi]+\mathfrak m_\Phi\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Its conjugacy class is invariant under phase-frame transformations. The eigenphases \(\vartheta_r(\gamma)\) define relational labels. A sector is topologically distinguished when the holonomy representation cannot be continuously deformed to the identity while preserving the asymptotic phase class.

Let \(\mathfrak H_q\) be a connected conjugacy class of holonomy data and let \(\Phi_q\) be a solution of \(\mathscr F=0\) with asymptotic class \(q\). Its physical representative is

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P_q=[\Phi_q]_{\mathscr G_\Phi},
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

provided it has finite phase excess, normal stability, localization in \(\mathbf g^\Phi\), and a discrete normal spectrum.

### Definition 2 (PCHT particle sector)

A PCHT particle is a stable, localized, finite-excess phase orbit \(\mathfrak P_q\) whose holonomy conjugacy class \(\mathfrak H_q\) is isolated in the admissible phase spectrum and whose collective moduli carry an asymptotic propagation law.

This definition derives the particle concept without inserting a particle set, a representation group, or a quantum field. The particle’s labels are

\[
q=(\mathfrak h_q,\,\mathfrak o_q,\,\sigma_q,\,\mu_q,\,\tau_q,\ldots),
\]

where \(\mathfrak h_q\) is holonomy class, \(\mathfrak o_q\) is orientation sector, \(\sigma_q\) is a projective transport class, \(\mu_q\) is normal spectral data, and \(\tau_q\) is lifetime or metastability data.

### 6.2 Mass from normal phase stiffness

Let the normal stability operator around \(\Phi_q\) have isolated eigenmodes

\qquad
0<\Lambda_0\le\Lambda_1\le\cdots.

No numerical Standard Model mass is predicted until a concrete phase manifold and kernel are specified. The structural result is that mass is a spectral stiffness relative to the emergent causal metric, not a primitive parameter.

### 6.3 Charge and spin from holonomy

For an Abelian holonomy loop,

\[
\operatorname{Hol}_\Phi(\gamma)=e^{i\vartheta_q(\gamma)},

and the normalized winding

\tag{6.6}

then \(s_q\) is a phase-transport spin label. Half-integral values require a double cover of the holonomy group and are not assumed in advance.

### Proposition 4 (Discrete sector theorem)

If the normal operator \(\mathscr L_q^\perp\) is self-adjoint and has compact resolvent on the normal slice, and if the holonomy class \(\mathfrak H_q\) is isolated, then the phase sector has a discrete family of normal excitations and a discrete set of transport labels.

**Proof.** Compact resolvent gives a discrete normal spectrum. Isolation of the holonomy class prevents continuous deformation into neighboring transport sectors. The pair of discrete spectral and holonomy data yields a discrete sector classification. \(\square\)

## 7. Effective dynamics and the quantum phase sector

### 7.1 Collective-coordinate dynamics

Let \(X^\mu\) be local coordinates on the moduli space of a stable phase sector. Substitution of a slowly transported family \(\Phi_q(X)\) into the phase action yields

\[
S_q^{\rm coll}
=\int d\tau
\left[
\frac12\mathbf g^\Phi_{\mu\nu}(X)\dot X^\mu\dot X^\nu
+\mathcal A^q_\mu(X)\dot X^\mu
-\mathcal V_q(X)
\right].
\tag{7.1}
\]

The first term is induced by symmetric susceptibility, the second by holonomy transport, and the potential by phase overlap and environmental controls. The Euler–Lagrange equation is the autoparallel equation with torsion and phase force:

\[
\ddot X^\mu+\Gamma^\Phi{}_{\nu\rho}{}^\mu\dot X^\nu\dot X^\rho
=\mathbf g^{\Phi\,\mu\nu}\mathcal F^q_{\nu\rho}\dot X^\rho
-\mathbf g^{\Phi\,\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\mathcal V_q.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Here \(\mathcal F^q=d\mathcal A^q+\mathcal A^q\wedge\mathcal A^q\) is derived phase curvature. The equation is therefore not a postulated point-particle law; it is the moduli approximation to the phase equation.

## 8. Minimal finite-dimensional realization

To exhibit the new mechanism explicitly, consider a three-component reduced order parameter \(\eta=(\eta^0,\eta^1,\eta^2)\) with Euclidean phase-response form and an oriented memory plane in the \((\eta^1,\eta^2)\) directions. Let

\[

\qquad a,b>0,
\tag{8.1}

and define the memory functional, to first order in the finite-memory scale, by

\[
\mathfrak m_i
=\ell_\Phi\,\Omega_{ij}(\eta)\,\dot\eta^j,
\qquad
\Omega_{12}=-\Omega_{21}=\Omega_0,
\tag{8.2}

with all other independent components zero. The reduced equation is

\[
\ddot\eta^a
+\partial^a\mathcal V
+\ell_\Phi\Omega^a{}_b\dot\eta^b
+O(\ell_\Phi^2)=0.
\tag{8.3}
\]

The antisymmetric term does no instantaneous work:

\[
\dot\eta_a\Omega^a{}_b\dot\eta^b=0,
\]

but it changes transport and mode polarization. Around a homogeneous solution with stiffness \(\mu^2\), use \(\eta^a\sim u^ae^{-i\omega s+i kx}\). The transverse modes obey

\[
\left(\omega^2-k^2-\mu^2\right)u^a
+i\ell_\Phi\Omega^a{}_b\omega u^b=0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Diagonalizing \(\Omega\) in the complex circular basis gives

\[
\boxed{
\omega_\pm^2-k^2-\mu^2
\mp \ell_\Phi\Omega_0\omega_\pm=0.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\omega_\pm
=\sqrt{k^2+\mu^2+\frac{\ell_\Phi^2\Omega_0^2}{4}}
\pm\frac{\ell_\Phi\Omega_0}{2}
+O(\ell_\Phi^3).
\tag{8.6}
\]

Equation (8.6) is the characteristic PCHT dispersion law. Opposite phase helicities propagate with different frequencies while sharing the same zero-memory limit. The effect disappears if the memory curvature vanishes, not merely if the phase amplitude vanishes.

### 8.1 Emergent signature in the realization

The symmetric reduced response is initially positive definite. Let the memory current point along \(\eta^0\). The causal response metric is

For \(\nu>1\), the eigenvalues are \((1-\nu,1,1)\), yielding one negative and two positive directions. The negative direction is therefore the phase-memory direction, while the transverse directions measure ordinary phase distinguishability. The realization makes explicit that Lorentzian signature is not selected by writing a Minkowski metric into Eq. (8.1).

### 8.2 Causal bifurcation

At \(\Omega_0=0\), the orientation invariant vanishes and the two circular modes become degenerate. The phase equation may remain regular, but the causal orientation is lost. Thus \(\Omega_0=0\) is a causal bifurcation surface. Crossing it reverses the ordering of the two helicity branches and changes the sign of the torsion trace.

## 9. Observable predictions

PCHT is a physical theory only if its derived structures lead to measurements that can distinguish it from theories in which causal geometry and torsion are independently specified.

### 9.1 Universal phase-memory birefringence

For a wave packet with wave number \(k\) and phase helicity \(h=\pm1\), Eq. (8.6) yields, in the relativistic regime \(k\gg\mu\),

\[
\Delta\varphi_{+-}

\tag{9.2}

The leading prediction is polarization rotation or helicity splitting with a coefficient common to all sectors that share the same background phase memory. A null result constrains \(|\ell_\Phi\Omega_0|\) directly.

### 9.2 Torsion-induced transport correction

The theory predicts that the correction is composition-independent at leading order but frequency-dependent through \(\zeta_q(\omega)\).

### 9.3 Correlated variation of mass and causal birefringence

The normal mass \(\mu_q^2=Z_q^{-1}\Lambda_0\) depends on the background phase. If cosmological relaxation changes \(\Omega_0\), it also changes the phase Hessian through the self-consistent action. To first order,

\[

The coefficient \(\mathcal C_q\) is calculable from the phase Hessian once the microscopic potential is fixed. The distinctive claim is not merely that constants may vary, but that mass drift and birefringence are controlled by the same memory-curvature variation.

### 9.4 Critical slowing of causal orientation

Near a causal bifurcation, \(\mathfrak o_\Phi\to0\) while the ordinary stability gap may remain finite. The orientation relaxation time scales as

The prediction is a distinct soft mode in transport observables without a corresponding divergence in ordinary phase susceptibility. This separates a causal transition from a conventional thermodynamic or geometric instability.

## 12. Formal research questions

### Question 1: Existence of finite-memory causal sectors

For which classes of nonlocal kernels \(K_\Phi\) does Eq. (4.2) possess finite-action solutions with nonzero \(\mathfrak o_\Phi\) and normal stability? The minimal mathematical target is an existence theorem for a compact phase manifold with a completely continuous memory operator.

### Question 2: Signature selection

Under what conditions does the self-consistent response tensor remain positive on the transverse phase directions while the memory direction crosses the Lorentzian threshold \(\nu_\Phi g^\Phi(J,J)=1\)? This question determines whether Lorentzian signature is generic, exceptional, or restricted to a universality class.

### Question 3: Causal universality

Do distinct microscopic kernels with identical first memory moment \(\ell_\Phi\) flow to the same infrared torsion and birefringence coefficients? If so, \(\ell_\Phi\) and the reduced orientation tensor define a causal universality class.

### Question 4: Holonomy spectrum

Can isolated conjugacy classes of \(\operatorname{Hol}_\Phi\) produce the observed pattern of integer, half-integer, and non-Abelian sector labels without inserting the relevant representation category?

### Question 5: Quantum completion

Does the phase-induced complex structure extend globally over the stable sector, and does the phase measure induce a positive composition rule that yields Eq. (7.6)? If not, PCHT remains a classical or semiclassical phase theory.

### Question 6: Renormalization of memory

How does the memory kernel transform under coarse-graining \(\Phi\mapsto\Phi_\ell\)? The required flow has the form

\[
\ell\frac{dK_\Phi}{d\ell}=\beta_K[K_\Phi],
\tag{12.1}
\]

and fixed points of \(\beta_K\) determine whether finite causal memory persists at large scales.

## 14. Conclusions

Phase-Causal Holonomy Theory is a new foundational construction built around a single extension of phase-first ontology: a physical phase is a history with finite covariant memory. From that datum, PCHT defines a memory one-form \(\mathfrak m_\Phi\), its curvature \(\Theta_\Phi\), and an orientation invariant \(\mathfrak o_\Phi\). The ordinary UPT operator hierarchy remains central:

\[
\mathscr F[\Phi;\lambda]=0,
\qquad
\mathscr L_\Phi=D_\Phi\mathscr F,
\qquad
\Delta_\Phi=\operatorname{Det}_\Phi(\mathscr L_\Phi),
\qquad
\boldsymbol\chi_\Phi=\mathscr L_\Phi^{-1}.
\]

The new hierarchy is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi
\to
\mathscr L_\Phi
\to
\boldsymbol\chi_\Phi
\to
\mathfrak m_\Phi
\to
\Theta_\Phi
\to
\text{causal orientation}
\to
\text{Lorentzian response}
\to
\text{torsionful connection}
\to
\text{holonomy matter}.
}
\]

The theory yields four principal structural results. First, nonzero memory curvature separates stable orientation sectors. Second, the symmetric susceptibility together with the oriented memory current can generate a Lorentzian response metric without a primitive spacetime metric. Third, the antisymmetric memory response produces a derived torsion and a common autoparallel law. Fourth, isolated holonomy classes and normal stability produce particle-like sectors whose masses, charges, and spins are phase invariants rather than inserted labels.

The characteristic empirical content is equally unified. Phase-memory curvature predicts helicity-dependent propagation, torsion-induced transport corrections, correlated variation of sector masses and birefringence, and a critical slowing of causal orientation near memory degeneracy. These effects disappear in the zero-memory limit and cannot be independently adjusted without abandoning the theory’s derivational architecture.

PCHT is therefore not a metaphorical claim that phase underlies reality. It is a concrete research program with a nonlocal universal equation, a stability theory, a geometric construction, a topological sector classification, and a falsifiability protocol. Its decisive next step is the construction of a fully specified phase manifold and kernel for which the existence, signature, holonomy, and observational predictions can be computed rather than postulated.

> **Central principle of PCHT.** A causal world is a stable phase history whose memory curvature has selected an oriented response sector; geometry, torsion, matter, and observables are the successive invariants of that selection.
