Abstract

Reversible computation studies computational processes whose logical evolution preserves recoverability of prior states. Classical reversible computing and quantum unitary evolution both suggest that information-preserving dynamics can reduce logical irreversibility and clarify the relationship between computation and physical laws.

Geometric approaches developed throughout the Structural Sector reinterpret information as organization in state space.

This paper develops Geometric Reversible Computing (GRC): a theoretical framework in which computation is represented as invertible evolution through structured computational geometry.

Rather than viewing reversibility as merely recovering previous values, this framework interprets reversibility as preservation of geometric accessibility.

The central proposal:

Computation is reversible when geometry remains reconstructible.

Logical evolution becomes constrained motion across information manifolds where admissible transformations preserve recoverability.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally established computing architecture.
