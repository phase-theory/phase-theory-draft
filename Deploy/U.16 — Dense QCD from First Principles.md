# Abstract

Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) is the fundamental quantum field theory of the strong interaction. At zero baryon density and high energies, QCD is remarkably successful. Lattice calculations, perturbative methods, effective field theories, and experimental measurements collectively support its validity across a wide range of scales.

At finite baryon density, however, QCD enters a largely unexplored regime. Nuclear matter, neutron-star interiors, quarkyonic matter, color superconductivity, and possible deconfined quark phases remain difficult to describe from first principles. The finite-density sign problem prevents direct lattice simulations in the most physically interesting regions of the QCD phase diagram.

Unified Finite-Density Quantum Field Theory (UFD-QFT) proposes that the obstacle is not the absence of a fundamental theory. QCD already contains the necessary microscopic physics. The missing ingredient is a geometric framework capable of organizing finite-density quantum interference, thermodynamic structure, topological transitions, information geometry, and computational complexity.

This document embeds QCD into the UFD-QFT framework by reformulating dense QCD as a geometric theory on density space. The conventional QCD phase diagram emerges as a projection of a higher-dimensional geometric structure whose dynamics are governed by density geometry, statistical gauge curvature, renormalization flows, and information organization.
