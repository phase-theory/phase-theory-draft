# Abstract

The interior of neutron stars contains matter at densities inaccessible to terrestrial experiments. While Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) determines the microscopic structure of dense matter, direct observation of these regimes remains impossible. Gravity therefore serves as the primary observational window into the finite-density QCD landscape.

Recent advances in multimessenger astronomy—including pulsar timing, X-ray radius measurements, gravitational-wave observations, and neutron-star merger events—have transformed dense-matter physics into an observational science. Measurements of masses, radii, tidal deformabilities, oscillation spectra, and merger remnants now provide empirical constraints on the equation of state of matter at extreme density.

Gravitational measurements do not merely constrain equations of state; they probe the underlying thermodynamic geometry, attractor structure, topological organization, and phase transitions embedded within finite-density quantum field theory.

This document develops a geometric framework in which gravitational observables become measurable signatures of the density manifold itself.
