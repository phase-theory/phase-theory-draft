# Abstract

Neutron stars are the densest long-lived objects in the observable universe. Born from core-collapse supernovae and stabilized by quantum pressure, they compress matter beyond nuclear density and provide the only known astrophysical laboratories capable of probing large regions of the finite-density Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) phase diagram.

Traditional neutron-star models describe stellar interiors using equations of state, hydrostatic equilibrium, transport theory, and nuclear microphysics. While enormously successful, these approaches treat the star as a collection of local thermodynamic states rather than as a geometric realization of the finite-density landscape itself.

Unified Finite-Density Quantum Field Theory (UFD-QFT) proposes a new interpretation. A neutron star is not merely composed of dense matter. It is a physical embedding of the density manifold. Radial position within the star corresponds to a trajectory through density space, crossing attractor basins, phase boundaries, topological transitions, and information-geometric structures.

This document develops a geometric framework in which neutron stars become finite-density manifolds realized in nature, providing direct physical embodiments of the QCD density landscape.
