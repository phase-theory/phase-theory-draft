# Abstract

The finite-density regime of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) remains one of the most significant unsolved computational problems in theoretical physics. Classical approaches based upon Euclidean lattice field theory encounter the sign problem, rendering large regions of the QCD phase diagram inaccessible to first-principles simulation.

Quantum computation offers a fundamentally different paradigm. Instead of sampling probabilities derived from complex amplitudes, quantum devices directly represent and manipulate the amplitudes themselves. Since quantum systems naturally evolve through interference rather than avoiding it, the sign problem may be transformed from an obstacle into a computational resource.

Recent developments in Hamiltonian lattice gauge theory, digital quantum simulation, analog quantum simulation, tensor-network quantum circuits, and fault-tolerant quantum computing suggest that finite-density QCD may ultimately become one of the defining applications of large-scale quantum computation.

Unified Finite-Density Quantum Field Theory (UFD-QFT) interprets quantum computation as a natural extension of density geometry. The finite-density manifold becomes encoded within quantum Hilbert space, and quantum algorithms become dynamical explorations of geometric structures that are inaccessible to classical computation.

This document develops a unified framework connecting Hamiltonian QCD, quantum simulation, density geometry, and quantum information theory.
