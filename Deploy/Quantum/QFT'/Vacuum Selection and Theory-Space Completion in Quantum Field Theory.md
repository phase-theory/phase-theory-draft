# Vacuum Selection and Theory-Space Completion in Quantum Field Theory

## A Comprehensive White Paper on the Landscape Problem Without String Theory

### Abstract

The vacuum selection problem is often presented as a problem of string theory: among an enormous number of possible vacua, why does nature realize this one? Yet the problem originates much earlier. Quantum Field Theory (QFT) itself possesses an immense space of possible vacua, phases, coupling constants, symmetry realizations, topological sectors, and renormalization-group (RG) trajectories. Even before introducing gravity or strings, QFT does not explain why a particular vacuum state and a particular set of couplings are realized.

This paper develops a new framework—**Theory-Space Completion (TSC)**—which reformulates vacuum selection as a dynamical process occurring in the full Wilsonian theory space of QFT. The central proposal is that vacuum selection is not a random choice among pre-existing vacua, nor a purely anthropic accident, but a consequence of a nonperturbative dynamical flow on theory space itself.

The framework introduces five principles:

1. **Theory Space as a Physical Manifold**
2. **Vacuum Stability Functional**
3. **Nonperturbative Theory-Space RG**
4. **Theory-Space Entropic Collapse**
5. **Universal Attractor Fixed Points**

Together these principles imply that generic QFTs evolve toward a small set of dynamically preferred vacua. The apparent landscape becomes an emergent transient phenomenon rather than a fundamental multiplicity of equally real universes.
