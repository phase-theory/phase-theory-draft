# Abstract

Between ordinary nuclear matter and asymptotically dense quark matter lies one of the least understood regions of the Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) phase diagram. This regime, characterized by baryon densities several times larger than nuclear saturation density, is believed to be relevant to neutron-star interiors and may occupy a substantial portion of the finite-density QCD landscape.

A compelling theoretical proposal is the existence of **quarkyonic matter**, a phase in which confinement persists while quark degrees of freedom dominate bulk thermodynamics. Originally motivated by large-(N_c) arguments, quarkyonic matter challenges the conventional dichotomy between confined hadronic matter and deconfined quark matter.

Unified Finite-Density Quantum Field Theory (UFD-QFT) interprets quarkyonic matter as a mixed geometric phase occupying the transitional region between the hadronic attractor basin and high-density quark attractors. In this regime, confinement topology remains intact while density geometry, information organization, and Fermi-surface structure acquire increasingly quark-like characteristics.

This document develops a geometric formulation of quarkyonic matter in which intermediate-density QCD is described as a hybrid phase characterized by competing attractors, partial confinement, mixed information geometry, and transitional renormalization structure.
