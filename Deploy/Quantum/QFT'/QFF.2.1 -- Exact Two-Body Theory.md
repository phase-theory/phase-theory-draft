# 1. Two-Particle States

## 1.1 Introduction

The fundamental dynamical problem of finite-volume quantum field theory begins with two interacting particles confined to a compact spatial manifold.

Unlike infinite-volume scattering theory, where asymptotic states form a continuum labeled by momenta,

[
|p_1,p_2\rangle,
]

finite-volume states belong to a discrete spectral Hilbert space.

The purpose of this chapter is to establish the exact structure of two-particle states in compact space.

---

## 1.2 Noninteracting Two-Particle Basis

Consider identical scalar particles of mass (m) in a cubic volume

[
V=L^3.
]

Momentum eigenvalues are

[
p_i=\frac{2\pi}{L}n_i,
\qquad
n_i\in\mathbb Z.
]

A two-particle state is

[
|p_1,p_2\rangle
===============

a^\dagger(p_1)
a^\dagger(p_2)
|0\rangle .
]

The energy is

[
E_0(p_1,p_2)
============

\omega(p_1)+\omega(p_2),
]

where

[
\omega(p)
=========

\sqrt{m^2+p^2}.
]

---

## 1.3 Total and Relative Momentum

Define

[
P=p_1+p_2,
]

[
k=\frac12(p_1-p_2).
]

The energy becomes

[
E(P,k)
======

\sqrt{m^2+\left(\frac P2+k\right)^2}
+
\sqrt{m^2+\left(\frac P2-k\right)^2}.

For

[
P=0,
]

[

]

The center-of-mass (CM) frame satisfies

[
