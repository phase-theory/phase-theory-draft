# 6. Wilsonian Renormalization and Short-Distance Structure

## 6.1 The Wilsonian Paradigm

The modern understanding of quantum field theory is fundamentally Wilsonian.

A quantum field theory defined at a microscopic scale (\Lambda_0) may be transformed into an effective theory at a lower scale (\Lambda) through integration of ultraviolet degrees of freedom.

Let

[
\phi=\phi_<+\phi_>
]

where

[
|p|<\Lambda,
\qquad
\Lambda<|p|<\Lambda_0.
]

Integrating over (\phi_>),

[
e^{-S_\Lambda[\phi_<]}
======================

\int D\phi_>
e^{-S_{\Lambda_0}[\phi_<+\phi_>]}.
]

This defines an effective action

[
S_\Lambda.
]

All local operators compatible with symmetries are generated:

[
S_\Lambda
=========

\sum_i g_i(\Lambda)
\int d^dx, \mathcal O_i(x).
]

Thus operator spaces naturally emerge from renormalization.

---

## 6.2 Short-Distance Universality

Consider two operators separated by

[
r=|x|.
]

When

[
r \ll \Lambda_{\rm IR}^{-1},
]

infrared details become irrelevant.

The product

[
\mathcal O_A(x)\mathcal O_B(0)
]

depends only on ultraviolet fluctuations.

This motivates the existence of a local expansion

[
\mathcal O_A(x)\mathcal O_B(0)
==============================

\sum_C
C_{AB}^{;;C}(x,\Lambda)
\mathcal O_C(0).
]

The OPE may therefore be viewed as the local manifestation of Wilsonian coarse-graining.

---

## 6.3 Operator Mixing

Under RG flow,

[
\mathcal O_A
\rightarrow
Z_A^{;B}\mathcal O_B.
]

Operators do not evolve independently.

Instead,

[
\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}
\mathcal O_A
============

-\gamma_A^{;B}
\mathcal O_B.
]

The anomalous-dimension matrix

[
\gamma_A^{;B}
]

acts as a connection on operator space.

Operator mixing is one indication that the operator space possesses intrinsic geometry.

This observation will later motivate the operator-bundle construction of NOET.

---

## 6.4 Wilsonian Interpretation of the OPE

Within Wilsonian language,

[
C_{AB}^{;;C}(x)
]

encodes the influence of integrated ultraviolet modes.

The OPE may therefore be interpreted as a projection:

[
\mathfrak O \otimes \mathfrak O
\rightarrow
\mathfrak O.
]

The product of operators generates infinitely many UV fluctuations.

The coefficient functions summarize their collective effect.

Perturbatively this projection is computable.

Nonperturbatively its existence remains unproven.

---

## 6.5 Limitation of the Wilsonian Framework

The Wilsonian construction assumes access to a flow generated by infinitesimal shell integration.

In strongly coupled gauge theories:

[
g(\Lambda)\sim 1,
]

the shell integration cannot generally be performed analytically.

The formal structure survives, but practical computation collapses.

Consequently Wilsonian reasoning explains why an OPE should exist, but not how to construct it exactly.

# 7. OPE in Conformal Field Theory

## 7.1 Symmetry Enhancement

Conformal field theories possess invariance under

[
SO(d+1,1).
]

This dramatically constrains correlation functions.

Primary operators satisfy

[
\mathcal O(x)
\rightarrow
\Omega(x)^{-\Delta}
\mathcal O(x).
]

Dimensions become intrinsic quantum numbers.

---

## 7.2 Exact Form of the OPE

For primary operators,

[
\mathcal O_A(x)
\mathcal O_B(0)
===============

\sum_C
\lambda_{ABC}
|x|^{\Delta_C-\Delta_A-\Delta_B}
\mathcal D_C(x,\partial)
\mathcal O_C(0).
]

All descendants are determined algebraically.

Unlike generic QFTs, no separate dynamical calculation is required.

---

## 7.3 Closure of Operator Algebras

In rational two-dimensional CFTs,

[
\mathcal O_i \times \mathcal O_j
================================

\sum_k N_{ij}^{;;k}
\mathcal O_k.
]

The OPE becomes an exact multiplication law.

Associativity implies

[
(\mathcal O_A\mathcal O_B)\mathcal O_C
======================================

\mathcal O_A(\mathcal O_B\mathcal O_C).
]

This produces highly restrictive consistency conditions.

---

## 7.4 Crossing Symmetry

Consider

[
\langle
\mathcal O_1
\mathcal O_2
\mathcal O_3
\mathcal O_4
\rangle.
]

Applying the OPE in different channels yields

[
\sum_C
\lambda_{12C}\lambda_{34C}
F_C(u,v)
========

\sum_D
\lambda_{13D}\lambda_{24D}
F_D(v,u).
]

This is the bootstrap equation.

Crossing symmetry effectively reconstructs the entire theory from OPE data.

---

## 7.5 Why CFT Does Not Solve the QCD Problem

Conformal theories possess:

[
\beta(g)=0.
]

QCD possesses

[
\beta(g)\neq0.
]

Furthermore:

[
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
]

introduces an intrinsic scale.

Confinement generates a mass gap.

Vacuum condensates appear.

The exact OPE algebra of CFT therefore cannot be transferred directly to strongly coupled gauge theories.

The conformal case demonstrates what is possible, but not how to achieve it in QCD.

# 8. Algebraic Quantum Field Theory Perspective

## 8.1 Local Nets of Algebras

Algebraic quantum field theory (AQFT) begins with local observables.

To every spacetime region (\Omega),

[
\Omega
\mapsto
\mathcal A(\Omega).
]

The theory is defined through algebraic relations rather than fields.

---

## 8.2 Haag–Kastler Axioms

The net satisfies:

### Isotony

[
\Omega_1\subset\Omega_2
\Rightarrow
\mathcal A(\Omega_1)
\subset
\mathcal A(\Omega_2).
]

### Locality

[
[\mathcal A(\Omega_1),
\mathcal A(\Omega_2)]
=0
]

for spacelike separation.

### Covariance

[
U(g)
\mathcal A(\Omega)
U(g)^{-1}
=========

\mathcal A(g\Omega).
]

These provide rigorous locality.

---

## 8.3 OPE from Local Algebras

Within AQFT, one seeks expansions of the form

[
A(x)B(y)
========

\sum_n
c_n(x-y)
\Phi_n(y).
]

The coefficients are distributions.

Several rigorous constructions exist in low-dimensional theories.

The expansion can often be shown to hold in the sense of matrix elements.

---

## 8.4 Strengths of AQFT

AQFT provides:

* exact locality,
* operator-valued distributions,
* rigorous Hilbert-space structure,
* mathematically controlled short-distance limits.

It offers perhaps the strongest current framework for discussing OPE existence.

---

## 8.5 Remaining Obstacle

AQFT establishes existence under specific assumptions but generally does not produce:

[
C_{AB}^{;;C}(x)
]

explicitly.

Nor does it derive QCD condensates.

The framework remains largely structural rather than constructive.

---

# 9. Lattice Field Theory Perspective

## 9.1 Discretized Spacetime

Lattice gauge theory replaces spacetime by

[
a\mathbb Z^d.
]

The path integral becomes

[
Z
=

\int DU
,e^{-S[U]}.
]

The ultraviolet regulator is exact.

---

## 9.2 Operator Products on the Lattice

Operators are represented as lattice observables:

[
\mathcal O(x)
\rightarrow
\mathcal O_n.
]

One may compute

[
\langle
\mathcal O_A(x)
\mathcal O_B(0)
\rangle
]

numerically.

This provides direct access to nonperturbative physics.

---

## 9.3 Determination of Condensates

Lattice methods compute quantities such as

[
\langle \bar qq\rangle,
]

and

[
\langle G^2\rangle.
]

Unlike sum rules, these emerge directly from the path integral.

Thus lattice QCD confirms that condensates are genuine dynamical quantities.

---

## 9.4 Numerical OPE Extraction

One may fit lattice correlators to

[
\sum_C
C_{AB}^{;;C}(x)
\langle\mathcal O_C\rangle.
]

This permits extraction of OPE coefficients.

However the coefficients are inferred numerically rather than derived analytically.

---

## 9.5 Fundamental Limitation

The lattice provides data but not a closed-form theory.

It answers:

[
\text{What is } C_{AB}^{;;C}?
]

numerically.

It does not answer:

[
\text{Why does the OPE exist?}
]

or

[
\text{What determines convergence?}
]

A conceptual framework remains absent.

---

# 10. Why Current OPE Methods Fail at Strong Coupling

## 10.1 Breakdown of Perturbation Theory

The perturbative OPE assumes

[
g\ll1.
]

In QCD,

[
g(\mu)
\rightarrow
O(1)
]

at low energies.

Diagrammatic expansions cease to converge.

The derivation itself disappears.

---

## 10.2 Infinite Operator Towers

Strong coupling generates contributions from infinitely many operators.

Schematically,

[
\mathcal O_A
\mathcal O_B
============

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
C_n
\mathcal O_n.
]

No truncation principle is known.

The structure of the tower itself becomes dynamical.

---

## 10.3 Vacuum Rearrangement

The perturbative vacuum

[
|0\rangle_{\rm pert}
]

is not the physical vacuum.

Instead,

[
|0\rangle_{\rm phys}
]

contains:

* condensates,
* topological sectors,
* instantons,
* flux tubes.

The OPE is usually expanded around the wrong vacuum.

---

## 10.4 Renormalon Ambiguities

Perturbative coefficients exhibit factorial growth:

[
c_n
\sim
n!.
]

The resulting ambiguity scales as

[
e^{-1/g^2}.
]

These ambiguities are of precisely the size expected from nonperturbative effects.

Therefore perturbative coefficients alone cannot define the OPE.

---

## 10.5 Absence of a Spectral Construction

Current approaches define

[
C_{AB}^{;;C}
]

through diagrams.

Yet the exact theory is defined by:

[
{E_n},
\qquad
\langle m|\mathcal O|n\rangle.
]

No accepted framework derives OPE coefficients directly from this spectral data.

This is the central missing element.

---

## 10.6 The Core Obstruction

All existing approaches share a common assumption:

[
\text{Short distance}
\Longrightarrow
\text{weak coupling}.
]

This implication is false in general.

Short-distance operator products may remain influenced by:

* confinement,
* topology,
* vacuum structure,
* spectral discreteness.

A successful nonperturbative OPE must therefore incorporate the exact spectrum from the outset.

---

Existing frameworks provide:

[
\boxed{
