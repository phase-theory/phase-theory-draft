Abstract

Arithmetic traditionally operates on symbolic objects manipulated through predefined algebraic rules. Classical processors implement arithmetic through circuits; quantum processors implement arithmetic through reversible transformations and structured state evolution.

Across the Structural Sector, qubits became geometric regions, gates became trajectories, circuits became manifolds, and registers became organized accessibility structures.

The next question emerges:

If information becomes geometry—

what becomes of arithmetic?

This paper develops Geometric Arithmetic Systems (GAS): a theoretical framework in which arithmetic operations emerge from structured transformations of computational geometry.

Rather than treating arithmetic as symbol manipulation, the framework interprets arithmetic as movement, deformation, composition, and projection within admissible state spaces.

The central proposal:

Arithmetic is not applied to information.

Arithmetic is geometry transforming itself.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research direction rather than an experimentally validated arithmetic architecture.
