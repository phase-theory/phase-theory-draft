Abstract

Computation is traditionally modeled through state transitions, logic operations, communication channels, and storage.

Information theory studies compression, recoverability, and computational limits. Research in gravitation and quantum information has also motivated questions about entropy, horizons, and the organization of accessible information.

Across the Structural Sector, information evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ computation

→ security

→ memory

→ preservation

→ informational geometry

→ relational organization

→ bridge accessibility.

A further question emerges:

If information becomes organized geometry—

can computation be represented through constrained accessibility and compressed reconstruction?

This paper develops Black-Hole Geometric Computation (BHGC): a speculative theoretical framework in which computation is represented as information organization under extreme accessibility constraints and boundary-based reconstruction.

This framework does not propose physical computation inside black holes, extraction of unlimited computational power, or violations of known physics.

Instead, it explores an abstract computational language inspired by concepts of bounded accessibility, compression, and reconstruction.

The central proposal:

Computation is not only transformation.

Computation may also be represented as controlled compression and recoverable accessibility.

This document presents speculative conceptual research rather than a physical computational model.
