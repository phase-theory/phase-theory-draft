Abstract

Modern quantum technologies are increasingly heterogeneous. Different physical systems provide different advantages:

* photons support communication,
* superconducting circuits provide controllability,
* spins offer localization,
* defects enable persistence,
* collective matter states provide protection.

Conventional architectures generally treat hybridization as an engineering integration problem.

This paper develops Hybrid Matter–Geometry Qubits (HMGQ): a theoretical framework in which hybridization itself becomes the computational principle.

Rather than assigning one qubit to one physical substrate, logical information is encoded into a shared geometric structure distributed across multiple physical realizations.

The central proposal:

A qubit need not belong to matter.

A qubit may belong to the geometry connecting multiple forms of matter.

This framework introduces:

* composite geometric state spaces,
* cross-substrate logical encoding,
* manifold transitions,
* geometry-mediated transport,
* hybrid protection,
* distributed quantum memory.

This document presents a theoretical research architecture rather than an experimentally demonstrated universal qubit platform.
