Abstract

Optimization is one of the central activities of computation.

Classical optimization techniques typically proceed through iterative updates, objective evaluation, gradient estimation, combinatorial exploration, or probabilistic search. Quantum optimization explores structured state evolution and specialized representations for selected optimization classes.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through successive reinterpretations:

operators

→ trajectories

→ geometry

→ topology

→ measurement

→ search.

Search identifies reachable possibilities.

Optimization asks:

Which reachable possibilities are preferred?

This paper develops Geometric Optimization Algorithms (GOA): a theoretical framework in which optimization is represented as controlled evolution across computational geometry toward admissible target regions.

Rather than treating optimization as repeated adjustment of variables, the framework interprets optimization as deformation and navigation of structured computational manifolds.

The central proposal:

Optimization does not improve solutions.

Optimization reshapes the geometry in which solutions become accessible.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally validated optimization architecture.
