Abstract

Conventional computing organizes information into registers—addressable structures that temporarily store and expose computational state. Quantum systems inherit this abstraction through qubit registers, where information is represented as tensor-product collections of quantum states.

As the Structural Sector evolved from geometric qubits to geometric circuits, a deeper question emerged:

If gates become trajectories and circuits become traversable manifolds—

what becomes of the register?

This paper develops Geometric Register Architectures (GRA): a theoretical framework in which registers are represented as structured geometric regions that organize accessibility, memory locality, logical identity, and computational transport.

Rather than viewing a register as a collection of storage elements, this framework treats a register as a computational geometry defining which states may coexist, interact, and evolve.

The central proposal:

A register is not a container of information.

A register is the geometry that determines how information can exist.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally validated memory architecture.
