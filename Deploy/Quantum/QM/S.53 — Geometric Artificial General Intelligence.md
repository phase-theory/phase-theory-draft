Abstract

Artificial intelligence systems are typically developed as collections of specialized mechanisms for perception, reasoning, memory, planning, prediction, and adaptation.

Modern machine learning systems demonstrate strong capabilities in constrained domains but generally rely on task-specific training, bounded objective functions, and limited transfer across unrelated contexts.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ optimization

→ routing

→ learning

→ neural representation

→ semantic organization.

A further question emerges:

If knowledge becomes geometry—

what becomes of general intelligence?

This paper develops Geometric Artificial General Intelligence (G-AGI): a theoretical framework in which general intelligence is represented as continuous restructuring and coordination of computational geometry across multiple domains.

Rather than treating intelligence as accumulation of capabilities, the framework interprets intelligence as organization and reorganization of representational accessibility.

The central proposal:

General intelligence is not possession of universal knowledge.

General intelligence is the ability to reorganize computational geometry across unfamiliar domains.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework and does not describe an existing AGI system or claim feasibility.
