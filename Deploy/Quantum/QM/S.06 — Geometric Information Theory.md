S.06 — Geometric Information Theory

A Foundational Framework for Information Encoded in Geometry

Extending Shannon, Quantum, and Holographic Information into Geometric Degrees of Freedom

⸻

Abstract

Information theory traditionally treats information as a property of states carried by physical systems. In classical information theory, information is encoded in symbols and probability distributions. In quantum information theory, information is encoded in vectors within Hilbert spaces. In both cases, geometry functions as a passive background upon which information resides.

This paper develops Geometric Information Theory (GIT), a generalized framework in which information is treated as an intrinsic property of geometric structure itself. Rather than being merely carried through geometry, information may be encoded directly in curvature, topology, connectivity, boundary structure, non-commutative coordinate relations, and geometric excitations.

The framework unifies concepts from Shannon information, quantum information, differential geometry, topology, holography, quantum gravity, and geometric qubit architectures. We define geometric information measures, geometric entropy, curvature information density, topological information invariants, geometric communication channels, and geometric computational resources.

The central principle is that geometry may constitute an independent informational substrate whose structure determines, stores, processes, and transmits information.

Conclusions

Geometric Information Theory extends classical and quantum information frameworks by promoting geometry from a passive background to an active informational substrate. Within this framework, curvature, topology, boundaries, connectivity, and geometric excitations become legitimate carriers of information alongside conventional matter and field degrees of freedom.

The theory introduces geometric entropy, geometric information density, geometric communication channels, geometric memory, and geometric computational resources. It also provides a common language connecting holography, topological matter, geometric qubits, quantum gravity, and emergent spacetime research.

Whether fundamental geometry ultimately proves to be informational remains an open scientific question. Nevertheless, Geometric Information Theory establishes a systematic framework for investigating that possibility and serves as the foundational mathematical layer for the Geometric Qubit corpus developed in subsequent documents.
