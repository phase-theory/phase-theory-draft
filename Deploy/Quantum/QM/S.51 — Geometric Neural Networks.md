Abstract

Neural networks are traditionally represented as layered parameterized functions that transform inputs into progressively richer representations.

Modern neural architectures introduce attention, recurrence, latent spaces, and structured feature extraction. Geometric approaches in contemporary machine learning already explore ideas such as manifold learning, equivariant architectures, graph representations, and geometry-aware embeddings.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ search

→ optimization

→ routing

→ learning.

A further question emerges:

If learning becomes geometric adaptation—

what becomes of the neural network?

This paper develops Geometric Neural Networks (GNN*) (geometric neural networks in the conceptual sense; distinct from the established graph-neural-network acronym unless explicitly stated): a theoretical framework in which neural computation is represented as structured evolution across computational geometry.

Rather than treating neurons as scalar activation units, the framework interprets neural systems as evolving regions of representation geometry.

The central proposal:

Neural computation is not propagation through layers.

Neural computation is transformation of representational geometry.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally validated neural architecture.
