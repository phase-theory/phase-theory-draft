Abstract

Superconducting quantum circuits represent one of the most mature experimental platforms for quantum information processing. Existing architectures encode information into engineered nonlinear oscillators and manipulate states using microwave control, tunable coupling, and measurement circuitry.

Most current approaches treat geometry primarily as implementation detail.

This paper develops Superconducting Geometric Architectures (SGA): a theoretical framework in which geometry becomes a primary computational resource.

Rather than viewing a superconducting processor as a collection of isolated qubits connected by control electronics, the architecture interprets the processor as a structured manifold whose geometry determines allowable information flow, logical evolution, protection mechanisms, and computational accessibility.

The framework introduces:

* geometric superconducting state spaces,
* circuit curvature,
* collective mode encoding,
* topological routing,
* holonomic control,
* defect-mediated memory,
* geometry-native logical operations.

This document presents a conceptual and mathematical architecture rather than a demonstrated replacement for existing superconducting quantum processors.
