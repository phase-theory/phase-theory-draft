Abstract

Quantum computation is conventionally expressed as sequences of unitary gates acting on localized qubits. In most architectures, gates are external control primitives imposed upon quantum states.

This paper develops Geometric Quantum Gates (GQG): a theoretical framework in which logical operations arise from controlled motion through quantum state geometry.

Rather than treating computation as a sequence of externally applied instructions, the framework interprets computation as navigation across structured state manifolds.

Logical transformations emerge from:

* geometric phase,
* state-space curvature,
* transport pathways,
* holonomies,
* topological constraints,
* manifold evolution.

The central proposal:

A gate is not an operation applied to a state.

A gate is a trajectory executed by a state.

This document presents a theoretical architecture and mathematical research direction rather than an experimentally validated gate model.
