Abstract

Networks determine how information moves across physical and computational systems.

Classical networks organize communication through nodes, links, routing, and protocols. Quantum networking introduces entanglement distribution, state transfer, coherence management, and non-classical communication constraints.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ routing

→ learning

→ intelligence

→ storage.

A further question emerges:

If information itself becomes geometry—

what becomes of networks?

This paper develops Geometric Quantum Networks (GQN): a theoretical framework in which communication is represented as structured evolution of distributed computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting a network as discrete endpoints connected by channels, the framework interprets a network as coordinated accessibility across distributed state manifolds.

The central proposal:

Networks do not connect locations.

Networks organize accessibility.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an experimentally validated quantum networking architecture.
