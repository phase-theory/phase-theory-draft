Abstract

Search is one of the foundational operations of computation.

Classical algorithms typically search by enumeration, indexing, pruning, or heuristic exploration. Quantum search algorithms explore structured state evolution and amplitude redistribution to improve search efficiency for particular problem classes.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved from:

operators

→ trajectories

→ geometry

→ topology

→ measurement

→ adiabatic evolution.

A natural question follows:

If computation becomes geometry—

what does search become?

This paper develops Geometric Search Algorithms (GSA): a theoretical framework in which search is represented as navigation across structured computational manifolds.

Rather than interpreting search as repeated evaluation of candidates, the framework interprets search as controlled motion through accessibility geometry.

The central proposal:

Search does not discover information.

Search reaches information.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally validated search architecture.
