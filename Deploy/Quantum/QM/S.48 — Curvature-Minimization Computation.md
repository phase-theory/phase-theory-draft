Abstract

Computation is commonly framed as execution of instructions, traversal of circuits, or optimization of objectives.

Across the Structural Sector, progressively deeper abstractions emerged:

* gates became trajectories,
* circuits became manifolds,
* registers became geometric organization,
* search became navigation,
* optimization became restructuring of computational geometry.

A natural continuation follows:

If geometry governs computation—

can computation emerge by reducing geometric tension itself?

This paper develops Curvature-Minimization Computation (CMC): a theoretical framework in which computation is represented as evolution toward lower-curvature computational configurations.

Rather than explicitly constructing outputs through operations, this framework interprets computation as relaxation of structured computational geometry under admissible constraints.

The central proposal:

Computation occurs when geometry reduces its own computational curvature.

Solutions emerge as stable low-curvature configurations.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally validated computational architecture.
