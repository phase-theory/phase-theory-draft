Abstract

The quantum Hall effect revealed that quantum matter can exhibit globally quantized behavior determined not by local microscopic details but by topological and geometric structure. Integer and fractional quantum Hall systems exhibit robust transport, protected edge states, quantized conductance, and rich emergent geometry.

These systems have become foundational to modern topological condensed matter theory and remain an active area of research. Certain fractional quantum Hall states are also studied as potential platforms for protected quantum information processing.

This paper develops Quantum Hall Geometric Qubits (QHGQ): a geometry-native quantum information framework in which logical states are encoded into Hall-state geometry, topological sectors, edge transport structure, and collective quantum manifolds.

This document presents a theoretical architecture and research direction—not an experimentally established qubit implementation.

The central proposal:

Quantum information may be encoded into geometric organization of Hall states rather than localized microscopic carriers.
