Abstract

Consensus determines whether distributed systems can act as coherent systems.

Classical distributed computing develops consensus through replication, leader election, quorum systems, Byzantine tolerance, synchronization, and agreement protocols. Quantum information introduces additional considerations including state distribution, measurement constraints, communication cost, and coordination limits.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ routing

→ networks

→ distributed computation.

A further question emerges:

If computation becomes distributed geometry—

how do distributed regions agree?

This paper develops Geometric Consensus Protocols (GCP): a speculative theoretical framework in which agreement is represented as convergence of accessibility across distributed computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting consensus as agreement among discrete nodes, the framework interprets consensus as stabilization of admissible organization across distributed state manifolds.

The central proposal:

Consensus is not voting among locations.

Consensus is stabilization of accessible structure.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than a replacement for established consensus algorithms.
