Abstract

Classical computation defines universality through the ability to emulate arbitrary algorithms using finite instruction sets. Quantum computation extends universality through collections of gates capable of approximating arbitrary unitary evolution.

Across the Structural Sector, progressively deeper abstractions emerged:

* qubits became geometric regions,
* gates became trajectories,
* circuits became manifolds,
* registers became accessibility structures,
* arithmetic became transformation of geometry.

The next question becomes unavoidable:

Can geometry itself become the universal computational primitive?

This paper develops Universal Geometric Computation (UGC): a theoretical framework in which all admissible computation is represented as structured evolution across computational geometry.

The framework proposes that universality is not achieved through a universal gate set but through closure properties of computational geometry.

The central proposal:

A system is computationally universal if its geometry can represent and transform any admissible computational structure.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research program rather than an experimentally demonstrated computational model.
