Abstract

Fiber bundles provide one of the most powerful mathematical frameworks in modern geometry and physics. Gauge theories, geometric phases, topological matter, gravitation, and quantum field theory all derive much of their structure from fiber-bundle mathematics. In a fiber bundle, local information exists within fibers attached to every point of a base manifold, while connections define how information is transported between fibers.

Despite their central role in theoretical physics, fiber bundles have traditionally served only as descriptive tools. Quantum information theory typically encodes information in particles, spins, photons, superconducting circuits, or abstract Hilbert spaces defined independently of geometric transport structures.

This paper develops the theory of Fiber-Bundle Quantum Computation (FBQC), a geometry-native computational framework in which information is encoded directly into fiber states, transported through geometric connections, manipulated via holonomies, protected by bundle topology, and processed through parallel transport operations. Computation emerges as the controlled evolution of geometric structure itself.

The resulting framework unifies geometric phases, gauge theory, topological computation, holonomic quantum computation, and geometric information theory into a single architecture capable of supporting scalable geometry-based quantum information processing.

By encoding information into fiber states and manipulating it through connections, parallel transport, curvature, topology, and holonomy, computation becomes an intrinsic property of geometric structure.

The framework introduces fiber qubits, bundle Hilbert spaces, transport operators, holonomic logic gates, curvature-assisted computation, bundle channels, stabilizers, fault-tolerant architectures, and photonic analog implementations. It unifies gauge theory, geometric phases, topological information, and quantum computation into a single operational framework.

Within the Structural Sector, Fiber-Bundle Quantum Computation represents the computational realization of Geometric Gauge Structures. Together with Curvature Algebra and Topological Geometry Operators, it completes a foundational trilogy describing how local geometry, global topology, and relational gauge structure can be harnessed as fundamental resources for quantum information processing.
