Abstract

Communication channels define the rules through which information moves between computational systems.

Classical communication theory models channels using signal propagation, bandwidth, noise, coding, and capacity. Quantum communication extends this framework through quantum states, coherence constraints, entanglement-assisted protocols, and information-theoretic limits.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ routing

→ networks

→ repeaters

→ distributed accessibility.

A further question emerges:

If communication becomes organized accessibility—

what becomes of the channel?

This paper develops Curvature-Based Communication Channels (CBCC): a theoretical framework in which communication channels are represented as structured accessibility induced by computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting channels as fixed transmission paths, the framework interprets channels as dynamically organized regions that shape information transport.

The central proposal:

Channels do not carry information.

Channels organize admissible propagation.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an experimentally validated communication architecture.
