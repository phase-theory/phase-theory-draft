Information systems are traditionally described through communication channels, routing, locality, storage, and computation.

Quantum information expands this picture through correlations and structured state relationships while preserving operational constraints on signaling and information transfer.

Across the Structural Sector, information evolved through:

→ preservation

A further question emerges:

If information becomes organized geometry—

can long-range accessibility be represented through structured topological bridges?

This paper develops Geometric Wormhole Information Theory (GWIT): a speculative theoretical framework in which information accessibility is represented through abstract bridge-like connectivity across organizational geometry.

This framework does not propose traversable physical wormholes, faster-than-light communication, or mechanisms that bypass relativity.

Instead, it studies a computational and informational language in which distant regions may be represented as adjacent under a chosen organizational structure.

The central proposal:

Communication is not only transmission across distance.

Communication may also be represented as reconstruction across structured connectivity.

This document presents speculative conceptual research rather than a physical communication theory.
