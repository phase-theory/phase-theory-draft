Abstract

Computation is traditionally modeled through registers, logic operations, memory hierarchies, and communication pathways.

Information theory extends this picture through compression, recoverability, and organization under constraints. Previous Structural Sector developments introduced reconstruction geometry, distributed accessibility, and boundary-inspired processing.

Across the Structural Sector, information evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ computation

→ security

→ memory

→ preservation

→ informational geometry

→ causal organization

→ constrained reconstruction.

A further question emerges:

If information becomes organized through boundaries—

can processing itself emerge from accessibility at organizational horizons?

This paper develops Horizon Information Processors (HIP): a speculative theoretical framework in which computation is represented through information encoded, transformed, and reconstructed across boundary-like organizational surfaces.

This framework does not propose physical processors operating at astrophysical horizons, nor extraction of computational power from black holes.

Instead, it studies abstract processing models where boundaries organize accessibility and reconstruction.

The central proposal:

Processing is not only internal execution.

Processing may also be represented as transformation of accessible boundaries.

This document presents speculative conceptual research rather than a realizable hardware architecture.
