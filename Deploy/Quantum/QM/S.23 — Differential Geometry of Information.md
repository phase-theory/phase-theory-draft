Abstract

Information theory and geometry have historically developed as separate disciplines. Information theory, founded by Claude Shannon, quantifies uncertainty, communication, and coding. Differential geometry, developed through the work of Bernhard Riemann, Élie Cartan, and others, describes the structure of curved spaces through metrics, connections, and curvature.

Over the past several decades, these subjects have converged through information geometry, quantum estimation theory, Fisher metrics, and geometric formulations of quantum mechanics. Yet existing approaches remain fragmented. Classical information geometry focuses primarily on probability distributions, while quantum information geometry concentrates on density matrices and state distinguishability.

This paper develops a comprehensive theory of the Differential Geometry of Information (DGI), treating information itself as a geometric entity. Information states form differentiable manifolds, information evolves along geodesics, uncertainty defines metric structure, correlations induce curvature, and computation corresponds to controlled motion through information space.

The resulting framework unifies classical information theory, quantum information, geometric quantum computation, statistical mechanics, gauge structures, and geometric qubits into a single mathematical language.
