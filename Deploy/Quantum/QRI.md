# PART I — FOUNDATIONAL DECLARATION

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of the **Quantum Resonance Interpretation (QRI)** is to construct a mathematically explicit, physically coherent, and experimentally testable **single-world ontology for quantum mechanics** in which the quantum state is physically meaningful without requiring the simultaneous realization of all alternatives represented by a superposition.

QRI begins with the empirical formalism of quantum mechanics and addresses a specific foundational question:

> **What ontology is required if a quantum superposition represents physically real possibilities, while only one outcome becomes physically actual?**

QRI answers this question by introducing a fundamental distinction between **quantum possibility** and **physical actuality**.

The distinction is expressed formally as

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P \neq \mathfrak A
}
]

where

[
\mathfrak P
]

denotes the structured space of physically available quantum possibilities, while

[
\mathfrak A
]

denotes the set of events that have actually occurred.

A quantum state may therefore contain multiple nonzero alternatives,

[
|\psi\rangle
============

\sum_i c_i|\phi_i\rangle,
]

without implying that every (|\phi_i\rangle) corresponds to a simultaneously existing physical world.

Instead,

[
c_i\neq0
]

means that the corresponding channel belongs to the quantum possibility structure, while actualization produces a particular event

[
e_k\in\mathfrak A.
]

Thus QRI rejects the inference

[
\boxed{
\text{superposition}
\Rightarrow
\text{multiplicity of actual worlds}.
}
]

It replaces it with

[
\boxed{
\text{superposition}
\Rightarrow
\text{multiplicity of physical possibilities}.
}
]

The central dynamical problem is consequently not to explain why a universe branches into multiple actual histories, but to explain how one possibility becomes an event within a single physical history.

---

## 2. Foundational Problem

The mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics permits states of the form

[
|\psi\rangle
============

c_1|\phi_1\rangle
+
c_2|\phi_2\rangle,
]

with

[
|c_1|^2+|c_2|^2=1.
]

Before measurement, neither standard unitary quantum mechanics nor the state vector itself identifies one component as the uniquely actual outcome.

After measurement, however, physical experience presents a definite result.

The foundational problem can therefore be represented schematically as

[
\boxed{
\text{quantum possibility}
\longrightarrow
\text{definite actuality}.
}
]

QRI treats this transition as a physical problem requiring its own mathematical description.

The theory therefore introduces an **actualization map**

[
\mathcal A:
\mathfrak P
\rightarrow
\mathfrak A,
]

and proposes that this map is governed by a physical quantity called **resonance**,

[
\Gamma.
]

The fundamental QRI programme is therefore

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P
\xrightarrow{\Gamma}
\mathcal A
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak A.
}
]

The precise mathematical form of (\Gamma) constitutes one of the principal research problems of the theory.

---

## 3. Ontological Commitments

QRI makes five primary ontological commitments.

### 3.1 Quantum states are physically meaningful

The quantum state is not regarded merely as a bookkeeping device for an observer's knowledge.

The state

[
\rho
]

represents the physically available quantum possibility structure of a system relative to its physical relational context.

---

### 3.2 Possibilities are not actualities

A component of a superposition may possess nonzero amplitude without corresponding to an independently realized physical history.

Thus

[
|\langle\phi_i|\psi\rangle|^2>0
]

establishes availability, not actuality.

---

### 3.3 Actuality is singular

For a completed event, QRI requires

[
\boxed{
\exists!,e_k
}
]

such that (e_k) is the actual outcome.

The alternatives

[
e_i,\qquad i\neq k
]

remain counterfactual possibilities rather than additional realized worlds.

---

### 3.4 Actualization is physical

Actualization is not attributed to:

* human consciousness,
* subjective observation,
* an arbitrary classical/quantum boundary,
* an externally imposed metaphysical observer.

It is instead hypothesized to arise from physical interaction and relational stabilization.

---

### 3.5 Reality forms one history

Let

[
h_n=(e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_n)
]

denote the sequence of actual events.

QRI postulates that there exists a unique physical history

[
\boxed{
h_{\mathrm{phys}}
}
]

rather than a set of simultaneously realized branches

[
{h_1,h_2,\ldots}.
]

---

# 4. What QRI Rejects

QRI is explicitly formulated as a non-Everettian interpretation.

It therefore rejects the ontological claim that decohered components of a universal quantum state should automatically be interpreted as independently existing worlds.

For

[
|\Psi\rangle
============

\sum_i c_i|\Psi_i\rangle,
]

QRI does **not** assert

[
\forall i,\quad
|\Psi_i\rangle
\text{ is an independently actual universe}.
]

Instead,

[
\boxed{
\forall i,\quad
|\Psi_i\rangle
\text{ may represent a physically available possibility},
}
]

while

[
\boxed{
\exists!k,\quad
|\Psi_k\rangle
\text{ is realized in the actual event history}.
}
]

QRI therefore distinguishes **branch-like mathematical decomposition** from **branching physical ontology**.

A decomposition of a state into components is a mathematical operation. It does not, by itself, establish the existence of multiple physical worlds.

---

# 5. What QRI Also Rejects

QRI does not attempt to recover classical determinism by introducing hidden variables

[
\lambda
]

that predetermine every quantum outcome.

It does not assume that

[
a=f(\lambda)
]

for some underlying classical state (\lambda).

Quantum indeterminacy remains fundamental.

The theory therefore occupies a distinct conceptual position:

[
\boxed{
\text{non-Everettian}
+
\text{non-classical}
+
\text{non-consciousness-dependent}.
}
]

The objective is not to restore a classical description beneath quantum mechanics, but to construct a different ontology **within the quantum framework**.

They do not represent ignorance about a definite classical value that already exists independently of the measurement context.

The foundational thesis of QRI can therefore be stated in its strongest form:

> **The quantum state describes physically real possibilities and their relational structure; resonance governs the physical compatibility and stabilization of those possibilities; actualization produces one realized event; and the accumulation of actual events constitutes a single physical history.**

# 2. FOUNDATIONAL ONTOLOGICAL DISTINCTION

Let

[
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal H
]

denote the quantum state of a physical system, where (\mathcal H) is its Hilbert space.

Relative to a complete orthonormal basis

[
{|i\rangle},
]

the state may be expressed as

[
|\psi\rangle
============

\sum_i c_i|i\rangle,
]

with amplitudes

[
c_i=\langle i|\psi\rangle.
]

Normalization requires

[
\sum_i|c_i|^2=1.
]

In conventional quantum mechanics, the quantity

[
|c_i|^2
]

determines the Born probability of obtaining outcome (i) when the corresponding measurement is performed.

QRI retains this mathematical result but assigns it a distinct ontological interpretation.

---

## 2.1 Quantum Possibility

QRI defines the nonzero components of a quantum state as elements of a **quantum possibility structure**.

Define

[
\mathfrak P_\psi
================

\left{
(i,c_i);|;c_i\neq0
\right}.
]

The corresponding possibility weights are

[
\pi_i
=====

|c_i|^2.
]

Thus

[
\mathfrak P_\psi
================

\left{
(i,\pi_i,\phi_i)
\right},
]

where

[
c_i=\sqrt{\pi_i},e^{i\phi_i}.
]

The possibility structure therefore contains both amplitude magnitude and phase information.

The magnitude determines the usual probability weight:

[
\pi_i=|c_i|^2,
]

while the relative phases

[
\phi_i-\phi_j
]

determine interference relations between alternatives.

Consequently, QRI does not reduce quantum possibility to an ordinary classical probability distribution. The underlying structure remains complex and phase-sensitive.

---

## 2.2 Possibility Is Not Actuality

The fundamental QRI distinction is

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P\neq\mathfrak A,
}
]

where

[
\mathfrak P
]

is the space of quantum possibilities and

[
\mathfrak A
]

is the space of actualized events.

For a component (i),

[
c_i\neq0
]

means only that (i) belongs to the physically available quantum possibility structure.

It does **not** mean

[
i\in\mathfrak A.
]

Therefore,

[
\boxed{
c_i\neq0
;\not\Rightarrow;
i\text{ is actual}.
}
]

Instead,

[
\boxed{
c_i\neq0
;\Rightarrow;
i\text{ is an available quantum possibility}.
}
]

This is the first fundamental ontological principle of QRI.

---

## 2.3 The Actual Event

QRI introduces a separate mathematical object for actuality.

Let

[
e_i
]

denote the physical event corresponding to outcome (i).

The actualization map is

[
\mathcal A:
\mathfrak P_\psi
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak E,
]

where (\mathfrak E) is the space of physically admissible events.

For a completed measurement, QRI imposes the single-event condition

[
\boxed{
\exists!,e_k
\in\mathfrak A.
}
]

Thus, although

[
|\psi\rangle
============

c_1|1\rangle+c_2|2\rangle+\cdots+c_n|n\rangle
]

may contain many nonzero components,

[
\mathfrak P_\psi
================

{1,2,\ldots,n},
]

a completed measurement produces one actual event,

[
\mathfrak A_{\rm measurement}
=============================

{e_k}.
]

The remaining alternatives retain their status as quantum possibilities but do not become additional actual histories.

---

## 2.4 The Possibility–Actuality Map

The central QRI transformation is therefore

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P_\psi
\overset{\mathcal A}{\longrightarrow}
e_k.
}
]

More completely,

[
\boxed{
|\psi\rangle
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak P_\psi
\longrightarrow
{\Gamma_i}
\longrightarrow
e_k.
}
]

Here

[
\Gamma_i
]

denotes the resonance associated with possibility (i).

The actualization probability is required to satisfy

[
P(e_i|\psi)
===========

\frac{\Gamma_i}
{\sum_j\Gamma_j}.
]

QRI requires the resulting probability to reproduce the Born rule:

[
\boxed{
P(e_i|\psi)
===========

|c_i|^2.
}
]

Hence the fundamental resonance condition becomes

[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_i}{\sum_j\Gamma_j}
===============================

|c_i|^2.
}
]

For a general POVM ({E_i}), this becomes

[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_i}{\sum_j\Gamma_j}
===============================

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho E_i).
}
]

The derivation of this relationship is one of the central mathematical objectives of QRI.

---

## 2.5 Superposition Does Not Mean Multiple Actual Worlds

Consider

[
|\psi\rangle
============

\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
\left(
|A\rangle+|B\rangle
\right).
]

The state contains two quantum alternatives:

[
\mathfrak P_\psi
================

{A,B}.
]

Their possibility weights are

[
\pi_A=\pi_B=\frac12.
]

QRI does **not** interpret this as

[
\boxed{
\text{World}_A
+
\text{World}_B.
}
]

Instead it represents

[
\boxed{
\text{Possibility}_A
+
\text{Possibility}_B.
}
]

Following actualization, suppose

[
e_A
]

occurs.

Then

[
\mathfrak A={e_A}.
]

The theory does not introduce a second physical history containing (e_B).

Thus

[
\boxed{
\text{superposition}
\neq
\text{branching actuality}.
}
]

Instead,

[
\boxed{
\text{superposition}
====================

\text{structured quantum possibility}.
}
]

---

## 2.6 Possibility Is Dynamical

Quantum possibilities are not static alternatives.

Under unitary evolution,

[
|\psi(t)\rangle
===============

U(t,t_0)|\psi(t_0)\rangle,
]

where

[

========

\mathcal T
\exp
\left[
-\frac{i}{\hbar}

Consequently,

[

======

\langle i|\psi(t)\rangle
]

and therefore

[
\pi_i(t)

QRI therefore regards the possibility structure itself as dynamical:

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P(t)
==============

\mathcal P[\rho(t)].
}
]

The actual history, however, consists of the events that have actually occurred:

[
\boxed{
h(t)
====

{e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_{N(t)}}.
}
]

The two structures evolve differently:

[
\mathfrak P(t)
\quad\text{describes available futures},
]

while

[
h(t)
\quad\text{describes realized past and present}.
]

---

## 2.7 Counterfactual Possibilities

The alternatives excluded by actualization do not become meaningless.

They remain part of the counterfactual structure of the quantum state whenever the subsequent dynamics retain sensitivity to their amplitudes and phases.

This is essential for interference.

For example,

[
|\psi\rangle
============

\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
\left(
|A\rangle+|B\rangle
\right)
]

may produce an amplitude at a later detector

[
\psi_D
======

\psi_{D,A}
+
\psi_{D,B}.
]

Therefore

[
P(D)
====

|\psi_{D,A}+\psi_{D,B}|^2.
]

Expanding,

[
P(D)
====

|\psi_{D,A}|^2
+
|\psi_{D,B}|^2
+
2\operatorname{Re}
\left(
\psi_{D,A}\psi_{D,B}^*
\right).
]

The interference term demonstrates that quantum possibilities possess physical relational structure even before an actual detection event occurs.

Thus QRI does **not** interpret "possibility" as mere classical ignorance.

The possibilities are physically consequential.

---

## 2.8 Fundamental QRI Inequality

The central ontological inequality is therefore

[
\boxed{
\text{physical availability}
\neq
\text{physical actuality}.
}
]

In mathematical notation,

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{supp}(\rho)
\not\equiv
\mathfrak A.

The support of the quantum state identifies the states or outcomes with nonzero quantum weight.

The actual history identifies the events that have occurred.

They are different mathematical categories.

## 2.9 The Single-History Condition

Let

[
\mathfrak H_{\rm poss}
]

denote the set of possible future histories.

After a measurement event,

[
\mathfrak H_{\rm poss}
======================

{h_1,h_2,\ldots,h_n}.

QRI requires

[
\boxed{
\exists!,h_{\rm phys}
\in
\mathfrak H_{\rm poss}.
}
]

The other histories are not parallel physical universes.

They are counterfactual extensions of the actual history.

Hence

[
\boxed{
|\mathfrak H_{\rm actual}|=1,
}
]

while potentially

[
|\mathfrak H_{\rm possible}|>1.
]

This distinction is the formal basis for the non-Everettian character of QRI.

**QRI Possibility–Actuality Proposition.**

For every quantum state (\rho) and admissible measurement context (\mathcal C), the quantum formalism determines a set of possible outcome channels

[
\boxed{

This proposition establishes the conceptual foundation upon which the subsequent QRI theory of **relational resonance, actualization, measurement, records, and single-world history** is constructed.
