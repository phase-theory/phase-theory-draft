# PART I — FOUNDATIONAL DECLARATION

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of the **Quantum Resonance Interpretation (QRI)** is to construct a mathematically explicit, physically coherent, and experimentally testable **single-world ontology for quantum mechanics** in which the quantum state is physically meaningful without requiring the simultaneous realization of all alternatives represented by a superposition.

QRI begins with the empirical formalism of quantum mechanics and addresses a specific foundational question:

> **What ontology is required if a quantum superposition represents physically real possibilities, while only one outcome becomes physically actual?**

QRI answers this question by introducing a fundamental distinction between **quantum possibility** and **physical actuality**.

The distinction is expressed formally as

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P \neq \mathfrak A
}
]

where

[
\mathfrak P
]

denotes the structured space of physically available quantum possibilities, while

[
\mathfrak A
]

denotes the set of events that have actually occurred.

A quantum state may therefore contain multiple nonzero alternatives,

[
|\psi\rangle
============

\sum_i c_i|\phi_i\rangle,
]

without implying that every (|\phi_i\rangle) corresponds to a simultaneously existing physical world.

Instead,

[
c_i\neq0
]

means that the corresponding channel belongs to the quantum possibility structure, while actualization produces a particular event

[
e_k\in\mathfrak A.
]

Thus QRI rejects the inference

[
\boxed{
\text{superposition}
\Rightarrow
\text{multiplicity of actual worlds}.
}
]

It replaces it with

[
\boxed{
\text{superposition}
\Rightarrow
\text{multiplicity of physical possibilities}.
}
]

The central dynamical problem is consequently not to explain why a universe branches into multiple actual histories, but to explain how one possibility becomes an event within a single physical history.

---

## 2. Foundational Problem

The mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics permits states of the form

[
|\psi\rangle
============

c_1|\phi_1\rangle
+
c_2|\phi_2\rangle,
]

with

[
|c_1|^2+|c_2|^2=1.
]

Before measurement, neither standard unitary quantum mechanics nor the state vector itself identifies one component as the uniquely actual outcome.

After measurement, however, physical experience presents a definite result.

The foundational problem can therefore be represented schematically as

[
\boxed{
\text{quantum possibility}
\longrightarrow
\text{definite actuality}.
}
]

QRI treats this transition as a physical problem requiring its own mathematical description.

The theory therefore introduces an **actualization map**

[
\mathcal A:
\mathfrak P
\rightarrow
\mathfrak A,
]

and proposes that this map is governed by a physical quantity called **resonance**,

[
\Gamma.
]

The fundamental QRI programme is therefore

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P
\xrightarrow{\Gamma}
\mathcal A
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak A.
}
]

The precise mathematical form of (\Gamma) constitutes one of the principal research problems of the theory.

---

## 3. Ontological Commitments

QRI makes five primary ontological commitments.

### 3.1 Quantum states are physically meaningful

The quantum state is not regarded merely as a bookkeeping device for an observer's knowledge.

The state

[
\rho
]

represents the physically available quantum possibility structure of a system relative to its physical relational context.

---

### 3.2 Possibilities are not actualities

A component of a superposition may possess nonzero amplitude without corresponding to an independently realized physical history.

Thus

[
|\langle\phi_i|\psi\rangle|^2>0
]

establishes availability, not actuality.

---

### 3.3 Actuality is singular

For a completed event, QRI requires

[
\boxed{
\exists!,e_k
}
]

such that (e_k) is the actual outcome.

The alternatives

[
e_i,\qquad i\neq k
]

remain counterfactual possibilities rather than additional realized worlds.

---

### 3.4 Actualization is physical

Actualization is not attributed to:

* human consciousness,
* subjective observation,
* an arbitrary classical/quantum boundary,
* an externally imposed metaphysical observer.

It is instead hypothesized to arise from physical interaction and relational stabilization.

---

### 3.5 Reality forms one history

Let

[
h_n=(e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_n)
]

denote the sequence of actual events.

QRI postulates that there exists a unique physical history

[
\boxed{
h_{\mathrm{phys}}
}
]

rather than a set of simultaneously realized branches

[
{h_1,h_2,\ldots}.
]

---

# 4. What QRI Rejects

QRI is explicitly formulated as a non-Everettian interpretation.

It therefore rejects the ontological claim that decohered components of a universal quantum state should automatically be interpreted as independently existing worlds.

For

[
|\Psi\rangle
============

\sum_i c_i|\Psi_i\rangle,
]

QRI does **not** assert

[
\forall i,\quad
|\Psi_i\rangle
\text{ is an independently actual universe}.
]

Instead,

[
\boxed{
\forall i,\quad
|\Psi_i\rangle
\text{ may represent a physically available possibility},
}
]

while

[
\boxed{
\exists!k,\quad
|\Psi_k\rangle
\text{ is realized in the actual event history}.
}
]

QRI therefore distinguishes **branch-like mathematical decomposition** from **branching physical ontology**.

A decomposition of a state into components is a mathematical operation. It does not, by itself, establish the existence of multiple physical worlds.

---

# 5. What QRI Also Rejects

QRI does not attempt to recover classical determinism by introducing hidden variables

[
\lambda
]

that predetermine every quantum outcome.

It does not assume that

[
a=f(\lambda)
]

for some underlying classical state (\lambda).

Quantum indeterminacy remains fundamental.

The theory therefore occupies a distinct conceptual position:

[
\boxed{
\text{non-Everettian}
+
\text{non-classical}
+
\text{non-consciousness-dependent}.
}
]

The objective is not to restore a classical description beneath quantum mechanics, but to construct a different ontology **within the quantum framework**.

---

# 6. Possibility as a Physical Category

QRI elevates possibility from a merely epistemic concept to a formal physical category.

Given a density operator

[
\rho
]

and a measurement context represented by a POVM

[
\mathcal C={E_i},
]

define

[
\pi_i
=====

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho E_i).
]

The set

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P(\rho,\mathcal C)
============================

{(E_i,\pi_i)}
}
]

constitutes the corresponding quantum possibility structure.

The quantities (\pi_i) are the standard quantum weights, but QRI assigns them a specific ontological interpretation:

[
\pi_i
=====

\text{weight of possible actualization channel }i.
]

They do not represent ignorance about a definite classical value that already exists independently of the measurement context.

---

# 7. Actuality as an Event

QRI defines actuality through events rather than through components of a wavefunction.

An event is represented abstractly as

[
e_i
===

(S,O_i,\mathcal R_i,x_i),
]

where:

* (S) is the physical system,
* (O_i) is the realized outcome,
* (\mathcal R_i) is the relevant physical relation,
* (x_i) specifies the spacetime localization when applicable.

The actualization process is then

[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(\rho,\mathcal C,\mathcal R)
======================================

e_k.
}
]

Only one (e_k) belongs to the realized history for a completed measurement event.

---

# 8. Resonance as the Missing Physical Bridge

The defining innovation of QRI is the proposal that actualization is not an unexplained primitive transition but is associated with a physical condition of **resonance**.

Let

[
\Gamma_i
========

\Gamma
[
\rho,
E_i,
\mathcal R,
H,
E
]
]

represent the resonance associated with candidate outcome (i).

Here (E) denotes the relevant environmental degrees of freedom.

The resonance functional is intended to encode the compatibility between:

[
\text{quantum amplitude},
]

[
\text{phase structure},
]

[
\text{system-apparatus coupling},
]

[
\text{environmental correlation},
]

and

[
\text{persistence of the resulting record}.
]

The candidate actualization probability is then expressed generally as

[
P_i
===

\frac{\Gamma_i}
{\sum_j\Gamma_j}.
]

The fundamental QRI requirement is that this reduce to the Born rule:

[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_i}
{\sum_j\Gamma_j}
================

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho E_i).
}
]

This equation is not merely an interpretation of the Born rule. It defines the central **resonance-derivation problem** of QRI.

A complete dynamical QRI must explain why the physical resonance structure generates precisely the observed quantum probability measure.

---

# 9. The Central Ontological Transformation

The foundational QRI process can therefore be represented as

[
\boxed{
\text{Quantum State}
\rightarrow
\text{Possibility Structure}
\rightarrow
\text{Relational Resonance}
\rightarrow
\text{Actual Event}
\rightarrow
\text{Physical Record}.
}
]

Mathematically,

[
\rho
\overset{\mathcal P}{\longrightarrow}
\mathfrak P
\overset{\Gamma}{\longrightarrow}
{\Gamma_i}
\overset{\mathcal A}{\longrightarrow}
e_k
\overset{\mathcal R_{\rm rec}}{\longrightarrow}
r_k.
]

The event (e_k) is then incorporated into the unique physical history:

[
h_{n+1}
=======

h_n\cup{e_k}.
]

The resulting history determines the relational conditions under which subsequent quantum possibilities develop.

Hence QRI is intrinsically recursive:

[
\boxed{
\rho_n
\rightarrow
\mathfrak P_n
\rightarrow
e_n
\rightarrow
h_n
\rightarrow
\rho_{n+1}.
}
]

---

# 10. Foundational Thesis

The foundational thesis of QRI can therefore be stated in its strongest form:

> **The quantum state describes physically real possibilities and their relational structure; resonance governs the physical compatibility and stabilization of those possibilities; actualization produces one realized event; and the accumulation of actual events constitutes a single physical history.**

In compact mathematical form,

[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P_t
\xrightarrow{;\Gamma_t;}
\mathfrak A_t
\subset
h_{\mathrm{phys}}.
}
]

The central distinction is consequently:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{Multiplicity of possibility does not imply multiplicity of actuality.}
}
]

This distinction provides the foundational basis upon which the remaining QRI corpus—relational structure, resonance dynamics, measurement, decoherence, probability, entanglement, relativistic covariance, classical emergence, and experimental falsifiability—is constructed.
