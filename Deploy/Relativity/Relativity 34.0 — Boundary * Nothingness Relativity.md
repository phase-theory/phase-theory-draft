# Relativity 34.0 — Boundary / Nothingness Relativity  
## The Relativity of Beginnings, Endings, Edges, and Creation

**White paper / academic preprint**

## Abstract

Boundary / Nothingness Relativity is the hypothesis that boundaries, beginnings, endings, edges, and even “nothing” are not absolute features of reality but description-relative structures. In quantum cosmology, ordinary initial conditions are replaced by global boundary proposals. The Hartle–Hawking no-boundary wavefunction,

\[
\Psi_{\text{HH}}[h,\phi]
=
\int_{\partial M=(h,\phi)}
\mathcal{D}g\,\mathcal{D}\Phi
\,
e^{-I_E[g,\Phi]/\hbar},
\]

is defined by a path integral over compact regular geometries with no initial boundary. The Vilenkin tunneling proposal instead imposes an outgoing-wave condition, often interpreted as creation from “nothing.” Final-state proposals, holographic screens, causal horizons, and canonical quantum gravity further suggest that beginnings and endings are not fundamental edges of reality. In canonical quantum gravity, the Hamiltonian constraint,

\[
\hat{\mathcal{H}}\Psi=0,
\]

implies that the universe is not evolved from an external time but constrained as a whole. The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Boundaries are not edges of reality. They are features of a description.}
}
\]

Boundary / Nothingness Relativity unifies the no-boundary proposal, tunneling cosmology, final-state boundary conditions, holographic boundaries, causal horizons, cobordism, and the Wheeler–DeWitt constraint into a single framework. It implies that “creation from nothing” need not mean creation from philosophical non-being. It may mean: a description with no external boundary condition, no prior classical spacetime, and no external time.

## 1. Introduction

Physics has always been concerned with boundaries.

A physical system is separated from its environment. A spacetime region has a boundary. A field theory requires boundary conditions. A scattering experiment has past and future asymptotic boundaries. A black hole has a horizon. A cosmology has an initial singularity, a future infinity, or both.

But what is a boundary?

In ordinary thinking, a boundary is an edge: the place where something stops and something else begins. The universe begins at the Big Bang. A black hole ends at a singularity. A laboratory system ends at its walls. A path integral ends at its boundary data.

Boundary / Nothingness Relativity challenges this picture.

It says that boundaries are not absolute edges of reality. They are features of a description.

A boundary may be:

1. a mathematical cut introduced to define a subsystem,
2. a causal horizon relative to an observer,
3. a holographic screen encoding interior information,
4. a boundary condition selecting a quantum state,
5. a cobordism interface between spatial slices,
6. a semiclassical approximation to a boundaryless quantum geometry,
7. a relational limit of observational access.

Thus the beginning of the universe may not be an edge of time. It may be a boundary condition in a global quantum description.

The end of the universe may not be an absolute termination. It may be a horizon, a boundary condition, or a limit of a description.

Even “nothing” may not mean metaphysical non-being. It may mean the absence of classical spacetime, the absence of external boundary data, or the absence of an external time parameter.

This is Boundary / Nothingness Relativity.

## 2. What Is a Boundary?

Mathematically, a manifold \(M\) may have a boundary \(\partial M\).

For an \(n\)-dimensional manifold with boundary,

\[
\partial M
\]

is an \((n-1)\)-dimensional manifold.

In field theory, one specifies boundary data on \(\partial M\). For a scalar field \(\phi\), one may impose Dirichlet boundary conditions,

\[
\phi|_{\partial M}
=
\phi_0,
\]

or Neumann boundary conditions,

\[
n^\mu\partial_\mu\phi|_{\partial M}
=
0,
\]

where \(n^\mu\) is the outward normal.

But the choice of boundary is not intrinsic to reality. It is part of the model.

One may divide a larger system into subsystems by introducing an artificial boundary \(\Sigma\). The physics of the whole should not depend on where the cut is placed, provided the gluing is done consistently.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A boundary is often a cut in a description, not an edge of the world.}
}
\]

## 3. Boundaries in Classical Field Theory

In classical field theory, boundaries are needed to make variational principles well-defined.

The Einstein–Hilbert action is

\[
I_{\text{EH}}
=
\frac{1}{16\pi G}
\int_M
d^4x\sqrt{-g}
\left(
R-2\Lambda
\right).
\]

When varied, it produces not only the Einstein equations but also a boundary term.

To impose Dirichlet boundary conditions on the induced metric \(h_{ij}\), one adds the Gibbons–Hawking–York boundary term,

\[
I_{\text{GHY}}
=
\frac{1}{8\pi G}
\int_{\partial M}
d^3x\sqrt{h}
\,K,
\]

where:

- \(h_{ij}\) is the induced metric on \(\partial M\),
- \(h=\det h_{ij}\),
- \(K=h^{ij}K_{ij}\) is the trace of the extrinsic curvature.

The extrinsic curvature is

\[
K_{ij}
=
h_i{}^\mu h_j{}^\nu
\nabla_\mu n_\nu,
\]

with \(n^\mu\) the unit normal to the boundary.

The full gravitational action is

\[
I
=
I_{\text{EH}}
+
I_{\text{GHY}}
+
I_{\text{matter}}
+
I_{\text{ct}},
\]

where \(I_{\text{ct}}\) may include counterterms needed for finiteness in asymptotically anti-de Sitter or de Sitter spacetimes.

Thus even classical gravity requires careful treatment of boundaries.

But the boundary is not absolute. It is part of the variational setup.

## 4. Boundaries as Interfaces and Gluing

A boundary can be understood as an interface.

Suppose a manifold \(M\) is divided into two regions:

\[
M
=
M_1\cup M_2,
\]

with common interface

\[
\Sigma
=
M_1\cap M_2.
\]

The boundary of \(M_1\) includes \(\Sigma\), and the boundary of \(M_2\) includes \(\Sigma\) with opposite orientation.

The path integral over \(M\) should be obtainable by gluing the path integrals over \(M_1\) and \(M_2\):

\[
Z(M)
=
\int_{\Sigma}
\mathcal{D}\varphi_\Sigma
\,
Z_{M_1}[\varphi_\Sigma]
Z_{M_2}[\varphi_\Sigma].
\]

Thus \(\Sigma\) is not an edge of reality. It is a place where descriptions are glued.

This is a central idea of Boundary Relativity:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Boundaries are interfaces for composition, not terminations of being.}
}
\]

## 5. The Universe and the Problem of External Boundaries

For ordinary systems, boundaries are defined relative to an environment.

A gas is bounded by a container. A laboratory is bounded by walls. A star is bounded by its surface.

But the universe, by definition, has no external environment.

Thus the universe cannot have an external boundary in the ordinary sense.

If the universe is spatially closed, its spatial sections may be compact without boundary:

\[
\partial\Sigma
=
\varnothing.
\]

For example, a three-sphere \(S^3\) has no edge.

Yet cosmology still speaks of beginnings: the Big Bang, initial singularity, initial quantum state.

Boundary / Nothingness Relativity asks:

\[
\text{Is the beginning an edge of time, or a boundary condition in a global description?}
\]

Quantum cosmology suggests the latter.

## 6. Canonical Quantum Gravity and the Hamiltonian Constraint

In canonical general relativity, spacetime is foliated into spatial slices \(\Sigma_t\).

The metric is decomposed as

\[
ds^2
=
-N^2dt^2
+
h_{ij}
(dx^i+N^i dt)
(dx^j+N^j dt),
\]

where:

- \(N\) is the lapse,
- \(N^i\) is the shift,
- \(h_{ij}\) is the spatial metric.

The Einstein equations become constraints.

The Hamiltonian constraint is

\[
\mathcal{H}
=
0.
\]

In quantum gravity, this becomes the Wheeler–DeWitt equation,

\[
\hat{\mathcal{H}}\Psi=0.
\]

The wavefunction of the universe \(\Psi[h_{ij},\phi]\) does not evolve in an external time. It satisfies a timeless constraint.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The universe is not evolved from outside time. It is constrained as a whole.}
}
\]

This radically changes the meaning of beginning.

A beginning is not an initial moment in an external time. It is a boundary condition on the solution space of the constraint.

## 7. Minisuperspace and the Wheeler–DeWitt Equation

To make the Wheeler–DeWitt equation explicit, one often restricts to homogeneous and isotropic minisuperspace.

Consider a closed Friedmann–Robertson–Walker metric,

\[
ds^2
=
-N^2(t)dt^2
+
a^2(t)d\Omega_3^2,
\]

with scale factor \(a(t)\).

Including a cosmological constant \(\Lambda\), the Euclidean action is schematically

\[
I_E
=
\int dt
\left[
-
\frac{3a\dot a^2}{N}
-
3Na
+
N\Lambda a^3
\right],
\]

up to numerical factors and boundary terms.

The Hamiltonian constraint takes the form

\[
\mathcal{H}
=
-
\frac{p_a^2}{12a}
-
3a
+
\Lambda a^3
=
0.
\]

Quantization gives

\[
p_a
\rightarrow
-i\hbar\frac{d}{da}.
\]

The Wheeler–DeWitt equation becomes

\[
\left[
\frac{\hbar^2}{12a}
\frac{d^2}{da^2}
-
3a
+
\Lambda a^3
\right]
\Psi(a)
=
0,
\]

again up to factor-ordering choices.

This equation has no external time parameter.

The wavefunction \(\Psi(a)\) describes the universe as a whole.

Boundary proposals differ by how they select a solution \(\Psi(a)\).

## 8. The Hartle–Hawking No-Boundary Proposal

The Hartle–Hawking no-boundary proposal defines the wavefunction of the universe by a Euclidean path integral over compact regular geometries.

For boundary data \((h,\phi)\),

\[
\Psi_{\text{HH}}[h,\phi]
=
\int_{\partial M=(h,\phi)}
\mathcal{D}g\,\mathcal{D}\Phi
\,
e^{-I_E[g,\Phi]/\hbar}.
\]

The key condition is that the geometries are compact and regular. They have only one boundary: the final boundary on which the data \((h,\phi)\) are specified.

There is no initial boundary.

Thus the proposal is called “no-boundary.”

It does not say that the universe has no boundary whatsoever. It says that the universe has no initial boundary.

The beginning is not an edge. It is a smooth rounding-off of geometry in the Euclidean regime.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The no-boundary proposal replaces an initial edge with a regular boundary condition.}
}
\]

## 9. Semiclassical No-Boundary Wavefunction

In the semiclassical approximation, the path integral is dominated by saddle-point geometries.

One writes

\[
\Psi_{\text{HH}}
\sim
\sum_{\text{saddles}}
A
e^{-I_E/\hbar}.
\]

For a positive cosmological constant, a dominant saddle is approximately a Euclidean four-sphere.

The Euclidean action is roughly

\[
I_E
\sim
-
\frac{3}{8G\Lambda},
\]

up to sign conventions and normalization.

The wavefunction then behaves approximately as

\[
\Psi_{\text{HH}}
\sim
\exp
\left(
\frac{3}{8G\hbar\Lambda}
\right),
\]

depending on contour and sign conventions.

The no-boundary proposal favors smooth compact geometries and can provide a natural setting for inflation.

The precise interpretation of the contour, measure, and probability remains an active research problem.

But the conceptual lesson is clear:

\[
\text{The beginning is not a singular boundary but a regular quantum condition.}
\]

## 10. The Vilenkin Tunneling Proposal

The tunneling proposal, associated with Vilenkin, gives a different boundary condition.

It selects an outgoing wavefunction at large scale factor.

In WKB form,

\[
\Psi(a)
\sim
e^{\pm iS(a)/\hbar}.
\]

The tunneling proposal chooses the outgoing mode:

\[
\Psi_T(a)
\sim
e^{+iS(a)/\hbar}
\]

in the classically allowed region.

This is often interpreted as the universe tunneling from “nothing” into classical existence.

Here “nothing” does not mean philosophical non-being.

It means:

1. no classical spacetime,
2. no prior boundary,
3. no external time,
4. no pre-existing material substrate.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Creation from nothing means emergence from a boundaryless quantum description, not from metaphysical non-existence.}
}
\]

## 11. Comparing No-Boundary and Tunneling

The no-boundary and tunneling proposals differ in boundary conditions.

The no-boundary proposal imposes regularity at small scale factor.

The tunneling proposal imposes outgoing-wave behavior at large scale factor.

Their semiclassical probabilities can differ.

Schematically, one may write:

\[
P_{\text{HH}}
\sim
e^{-2I_E/\hbar},
\]

or

\[
P_T
\sim
e^{+2I_E/\hbar},
\]

depending on conventions.

The difference is not merely technical. It reflects different assumptions about what counts as a physically admissible boundary condition for the universe.

Boundary / Nothingness Relativity interprets this as follows:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Beginnings are not absolute events. They are boundary-condition choices within a global quantum description.}
}
\]

## 12. Final-State Proposals

If beginnings are boundary conditions, endings may be too.

Final-state proposals impose conditions at the future boundary rather than, or in addition to, the initial boundary.

In black-hole physics, Horowitz and Maldacena proposed a final-state boundary condition at the singularity. The black-hole interior is constrained by a final projection,

\[
\langle \Phi_{\text{final}}|,
\]

which can restore unitary evolution of exterior radiation.

In cosmology, one may consider a two-boundary amplitude,

\[
\mathcal{A}
=
\langle \Psi_f|
U
|\Psi_i\rangle,
\]

or, in a timeless formulation,

\[
\mathcal{A}
=
\langle \Psi_f|
\mathcal{P}
|\Psi_i\rangle,
\]

where \(\mathcal{P}\) projects onto solutions of the Hamiltonian constraint.

Thus the universe may be constrained by both initial and final conditions.

This connects Boundary / Nothingness Relativity to Time-Symmetric Relativity.

The arrow of time may arise from asymmetric boundary conditions, not from asymmetric fundamental laws.

## 13. Holographic Boundaries

Holography gives boundaries a new role.

In AdS/CFT, the boundary of anti-de Sitter space is not an edge where physics stops. It is the place where the dual field theory lives.

The dictionary is

\[
Z_{\text{CFT}}[J]
=
Z_{\text{gravity}}
\left[
\phi\big|_{\partial}=J
\right].
\]

Boundary sources \(J\) couple to boundary operators.

The bulk is encoded in boundary data.

Thus the boundary is not a limit of reality. It is an encoding surface.

Similarly, in de Sitter space, the cosmological horizon may function as a holographic screen. The static-patch observer has access only to degrees of freedom inside the horizon.

The entropy bound is

\[
S
\leq
\frac{A}{4G\hbar}.
\]

Thus boundaries and screens are not edges. They are informational interfaces.

## 14. Causal Horizons as Relative Boundaries

A causal horizon is a boundary relative to an observer.

An event horizon separates events that can signal to an observer from events that cannot.

A Rindler horizon appears for an accelerated observer in Minkowski space.

A de Sitter horizon appears for a static observer in de Sitter space.

A black-hole horizon appears for observers outside a black hole.

These horizons are not absolute material surfaces. They are observer-relative causal boundaries.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Horizons are boundaries defined by causal access.}
}
\]

Boundary / Nothingness Relativity generalizes this: all boundaries may be relative to a description, observer, coarse-graining, or causal frame.

## 15. Boundaries in Quantum Field Theory

Quantum field theory also reveals the relativity of boundaries.

A boundary condition may define a state.

For example, in conformal field theory, a boundary state \(\ket{B}\) satisfies

\[
\left(
L_n-\bar L_{-n}
\right)
\ket{B}
=
0.
\]

Boundary conditions can change the spectrum, produce Casimir energies, and define boundary renormalization-group flows.

The \(g\)-theorem states that boundary entropy decreases along boundary renormalization-group flows:

\[
g_{\text{UV}}
>
g_{\text{IR}}.
\]

Thus boundaries are physical structures, but they are not absolute edges. They are choices of interface, state, or termination condition.

## 16. Entanglement Cuts and Artificial Boundaries

In quantum many-body systems and quantum field theory, one often divides space into regions:

\[
\Sigma
=
A\cup \bar A.
\]

The boundary between \(A\) and \(\bar A\) is an entanglement cut.

The reduced density matrix is

\[
\rho_A
=
\operatorname{Tr}_{\bar A}
\ket{\Psi}\bra{\Psi}.
\]

The entanglement entropy is

\[
S_A
=
-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_A\ln\rho_A.
\]

The boundary of \(A\) controls the leading area-law contribution:

\[
S_A
\sim
\frac{\operatorname{Area}(\partial A)}{\epsilon^{d-2}}.
\]

But the cut is not fundamental. It is a choice of subsystem.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Many boundaries are entanglement cuts, not edges of reality.}
}
\]

## 17. Gauge Boundaries and Edge Modes

In gauge theories, introducing a boundary creates new degrees of freedom: edge modes.

Gauge transformations that are trivial in the bulk may become physical at the boundary.

The boundary Hilbert space factorization is subtle:

\[
\mathcal{H}
\neq
\mathcal{H}_A
\otimes
\mathcal{H}_{\bar A}

in a naive sense.

Edge modes restore consistency.

Thus the boundary is not merely a geometric surface. It is a physical interface with its own degrees of freedom.

But again, the boundary is introduced by the description.

## 18. Topology, Cobordism, and Boundarylessness

Topology provides a natural language for boundary relativity.

A manifold may be:

1. with boundary,
2. without boundary,
3. a cobordism between boundaries.

A cobordism \(M\) from \(\Sigma_i\) to \(\Sigma_f\) satisfies

\[
\partial M
=
\overline{\Sigma_i}
\sqcup
\Sigma_f.
\]

A closed manifold has no boundary:

\[
\partial M
=
\varnothing.
\]

The no-boundary proposal uses compact manifolds without initial boundary.

Thus the universe may be described by a boundaryless or partially boundaryless topology.

In topological quantum field theory, amplitudes are functors,

\[
Z:
\mathbf{Cob}_n
\to
\mathbf{Vect}.
\]

Boundaries are inputs and outputs, not absolute edges.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Topology treats boundaries as compositional structures.}
}
\]

## 19. Nothingness: A Taxonomy

The word “nothing” is ambiguous.

Boundary / Nothingness Relativity requires a taxonomy.

### 19.1 Empty Space

Empty Minkowski space is not nothing. It is a spacetime with a vacuum state.

### 19.2 Vacuum State

The quantum vacuum is not nothing. It is a state with fluctuations, entanglement, and symmetry structure.

### 19.3 No Classical Spacetime

A quantum gravitational regime may have no classical geometry. This is closer to physical “nothing.”

### 19.4 No Boundary Condition

A no-boundary proposal has no initial boundary. This is a specific technical meaning of “no-boundary.”

### 19.5 No External Time

In canonical quantum gravity, there is no external time parameter. The universe is constrained as a whole.

### 19.6 Philosophical Absolute Nothing

Absolute non-being is not a physical state. Physics cannot describe absolute nothingness, because description already introduces structure.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical “nothing” means absence of classical spacetime or boundary data, not metaphysical non-being.}
}
\]

## 20. Creation from Nothing

The phrase “creation from nothing” is often misunderstood.

In Boundary / Nothingness Relativity, it means one or more of the following:

1. There is no prior classical spacetime.
2. There is no external time in which creation occurs.
3. There is no external cause or environment.
4. There is no initial boundary condition.
5. The universe is defined by a global quantum constraint or path integral.

It does not mean that being arises from absolute non-being.

The path integral still has:

- a measure,
- an action,
- a class of geometries,
- boundary data at the final surface,
- quantum laws.

Thus “nothing” is not lawless non-existence. It is the absence of a classical prior.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Creation from nothing is emergence from a boundaryless quantum structure.}
}
\]

## 24. Formal Framework of Boundary Relativity

Let \(D\) be a physical description.

A boundary in \(D\) is a structure

\[
B_D
=
(\Sigma_D, \mathcal{B}_D, \mathcal{C}_D),
\]

where:

- \(\Sigma_D\) is a geometric or causal surface,
- \(\mathcal{B}_D\) is boundary data,
- \(\mathcal{C}_D\) is a boundary condition or constraint.

Two descriptions \(D\) and \(D'\) are boundary-equivalent if their physical predictions agree after gluing or transformation:

\[
P_D(O)
=
P_{D'}(O').
\]

A boundary is absolute only if it is invariant under all admissible description changes.

Most boundaries are not.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical boundaries are equivalence-class features, not primitive edges.}
}
\]

## 25. Axioms of Boundary / Nothingness Relativity

The framework may be organized around twelve axioms.

### Axiom 1: Boundaries Are Description-Relative

A boundary is part of a model, not necessarily part of reality itself.

### Axiom 2: Beginnings Are Boundary Conditions

A beginning is not necessarily an edge of time. It may be a regularity or tunneling condition.

### Axiom 3: Endings Are Boundary Conditions

An ending may be a final constraint, horizon, or limit of description.

### Axiom 4: The Universe Has No External Boundary

The universe as a whole cannot be bounded by an external environment.

### Axiom 5: The Universe Is Constrained as a Whole

Canonical quantum gravity describes the universe by constraints, not external evolution.

### Axiom 6: Nothing Is Not Absolute Non-Being

Physical nothingness means absence of classical spacetime or boundary data.

### Axiom 7: Holographic Boundaries Encode Interiors

Boundaries may be encoding surfaces rather than edges.

### Axiom 8: Horizons Are Observer-Relative Boundaries

Causal boundaries depend on observer access.

### Axiom 9: Entanglement Cuts Are Artificial Boundaries

Subsystem boundaries are choices, not fundamental divisions.

### Axiom 10: Gluing Must Preserve Physics

Physical predictions must be invariant under admissible changes of boundary cut.

### Axiom 11: Boundarylessness Is Possible

A complete description may have no initial boundary.

### Axiom 12: Creation Is Emergence

Creation from nothing means emergence from a boundaryless quantum structure.
