# Relativistic Dynamics: Equivariant Flows, Quotient Trajectories, and the Evolution of Physical Orbits

## Abstract

Relativistic Dynamics is the derived theory of Relativity Mechanics that addresses the primitive structural question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How do physical states evolve?}
}
\]

In standard formulations, dynamics is prescribed as a differential equation $\dot{x} = f(x)$ on a space of configurations, fields, or states. However, if physical reality is constituted by equivalence classes of admissible descriptions (orbits), then physical evolution cannot depend on the choice of representative. The dynamical law must be defined directly on the invariant quotient space $Q = \Omega/G$. 

This paper develops the rigorous mathematical framework of Relativistic Dynamics. We demonstrate that a dynamical law on the description space $\Omega$ is physically meaningful if and only if its generating vector field is equivariant with respect to the admissibility group $G$. Equivariant vector fields descend uniquely to the quotient manifold, defining physical trajectories as orbit trajectories. We formalize invariant Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics, Noether's theorem, constrained dynamics, and relational time. We show that the physical dynamics of Newtonian mechanics, general relativity, gauge theories, and quantum mechanics are all specializations of quotient flows. 

The central principle is established:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical evolution is the motion of orbits, not the motion of representatives.}
}
\]

## 1. Introduction

The architecture of Relativity Mechanics identifies the physical object with an orbit $[\omega] = G \cdot \omega$ in a description space $\Omega$ under an admissibility group $G$. Invariant Theory establishes that measurable quantities are functions on the quotient space $Q = \Omega/G$. Observer Theory and Reference Transformation Theory govern how these invariants are accessed and related. 

Relativistic Dynamics now addresses the temporal or evolutionary structure of the theory. If the physical state is an orbit, then the physical history must be a sequence of orbits. A naive differential equation $\dot{\omega} = X(\omega)$ on the description space $\Omega$ generally fails to define a physical evolution, because the velocity vector $X(\omega)$ may depend on the arbitrary descriptive choices encoded in $\omega$. 

For evolution to be physically well-defined, the flow generated by $X$ must commute with the admissibility action. When this condition is met, the vector field descends to a unique vector field $\bar{X}$ on the quotient space $Q$. The physical trajectory is then an integral curve of $\bar{X}$ on $Q$. 

Thus, Relativistic Dynamics replaces the classical paradigm of configuration-space trajectories with the paradigm of orbit trajectories.

## 2. Equivariant Vector Fields and Descent to the Quotient

Let $\mathcal{R} = (\Omega, G, \triangleright, I)$ be a Relativity Mechanics schema, where $I: \Omega \to Q = \Omega/G$ is the invariant projection. We assume $\Omega$ is a smooth manifold and $G$ is a Lie group acting smoothly and properly on $\Omega$.

### 2.1 The Condition for Physical Evolution

A dynamical law on $\Omega$ is specified by a vector field $X \in \Gamma(T\Omega)$. The evolution of a description is given by the integral curve $\omega(t)$ satisfying:

\[
\frac{d\omega}{dt} = X(\omega).
\]

Let $\Phi_t: \Omega \to \Omega$ be the flow of $X$. For this evolution to represent a physical process, the physical state at time $t$ must be independent of the initial representative. That is, if two descriptions $\omega$ and $\omega' = g \cdot \omega$ represent the same physical object, their evolutions must remain in the same orbit for all $t$:

\[
[\Phi_t(g \cdot \omega)] = [\Phi_t(\omega)].
\]

More strongly, the evolution must commute with the admissibility action:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t(g \cdot \omega) = g \cdot \Phi_t(\omega), \quad \forall g \in G, \, \forall t.
}
\]

### 2.2 Infinitesimal Equivariance

Differentiating the commutation condition with respect to $t$ at $t=0$ yields the infinitesimal condition. Let $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$ be a Lie algebra element, and let $\xi_\Omega \in \Gamma(T\Omega)$ be its fundamental vector field:

\[
\xi_\Omega(\omega) = \left. \frac{d}{ds} \right|_{s=0} \exp(s\xi) \cdot \omega.
\]

The commutation of the flows implies that the Lie bracket of $X$ and $\xi_\Omega$ vanishes:

\[
\boxed{
[X, \xi_\Omega] = 0, \quad \forall \xi \in \mathfrak{g}.
}
\]

Equivalently, the Lie derivative of $X$ along the gauge directions vanishes: $\mathcal{L}_{\xi_\Omega} X = 0$. A vector field satisfying this condition is called **$G$-equivariant**. In terms of the pushforward $(g_*)_\omega : T_\omega\Omega \to T_{g\cdot\omega}\Omega$, equivariance is written as:

\[
X(g \cdot \omega) = (g_*)_\omega X(\omega).
\]

### 2.3 The Descent Theorem

**Theorem 2.1 (Descent of Dynamics).**  
Let $X \in \Gamma(T\Omega)$ be a $G$-equivariant vector field. Then there exists a unique vector field $\bar{X} \in \Gamma(TQ)$ on the quotient space $Q = \Omega/G$ such that:

\[
I_* X = \bar{X} \circ I.
\]

**Proof.**  
Since $I$ is a surjective submersion (assuming a free and proper action), the pushforward $I_*$ is surjective on each tangent space. We define $\bar{X}$ at a point $q = [\omega] \in Q$ by:

\[
\bar{X}(q) = (I_*)_\omega X(\omega).
\]

We must show this is independent of the representative $\omega$. Let $\omega' = g \cdot \omega$. Then:

\[
(I_*)_{\omega'} X(\omega') = (I_*)_{g \cdot \omega} \left( (g_*)_\omega X(\omega) \right).
\]

Since $I \circ g = I$ for all $g \in G$, the chain rule gives $(I_*)_{g \cdot \omega} \circ (g_*)_\omega = (I_*)_\omega$. Therefore:

\[
(I_*)_{\omega'} X(\omega') = (I_*)_\omega X(\omega) = \bar{X}(q).
\]

Thus $\bar{X}$ is well-defined. Uniqueness follows from the surjectivity of $I_*$. $\square$

The physical trajectory is the integral curve $q(t) = I(\omega(t))$ on $Q$, satisfying the quotient dynamical equation:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dq}{dt} = \bar{X}(q).
}
\]

## 3. Invariant Lagrangian Dynamics

In the Lagrangian formulation, the description space is the tangent bundle $\Omega = T\mathcal{C}$ of a configuration manifold $\mathcal{C}$. The admissibility group $G$ acts on $\mathcal{C}$, and this action lifts naturally to $T\mathcal{C}$.

### 3.1 Invariant Action Principle

Let $L: T\mathcal{C} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a Lagrangian. The action functional is:

\[
S[\omega] = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} L(\omega, \dot{\omega}) \, dt.
\]

The Lagrangian is admissible if the action is invariant under $G$ up to a boundary term:

\[
L(g \cdot \omega, g_* \dot{\omega}) = L(\omega, \dot{\omega}) + \frac{d}{dt} F_g(\omega, t).
\]

If $F_g = 0$, the Lagrangian is strictly invariant. By Hamilton's principle, the Euler-Lagrange equations are equivariant. If $\omega(t)$ is a solution, then $g \cdot \omega(t)$ is also a solution. 

### 3.2 Lagrangian Reduction

If the action of $G$ on $\mathcal{C}$ is free and proper, the quotient $\mathcal{C}/G$ is a smooth manifold. The tangent bundle $T(\mathcal{C}/G)$ is naturally identified with $(T\mathcal{C})/G$. 

A strictly invariant Lagrangian $L$ descends to a reduced Lagrangian $\bar{L}: T(\mathcal{C}/G) \to \mathbb{R}$. The Euler-Lagrange equations for $\bar{L}$ on the quotient space yield the physical dynamics directly, without reference to the redundant variables in $\mathcal{C}$. This procedure is known as **Lagrangian reduction** (or Routh reduction when cyclic variables are partially eliminated).

## 4. Hamiltonian Dynamics and Symplectic Reduction

In the Hamiltonian formulation, the description space $\Omega$ is a symplectic manifold $(\Omega, \omega_{symp})$. The admissibility group $G$ acts on $\Omega$ by symplectomorphisms.

### 4.1 Invariant Hamiltonians

Let $H: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ be the Hamiltonian. The Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$ is defined by:

\[
\iota_{X_H} \omega_{symp} = dH.
\]

If $H$ is $G$-invariant, i.e., $H(g \cdot \omega) = H(\omega)$, then $dH$ is invariant. Since $\omega_{symp}$ is invariant, $X_H$ is necessarily $G$-equivariant:

\[
[X_H, \xi_\Omega] = 0, \quad \forall \xi \in \mathfrak{g}.
\]

Thus, an invariant Hamiltonian automatically generates a physical flow on the quotient.

### 4.2 Marsden-Weinstein Reduction

If the $G$-action is Hamiltonian, there exists a moment map $\mu: \Omega \to \mathfrak{g}^*$ satisfying:

\[
\iota_{\xi_\Omega} \omega_{symp} = d\mu_\xi, \quad \text{where } \mu_\xi(\omega) = \langle \mu(\omega), \xi \rangle.
\]

The equivariance of $X_H$ implies that the moment map is conserved:

\[
\frac{d\mu_\xi}{dt} = \{ \mu_\xi, H \} = \mathcal{L}_{X_H} \mu_\xi = 0.
\]

To obtain the true physical phase space, one restricts to a level set of the moment map (typically $\mu^{-1}(0)$) and quotients by the group action. The **reduced phase space** is:

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_{phys} = \mu^{-1}(0) / G.
}
\]

By the Marsden-Weinstein theorem, $\Omega_{phys}$ inherits a unique reduced symplectic form $\omega_{red}$ such that:

\[
i^* \omega_{symp} = \pi^* \omega_{red},
\]

where $i: \mu^{-1}(0) \hookrightarrow \Omega$ is the inclusion and $\pi: \mu^{-1}(0) \to \Omega_{phys}$ is the projection. The invariant Hamiltonian $H$ descends to $H_{red}: \Omega_{phys} \to \mathbb{R}$, generating the physical dynamics on the reduced space.

## 5. Noether's Theorem and the Nature of Symmetries

Relativistic Dynamics clarifies the distinction between descriptive redundancy and physical symmetry through the moment map.

### 5.1 Admissibility vs. Physical Symmetry

Let $G_{adm}$ be the admissibility group (e.g., gauge transformations, diffeomorphisms) and $G_{sym}$ be a physical symmetry group (e.g., Poincaré group, global flavor symmetries).

1. **Admissibility (Gauge):** The transformations in $G_{adm}$ relate descriptions of the *same* physical state. The orbits of $G_{adm}$ are the physical states. The moment map components $\mu_\xi$ for $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}_{adm}$ are not physical observables; they are **constraints** that must vanish on the physical phase space:
   \[
   \mu_\xi \approx 0.
   \]
   The conserved quantities associated with gauge symmetries are trivial (zero).

2. **Physical Symmetry:** The transformations in $G_{sym}$ map one physical state to a *different* physical state. The moment map components $\mu_\xi$ for $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}_{sym}$ are non-trivial, gauge-invariant observables. They are the **Noether charges** (e.g., energy, momentum, angular momentum):
   \[
   Q_\xi = \mu_\xi.
   \]

Thus, Noether's theorem in Relativity Mechanics states that physical conservation laws arise from symmetries that act *non-trivially* on the quotient space $\Omega/G_{adm}$.

## 6. Constrained Dynamics and Gauge Orbits

In many physical theories, the Lagrangian is singular, leading to primary constraints. The Dirac-Bergmann algorithm systematically identifies the admissibility group and the physical dynamics.

### 6.1 First-Class Constraints as Admissibility Generators

Let $\phi_a \approx 0$ be the constraints on the phase space $\Omega$. If they close under the Poisson bracket:

\[
\{ \phi_a, \phi_b \} = f_{ab}^c \phi_c,
\]

they are first-class. The total Hamiltonian is:

\[
H_T = H_0 + \lambda^a \phi_a,
\]

where $\lambda^a$ are arbitrary Lagrange multipliers. 

The vector fields generated by $\phi_a$ are tangent to the gauge orbits. Evolution along these directions corresponds to changing the descriptive representative without changing the physical state. The physical trajectory is the projection of the total flow onto the reduced phase space $\Omega_{phys}$.

### 6.2 Evolution of Observables

A physical observable $O$ must be gauge-invariant: $\{O, \phi_a\} \approx 0$. Its evolution is given by:

\[
\dot{O} = \{O, H_T\} \approx \{O, H_0\}.
\]

The arbitrary multipliers $\lambda^a$ drop out, confirming that the physical evolution on the quotient is deterministic and independent of the gauge choice.

## 7. The Problem of Time and Relational Evolution

In generally covariant theories, such as General Relativity, the Hamiltonian is entirely composed of constraints:

\[
H = \int d^3x \left( N \mathcal{H} + N^i \mathcal{H}_i \right) \approx 0.
\]

Here, $\mathcal{H}$ is the Hamiltonian constraint (generating time diffeomorphisms) and $\mathcal{H}_i$ are the momentum constraints (generating spatial diffeomorphisms). 

### 7.1 The Frozen Formalism

For any Dirac observable $O$ (an observable commuting with all constraints), the evolution equation yields:

\[
\dot{O} = \{O, H\} \approx 0.
\]

This suggests that nothing evolves—the "Problem of Time."

### 7.2 Relational Resolution

Relativistic Dynamics resolves this by recognizing that evolution is not a flow with respect to an external, unphysical parameter $t$. Instead, evolution is a **relational trajectory** in the physical quotient space (Superspace).

To describe evolution, one selects a physical variable $T$ to serve as an internal clock. The evolution of another observable $O$ is defined relationally:

\[
\boxed{
O(\tau) = O \big|_{T = \tau}.
}
\]

The physical history is a one-dimensional curve in the reduced phase space $\Omega_{phys}$, parameterized by the physical reading of the clock $T$. The "frozen" Hamiltonian constraint simply enforces that the physical trajectory is a valid geodesic or dynamical path in Superspace, rather than generating flow in an external time.

## 8. Relativistic Dynamics in Fundamental Theories

### 8.1 Newtonian Mechanics
The description space is the space of particle trajectories. The admissibility group is the Galilei group. The quotient space is the space of relative configurations. The physical dynamics is the evolution of relative separations $\mathbf{r}_{ij}(t)$, governed by the reduced Lagrangian. The center-of-mass motion is pure gauge and is quotiented out.

### 8.2 General Relativity
The description space is the space of Lorentzian metrics $\text{Met}(M)$. The admissibility group is $\text{Diff}(M)$. The quotient space is **Superspace**, $\mathcal{S} = \text{Met}(M)/\text{Diff}(M)$. The physical dynamics is the trajectory of the 3-geometry through Superspace, governed by the Einstein-Hilbert action. The Hamiltonian constraint ensures the trajectory is extremal in Superspace.

### 8.3 Gauge Theories (Yang-Mills)
The description space is the space of connections $\mathcal{A}$. The admissibility group is the gauge group $\mathcal{G}$. The quotient space is the moduli space of connections $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}$. The physical dynamics is the evolution of gauge-invariant observables, such as Wilson loops $W_R(\gamma)$, governed by the Yang-Mills Hamiltonian on the reduced phase space.

### 8.4 Quantum Mechanics
The description space is the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} \setminus \{0\}$. The admissibility group is $U(1)$ (global phase). The quotient space is the projective Hilbert space $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$. 
The Schrödinger equation $i\hbar \dot{\psi} = \hat{H}\psi$ generates the vector field $X_H(\psi) = -\frac{i}{\hbar} \hat{H}\psi$. This vector field is $U(1)$-equivariant. It descends to a Hamiltonian vector field $\bar{X}_H$ on $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$ with respect to the Fubini-Study symplectic form. The physical quantum trajectory is the flow of the ray $[\psi(t)]$ in projective space.

## 9. Conclusion

Relativistic Dynamics provides the definitive mathematical formulation of temporal evolution in relational physics. By demanding that dynamical laws be independent of descriptive redundancy, we are led to the requirement of equivariant vector fields. These vector fields descend uniquely to the quotient space $\Omega/G$, defining physical trajectories as orbit trajectories.

This framework unifies the dynamical principles of classical mechanics, field theory, general relativity, and quantum mechanics. It resolves the problem of time by redefining evolution as relational correlation rather than external flow. It clarifies the role of constraints as generators of descriptive redundancy, and distinguishes them from physical symmetries that generate conserved Noether charges.

The central principle of Relativistic Dynamics is absolute:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical evolution is the motion of orbits, not the motion of representatives.}
}
\]

In this formulation, the universe does not evolve through a sequence of coordinate configurations or gauge potentials. It evolves through a sequence of invariant equivalence classes, tracing a definitive, observer-independent trajectory through the space of physical reality.
