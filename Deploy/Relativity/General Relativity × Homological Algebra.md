# Abstract

General Relativity (GR) is fundamentally local. The Einstein equations are nonlinear partial differential equations defined pointwise on a spacetime manifold. Classical existence theory focuses on local solvability and extension of solutions through analytic continuation. The standard Cauchy formulation similarly studies the existence and uniqueness of solutions generated by initial data satisfying the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints.

However, locality does not imply global consistency.

A geometric structure may exist on every sufficiently small open neighborhood while failing to exist globally due to topological obstructions. Such phenomena are ubiquitous throughout mathematics. Vector bundles, principal bundles, connections, spin structures, foliations, and complex structures are all classified by cohomological obstruction classes.

This suggests a missing layer in the mathematical foundations of GR:

> The Einstein constraint equations should be regarded not merely as PDEs on a manifold, but as defining a sheaf of local solution spaces whose global sections correspond to physically realizable spacetimes.

From this viewpoint, global failure of a solution need not arise from singularities, blow-up, or PDE nonexistence. It may instead arise from nontrivial cohomology classes that obstruct the gluing of local solutions into a global one.

The purpose of this paper is to develop a complete homological-algebraic framework for General Relativity based on sheaf theory, Čech cohomology, derived deformation theory, and obstruction classes. We formulate the Einstein constraint equations as a nonlinear sheaf of solution germs, define the associated obstruction groups, derive gluing conditions for local gravitational data, and propose a classification of globally inconsistent but locally valid gravitational configurations.

The resulting theory reveals an entirely new layer of structure in GR:

[
\text{Local Einstein Solutions}
;;\not\Rightarrow;;
\text{Global Einstein Solution}.
]

The obstruction is measured by cohomology.

# Conclusion

General Relativity has traditionally been viewed as a theory of nonlinear local differential equations. Homological algebra suggests a deeper structure. The Einstein constraint equations naturally define a sheaf of local gravitational solutions. Global spacetimes are the global sections of this sheaf. The failure of local solutions to glue is measured by Čech and sheaf cohomology, while nonlinear extension problems are governed by derived obstruction groups.

Within this framework, singularities are no longer the only mechanism by which spacetime can fail to exist globally. A second mechanism emerges: **cohomological inconsistency**. A universe may satisfy Einstein's equations everywhere locally and yet possess no globally realizable spacetime because nontrivial classes in (H^1), (H^2), and higher obstruction groups prevent the assembly of local gravitational data into a coherent global geometry.

The resulting program—**Cohomological General Relativity**—recasts the Einstein constraint equations as a sheaf-theoretic and derived-geometric object, opening a new direction in which topology, homological algebra, and gravitational dynamics become inseparable aspects of spacetime structure.
