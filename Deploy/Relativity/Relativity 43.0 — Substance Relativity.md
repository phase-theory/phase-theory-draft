# Relativity 43.0 — Substance Relativity  
## The Frame-Relativity of Particles, Fields, Strings, Qubits, and Physical “Things”

**White paper / academic preprint**

---

## Abstract

Substance Relativity is the hypothesis that particles, fields, strings, branes, qubits, and other physical “things” are not fundamental substances. They are effective representations relative to a description, scale, phase, observer, or categorical frame. What is invariant is not the substance but the relational and processual structure preserved under admissible transformations. In different descriptions, the same physical content may be represented as a particle, a field excitation, a string mode, a brane intersection, a quasiparticle, a detector click, or an information pattern:

\[
\text{particle}
\sim
\text{field excitation}
\sim
\text{string mode}
\sim
\text{information pattern}.
\]

Different ontologies may preserve the same observable algebra. The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Substance is frame-relative; only invariant process-structure is physical.}
}
\]

Substance Relativity is the ontological completion of gauge relativity, duality relativity, semantic relativity, and meta-relativity. Gauge theory shows that potentials are not substances but representatives of equivalence classes. Duality shows that different fundamental objects can describe the same physics. Meta-relativity shows that descriptions are quotiented by equivalence. Substance Relativity extends this to ontology itself: the entities of a theory are not absolute furniture of reality. They are stable patterns in an invariant process-structure.

## 1. Introduction

Physics has repeatedly changed its answer to the question:

\[
\text{What are the basic things?}
\]

Ancient atomism said: atoms and void.

Newtonian mechanics said: particles in absolute space and time.

Faraday and Maxwell said: fields.

Einstein said: geometry.

Quantum field theory said: operator-valued fields and particle excitations.

String theory said: strings and branes.

Quantum information theory says: qubits, channels, and entanglement patterns.

Each of these ontologies has been successful within a domain.

Substance Relativity draws a radical conclusion from this history:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No physical “thing” is absolutely fundamental.}
}
\]

Particles are not little substances. Fields are not absolute substances. Strings are not absolute substances. Qubits are not absolute substances.

They are effective entities within a frame.

What is physical is not the entity considered as a substance. What is physical is the invariant relational and processual structure preserved across frames.

This is Substance Relativity.

## 2. Substance Ontology and Its Problems

A substance ontology holds that reality consists of basic things that possess properties.

For example:

\[
\text{electron}
=
\text{a substance with mass, charge, spin}.
\]

The substance is the bearer of properties.

But modern physics undermines this picture.

In gauge theory, the vector potential \(A_\mu\) is not uniquely physical. It is defined only up to gauge transformations:

\[
A_\mu
\rightarrow
A_\mu+\partial_\mu\lambda.
\]

The physical object is the gauge orbit,

\[
[A_\mu].
\]

In quantum field theory, particles are not persistent little objects. They are excitations of fields, and even the particle concept depends on the choice of vacuum and observer.

In string theory, what appears as a particle may be a vibrational mode of a string.

In holography, what appears as a bulk particle may be a boundary operator excitation.

In condensed matter, what appears as a particle may be a quasiparticle: a collective excitation of a many-body system.

Thus the “thing” is not absolute.

It is representation-relative.

## 3. The Core Principle

Substance Relativity asserts:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Substance is frame-relative; only invariant process-structure is physical.}
}
\]

A frame may be:

1. a scale,
2. a phase,
3. a theory,
4. a representation,
5. a gauge choice,
6. a duality frame,
7. an observer,
8. a measurement context,
9. a categorical semantics,
10. an information-theoretic encoding.

Within a frame, entities appear as substances.

Across frames, those entities transform into different entities.

The invariant is not the entity. It is the structure of relations, processes, observables, and correlations.

## 4. Formal Framework: Descriptions, Entities, and Observables

Let a physical description be a tuple,

\[
D
=
\left(
\mathcal{E}_D,
\mathcal{A}_D,
\omega_D,
P_D
\right),
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal{E}_D\) is an ontology of entities,
- \(\mathcal{A}_D\) is an algebra of observables,
- \(\omega_D\) is a state or state space,
- \(P_D\) is a probability rule.

The ontology \(\mathcal{E}_D\) contains the “things” of the description:

\[
\mathcal{E}_D
=
\{
\text{particles},
\text{fields},
\text{strings},
\text{branes},
\text{qubits},
\ldots
\}.
\]

The observable algebra \(\mathcal{A}_D\) contains the measurable quantities.

Two descriptions \(D\) and \(D'\) are physically equivalent if there exists an isomorphism of observable algebras,

\[
\Phi:
\mathcal{A}_D
\rightarrow
\mathcal{A}_{D'},
\]

preserving states and probabilities:

\[
\omega_D(O)
=
\omega_{D'}(\Phi(O)).
\]

The entities in \(\mathcal{E}_D\) and \(\mathcal{E}_{D'}\) need not be the same.

They may be different ontological presentations of the same observable structure.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Entities are description-relative; observable structure is invariant.}
}
\]

## 5. Substance Equivalence

Define substance equivalence as follows.

An entity \(e\in\mathcal{E}_D\) and an entity \(e'\in\mathcal{E}_{D'}\) are substance-equivalent,

\[
e\sim e',
\]

if they induce the same observable structure:

\[
\mathcal{A}_D(e)
\cong
\mathcal{A}_{D'}(e').
\]

For example:

\[
\text{photon as particle}
\sim
\text{photon as electromagnetic field excitation}.
\]

Similarly,

\[
\text{graviton as particle}
\sim
\text{graviton as metric perturbation}
\sim
\text{graviton as closed-string mode}.
\]

The substance changes. The invariant structure remains.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A physical object is an equivalence class of substance presentations.}
}
\]

## 6. Particles as Effective Substances

In classical mechanics, a particle is a pointlike substance with position and momentum:

\[
(q^i,p_i).
\]

In quantum mechanics, a particle is represented by a wavefunction:

\[
\psi(x).
\]

In relativistic quantum theory, a particle is an irreducible representation of the Poincaré group.

The Casimir invariants are:

\[
P_\mu P^\mu
=
m^2c^2,
\]

\[
W_\mu W^\mu
=
-m^2c^2s(s+1)\hbar^2,
\]

where \(W_\mu\) is the Pauli–Lubanski vector.

Thus a particle is not a little object. It is a representation-theoretic structure.

This is already Substance Relativity.

The “particle” is a stable pattern in the representation theory of spacetime symmetry.

## 7. Particles as Field Excitations

In quantum field theory, particles are excitations of fields.

A scalar field may be expanded as

\[
\hat{\phi}(x)
=
\int
\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}}
\left(
a_{\mathbf{p}}e^{-ip\cdot x}
+
a_{\mathbf{p}}^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}
\right).
\]

The vacuum satisfies

\[
a_{\mathbf{p}}\ket{0}=0.
\]

A one-particle state is

\[
\ket{\mathbf{p}}
=
a_{\mathbf{p}}^\dagger\ket{0}.
\]

Thus the particle is not fundamental. It is a quantum of the field.

The field itself is not a classical substance either. It is an operator-valued distribution.

Thus the substance has already dissolved into algebraic structure.

## 8. Observer-Relative Particles

The particle concept is not only theory-relative. It is observer-relative.

In curved spacetime or accelerated frames, different observers define different vacua.

A field expansion in one basis is

\[
\hat{\phi}
=
\sum_i
\left(
a_i u_i
+
a_i^\dagger u_i^*
\right).
\]

Another observer uses another basis:

\[
\hat{\phi}
=
\sum_j
\left(
b_j v_j
+
b_j^\dagger v_j^*
\right).
\]

The operators are related by a Bogoliubov transformation:

\[
b_j
=
\sum_i
\left(
\alpha_{ji}a_i
+
\beta_{ji}a_i^\dagger
\right).
\]

If \(\beta_{ji}\neq 0\), then the vacuum of one observer contains particles for another:

\[
b_j\ket{0_a}
\neq
0.
\]

This is the mathematical basis of the Unruh effect and Hawking radiation.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Particles are not absolute substances. They are observer-relative excitations.}
}
\]

## 9. Algebraic Quantum Field Theory and the Primacy of Observables

Algebraic quantum field theory makes Substance Relativity precise.

Instead of beginning with particles or fields, one begins with local observable algebras.

To each spacetime region \(\mathcal{O}\), one assigns an algebra,

\[
\mathcal{O}
\mapsto
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}).
\]

The algebras satisfy isotony:

\[
\mathcal{O}_1\subseteq\mathcal{O}_2
\quad
\Rightarrow
\quad
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}_1)
\subseteq
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}_2).
\]

They satisfy locality:

\[
[\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}_1),\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}_2)]
=
0
\]

when \(\mathcal{O}_1\) and \(\mathcal{O}_2\) are spacelike separated.

A state is a positive linear functional,

\[
\omega:
\mathcal{A}
\rightarrow
\mathbb{C}.
\]

The Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction gives a Hilbert-space representation:

\[
(\mathcal{H}_\omega,\pi_\omega,\Omega_\omega).
\]

Particles may appear in certain representations, especially asymptotically.

But the invariant structure is the net of observable algebras.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The observable algebra is more fundamental than the particle ontology.}
}
\]

## 10. Gauge Theory and the Dissolution of Field Substance

Gauge theory further dissolves substance.

The electromagnetic potential \(A_\mu\) appears to be a field-substance.

But gauge transformations show that \(A_\mu\) is not uniquely physical:

\[
A_\mu
\sim
A_\mu+\partial_\mu\lambda.
\]

The physical object is the equivalence class,

\[
[A_\mu].
\]

Gauge-invariant observables include the field strength,

\[
F_{\mu\nu}
=
\partial_\mu A_\nu
-
\partial_\nu A_\mu,
\]

and Wilson loops,

\[
W(C)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\mathcal{P}
\exp
\oint_C A.
\]

Thus the electromagnetic field is not a substance.

It is a gauge-invariant relational structure.

The same applies to nonabelian gauge fields:

\[
A_\mu
\rightarrow
g^{-1}A_\mu g
+
g^{-1}\partial_\mu g.
\]

The physical reality is the gauge orbit.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Gauge fields are not substances. They are equivalence classes of connections.}
}
\]

## 11. Strings, Branes, and Ontological Democracy

String theory replaces point particles with one-dimensional extended objects.

A string is described by embedding coordinates,

\[
X^\mu(\sigma,\tau).
\]

Its vibrational modes give rise to particles.

The mass spectrum is schematically

\[
M^2
=
\frac{1}{\alpha'}
\left(
N-a
\right),
\]

where \(N\) is an excitation number and \(a\) is a normal-ordering constant.

The graviton appears as a closed-string excitation.

Gauge bosons appear as open-string excitations.

Thus particles are string modes.

But string theory also contains branes: extended objects of various dimensions.

Open strings can end on D-branes. Branes can carry gauge fields. Different string theories are related by dualities.

M-theory unifies them in a higher-dimensional structure.

No single object — particle, string, or brane — is absolutely fundamental.

This is ontological democracy:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Strings and branes are not absolute substances. They are dual presentations of a deeper structure.}
}
\]

A bulk particle may correspond to a boundary operator excitation.

A bulk geometry may correspond to boundary entanglement.

Thus the ontology of the bulk is not absolute.

It is dual-relative.

Substance Relativity says:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What counts as a fundamental object depends on the duality frame.}
}

## 13. Qubits and Information Patterns

Quantum information theory introduces another ontology: qubits, channels, entanglement, and circuits.

A qubit is a two-dimensional Hilbert space,

\[
\mathcal{H}
=
\mathbb{C}^2.
\]

A general pure state is

\[
\ket{\psi}
=
\alpha\ket{0}
+
\beta\ket{1}.
\]

But qubits are not ultimate substances either.

A qubit is a frame for encoding information.

Physical systems can encode qubits in many ways:

1. spin states,
2. photon polarizations,
3. superconducting circuits,
4. ion levels,
5. topological anyon states.

The qubit is not the substance. It is the informational pattern.

In holography, spacetime geometry may be reconstructed from entanglement patterns.

The Ryu–Takayanagi formula relates entanglement entropy to area:

\[
S(A)
=
\frac{\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4G_N\hbar}.
\]

Thus geometry appears as an information pattern.

But information itself is not a substance.

It is relational structure.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Qubits are not ultimate stuff. They are frames for relational information.}
}
\]

## 14. Quasiparticles and Phase-Relative Substance

Condensed matter physics provides vivid examples of Substance Relativity.

In a solid, the fundamental microscopic degrees of freedom may be electrons and nuclei.

But the low-energy ontology may contain:

1. phonons,
2. magnons,
3. excitons,
4. polarons,
5. Cooper pairs,
6. anyons,
7. emergent gauge fields.

A phonon is not a fundamental substance. It is a collective excitation of a lattice.

A magnon is a collective spin excitation.

An anyon is an emergent excitation in a topologically ordered phase.

The same microscopic system may have different effective ontologies in different phases.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Substance is phase-relative.}
}
\]

What exists as a particle in one phase may not exist in another.

## 15. Solitons and Particles as Stable Processes

Some particle-like objects are solitons: stable, localized field configurations.

Examples include:

1. kinks,
2. vortices,
3. monopoles,
4. skyrmions,
5. instantons,
6. domain walls.

A soliton is not a substance inserted into a field. It is a stable pattern of the field.

Its stability is often topological.

A topological charge may be defined as

\[
Q
=
\int d^dx\,
\rho(x),
\]

where \(\rho\) is a topological density.

The soliton persists because \(Q\) is conserved.

Thus a particle-like object may be a topologically protected process-pattern.

This supports Substance Relativity:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A particle may be a stable topological process, not a substance.}
}
\]

## 17. Categorical Formulation

Category theory gives a natural formulation.

Let \(\mathbf{Proc}\) be a category of physical processes.

Objects are systems or interfaces.

Morphisms are processes:

\[
f:A\rightarrow B.
\]

Composition is sequential process:

\[
g\circ f.
\]

Tensor product is parallel process:

\[
f\otimes g.
\]

A physical theory is a functor,

\[
Z:
\mathbf{Proc}
\rightarrow
\mathbf{Hilb},
\]

or more generally to a category of algebras, probabilities, or relations.

Substances correspond to objects in a chosen presentation.

But the physical content is the functor up to natural isomorphism:

\[
Z\cong Z'.
\]

Thus objects are not fundamental. The invariant is the process-functor.

This is categorical Substance Relativity.

## 18. Substance Relativity and Identity Relativity

Identity Relativity says that identity is relational.

Substance Relativity says that substance is relational.

They are complementary.

If an object’s identity is its structural role, then the object is not a substratum.

It is a node in a web of relations.

The Yoneda lemma expresses this mathematically:

\[
X
\cong
\mathrm{Hom}(-,X).
\]

An object is determined by its relations to all other objects.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Substance is replaced by relational role.}
}
\]

## 19. Substance Relativity and Semantic Relativity

Semantic Relativity says that the meaning of physical terms is model-relative.

Substance Relativity applies this to ontology.

The word “particle” means different things in different theories.

The word “field” means different things in classical physics, quantum field theory, and effective field theory.

The word “string” means different things in perturbative string theory, M-theory, and holography.

The word “qubit” means different things in quantum computing, quantum foundations, and holographic error correction.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Ontological terms are semantic frames, not absolute names of reality.}
}
\]

## 20. Substance Relativity and Meta-Relativity

Meta-Relativity says that reality is the invariant content of equivalent descriptions.

Substance Relativity specifies the ontological consequence:

\[
\text{The entities of a description are not reality itself.}
\]

Reality is the equivalence class of descriptions, not the furniture of one description.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Substances are representatives; reality is the equivalence class.}

## 21. Effective Substances Are Real

Substance Relativity is not eliminativism.

It does not say that particles, fields, or qubits do not exist.

It says that they exist effectively.

A wave in water is real. But it is not a substance independent of the water, the boundary conditions, and the scale of description.

A phonon is real. But it is not a fundamental particle independent of the lattice phase.

A particle is real. But it is not an absolute substance independent of field, observer, and representation.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Effective substances are real within their domains of validity.}
}
\]

Substance Relativity denies absoluteness, not reality.

## 24. Axioms of Substance Relativity

The framework may be organized around twelve axioms.

### Axiom 1: No Substance Is Absolute

No entity is fundamental in all frames.

### Axiom 2: Entities Are Frame-Relative

Particles, fields, strings, branes, and qubits are effective entities within descriptions.

### Axiom 3: Observable Structure Is Invariant

Physical reality is preserved under isomorphisms of observable algebras.

### Axiom 4: Substances Are Equivalence Classes

A physical object is an equivalence class of substance presentations.

### Axiom 5: Gauge Redundancy Dissolves Substance

Gauge-related configurations are not distinct substances.

### Axiom 6: Duality Relativizes Ontology

Dual descriptions may use different entities for the same physics.

### Axiom 7: Scale Determines Ontology

Different scales reveal different effective entities.

### Axiom 8: Phase Determines Ontology

Different phases support different quasiparticles and emergent objects.

### Axiom 9: Process Is Primary

Processes and relations precede substances.

### Axiom 10: Information Is Relational

Qubits and information patterns are not ultimate substances.

### Axiom 11: Effective Substances Are Real

Effective entities are real within their domains.

### Axiom 12: Reflexivity Is Required

Substance Relativity applies to its own ontological vocabulary.

Thus particles are excitations of fields.
