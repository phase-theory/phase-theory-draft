# Relativity 0.0 — Relativity  
## The Primordial Principle of Invariance Under Transformation

**White paper / academic preprint**

---

## Abstract

Relativity 0.0 is the zeroth formulation of the entire relativistic program. It is not yet special relativity, not yet general relativity, not yet quantum reference frames, not yet semantic, logical, ecological, planetary, or eschatological relativity. It is the primordial principle from which all later versions derive: physical statements are meaningful only relative to frames, and physical reality is the invariant content preserved under transformations between frames. In its purest form, Relativity 0.0 asserts:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No frame is absolute; reality is the invariant under admissible transformations.}
}
\]

A frame may be a coordinate system, an observer, a measuring apparatus, a biological sensorium, a technological instrument, a language, a model, a civilization, or a cosmic horizon. A transformation may be a change of motion, geometry, scale, logic, ontology, instrumentation, or perspective. The task of relativity is not to declare everything arbitrary, but to discover what remains unchanged when the frame changes. Relativity 0.0 is therefore both a physical principle and a universal method: identify the frame, identify the transformations, identify the invariants. This white paper reconstructs Relativity 0.0 as the seed axiom of the entire sequence. It shows how Galilean relativity, special relativity, general relativity, gauge invariance, and later generalized relativities are all special cases of one structural idea: objectivity is invariant structure under transformation.

## 4. The Formal Structure of Relativity 0.0

Let \(\Phi\) be the space of frames.

Let \(G\) be a group, groupoid, or category of admissible transformations acting on \(\Phi\).

For each frame \(\phi\in\Phi\), let there be a description,

\[
D(\phi).
\]

A transformation \(g\in G\) maps one frame to another:

\[
\phi
\mapsto
g\phi.
\]

The description changes accordingly:

\[
D(\phi)
\mapsto
D(g\phi).
\]

A quantity \(I\) is invariant if:

\[
I(g\phi)
=
I(\phi)
\]

for all admissible \(g\).

A quantity \(Q\) is covariant if it transforms in a definite way:

\[
Q(g\phi)
=
\rho(g)Q(\phi),
\]

where \(\rho(g)\) is a representation of the transformation group.

Physical reality is identified not with the raw description \(D(\phi)\), but with the invariant or covariant structure preserved under \(G\).

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Reality is the invariant content of descriptions under frame transformations.}
}
\]

## 7. Special Relativity: The Invariant Interval

Special relativity radicalizes Galilean relativity.

Einstein’s two postulates are:

1. the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames,
2. the speed of light in vacuum is invariant.

The transformation between inertial frames is not Galilean but Lorentzian.

For relative velocity \(v\) along the \(x\)-axis:

\[
t'
=
\gamma
\left(
t-\frac{vx}{c^2}
\right),
\]

\[
x'
=
\gamma
\left(
x-vt
\right),
\]

\[
y'
=
y,
\]

\[
z'
=
z,
\]

where

\[
\gamma
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.
\]

The invariant is the spacetime interval:

\[
ds^2
=
-c^2dt^2
+
dx^2
+
dy^2
+
dz^2.
\]

All inertial observers agree on \(ds^2\).

They disagree on time intervals, spatial distances, and simultaneity.

Thus special relativity teaches:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Time and space are frame-relative; spacetime interval is invariant.}
}
\]

This is Relativity 0.0 applied to motion and light.

## 8. General Relativity: The Invariance of Geometry

General relativity extends the zeroth principle further.

It removes the privilege of coordinates altogether.

A coordinate transformation is now arbitrary:

\[
x^\mu
\mapsto
x'^\mu(x).
\]

The metric transforms as a tensor:

\[
g'_{\mu\nu}(x')
=
\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}
\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}
g_{\alpha\beta}(x).
\]

The line element is invariant:

\[
ds^2
=
g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu.
\]

The Einstein field equations are tensor equations:

\[
G_{\mu\nu}
+
\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=
\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}
T_{\mu\nu}.
\]

Because both sides are tensors, the equation holds in all coordinate systems.

Thus general relativity teaches:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Coordinates are arbitrary; geometric relations are physical.}
}
\]

This is Relativity 0.0 applied to geometry itself.
