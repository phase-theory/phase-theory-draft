# The Elevenfold Derived Architecture of Relativity Mechanics

## Abstract

Relativity Mechanics is not merely a reformulation of known symmetry principles. It is a foundational meta-theory of physical description. Its primitive object is the relativity schema

\[
\mathcal R=(\Omega,G,\triangleright,I),
\]

where \(\Omega\) is a description space, \(G\) is an admissibility group, \(\triangleright\) is the action of \(G\) on \(\Omega\), and

\[
I:\Omega\longrightarrow \Omega/G
\]

is the invariant projection. Physical states are not individual descriptions \(\omega\in\Omega\), but orbits

\[
[\omega]=G\cdot\omega.
\]

Physical observables are invariant functions on \(\Omega\), and physical dynamics must descend to the quotient \(\Omega/G\).

The present paper develops the consequence that every descendant theory of Relativity Mechanics must arise by adding exactly one additional mathematical structure to the relativity schema, rather than by introducing unrelated physical postulates. The resulting architecture consists of eleven derived theories:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Orbit Theory},\\
&\text{Invariant Theory},\\
&\text{Observer Theory},\\
&\text{Reference Transformation Theory},\\
&\text{Constraint Mechanics},\\
&\text{Relativistic Dynamics},\\
&\text{Relativistic Information Theory},\\
&\text{Relativistic Quantum Theory},\\
&\text{Relativity Geometry},\\
&\text{Gauge Theory of Admissibility},\\
&\text{Frame Theory}.
\end{aligned}
\]

General relativity, Einstein–Cartan theory, Yang–Mills theories, quantum reference-frame theories, Framet Theory, and future relational physical theories then appear as theorem-level specializations of this architecture. Frame Theory is therefore not the foundation of Relativity Mechanics; it is a high-level specialization obtained after orbit, invariant, observer, reference-transformation, constraint, dynamical, informational, quantum, geometric, and gauge structures have been developed.

The central principle remains:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical reality is represented by invariant equivalence classes of admissible descriptions.}
}
\]

## 1. The Foundational Schema of Relativity Mechanics

### 1.1 Description spaces and admissibility groups

Relativity Mechanics begins with the observation that physical systems are never given as bare objects. They are given through descriptions. A description may involve coordinates, frames, bases, gauges, observers, trivializations, parametrizations, or reference systems.

Let \(\Omega\) denote the space of all admissible descriptions of a physical system. An element

\[
\omega\in\Omega
\]

is a complete description relative to some admissible presentation.

Let \(G\) be the group of transformations that preserve physical content while changing the description. The group action is

\[
\triangleright:G\times\Omega\to\Omega.
\]

For \(g\in G\), we write

\[
g\cdot \omega:=g\triangleright\omega.
\]

The orbit of \(\omega\) is

\[
[\omega]=G\cdot\omega=\{g\cdot\omega\mid g\in G\}.
\]

The quotient space

\[
\Omega/G
\]

is the invariant description space.

The invariant projection is

\[
I:\Omega\to\Omega/G,
\]

\[
I(\omega)=[\omega].
\]

The quadruple

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R=(\Omega,G,\triangleright,I)
}
\]

is the relativity schema.

---

### 1.2 Axioms of Relativity Mechanics

The foundational axioms are the following.

#### Axiom I: Description

Every physical system admits a space of admissible descriptions \(\Omega\).

#### Axiom II: Admissibility

There exists a group \(G\) acting on \(\Omega\) whose elements relate descriptions of the same physical content.

#### Axiom III: Orbit Ontology

The physical state is not a point \(\omega\in\Omega\), but the orbit

\[
[\omega]=G\cdot\omega.
\]

#### Axiom IV: Invariant Observability

A quantity is physically observable only if it is constant on admissibility orbits:

\[
f(g\cdot\omega)=f(\omega).
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal O_{\rm phys}=C^\infty(\Omega)^G\cong C^\infty(\Omega/G).
\]

#### Axiom V: Compatible Dynamics

A dynamical law is physically meaningful only if it descends to the quotient. If \(X\) is a vector field on \(\Omega\), then \(X\) must satisfy

\[
X(g\cdot\omega)=g_*X(\omega),
\]

so that there exists a descended vector field \(\bar X\) on \(\Omega/G\) with

\[
I_*X=\bar X I.
\]

---

### 1.3 Principle of single-structure descent

A descendant theory of Relativity Mechanics is not obtained by adding independent physical postulates. It is obtained by adding one additional mathematical structure to the relativity schema.

Formally, a derived theory \(T\) has the form

\[
T=\mathcal R+S_T,
\]

where \(S_T\) is a single additional structure of a specified type.

The eleven structures are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{\rm OT}&=\text{orbit topology and orbit-type stratification},\\
S_{\rm IT}&=\text{algebra of invariant observables},\\
S_{\rm ObT}&=\text{observer maps and reference systems},\\
S_{\rm RTT}&=\text{morphisms between admissibility structures},\\
S_{\rm CM}&=\text{constraint algebras and admissible submanifolds},\\
S_{\rm RD}&=\text{flows on quotient spaces},\\
S_{\rm RIT}&=\text{information functionals on orbit spaces},\\
S_{\rm RQT}&=\text{Hilbert spaces carrying admissibility actions},\\
S_{\rm RG}&=\text{geometry on quotient spaces},\\
S_{\rm GTA}&=\text{principal bundles with arbitrary structure group},\\
S_{\rm FT}&=\text{local spacetime frame bundles}.
\end{aligned}
\]

No descendant theory introduces a new primitive ontology. All physical being remains orbital.

# Part I — Orbit Theory

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{What is a physical object?}
}
\]

## Answer

A physical object is an orbit

\[
[\omega]=G\cdot\omega.
\]

Orbit Theory is the first derived theory of Relativity Mechanics. It studies the topology, stratification, and classification of admissibility orbits.

---

## 3. Formal structure of Orbit Theory

The additional structure of Orbit Theory is the orbit structure itself, regarded as an object of study:

\[
S_{\rm OT}=
\left\{
\text{orbit topology and stabilizer stratification of }G\curvearrowright\Omega
\right\}.
\]

For each \(\omega\in\Omega\), define the stabilizer subgroup

\[
G_\omega=\{g\in G\mid g\cdot\omega=\omega\}.
\]

Two points on the same orbit have conjugate stabilizers:

\[
G_{g\cdot\omega}=gG_\omega g^{-1}.
\]

Therefore orbit types are classified by conjugacy classes of stabilizers.

If \(H\subset G\) is a subgroup, define the stratum

\[
\Omega_{(H)}
=
\{\omega\in\Omega\mid G_\omega\text{ is conjugate to }H\}.
\]

The quotient inherits a stratification

\[
\Omega/G
=
\bigsqcup_{(H)}
\Omega_{(H)}/G.
\]

Thus physical state space is generally not a smooth manifold but a stratified orbit space.

---

## 4. Ontological consequence

The primitive ontology of Relativity Mechanics is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical objects are orbits, not representatives.}
}
\]

This replaces substance-based ontology with equivalence-class ontology.

A particle, field, geometry, observer, or quantum state is not fundamentally a representative description. It is an orbit under the relevant admissibility group.

# Part II — Invariant Theory

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{What can be measured?}
}
\]

## Answer

Only invariant functions are measurable:

\[
f(g\cdot\omega)=f(\omega).
\]

Invariant Theory is the theory of physical observables.

---

## 5. Algebra of physical observables

The observable algebra is

\[
\mathcal O_{\rm phys}
=
C^\infty(\Omega)^G.
\]

If the quotient is smooth,

\[
C^\infty(\Omega)^G\cong C^\infty(\Omega/G).
\]

Thus an observable is equivalently a function on the orbit space.

For compact \(G\), one may construct invariants by averaging. Let \(dg\) be normalized Haar measure on \(G\). Define the Reynolds operator

\[
\mathcal R_G(f)(\omega)
=
\int_G f(g\cdot\omega)\,dg.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal R_G(f)\in C^\infty(\Omega)^G.
\]

Invariant Theory studies:

1. generators of the invariant algebra,
2. relations among invariants,
3. separating invariants,
4. complete invariant systems,
5. singular invariants on stratified quotients.

---

## 6. Measurement as invariant evaluation

A measurement is a map

\[
\mu:\Omega/G\to\mathbb R
\]

or, equivalently,

\[
f=\mu\circ I:\Omega\to\mathbb R
\]

with

\[
f(g\cdot\omega)=f(\omega).
\]

Therefore measurement is not evaluation of a representative. It is evaluation of an orbit invariant.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Measurement is invariant extraction from an admissibility class.}
}
\]

# Part III — Observer Theory

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{Who or what describes?}
}
\]

## Answer

An observer is a mathematical map from a physical description space to an observer description space.

---

## 7. Observer maps

An observer structure is a triple

\[
\mathsf O=(D_O,O,\mathcal M_O),
\]

where:

1. \(D_O\) is the observer’s description space,
2. \(O:\Omega\to D_O\) is the observation map,
3. \(\mathcal M_O\subset C^\infty(D_O)\) is the set of reportable quantities.

The observer map extracts from a total description \(\omega\) the portion accessible to the observer:

\[
\omega\mapsto O(\omega).
\]

If the observer is itself physical, it must transform consistently under admissibility transformations. Suppose \(G\) acts on \(D_O\) through a representation \(\rho\). Then \(O\) is admissible if

\[
O(g\cdot\omega)=\rho(g)O(\omega).
\]

A report \(m\in C^\infty(D_O)\) is observer-invariant if

\[
m(\rho(g)d)=m(d).
\]

Then the pulled-back quantity

\[
m_O(\omega)=m(O(\omega))
\]

is a physical observable:

\[
m_O(g\cdot\omega)
=
m(O(g\cdot\omega))
=
m(\rho(g)O(\omega))
=
m(O(\omega))
=
m_O(\omega).
\]

Thus observer reports become physical observables only when they are invariant under the observer’s admissibility action.

---

## 8. Relational observables

A particularly important class of observer structures is given by reference variables.

Let \(T^a:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) be clock or reference fields. Let \(A:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) be a quantity to be relationalized. Define

\[
A(\tau)=A\big|_{T=\tau}.
\]

If \(T^a\) and \(A\) transform covariantly, then \(A(\tau)\) can be invariant under the admissibility group.

This is the general form of relational measurement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{An observer is a relational extractor of invariants.}
}
\]

# Part IV — Reference Transformation Theory

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{How are different physical descriptions related?}
}
\]

## Answer

By morphisms between admissibility structures.

---

## 9. The category of admissibility structures

Define a category \(\mathbf{Rel}\) whose objects are relativity schemas

\[
\mathcal R=(\Omega,G,\triangleright,I).
\]

A morphism

\[
\Phi:\mathcal R_1\to\mathcal R_2
\]

is a pair

\[
(\Phi,\alpha),
\]

where

\[
\Phi:\Omega_1\to\Omega_2
\]

and

\[
\alpha:G_1\to G_2
\]

is a group homomorphism satisfying the equivariance condition

\[
\Phi(g\cdot\omega)=\alpha(g)\cdot\Phi(\omega).
\]

Then \(\Phi\) descends to a map between quotient spaces:

\[
\bar\Phi:\Omega_1/G_1\to\Omega_2/G_2,
\]

defined by

\[
\bar\Phi([\omega])=[\Phi(\omega)].
\]

The map is well defined because if \(\omega'\!=g\cdot\omega\), then

\[
\Phi(\omega')
=
\Phi(g\cdot\omega)
=
\alpha(g)\cdot\Phi(\omega),
\]

so \(\Phi(\omega')\) and \(\Phi(\omega)\) lie on the same \(G_2\)-orbit.

---

## 10. Types of reference transformations

Reference Transformation Theory classifies maps between physical frameworks.

Examples include:

1. **Gauge transformations**: maps within a fixed quotient.
2. **Frame transformations**: changes of local reference frames.
3. **Theory reductions**: maps from a finer theory to a coarser theory.
4. **Symmetry contractions**: e.g. Poincaré to Galilei in the limit \(c\to\infty\).
5. **Dualities**: isomorphisms between distinct admissibility descriptions.
6. **Coarse-grainings**: maps from microscopic to macroscopic orbit spaces.

Thus Reference Transformation Theory provides the mathematical language for comparing physical theories within Relativity Mechanics.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Changing description frameworks is a morphism of admissibility structures.}
}
\]

# Part V — Constraint Mechanics

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{Which descriptions are physically admissible?}
}
\]

## Answer

Those satisfying constraint equations generated by the admissibility structure.

---

## 11. Constraint surfaces

A constraint system is a map

\[
C:\Omega\to V,
\]

where \(V\) is a vector space or algebra. The admissible state manifold is

\[
\Sigma=C^{-1}(0).
\]

In components,

\[
\Sigma=\{\omega\in\Omega\mid C_a(\omega)=0\}.
\]

If the constraints generate admissibility transformations, they are first-class. In Hamiltonian form,

\[
\{C_a,C_b\}=f_{ab}{}^c C_c.
\]

The physical phase space is

\[
\mathcal Q_{\rm phys}=\Sigma/G_C,
\]

where \(G_C\) is the group generated by the constraints.

---

## 12. Constraint algebras and gauge reduction

In gauge theory and gravity, constraints are not obstacles. They are the infinitesimal expression of admissibility.

Examples:

| Theory | Constraint | Admissibility meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Electromagnetism | Gauss law | \(U(1)\) gauge admissibility |
| Yang–Mills | Non-Abelian Gauss law | Internal gauge admissibility |
| General relativity | Diffeomorphism constraint | Spatial coordinate admissibility |
| General relativity | Hamiltonian constraint | Refoliation/admissibility of time slicing |
| String theory | Virasoro constraints | Worldsheet diffeomorphism/Weyl admissibility |

In quantum theory, constraints become operator conditions:

\[
\hat C_a|\psi_{\rm phys}\rangle=0.
\]

Thus Constraint Mechanics is the general theory of selecting physical orbits from an enlarged description space.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Constraints are the local generators of admissibility orbits.}
}
\]

# Part VI — Relativistic Dynamics

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{How do physical states evolve?}
}
\]

## Answer

Physical evolution is a flow on the orbit space.

---

## 13. Equivariant vector fields

Let \(X\in\Gamma(T\Omega)\). The evolution equation on descriptions is

\[
\frac{d\omega}{dt}=X(\omega).
\]

This evolution is physically meaningful only if \(X\) is \(G\)-equivariant:

\[
X(g\cdot\omega)=g_*X(\omega).
\]

Then there exists a unique vector field \(\bar X\) on \(\Omega/G\) such that

\[
I_*X=\bar X I.
\]

The physical trajectory is

\[
[\omega(t)]\in\Omega/G.
\]

It satisfies

\[
\frac{d[\omega]}{dt}=\bar X([\omega]).
\]

Thus dynamics is not a trajectory of representatives but a trajectory of equivalence classes.

---

## 14. Invariant action principles

Let

\[
S[\omega]=\int L(\omega,\dot\omega)\,dt.
\]

The action is admissible if

\[
S[g\cdot\omega]=S[\omega].
\]

Then the Euler–Lagrange equations are \(G\)-covariant. If \(\omega(t)\) is a solution, then \(g\cdot\omega(t)\) is also a solution.

The dynamics descends to the quotient.

---

## 15. Noether theorem in Relativistic Dynamics

For each Lie algebra element \(\xi\in\mathfrak g\), let

\[
\xi_\Omega(\omega)
=
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}
\exp(t\xi)\cdot\omega.
\]

If the Lagrangian is invariant up to a total derivative,

\[
\delta_\xi L=\frac{dF_\xi}{dt},
\]

then

\[
J_\xi=
\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot\omega^i}\xi_\Omega^i
-
F_\xi
\]

is conserved:

\[
\frac{dJ_\xi}{dt}=0.
\]

In Hamiltonian form, if \(\mu:\Omega\to\mathfrak g^*\) is the moment map, then

\[
\frac{d\mu_\xi}{dt}=0.
\]

Thus conservation laws are invariant consequences of admissibility.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Dynamics is the motion of orbits, not of representatives.}
}
\]

# Part VII — Relativistic Information Theory

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{What is physical information?}
}
\]

## Answer

Information is distinguishability of admissibility orbits.

---

## 16. Probability measures on orbit spaces

Let \(\mu\) be a \(G\)-invariant measure on \(\Omega\). It induces a measure

\[
\nu=I_*\mu
\]

on \(\Omega/G\). For a measurable set of orbits \(U\subset\Omega/G\),

\[
\nu(U)=\mu(I^{-1}(U)).
\]

The information associated with observing an orbit in \(U\) is

\[
\mathcal I(U)=-\log \nu(U).
\]

If \(\Omega/G\) admits a density \(p\), the relational entropy is

\[
S[\nu]=-\int_{\Omega/G} p([\omega])\log p([\omega])\,d\nu([\omega]).
\]

Information is therefore not stored in coordinate data, gauge representatives, or basis choices. It is stored in invariant orbit structure.

---

## 17. Invariant distinguishability

Two descriptions contain the same physical information if they lie on the same orbit. Two orbits are physically distinguishable only by invariant observables.

A natural distinguishability functional is the Fisher information on the quotient:

\[
\mathcal F_{ij}
=
\int_{\Omega/G}
p([\omega])
\partial_i\log p([\omega])
\partial_j\log p([\omega])
\,d\nu([\omega]).
\]

In quantum theory, this becomes invariant quantum Fisher information.

---

## 18. Informational interpretation

Relativistic Information Theory replaces the classical idea that information is a property of configurations with the stronger principle:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical information is invariant orbit distinguishability.}
}
\]

This provides the informational basis for Framet Theory, quantum reference frames, and relational quantum information.

# Part VIII — Relativistic Quantum Theory

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{What is a quantum state in relational terms?}
}
\]

## Answer

A quantum state is an equivalence class of Hilbert-space descriptions under an admissibility action.

---

## 19. Hilbert spaces with admissibility actions

Relativistic Quantum Theory adds a Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\) carrying a projective unitary representation of \(G\):

\[
U:G\to PU(\mathcal H).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
U(g)U(h)=e^{i\alpha(g,h)}U(gh).
\]

A quantum description is a vector

\[
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal H.
\]

The admissibility group acts by

\[
|\psi\rangle\mapsto U(g)|\psi\rangle.
\]

The physical quantum state is the orbit

\[
[|\psi\rangle]_G.
\]

If phase is also quotiented, the pure-state space is

\[
\mathbb P(\mathcal H)/G.
\]

For gauge constraints, the physical Hilbert space is instead

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
=
\mathcal H^G
\]

or, more generally, the BRST cohomology

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
=
H^\bullet(Q_{\rm BRST}).
\]

---

## 20. Quantum observables as commutants

The quantum observable algebra is the invariant subalgebra:

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm phys}
=
\mathcal A^G
=
\{A\in\mathcal A\mid U(g)AU(g)^\dagger=A\}.
\]

Equivalently, physical observables commute with the admissibility action:

\[
[A,U(g)]=0.
\]

Thus quantum observables are invariant operators.

---

## 21. Decoherence-free subspaces and logical qubits

Suppose \(G\) is compact. By representation theory,

\[
\mathcal H
\cong
\bigoplus_\lambda
\mathcal H_\lambda\otimes \mathcal M_\lambda,
\]

where \(G\) acts only on \(\mathcal H_\lambda\):

\[
U(g)
=
\bigoplus_\lambda
\rho_\lambda(g)\otimes \mathbf 1_{\mathcal M_\lambda}.
\]

The multiplicity spaces \(\mathcal M_\lambda\) are unaffected by the admissibility action. They naturally encode protected logical information.

In particular:

1. trivial representations encode singlets,
2. multiplicity spaces encode decoherence-free subsystems,
3. logical qubits may be stored in invariant or protected sectors,
4. quantum error-correcting codes may be understood as orbit-protected subspaces.

Thus quantum reference frames, gauge-invariant quantum information, and relational quantum computation arise naturally.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Quantum states are relational orbit structures in Hilbert space.}
}
\]

## 22. Geometry from invariant structures

Relativity Geometry studies metrics, connections, curvature, symplectic forms, Poisson structures, fiber bundles, and topological invariants on quotient spaces.

If \(\Omega\) is a principal \(G\)-bundle over \(Q=\Omega/G\), then a connection on \(\Omega\) is a \(\mathfrak g\)-valued one-form \(A\) satisfying

\[
A(\xi_\Omega)=\xi,
\]

\[
R_g^*A=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}A.
\]

The curvature is

\[
F=dA+\frac12[A,A].
\]

The horizontal distribution

\[
\mathcal H=\ker A
\]

defines parallel transport of descriptions. Curvature measures the failure of local frame choices to integrate into global descriptions.

---

## 23. Quotient metrics

Suppose \(\Omega\) has a \(G\)-invariant Riemannian metric \(h\). If the action is free and proper, then \(\pi:\Omega\to Q\) is a Riemannian submersion and \(Q\) inherits a metric \(g_Q\) defined by

\[
g_Q(\bar X,\bar Y)
=
h(X^H,Y^H),
\]

where \(X^H,Y^H\) are horizontal lifts.

Thus quotient geometry is induced, not assumed.

---

## 24. Symplectic reduction

If \((\Omega,\omega)\) is symplectic and \(G\) acts Hamiltonianly with moment map

\[
\mu:\Omega\to\mathfrak g^*,
\]

then the reduced phase space is

\[
\Omega//G=\mu^{-1}(0)/G.
\]

The reduced symplectic form \(\omega_{\rm red}\) satisfies

\[
\pi^*\omega_{\rm red}=i^*\omega,
\]

where

\[
i:\mu^{-1}(0)\hookrightarrow\Omega,
\]

\[
\pi:\mu^{-1}(0)\to\mu^{-1}(0)/G.
\]

Therefore Hamiltonian geometry is also a quotient construction.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Geometry is the invariant differential structure of orbit spaces.}
}
\]

# Part X — Gauge Theory of Admissibility

## Primitive question

\[
\boxed{
\text{What if admissibility transformations are internal and local?}
}
\]

## Answer

Gauge theory arises.

---

## 25. Principal bundles and gauge admissibility

Let \(M\) be a base manifold and \(H\) a Lie group. Let

\[
\pi:P\to M
\]

be a principal \(H\)-bundle. The gauge group is

\[
\mathcal G=\operatorname{Aut}(P).
\]

A gauge field is a connection \(A\in\mathcal A\), where \(\mathcal A\) is the affine space of connections on \(P\).

The description space is

\[
\Omega_{\rm gauge}=\mathcal A\times\Gamma(E),
\]

where \(E=P\times_H V\) is an associated matter bundle.

The admissibility group is

\[
G_{\rm gauge}=\mathcal G.
\]

The quotient is

\[
\mathcal A/\mathcal G.
\]

---

## 26. Gauge transformations and curvature

Locally, a connection is

\[
A=A_\mu dx^\mu,
\]

with \(A_\mu\in\mathfrak h\). A gauge transformation \(u:M\to H\) acts by

\[
A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu^u
=
uA_\mu u^{-1}
-
(\partial_\mu u)u^{-1}.
\]

The curvature is

\[
F_{\mu\nu}
=
\partial_\mu A_\nu
-
\partial_\nu A_\mu
+
[A_\mu,A_\nu].
\]

It transforms covariantly:

\[
F_{\mu\nu}\mapsto uF_{\mu\nu}u^{-1}.
\]

The Yang–Mills action is

\[
S_{\rm YM}[A]
=
-\frac{1}{2g^2}
\int_M \operatorname{tr}(F\wedge *F).
\]

It is invariant under gauge transformations.

---

## 27. Gauge interactions as admissibility theories

Choosing different structure groups yields different gauge theories.

| Structure group \(H\) | Theory |
|---|---|
| \(U(1)\) | Electromagnetism |
| \(SU(2)\) | Weak isospin gauge theory |
| \(SU(3)\) | Quantum chromodynamics |
| \(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) | Standard Model gauge sector |
| \(SO(1,3)\), \(Spin(1,3)\) | Local Lorentz/frame gauge theory |
| \(Diff(M)\) | Diffeomorphism admissibility in gravity |

Thus gauge theories are not independent additions to Relativity Mechanics. They are admissibility theories with local internal structure groups.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Gauge symmetry is local admissibility.}
}
\]

## 28. Frame bundles and coframes

Let \(M\) be a four-dimensional manifold. The frame bundle \(FM\) is a principal \(GL(4,\mathbb R)\)-bundle. For Lorentzian geometry, one uses the orthonormal frame bundle or spin bundle with structure group

\[
SO(1,3)

\[
e^I=e^I_\mu dx^\mu,

The metric is not primitive. It is the invariant

\[
\boxed{

## 31. Einstein–Hilbert and Palatini actions

The Einstein–Hilbert action can be written in frame form as

\[
S[e]
=
\frac{1}{2\kappa}
\int \epsilon_{IJKL}
e^I\wedge e^J\wedge R^{KL},
\]

where

\[
\kappa=8\pi G.
\]

In the Palatini formulation, \(e^I\) and \(\omega^{IJ}\) are varied independently.

Variation with respect to \(e^I\) gives

\[
\epsilon_{IJKL}e^J\wedge R^{KL}=0,
\]

which is equivalent to the Einstein equation in vacuum.

Variation with respect to \(\omega^{IJ}\) gives

\[
D(e^I\wedge e^J)=0.
\]

In the absence of spin sources, this implies vanishing torsion:

\[
T^I=0.
\]

The connection becomes the Levi-Civita connection, and one recovers general relativity.
