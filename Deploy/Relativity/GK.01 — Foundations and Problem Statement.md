General Relativity × Knot Theory

Topology of Closed Null Geodesics and Link Invariants of Spacetime

Part I — Foundations and Problem Statement

⸻

Abstract

General relativity traditionally characterizes spacetime through local curvature invariants, causal structure, and global manifold topology. Knot theory, in contrast, studies embeddings of closed curves and their equivalence under continuous deformation. Although both disciplines concern geometry and global structure, their interaction has remained limited. Existing work on topological photonics and optical knots has demonstrated that electromagnetic field lines in flat spacetime may form stable knot and link configurations. However, no corresponding framework presently exists for the topology of the null geodesic flow itself in curved Lorentzian manifolds.

This work proposes the foundational structure for a geometric-topological theory in which the family of closed null geodesics of a spacetime becomes an invariant object. Rather than classifying spacetimes through manifold topology alone, the proposal is to classify them through the knot and link structure generated by the flow of light.

The central hypothesis is that certain Lorentzian manifolds admit sufficiently rich closed-null orbit families whose knot-theoretic invariants define new global observables independent of local curvature scalars. We introduce the conceptual framework, define the relevant geometric objects, and formulate the guiding mathematical program.

1. Introduction

General relativity is fundamentally a theory of causal geometry.

The metric tensor
[
g_{\mu\nu}
]
determines not merely distances but the entire set of admissible trajectories through spacetime. Among these trajectories, null geodesics occupy a distinguished role.

For a Lorentzian manifold
[
(M,g),
]
null geodesics define the propagation of light:

[
g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=0.
]

The total collection of null trajectories forms a dynamical system:

[
\Phi_t:T^\ast M\rightarrow T^\ast M,
]

the null geodesic flow.

Conventional gravitational analysis studies:

* local curvature,
* geodesic deviation,
* causal cones,
* horizons,
* singularities.

The topology generated by the global arrangement of null trajectories has not become an independent classification principle.

This paper begins from the observation that closed null geodesics naturally define embedded closed curves.

Whenever multiple such trajectories coexist, they may link, braid, and knot.

This suggests a new question:

Can a spacetime possess a measurable knot structure generated entirely by its null geodesic flow?

If yes, knot invariants become geometric observables.

2.1 Geometry Beyond Curvature

Two spacetimes may possess:

[

R’_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
]

yet share similar optical behavior.

Conversely:

[
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

R’_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
]

This distinction motivates searching for invariants of trajectories rather than tensors.

Define the null orbit family:

[
\mathcal N(M,g)

{
\gamma:
\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0,
\quad
g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=0

]

The classical viewpoint regards (\mathcal N) as a collection.

We instead interpret it as a topological object.

2.2 Closed Null Geodesics

A null geodesic is closed if

[
\exists T>0:
\quad
\gamma(T)=\gamma(0).
]

Closed null curves occur under multiple mechanisms:

A. Rotational trapping

Photon regions produce periodic optical trajectories.

B. Global identification

Compactified dimensions may close null paths.

C. Frame-dragging

Strong rotation may induce optical winding.

D. Nontrivial causal topology

Closed causal structures may admit periodic null motion.

Whenever

[
\gamma_i\cap\gamma_j=\varnothing,
]

each trajectory defines an embedded loop.

Thus:

[
\gamma_i:S^1\hookrightarrow M.
]

The set

[
{\gamma_i}
]

inherits knot-theoretic structure.
