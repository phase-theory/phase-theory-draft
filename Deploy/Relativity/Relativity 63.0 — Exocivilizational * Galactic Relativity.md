# Relativity 63.0 — Exocivilizational / Galactic Relativity  
## Cosmic Objectivity as Slow Convergence Across Civilizational Horizons

**White paper / academic preprint**

## Abstract

Exocivilizational / Galactic Relativity is the hypothesis that if intelligence emerges elsewhere, the universe contains many civilizations with radically different sensory frames, technologies, values, temporal scales, ecologies, instruments, and sciences. Galactic Relativity extends Collective-Verification Relativity beyond humanity. It proposes that cosmic objectivity cannot be identified with human consensus, terrestrial biology, or planetary civilization. Instead, it must be understood as a slow, causal, cross-civilizational process of translation, calibration, and long-delay verification. Different civilizations may inhabit different Umwelten, build different noospheres, and discover different invariant structures. Their communication is limited by relativistic causal structure, finite bandwidth, noise, mortality, and semantic divergence. Formally, let civilizations \(C_1,C_2,\ldots,C_N\) occupy different causal regions, each with observational frame \(\mathcal{F}_i\). Their shared invariant structure is

\[
\Theta^*
=
\bigcap_i
\mathrm{Inv}(\mathcal{F}_i),
\]

where \(\mathrm{Inv}(\mathcal{F}_i)\) denotes the invariant structures accessible to civilization \(i\). The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cosmic objectivity is slow convergence across causal and civilizational horizons.}
}
\]

Galactic Relativity does not assume a universal galactic mind. It proposes a possible federation of finite observers separated by light, time, embodiment, and form. It does not guarantee that such observers can understand one another. It asserts only that if mutual understanding is possible, it must be constructed through patient verification across horizons.

## 1. Introduction

Humanity has begun to imagine intelligence beyond Earth.

We search for biosignatures.

We search for technosignatures.

We listen for radio signals.

We consider optical beacons, laser pulses, megastructures, atmospheric pollution, and artificial heat.

We ask whether other minds might exist among the stars.

But if they do, a deeper problem appears.

The problem is not merely detection.

The problem is understanding.

Another civilization may not share our senses.

It may not share our timescales.

It may not share our mathematics, our instruments, our values, or our metaphors.

It may be biological, machine, hybrid, swarm-like, oceanic, atmospheric, or something for which we have no category.

It may observe the universe through chemical gradients, magnetic fields, gravitational waves, neutrinos, quantum correlations, or modalities unknown to us.

Its science may be valid but unrecognizable.

Its objectivity may be built from frames we have not yet learned to translate.

Exocivilizational / Galactic Relativity begins here.

It asks:

\[
\text{What becomes of objectivity when intelligence is not human, not terrestrial, and not synchronized with us?}
\]

The answer is that objectivity becomes galactic.

It becomes slow.

It becomes causal.

It becomes inter-civilizational.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galactic objectivity is not given. It is constructed across horizons.}
}
\]

## 2. The Core Principle

The central principle of Exocivilizational / Galactic Relativity is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cosmic objectivity is slow convergence across causal and civilizational horizons.}
}
\]

This principle contains four ideas.

1. **Cosmic**: objectivity is not limited to Earth or humanity.
2. **Objectivity**: knowledge is not mere opinion; it seeks invariant structure.
3. **Slow convergence**: verification across interstellar distances is delayed by light and time.
4. **Civilizational horizons**: each civilization has finite senses, instruments, values, and lifespans.

Galactic Relativity does not claim that all civilizations will agree.

It claims that any shared truth among them must be earned through translation and verification.

## 8. Communication Under Relativistic Constraints

Interstellar communication is constrained by the finite speed of light.

If two civilizations are separated by distance \(d\), the one-way communication delay is

\[
\tau
=
\frac{d}{c}.
\]

A round-trip exchange requires at least

\[
2\tau
=
\frac{2d}{c}.
\]

For a civilization 100 light-years away, a single exchange takes 200 years.

For a civilization 10,000 light-years away, it takes 20,000 years.

Thus galactic conversation is not dialogue in the human sense.

It is slow exchange across generations.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galactic communication is intergenerational communication.}
}
\]

## 9. Communication Windows

Civilizations may not overlap in time.

Let \(L_i\) be the communicative lifespan of civilization \(i\).

Let \(t_i\) be the time at which it becomes communicative.

Communication between civilizations \(i\) and \(j\) is possible only if their temporal existence and light-travel constraints overlap.

A simplified condition is:

\[
|t_i - t_j|
\lesssim
L_i + L_j - \frac{d_{ij}}{c}.
\]

If civilizations arise too far apart in time, they may never exchange messages.

If their lifespans are short, the galaxy may be full of signals that miss one another.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galactic objectivity requires overlapping communicative windows.}
}
\]
