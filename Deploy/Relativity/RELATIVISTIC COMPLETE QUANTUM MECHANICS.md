A Covariant Completion of Quantum Theory Through Relativity, Causal Structure, and Relational Time

Abstract

Quantum mechanics provides an extraordinarily successful account of microscopic phenomena, yet its standard formulation leaves unresolved questions concerning the ontological status of the quantum state, the role of measurement, the physical meaning of wave-function reduction, the relationship between quantum nonlocal correlations and relativistic causality, and the status of time itself. These questions become particularly acute when quantum theory is placed alongside special and general relativity.

This paper develops a theoretical programme termed Relativistic Complete Quantum Mechanics (RCQM). The central hypothesis is that the conventional quantum state is not necessarily the complete description of physical reality. Instead, the fundamental physical state is proposed to be a covariant object defined over spacetime, together with underlying relational degrees of freedom from which ordinary quantum probabilities and effective quantum states emerge.

RCQM replaces absolute temporal evolution with covariant relational evolution between spacelike hypersurfaces and ultimately with a fundamental constraint equation. Physical states are represented as spacetime-supported structures rather than objects evolving against an external universal clock. Measurement is formulated as a local interaction between quantum subsystems and apparatus degrees of freedom rather than as an instantaneous global state reduction. Entanglement is interpreted as nonseparability of the joint relativistic state rather than as superluminal causal influence.

The framework introduces a generalized ontic state

[
\Omega=(g_{\mu\nu},\Psi,\Lambda),
]

where (g_{\mu\nu}) represents spacetime geometry, (\Psi) represents the covariant quantum state, and (\Lambda) represents additional physical structure not explicitly encoded by the conventional wavefunction. The complete theory is proposed to satisfy a covariant constraint

[
\widehat{\mathcal C}|\Omega\rangle=0,
]

from which relational quantum dynamics should emerge in appropriate semiclassical limits.

The programme is deliberately constrained by established empirical requirements. Any realization must recover quantum field theory, Lorentz covariance, the Born rule, Bell correlations, no-signalling, general relativity, and ordinary quantum mechanics in their respective domains. The paper develops the mathematical architecture required for such a completion and identifies the unresolved problems that must be solved before RCQM can be considered a physical theory rather than a theoretical framework.

Part I — The Einsteinian Problem

1. Introduction

The conceptual foundations of quantum mechanics remain unusual compared with those of classical physics.

Classical mechanics begins with physical states and equations governing their evolution. Relativity reformulates those states and equations so that they are compatible with spacetime covariance. Quantum mechanics instead introduces a complex state vector

[
|\psi\rangle
]

or wavefunction

[
\psi(\mathbf x,t),
]

whose relationship to physical reality is more subtle.

The conventional formulation successfully predicts measurement statistics, but it does not uniquely specify what the quantum state represents ontologically.

Einstein’s objection to quantum mechanics was therefore deeper than a disagreement with particular calculations. The concern was whether the quantum description constituted a complete account of physical reality.

The present work takes that question seriously.

The objective is not to reject quantum mechanics, nor to deny its empirical success. Instead, the objective is:

[
\boxed{
\text{to determine whether quantum mechanics can emerge as an effective description of a deeper relativistic theory.}
}
]

The guiding hypothesis is that the missing structure is not an arbitrary hidden parameter appended to quantum mechanics, but a covariant description of physical reality itself.
