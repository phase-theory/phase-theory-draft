## Abstract

Phase / Landscape Relativity is the hypothesis that effective laws and ontologies are phase-relative. The same underlying structure may possess different effective descriptions, different particles, different vacua, different geometries, and different symmetries in different phases. A partition function may be dominated by different saddles,

\[
Z
=
\sum_{\text{saddles }s}
e^{-I_s},
\]

and different phases correspond to different dominant saddles, order parameters, universality classes, and emergent symmetries. The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Laws are phase-relative; the invariant is the full phase diagram and its universal data.}
}
\]

What counts as a particle, vacuum, geometry, or symmetry may depend on the phase of the underlying theory. Water molecules form ice, liquid, or vapor. Magnets possess magnons only below a critical temperature. QCD confines hadrons at low temperature but liberates quarks and gluons in a quark-gluon plasma. Topological phases support anyons that do not exist outside the phase. String compactifications generate landscapes of vacua with different low-energy constants. Quantum gravity may have geometric and nongeometric phases. Phase / Landscape Relativity unifies statistical mechanics, quantum field theory, condensed matter physics, QCD, holography, quantum phase transitions, false-vacuum decay, and the string landscape into a single relativistic principle. It asserts that no single phase’s ontology or effective law is absolute. The invariant physical content is the entire phase structure together with its universal data.

## 1. Introduction

Physics often speaks as if there is one set of laws.

But in practice, physics uses many effective laws.

Hydrodynamics governs fluids. Kinetic theory governs dilute gases. Lattice vibrations govern solids. Quasiparticles govern condensed matter. Hadrons govern low-energy QCD. Quarks and gluons govern high-energy QCD. Semiclassical geometry governs weakly curved spacetime. Quantum gravity governs Planckian regimes.

These are not contradictory laws.

They are phase-relative laws.

A phase is a regime of a theory in which certain degrees of freedom, symmetries, and effective descriptions are stable.

Different phases of the same underlying theory may have different ontologies.

In one phase, the fundamental entities may be atoms.

In another, phonons.

In another, quasiparticles.

In another, quarks.

In another, strings.

In another, geometry itself.

Phase / Landscape Relativity says:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Effective laws and entities are relative to phases.}
}
\]

The invariant is not the law valid in one phase.

The invariant is the full phase diagram, including transitions, universal data, symmetries, anomalies, and saddle structure.

## 2. What Is a Phase?

A phase is a stable regime of a physical system.

In thermodynamics, phases include solid, liquid, gas, plasma, superfluid, superconductor, ferromagnet, and crystal.

In quantum field theory, phases include confined, deconfined, Higgs, Coulomb, topological, and conformal phases.

In quantum gravity, phases may include semiclassical spacetime, black-hole phases, thermal phases, topological phases, and nongeometric phases.

A phase is usually characterized by:

1. a dominant saddle or vacuum,
2. an order parameter,
3. a symmetry realization,
4. a spectrum of excitations,
5. an effective action,
6. a universality class,
7. a pattern of correlations,
8. a topology or topological order,
9. a set of emergent entities.

Thus a phase is not merely a state.

It is an effective world.

## 3. Partition Functions and Saddles

The partition function encodes the statistical structure of a theory.

In Euclidean form,

\[
Z
=
\int
\mathcal{D}\phi
\,
e^{-S_E[\phi]/\hbar}.
\]

In the semiclassical limit, the integral is dominated by saddles \(\phi_s\) satisfying

\[
\frac{\delta S_E}{\delta\phi}
=
0.
\]

Then

\[
Z
\approx
\sum_s
e^{-S_E[\phi_s]/\hbar}
\left[
\det S_E''[\phi_s]
\right]^{-1/2}.
\]

Writing

\[
I_s
=
S_E[\phi_s]/\hbar,
\]

one has schematically

\[
Z
=
\sum_{\text{saddles }s}
e^{-I_s}.
\]

The dominant saddle minimizes the effective free energy.

At finite temperature,

\[
F
=
- T \ln Z.
\]

The phase with lowest free energy dominates.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Phases are saddle-dominance regimes.}
}
\]

## 4. Phase Transitions

A phase transition occurs when the dominant saddle or the analytic structure of the free energy changes.

The free energy density is

\[
f
=
-
\frac{1}{\beta V}
\ln Z.
\]

A phase transition is a nonanalyticity in \(f\) as a control parameter is varied.

Control parameters include:

1. temperature \(T\),
2. coupling constants \(g_i\),
3. chemical potential \(\mu\),
4. external fields \(h\),
5. topological angles \(\theta\),
6. number of colors or flavors,
7. compactification moduli,
8. cosmological parameters.

First-order transitions involve discontinuous changes in order parameters.

Continuous transitions involve diverging correlation length and universality.

Topological transitions may occur without local order parameters.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Phase transitions are boundaries between effective worlds.}
}
\]

Goldstone modes appear when continuous global symmetries are broken.

The Higgs mechanism appears when gauge symmetries are broken.

Thus the same underlying theory may have different effective particles and symmetries in different phases.

## 8. Phase-Relative Ontology

Different phases support different entities.

### 8.1 Solid Phase

In a crystal, atoms form a lattice.

The low-energy entities are phonons.

Phonons do not exist as well-defined particles in a gas.

### 8.2 Ferromagnetic Phase

In a magnet, spin waves or magnons exist below the Curie temperature.

Above the transition, magnons are not stable entities.

### 8.3 Superconducting Phase

In a superconductor, Cooper pairs and collective modes exist.

The electromagnetic field acquires an effective mass through the Meissner effect.

### 8.4 QCD Phases

At low temperature, the entities are hadrons: protons, neutrons, pions.

At high temperature, the entities are quarks and gluons in a quark-gluon plasma.

Both descriptions arise from the same underlying QCD.

### 8.5 Topological Phases

In fractional quantum Hall states, the entities are anyons.

Anyons obey braid statistics:

\[
\psi
\rightarrow
\rho(\beta)\psi,
\]

where \(\rho\) is a representation of the braid group.

These entities do not exist outside the topological phase.

### 8.6 Semiclassical Gravity

In a semiclassical phase, the entity is spacetime geometry.

In a strongly quantum phase, geometry may not exist.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What exists is phase-relative.}
}
\]

The dominant saddle minimizes the effective free energy.

## Appendix E: Hawking–Page Transition

The gravitational partition function contains competing saddles:

\[
Z_{\text{grav}}
\approx
e^{-I_{\text{thermal AdS}}}
+
e^{-I_{\text{AdS-BH}}}.
\]

At low temperature, thermal AdS dominates.

At high temperature, the AdS black hole dominates.

This is a geometric phase transition dual to confinement/deconfinement.
