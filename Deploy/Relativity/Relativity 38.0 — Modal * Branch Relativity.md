## The Relativity of Actuality and the Invariance of Possibility Structure

**White paper / academic preprint**

## Abstract

Modal / Branch Relativity is the hypothesis that actuality is not absolute. What is “actual” may be relative to a branch, history, decoherent realm, or measure over possibilities. In path-integral physics, physical amplitudes are not assigned to one actual trajectory but to a weighted sum over histories,

\[
\mathcal{A}
=
\sum_{\gamma}
e^{iS[\gamma]/\hbar}.
\]

In Everettian quantum mechanics, the universal state contains many branches, and “our outcome” is indexical within the branch structure. In consistent histories, probabilities are assigned only within decoherent realms, not to a single privileged history. In quantum cosmology, the universe may be described by a wavefunction over geometries and histories, with classical spacetimes emerging as semiclassical branches. The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Actuality is branch-relative; the invariant is the full possibility structure with its measure.}
}
\]

Modal / Branch Relativity does not deny reality. It redefines reality. The basic object is not “what happened” from a view outside the world. The basic object is the amplitude, measure, or weight over what can happen. Actuality is the location of an observer within that structure. This framework unifies path integrals, Everettian branching, decoherent histories, quantum cosmology, self-locating probability, and the measure problem into a single relativistic principle.

## 1. Introduction

Classical physics assumes that one history is actual.

A particle follows one trajectory. A field evolves through one configuration. The universe unfolds along one timeline. Measurement reveals one outcome. The actual is absolute, and the possible is merely potential.

Quantum physics destabilizes this assumption.

In the path-integral formulation, a particle does not travel along one trajectory. The amplitude is a sum over all trajectories:

\[
\langle q_f,t_f|q_i,t_i\rangle
=
\int_{q_i}^{q_f}
\mathcal{D}q(t)
\,
e^{iS[q]/\hbar}.
\]

In Everettian quantum mechanics, measurement does not select one outcome absolutely. The universal state contains all outcomes in decohered branches:

\[
\sum_i c_i
\ket{s_i}
\ket{O_i}
\ket{E_i}.
\]

In consistent histories, one does not assign probabilities to arbitrary histories. One assigns probabilities only within decoherent realms.

In quantum cosmology, the universe may not have one classical history. It may be described by a wavefunction over geometries:

\[
\Psi[h_{ij},\phi].
\]

These developments suggest a radical possibility:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Actuality may not be absolute. It may be relative to a branch or history.}
}
\]

Modal / Branch Relativity makes this explicit.

It says that the invariant physical structure is not a single actual history. It is the full modal structure: the space of histories, branches, amplitudes, weights, and measures.

## 2. Actuality, Possibility, and Indexicality

Modal / Branch Relativity distinguishes three notions:

1. **Possibility**: what can occur within the physical structure.
2. **Actuality**: what occurs relative to a branch, history, or observer-location.
3. **Invariant reality**: the full structure of possibilities with their amplitudes or weights.

In classical physics, actuality and reality coincide:

\[
\text{Reality}
=
\text{the one actual history}.
\]

In Modal / Branch Relativity, they separate:

\[
\text{Reality}
=
\text{the full modal structure},
\]

\[
\text{Actuality}
=
\text{indexical location within that structure}.
\]

The word “actual” functions like “here” or “now.”

“Here” is not an absolute location in space. It is indexical: it depends on where the speaker is.

“Now” is not an absolute moment in time. It is indexical: it depends on the temporal location of the speaker.

Similarly, “actual” may be indexical: it depends on the branch or history in which the speaker is located.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{“Actual” is to branch what “here” is to space.}
}
\]

This is the core semantic move of Modal / Branch Relativity.

## 3. Path Integrals and the Plurality of Histories

The path-integral formulation provides the first major motivation.

In ordinary quantum mechanics, the transition amplitude from an initial configuration \(q_i\) at time \(t_i\) to a final configuration \(q_f\) at time \(t_f\) is

\[
\langle q_f,t_f|q_i,t_i\rangle
=
\int_{q_i}^{q_f}
\mathcal{D}q(t)
\,
e^{iS[q]/\hbar}.
\]

The amplitude is not the property of one path. It is a sum over all paths.

Each path contributes a phase,

\[
e^{iS[q]/\hbar}.
\]

No single path is absolutely actual in the quantum amplitude.

In the classical limit,

\[
\hbar \to 0,
\]

the dominant contributions come from stationary points of the action:

\[
\delta S[q]=0.
\]

These stationary paths satisfy the classical equations of motion.

Thus classical history emerges as an approximation to the full modal structure.

The classical trajectory is not fundamental. It is a saddle point in the space of possibilities.

## 4. Quantum Measure over Histories

The path integral defines a quantum measure over histories.

For a set of histories \(A\), define the amplitude

\[
\mathcal{A}(A)
=
\sum_{\gamma\in A}
e^{iS[\gamma]/\hbar}.
\]

The quantum measure is

\[
\mu(A)
=
|\mathcal{A}(A)|^2.
\]

This measure is not generally additive:

\[
\mu(A\cup B)
\neq
\mu(A)+\mu(B)
\]

when interference is present.

Instead, quantum measure theory satisfies grade-2 additivity:

\[
\mu(A\cup B\cup C)
=
\mu(A\cup B)
+
\mu(A\cup C)
+
\mu(B\cup C)
-
\mu(A)
-
\mu(B)
-
\mu(C).
\]

This expresses the fact that quantum theory has pairwise interference but no third-order interference.

Thus the basic object is not a probability distribution over actual histories. It is a quantum measure over possible histories.

Classical probability emerges only when interference is suppressed.

## 5. Decoherent Histories and Realms

The consistent or decoherent histories formulation makes this emergence precise.

Let \(\alpha\) label coarse-grained histories. Each history has a class operator \(C_\alpha\).

Given an initial state \(\rho\), the decoherence functional is

\[
D(\alpha,\beta)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(
C_\alpha
\rho
C_\beta^\dagger
\right).
\]

If the off-diagonal terms vanish approximately,

\[
D(\alpha,\beta)
\approx
0
\quad
\text{for}
\quad
\alpha\neq\beta,
\]

then the histories decohere.

One may then assign probabilities,

\[
p(\alpha)
=
D(\alpha,\alpha).
\]

A decoherent set of histories is called a realm.

Within a realm, one can speak as if one history occurs.

But the realm itself is a coarse-grained structure within the full quantum measure.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Classical histories are decoherent branches within a quantum measure.}
}
\]

## 6. Everettian Branching

Everettian quantum mechanics provides the clearest branch ontology.

Consider a measurement interaction.

A system begins in a superposition,

\[
\ket{\psi}_S
=
\sum_i c_i \ket{s_i}.
\]

The observer begins in a ready state \(\ket{O_0}\), and the environment in \(\ket{E_0}\).

Unitary evolution gives

\[
\sum_i c_i \ket{s_i}\ket{O_0}\ket{E_0}
\rightarrow
\sum_i c_i \ket{s_i}\ket{O_i}\ket{E_i}.
\]

The observer states \(\ket{O_i}\) record different outcomes.

The environment states \(\ket{E_i}\) become approximately orthogonal:

\[
\langle E_i|E_j\rangle
\approx
\delta_{ij}.
\]

Thus the global state becomes a superposition of effectively noninterfering branches:

\[
\ket{\Psi}
=
\sum_i c_i
\ket{s_i}
\ket{O_i}
\ket{E_i}.
\]

Each branch contains an observer who sees a definite outcome.

No collapse is required.

The appearance of collapse is branch-relative.

## 7. Branch Weights

Each branch has a weight,

\[
w_i
=
|c_i|^2.
\]

The total weight is normalized:

\[
\sum_i w_i
=
1.
\]

The branch weight is not merely a subjective probability. It is an objective measure derived from the Hilbert-space norm.

The Born rule identifies branch weight with probability:

\[
P(i)
=
w_i
=
|c_i|^2.
\]

In Modal / Branch Relativity, this probability is interpreted as self-locating probability:

\[
P(\text{I am in branch } i)
=
w_i.
\]

The invariant is not the indexical fact “I am in branch \(i\).”

The invariant is the full branch structure,

\[
\{w_i,\ket{\Psi_i}\},
\]

together with the global state \(\ket{\Psi}\).

## 8. Actuality as Branch-Relative

In Modal / Branch Relativity, actuality is a relation between an observer and a branch.

Let \(b\) label a branch and \(O\) an observer within that branch.

Define

\[
\mathrm{Actual}(O,b)
\]

to mean:

\[
\text{Observer } O \text{ is located in branch } b.

This is not an absolute predicate.

It is indexical.

From inside branch \(b\), the observer says:

\[
\text{Outcome } o_b \text{ is actual.}
\]

From inside branch \(b'\), another observer says:

\[
\text{Outcome } o_{b'} \text{ is actual.}

Both statements are true relative to their branches.

The global state contains both.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Actuality is not a global property of a branch. It is a relational property between observer and branch.}
}
\]

Modal / Branch Relativity says something more disciplined:

\[
\text{All physically realized branches or histories are real, weighted by amplitude or measure.}

Thus Modal / Branch Relativity is a physicalized modal realism.

It replaces “all possible worlds” with:

## 12. Preferred Basis and Decoherence

A common objection to Everettian branching is the preferred-basis problem:

\[
\text{What selects the basis in which branching occurs?}
\]

Decoherence provides the answer.

The environment selects pointer states that are stable under interaction.

The reduced density matrix of a system becomes approximately diagonal in the pointer basis:

\[
\rho_S
=
\operatorname{Tr}_E
\ket{\Psi}\bra{\Psi}
\approx
\sum_i
|c_i|^2
\ket{s_i}\bra{s_i}.
\]

The off-diagonal terms are suppressed:

\[
\rho_{ij}
\approx
0
\quad
\text{for}
\quad
i\neq j.
\]

Thus the branch basis is selected dynamically by environmental interaction.

Branches are not arbitrary decompositions. They are the decompositions in which records are stable.

P(i)

P(\text{I am in branch } i).

The Born rule assigns this self-locating probability:

\[

\[
\Psi(a,\phi),

Thus our universe may be one branch within a cosmological wavefunction.

\[
\Psi_{\text{HH}}[h,\phi]

\mathcal{D}g\,\mathcal{D}\Phi

\boxed{
\text{The universe is not necessarily one actual history. It may be a modal structure of histories.}

\sum_i
|c_i|^2
\ket{s_i}\bra{s_i}.
\]

Thus interference is suppressed and branches become effectively independent.

---

## Appendix C: Self-Locating Probability

Let branches be labeled by \(b\in\mathcal{B}\).

Let branch weights be \(w(b)\).

If an observer knows the global state but not their branch location, the self-locating probability is

\[
P(b)
=
\frac{w(b)}
{\sum_{b'}w(b')}.
\]

For normalized quantum branches,

\[
P(b)
=
|c_b|^2.
\]

---

## Appendix D: Quantum Measure Grade-2 Additivity

For disjoint sets of histories \(A,B,C\),

\[
\mu(A\cup B\cup C)
=
\mu(A\cup B)
+
\mu(A\cup C)
+
\mu(B\cup C)
-
\mu(A)
-
\mu(B)
-
\mu(C).
\]

This expresses pairwise interference without third-order interference.
