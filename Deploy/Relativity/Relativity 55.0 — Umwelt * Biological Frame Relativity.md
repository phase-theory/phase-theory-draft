## Abstract

Umwelt / Biological Frame Relativity is the hypothesis that each biological species inhabits a species-specific perceptual and operational world, or Umwelt. An Umwelt is not merely a subjective perspective. It is a physically structured sensorimotor frame determined by sensory channels, motor capacities, ecological niche, metabolic constraints, and evolutionary history. Formally, an Umwelt may be written as

\[
U
=
(S,E,N,H),
\]

where \(S\) is sensorium, \(E\) is effector capacity, \(N\) is niche, and \(H\) is evolutionary history. The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The lived physical world is relative to biological form.}
}
\]

A bat, a bee, a dog, an electric fish, a human, and a deep-sea worm do not inhabit the same operational world, even though they are embedded in the same physical universe. Each organism samples different physical variables, acts through different effectors, and evolves under different ecological pressures. The invariant is not the world-as-seen-by-one-species. The invariant is the structure that can be translated, calibrated, or modeled across Umwelten. Umwelt / Biological Frame Relativity extends embodied-observer relativity into biology. It unifies sensory ecology, evolutionary theory, cybernetics, biosemiotics, and comparative neuroscience into a relativistic framework. It denies neither physical reality nor biological objectivity. It redefines objectivity as cross-Umwelt invariance.

## 1. Introduction

The physical universe contains vastly more structure than any one organism can access.

Electromagnetic fields extend from radio waves to gamma rays. Chemical gradients permeate air, water, and soil. Mechanical vibrations travel through solids, liquids, and gases. Electric fields exist in aquatic environments. Magnetic fields span the planet. Thermal gradients structure ecosystems. Gravitational fields orient bodies. Information is embedded in light, sound, odor, touch, voltage, and molecular binding.

No organism senses all of this.

Each organism senses a narrow, species-specific subset.

A human sees visible light, hears audible sound, feels touch, tastes chemicals, and smells volatile molecules.

A bat navigates by ultrasonic echoes.

A bee sees ultraviolet patterns and polarized light.

A dog lives in an olfactory world.

An electric fish senses voltage gradients.

A pit viper senses infrared radiation.

A whale senses low-frequency sound across ocean basins.

A bacterium senses chemical gradients and moves through run-and-tumble dynamics.

Each organism does not merely have different opinions about the same world.

It inhabits a different operational world.

This is the Umwelt.

Umwelt / Biological Frame Relativity says:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A biological species does not merely live in an environment. It lives in a species-specific world.}
}
\]

## 2. The Concept of Umwelt

The term Umwelt was developed by Jakob von Uexküll.

It means the self-world of an organism.

An Umwelt is not the objective environment described by physics.

It is the meaningful world disclosed by an organism’s sensorimotor organization.

For a tick, the relevant world may consist of butyric acid, temperature, and tactile cues.

For a bee, the relevant world includes flower color patterns, ultraviolet markers, polarization, and nectar reward.

For a bat, the relevant world is structured by echo delay, amplitude, Doppler shift, and acoustic texture.

The Umwelt is therefore not a mental illusion.

It is a biological frame of access to physical reality.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{An Umwelt is a biologically stabilized frame of physical access.}
}
\]

## 3. Formal Definition of an Umwelt

Let an Umwelt be represented as

\[
U
=

\]

where:

- \(S\) is the sensorium,
- \(E\) is the effector capacity,

In more detailed formulations, one may include metabolism explicitly:

\[
U
=
(S,E,N,M,H),

where \(M\) is metabolic constraint. For compactness, metabolic constraints may also be treated as part of niche and evolutionary history.

Each component shapes the organism’s world.

It includes:

1. sensory modalities,
2. receptor types,
3. bandwidths,
4. thresholds,
5. resolutions,
6. temporal integration windows,
7. noise characteristics,

- magnetoreceptors,
- proprioceptors,

The sensorium defines a sensory map,

\[
S_U:
\mathcal{P}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{O}_U,
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal{P}\) is the physical environment,
- \(\mathcal{O}_U\) is the organism’s observation space.

The organism does not sense \(\mathcal{P}\) in full.

It senses only the projection allowed by \(S_U\).

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The sensorium is a biological filter.}
}

## 5. Sensory Bandwidth and Resolution

Every sensory channel has physical limits.

Let a physical signal be \(x(t)\).

A sensory channel transforms it into a response \(r(t)\):

\[
r(t)
=
\int K(t-t')x(t')dt'
+
n(t),
\]

where:

- \(K\) is the sensory response kernel,
- \(n(t)\) is noise.

The channel has:

1. a frequency bandwidth,
2. a dynamic range,
3. a minimum detectable signal,
4. a maximum tolerable signal,
5. a temporal resolution,
6. a spatial resolution.

For example, human vision has spectral sensitivity roughly between 400 and 700 nanometers.

Bats use ultrasound often above 20 kHz.

Bees sense ultraviolet wavelengths inaccessible to humans.

Electric fish sense microvolt-scale voltage changes.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sensory bandwidth defines the boundaries of the Umwelt.}
}
\]

## 7. Affordances

The ecological psychologist James Gibson introduced the concept of affordances.

An affordance is a possibility for action offered by the environment to an organism.

For a bird, a branch affords perching.

For a fish, water affords swimming.

For a bat, air affords echolocative flight.

For a human, a surface affords walking, sitting, or climbing.

Affordances are not intrinsic properties of objects alone.

They are relational properties between environment and embodiment.

Formally, the affordance set of an environment state \(p\) for Umwelt \(U\) is

\[
\mathrm{Aff}_U(p)
=
\{
a\in\mathcal{A}_U
\mid
a\text{ is possible and effective in }p
\}.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The environment appears to organisms as a field of affordances.}
}
\]

Neurons consume energy.

\[
B
=
B_{\text{sensory}}
+
B_{\text{neural}}
+
B_{\text{motor}}

B_{\text{maintenance}}

\[
C_S
\leq

Evolution optimizes not maximal information, but fitness-relevant information under metabolic constraint.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Metabolism bounds the Umwelt.}

## 11. Observational Equivalence Within an Umwelt

\[
S_U(p)
=
S_U(p').

More generally, if the organism can act, then equivalence requires indistinguishability under all possible sensorimotor loops:

\[
P(o\mid p,a,U)
=
P(o\mid p',a,U)
\]

for all accessible actions \(a\).

Define the equivalence relation,

\[
p
\sim_U
p'.

The lived world of the organism is the quotient,

\[
W_U
=
\mathcal{P}/\sim_U.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The Umwelt is the world quotient defined by biological indistinguishability.}
}

## 12. Information Capacity of an Umwelt

The information capacity of an Umwelt is bounded by sensory channels.

For a sensory channel with input \(X\) and response \(Y\), mutual information is

\[
I(X;Y)
=
\sum_{x,y}
P(x,y)
\log
\frac{P(x,y)}{P(x)P(y)}.
\]

The channel capacity is

\[
C_U
=
\max_{p(x)} I(X;Y).
\]

The total Umwelt capacity is constrained by the sum of channel capacities, metabolic costs, and neural processing limits.

Thus:

\[
C_U
\leq
\sum_i C_i,
\]

where \(i\) indexes sensory channels.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{An Umwelt has a finite information capacity.}
}
\]

## 37. Conclusion

Relativity 55.0, Umwelt / Biological Frame Relativity, asserts that each biological species inhabits a species-specific perceptual and operational world.

An Umwelt is defined by sensorium, effectors, niche, metabolism, and evolutionary history:

\[
U
=

The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The lived physical world is relative to biological form.}
}
\]

A bat, a bee, a dog, an electric fish, and a human do not live in the same operational world.

They live in different projections of physical reality, shaped by evolutionary history and ecological need.

But this does not destroy objectivity.

Objectivity is the discovery of structures that can be translated, calibrated, and preserved across Umwelten.

Physics remains real.

Biology remains real.

But neither is absolute from one species’ point of view.

The invariant is not the world-as-seen-by-one-species.

It is the structure that survives translation across biological frames.

This is Umwelt / Biological Frame Relativity.
