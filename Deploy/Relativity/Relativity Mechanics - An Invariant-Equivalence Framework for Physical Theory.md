# Relativity Mechanics: An Invariant-Equivalence Framework for Physical Theory and the Emergence of Newtonian Mechanics, General Relativity, Quantum Theory, Quantum Field Theory, and String Theory

## Abstract

Relativity Mechanics develops a general mathematical theory of physical description. Rather than beginning with spacetime, fields, particles, metrics, or quantum states, it begins with a more primitive observation: every physical system admits multiple descriptions related by transformations that preserve physical content. The fundamental object of physics is therefore not an individual description but the equivalence class of admissible descriptions generated by a symmetry action. Physical reality is represented by invariants of these equivalence classes rather than by any particular representative.

The theory introduces the concept of a relative description space \(\Omega\), whose elements represent all admissible descriptions of a single physical system. A transformation group \(G\) acts upon this space through

\[
\triangleright:G\times\Omega\rightarrow\Omega,
\]

partitioning \(\Omega\) into equivalence classes, or orbits,

\[
[\omega]=G\cdot\omega.
\]

The physically meaningful state is identified not with a point \(\omega\in\Omega\), but with its orbit under the admissibility group. Observable quantities are functions on the quotient space

\[
\Omega/G,
\]

which constitutes the invariant description space of the theory.

Relativity Mechanics therefore replaces configuration-based ontology with orbit-based ontology. Individual descriptions possess no absolute physical significance apart from the admissibility relations that connect them. The mathematical structure of a physical theory is determined by the choice of admissibility group, the geometry of its action, and the invariant structures induced on the corresponding quotient space. Different physical theories arise by specifying different description spaces, transformation groups, admissibility conditions, and dynamical laws on the resulting orbit manifold.

Within this framework, the theory introduces the relativity schema

\[
\mathcal R=(\Omega,G,\triangleright,I),
\]

where \(\Omega\) is the description space, \(G\) is the admissibility group, \(\triangleright\) denotes its action, and

\[
I:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega/G
\]

is the invariant projection. This schema provides a universal mathematical language for constructing physical theories independent of any particular geometric, quantum, or field-theoretic assumptions.

Relativity Mechanics also establishes a hierarchy of descriptive structures obtained by enlarging admissibility groups. Conventional state descriptions correspond to trivial admissibility. More sophisticated structures incorporate additional relational freedoms while preserving invariant content. Theories such as gauge theories, quantum reference-frame theories, local-frame gravitation, and other symmetry-based formulations appear as specializations of the same underlying formalism, differing only in the choice of admissibility group and the geometry of the corresponding orbit space.

Dynamics is formulated directly on invariant structures rather than on arbitrary representatives. Physical evolution is therefore required to commute with admissibility transformations, ensuring that trajectories are well-defined on the quotient space. Conservation laws arise from invariant group actions through generalized Noether principles, while observables are characterized as functions invariant under admissible transformations.

Relativity Mechanics thus provides a universal mathematical foundation for relational physics. It is not itself a theory of gravitation, quantum mechanics, gauge fields, or spacetime, but a theory describing how any physical theory should distinguish invariant physical content from arbitrary descriptive freedom. Existing theories emerge as realizations of this framework once their admissibility groups and associated dynamical structures are specified.

Its central principle is

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical reality is represented by invariant equivalence classes of admissible descriptions, not by individual descriptions themselves.}
}
\]

In this formulation, relativity becomes a universal mathematical principle governing the relationship between description, symmetry, and physical information. Frame Theory, gauge theories, general relativity, quantum mechanics, quantum field theory, string theory, and future relational physical theories arise as particular realizations of this more fundamental architecture.

## 1. Introduction

Physics has historically progressed by identifying which elements of a description are physically meaningful and which are artifacts of the chosen representation. Galilean relativity removes absolute uniform motion. Special relativity removes absolute simultaneity. General relativity removes absolute coordinates. Gauge theory removes absolute phase, local frame, and internal orientation. Quantum theory removes the absolute status of basis-dependent amplitudes. In each case, the physical content is not a particular description but an equivalence class of descriptions related by transformations that leave observable content unchanged.

Relativity Mechanics elevates this pattern into a foundational mathematical framework. Its central claim is that a physical theory is determined not primarily by its objects — particles, fields, metrics, wavefunctions, strings — but by its admissibility structure: the group of transformations under which descriptions are to be identified.

A physical description, in this framework, is never a bare state. It is a presented state: a state expressed relative to coordinates, frames, bases, trivializations, gauges, or parametrizations. The space of all such presented states is the description space \(\Omega\). The admissibility group \(G\) acts on \(\Omega\) by changing the presentation while preserving the physical system being described. The physical state is then the orbit

\[
[\omega]=G\cdot\omega,
\]

and the true state space of the theory is the quotient

\[
\mathcal Q=\Omega/G.
\]

Observables are not arbitrary functions on \(\Omega\), but invariant functions

\[
\mathcal O(\Omega)=C^\infty(\Omega)^G\cong C^\infty(\Omega/G),
\]

possibly generalized to distributional, algebraic, or noncommutative invariants in quantum theories.

The purpose of this paper is to show that the principal structures of modern theoretical physics — Newtonian mechanics, general relativity, quantum mechanics, quantum field theory, and string theory — arise as specializations of this single schema.

The organization is as follows. Section 2 develops the formal architecture of Relativity Mechanics: description spaces, admissibility groups, invariant projections, quotient dynamics, Noether theory, and quantization. Section 3 recovers Newtonian physics as Galilean Relativity Mechanics. Section 4 recovers general relativity as diffeomorphism- and local-frame-invariant Relativity Mechanics. Section 5 recovers quantum physics and nonrelativistic quantum mechanics as projective-Hilbert and algebraic Relativity Mechanics. Section 6 recovers quantum field theory as gauge- and spacetime-relational Relativity Mechanics. Section 7 recovers string theory as worldsheet-diffeomorphism- and Weyl-invariant Relativity Mechanics. Section 8 gives a comparative synthesis. Section 9 concludes.

## 2. Formal Architecture of Relativity Mechanics

### 2.1 Description spaces and admissibility groups

Let a physical system be given. A description of that system is not identified with the system itself. It is a presentation of the system relative to auxiliary descriptive structure: coordinates, frames, bases, gauges, parametrizations, or reference systems.

A **relative description space** is a space \(\Omega\) whose points \(\omega\in\Omega\) are complete admissible descriptions of the system. The space \(\Omega\) may be a manifold, infinite-dimensional function space, bundle, Hilbert-space coordinate space, field configuration space, or categorical object.

An **admissibility group** \(G\) is a group of transformations of descriptions that preserve physical content. It acts on \(\Omega\) by

\[
\triangleright:G\times\Omega\rightarrow\Omega,
\]

satisfying

\[
e\triangleright \omega=\omega,
\]

\[
g_1\triangleright(g_2\triangleright\omega)=(g_1g_2)\triangleright\omega.
\]

The orbit of \(\omega\) is

\[
[\omega]=G\cdot\omega=\{g\triangleright\omega\mid g\in G\}.
\]

The physical state is the orbit, not the representative.

The invariant projection is

\[
I:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega/G,
\]

\[
I(\omega)=[\omega].
\]

The quadruple

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R=(\Omega,G,\triangleright,I)}
\]

is called a **relativity schema**.

If the action is free and proper, \(\Omega\to\Omega/G\) is a principal \(G\)-bundle. If the action has stabilizers, singularities, or reducible gauge symmetries, the quotient is more properly treated as a stratified space, orbifold, or stack

\[
[\Omega/G].
\]

The stacky formulation is especially natural for gauge theories and string theory, where residual automorphisms may remain.

---

### 2.2 Observables as invariant functions

A function \(f:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) is physically meaningful only if it is constant on admissibility orbits:

\[
f(g\triangleright\omega)=f(\omega),\qquad \forall g\in G.
\]

The algebra of physical observables is therefore

\[
\mathcal O_{\rm phys}=C^\infty(\Omega)^G.
\]

When the quotient is well behaved,

\[
C^\infty(\Omega)^G\cong C^\infty(\Omega/G).
\]

Thus observables are functions on the invariant description space.

For quantum or singular systems, this algebra may be replaced by:

1. a \(C^*\)-algebra of bounded observables,
2. a von Neumann algebra,
3. a BRST cohomology algebra,
4. a reduced phase-space algebra,
5. a noncommutative invariant algebra.

The principle remains unchanged:

\[
\boxed{\text{Observables are invariants of the admissibility action.}}
\]

---

### 2.3 Dynamics on the quotient

Let a dynamical law on \(\Omega\) be given by a vector field \(X\in\Gamma(T\Omega)\), so that descriptions evolve according to

\[
\frac{d\omega}{dt}=X(\omega).
\]

For this dynamics to define a physical evolution on equivalence classes, it must be independent of the representative. The necessary and sufficient condition is \(G\)-equivariance:

\[
X(g\triangleright\omega)=(g_*)X(\omega),
\]

for all \(g\in G\), \(\omega\in\Omega\).

Equivalently, the flow \(\Phi_t\) of \(X\) must commute with the admissibility action:

\[
\Phi_t\circ \triangleright_g=\triangleright_g\circ\Phi_t.
\]

Then there exists a unique descended vector field \(\bar X\) on \(\Omega/G\) such that

\[
I_*X=\bar X I.
\]

Thus the physical dynamics is

\[
\frac{d[\omega]}{dt}=\bar X([\omega]).
\]

This is the dynamical core of Relativity Mechanics:

\[
\boxed{\text{Physical evolution is well-defined only when it commutes with admissibility transformations.}}
\]

If the dynamics does not commute with \(G\), then the evolution depends on descriptive choices and is therefore not physically well defined.

---

### 2.4 Constraints and gauge reduction

Many physical theories possess constraints. In Relativity Mechanics, first-class constraints are infinitesimal generators of admissibility transformations.

Let \(\mathcal C\subset\Omega\) be the constraint surface,

\[
\mathcal C=\{\omega\in\Omega\mid \phi_a(\omega)=0\},
\]

and let the constraints satisfy a closed algebra

\[
\{\phi_a,\phi_b\}=f_{ab}{}^{c}\phi_c.
\]

The gauge orbits generated by \(\phi_a\) are admissibility orbits. The physical phase space is the reduced space

\[
\mathcal Q_{\rm phys}=\mathcal C/G.
\]

In symplectic form, if \(G\) acts Hamiltonianly with moment map

\[
\mu:\Omega\to\mathfrak g^*,
\]

the Marsden–Weinstein reduced space is

\[
\Omega//G=\mu^{-1}(0)/G.
\]

Thus constrained dynamics is a special case of quotient dynamics.

---

### 2.5 Generalized Noether theorem

Let \(G\) be a Lie group with Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\). For each \(\xi\in\mathfrak g\), define the infinitesimal generator

\[
\xi_\Omega(\omega)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(t\xi)\triangleright\omega.
\]

Suppose the action functional

\[
S[\omega]=\int L(\omega,\dot\omega)\,dt
\]

is invariant under \(G\) up to a boundary term:

\[
\delta_\xi L=\frac{dF_\xi}{dt}.
\]

Then the Noether charge

\[
J_\xi=\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot\omega^i}\xi_\Omega^i-F_\xi
\]

is conserved along solutions:

\[
\frac{dJ_\xi}{dt}=0.
\]

In Hamiltonian form, if \((\Omega,\omega)\) is symplectic and the \(G\)-action is Hamiltonian, there exists a moment map \(\mu:\Omega\to\mathfrak g^*\) such that

\[
\iota_{\xi_\Omega}\omega=d\mu_\xi.
\]

If the Hamiltonian \(H\) is \(G\)-invariant, then

\[
\frac{d\mu_\xi}{dt}=\{\mu_\xi,H\}=0.
\]

Therefore conservation laws arise from admissibility symmetries.

In gauge theories, the same moment maps become constraints:

\[
\mu_\xi\approx0.
\]

In quantum theory, the corresponding generators become operators satisfying

\[
[\hat H,\hat J_\xi]=0,
\]

or, for gauge generators,

\[
\hat J_\xi|\psi_{\rm phys}\rangle=0.
\]

---

### 2.6 Quantization as invariant theory

Quantization in Relativity Mechanics is not an independent ontology but a refinement of the invariant-description principle.

Classically, physical observables are

\[
C^\infty(\Omega)^G.
\]

Quantization replaces this commutative algebra by a noncommutative algebra \(\mathcal A\), together with an admissibility action. Physical observables are then the invariant subalgebra

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm phys}=\mathcal A^G,
\]

or, in the presence of constraints, the BRST cohomology

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm phys}=H^\bullet(Q_{\rm BRST}).
\]

Physical states are equivalence classes of quantum descriptions under admissibility transformations. In Dirac quantization,

\[
\hat\phi_a|\psi_{\rm phys}\rangle=0,
\]

where \(\hat\phi_a\) are quantum constraints generating admissibility.

Thus quantum physics is not outside Relativity Mechanics. It is Relativity Mechanics over noncommutative description spaces.

## 3. Newtonian Physics as Galilean Relativity Mechanics

Newtonian mechanics is often presented as a theory of particles moving in absolute space and time. Relativity Mechanics reinterprets it as a theory of equivalence classes of inertial descriptions.

### 3.1 Description space

For \(N\) Newtonian particles, let a description consist of particle trajectories expressed in an inertial frame:

\[
\omega=\bigl(t,\mathbf x_1(t),\dots,\mathbf x_N(t)\bigr).
\]

More relationally, we may include the frame itself:

\[
\omega=(F,\mathbf x_i^F(t)),
\]

where \(F\) consists of:

1. a choice of spatial origin,
2. a choice of spatial orientation,
3. a choice of inertial velocity,
4. a choice of time origin.

The description space is

\[
\Omega_N=\{\text{particle trajectories in inertial frames}\}.
\]

---

### 3.2 Galilean admissibility group

The admissibility group is the Galilei group

\[
G_{\rm Gal}=\mathbb R^3_{\rm translations}\rtimes
\bigl(SO(3)\ltimes \mathbb R^3_{\rm boosts}\bigr)
\times \mathbb R_{\rm time}.
\]

An element \(g=(\mathbf a,\mathbf v,R,s)\) acts on spacetime events by

\[
t'=t+s,
\]

\[
\mathbf x'=R\mathbf x+\mathbf v t+\mathbf a.
\]

For particle trajectories,

\[
(g\triangleright \mathbf x_i)(t')=
R\mathbf x_i(t)+\mathbf v t+\mathbf a.
\]

The invariant projection is

\[
I_N:\Omega_N\to\Omega_N/G_{\rm Gal}.
\]

The physical content of Newtonian mechanics is not the absolute coordinate trajectory \(\mathbf x_i(t)\), but the Galilean equivalence class

\[
[\mathbf x_i(t)]_{\rm Gal}.
\]

---

### 3.3 Invariant dynamics

Consider the Lagrangian

\[
L=\sum_{i=1}^N\frac12 m_i\dot{\mathbf x}_i^2
-
V(\{\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j\}).
\]

The potential depends only on relative separations, ensuring invariance under spatial translations and rotations.

Under a Galilean boost,

\[
\mathbf x_i\mapsto \mathbf x_i+\mathbf v t,
\]

the velocities transform as

\[
\dot{\mathbf x}_i\mapsto\dot{\mathbf x}_i+\mathbf v.
\]

The Lagrangian changes by a total time derivative:

\[
L\mapsto
L+\frac{d}{dt}
\left(
\sum_i m_i\mathbf x_i\cdot\mathbf v
+
\frac12\sum_i m_i\mathbf v^2 t
\right).
\]

Therefore the action is invariant. The Euler–Lagrange equations are Galilean covariant:

\[
m_i\ddot{\mathbf x}_i
=
-\frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf x_i}.
\]

If \(\mathbf x_i(t)\) is a solution, then

\[
R\mathbf x_i(t)+\mathbf v t+\mathbf a
\]

is also a solution.

Thus Newtonian dynamics commutes with the Galilean admissibility action and descends to the quotient:

\[
\frac{d[\omega]}{dt}=\bar X_N([\omega]).
\]

---

### 3.4 Noether charges

Galilean invariance produces the standard conservation laws.

Spatial translations give total momentum:

\[
\mathbf P=\sum_i m_i\dot{\mathbf x}_i.
\]

Rotations give total angular momentum:

\[
\mathbf J=\sum_i \mathbf x_i\times m_i\dot{\mathbf x}_i.
\]

Time translations give energy:

\[
E=\sum_i\frac12 m_i\dot{\mathbf x}_i^2+V.
\]

Boosts give the center-of-mass charge:

\[
\mathbf K=\sum_i m_i\mathbf x_i-t\mathbf P.
\]

Conservation of \(\mathbf K\) expresses uniform motion of the center of mass:

\[
\frac{d\mathbf K}{dt}=0.
\]

These are Noether invariants of the Galilean admissibility group.

---

### 3.5 Invariant physical content

The quotient \(\Omega_N/G_{\rm Gal}\) removes:

1. absolute spatial position,
2. absolute spatial orientation,
3. absolute uniform velocity,
4. absolute time origin.

It retains:

1. relative separations \(\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j\),
2. relative velocities \(\dot{\mathbf x}_i-\dot{\mathbf x}_j\),
3. accelerations,
4. mass ratios,
5. temporal intervals.

Thus Newtonian physics is the relativity schema

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{\rm Newton}=
(\Omega_N,G_{\rm Gal},\triangleright,I_N).
}
\]

Newtonian mechanics is therefore not abolished by Relativity Mechanics. It is recovered as the theory whose admissibility group is the Galilei group.

---

### 3.6 Newton–Cartan geometrization

Newtonian gravity may be recast in a generally covariant form using Newton–Cartan geometry. The description space includes manifolds equipped with:

1. a temporal one-form \(t_\mu\),
2. a degenerate spatial metric \(h^{\mu\nu}\),
3. a compatible connection \(\nabla\),

satisfying

\[
\nabla_\mu t_\nu=0,
\]

\[
\nabla_\mu h^{\nu\rho}=0,
\]

\[
h^{\mu\nu}t_\nu=0.
\]

The field equation is

\[
R_{\mu\nu}=4\pi G\rho\, t_\mu t_\nu.
\]

Here the admissibility group is enlarged to spacetime diffeomorphisms preserving the Newtonian temporal structure. This demonstrates that even geometrized Newtonian gravity fits the orbit-based logic of Relativity Mechanics.

## 4. General Relativity as Diffeomorphism- and Local-Frame-Invariant Relativity Mechanics

General relativity is the paradigmatic example of Relativity Mechanics. Its physical content is not a metric in a coordinate system but a diffeomorphism equivalence class of geometric structures.

### 4.1 Description space

Let \(M\) be a smooth four-manifold. The metric description space is

\[
\Omega_{\rm met}=\Gamma\bigl(S^2T^*M\bigr)_{\rm Lor},
\]

the space of Lorentzian metrics \(g_{\mu\nu}\) on \(M\), possibly coupled to matter fields \(\psi\):

\[
\Omega_{\rm GR}=\{(g_{\mu\nu},\psi)\}.
\]

A description includes a coordinate chart. Coordinates are not physical; they are descriptive presentations.

---

### 4.2 Diffeomorphism admissibility group

The admissibility group is the diffeomorphism group

\[
G_{\rm GR}=\operatorname{Diff}(M).
\]

For \(\phi\in\operatorname{Diff}(M)\),

\[
\phi\triangleright g_{\mu\nu}=(\phi^*g)_{\mu\nu},
\]

\[
\phi\triangleright\psi=\phi^*\psi.
\]

Infinitesimally, for a vector field \(\xi^\mu\),

\[
\delta_\xi g_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}
=
\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu+\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu.
\]

The invariant projection is

\[
I_{\rm GR}:\Omega_{\rm GR}\to\Omega_{\rm GR}/\operatorname{Diff}(M).
\]

The physical gravitational field is not \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\) but the equivalence class

\[
[g_{\mu\nu}].
\]

The quotient

\[
\mathcal S=\operatorname{Met}(M)/\operatorname{Diff}(M)
\]

is often called superspace.

---

### 4.3 Einstein–Hilbert action

The Einstein–Hilbert action is

\[
S_{\rm EH}[g]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}
\int_M d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,R.
\]

It is invariant under diffeomorphisms:

\[
S_{\rm EH}[\phi^*g]=S_{\rm EH}[g].
\]

Variation with respect to \(g^{\mu\nu}\) gives

\[
\delta S_{\rm EH}
=
\frac{1}{16\pi G}
\int_M d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,
G_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}
+
\text{boundary terms},
\]

where

\[
G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12 Rg_{\mu\nu}.
\]

With matter action \(S_{\rm matter}\),

\[
\delta S_{\rm matter}
=
-\frac12\int_M d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,
T_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}.
\]

The stationary action principle yields

\[
\boxed{
G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}.
}
\]

Because the action is diffeomorphism invariant, the contracted Bianchi identity implies

\[
\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu}=0,
\]

and therefore

\[
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0.
\]

Thus the dynamical law is compatible with the admissibility group.

---

### 4.4 Tetrad and local Lorentz formulation

A deeper relational description uses local frames. Let \(e^I_\mu\) be a tetrad,

\[
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{IJ}e^I_\mu e^J_\nu,
\]

with \(\eta_{IJ}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\).

The description space includes tetrads and spin connections:

\[
\Omega_{\rm frame}=\{(e^I_\mu,\omega_\mu{}^{IJ},\psi)\}.
\]

The admissibility group is enlarged to

\[
G_{\rm frame}=\operatorname{Diff}(M)\ltimes SO(1,3)_{\rm local}.
\]

A local Lorentz transformation \(\Lambda^I{}_J(x)\) acts by

\[
e^I_\mu\mapsto \Lambda^I{}_J e^J_\mu,
\]

\[
\omega_\mu\mapsto
\Lambda\omega_\mu\Lambda^{-1}
-
(d\Lambda)\Lambda^{-1}.
\]

The curvature is

\[
F^{IJ}=d\omega^{IJ}+\omega^I{}_K\wedge\omega^{KJ}.
\]

The Einstein–Hilbert action can be written as

\[
S[e,\omega]
=
\frac{1}{16\pi G}
\int \epsilon_{IJKL}\,
e^I\wedge e^J\wedge F^{KL}.
\]

The physical metric is recovered after quotienting by local Lorentz transformations:

\[
[g_{\mu\nu}]=[e^I_\mu]_{SO(1,3)}.
\]

Thus general relativity is a frame theory, and frame theory is a special case of Relativity Mechanics.

---

### 4.5 Hamiltonian constraints and observables

In ADM variables, spacetime is foliated by spatial hypersurfaces \(\Sigma_t\). The canonical variables are the spatial metric \(h_{ij}\) and its momentum \(\pi^{ij}\).

The theory has first-class constraints:

\[
\mathcal H_\perp\approx0,
\]

\[
\mathcal H_i\approx0.
\]

The scalar constraint \(\mathcal H_\perp\) generates normal deformations, while the vector constraints \(\mathcal H_i\) generate spatial diffeomorphisms. Together they generate hypersurface-deformation admissibility transformations.

The physical phase space is

\[
\Gamma_{\rm phys}=
\{\mathcal H_\perp=0,\ \mathcal H_i=0\}/\operatorname{Diff}.
\]

Local field values such as

\[
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
\]

are not diffeomorphism-invariant observables. Physical observables must be relational, for example:

\[
\mathcal O_{A,B}(\tau)=A\big|_{B=\tau},
\]

where \(B\) is a scalar field used as a physical reference.

Thus general relativity is the relativity schema

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{\rm GR}=
(\Omega_{\rm GR},\operatorname{Diff}(M)\ltimes SO(1,3),\triangleright,I_{\rm GR}).
}
\]

## 5. Quantum Physics and Quantum Mechanics as Projective-Hilbert Relativity Mechanics

Quantum theory is naturally relational. A quantum state is not a set of basis-dependent amplitudes, nor is it a vector with absolute phase. It is an equivalence class of descriptions.

### 5.1 Quantum descriptions as basis-dependent presentations

Let \(\mathcal H\) be a Hilbert space. A quantum description relative to an orthonormal basis \(B=\{|e_i\rangle\}\) is a set of components

\[
\psi_i=\langle e_i|\psi\rangle.
\]

The pair

\[
\omega=(B,\{\psi_i\})
\]

is a presented quantum state.

A change of basis \(U\in U(\mathcal H)\) acts by

\[
|e_i\rangle\mapsto U|e_i\rangle,
\]

\[
\psi_i\mapsto \psi'_i=\langle e_i|U^\dagger|\psi\rangle.
\]

The abstract vector

\[
|\psi\rangle=\sum_i\psi_i|e_i\rangle
\]

is invariant under this passive basis transformation.

Therefore basis dependence is descriptive redundancy.

---

### 5.2 Phase admissibility and projective Hilbert space

Quantum states are further invariant under global phase:

\[
|\psi\rangle\mapsto e^{i\theta}|\psi\rangle.
\]

The admissibility group includes

\[
G_{\rm phase}=U(1).
\]

The pure-state description space is

\[
\Omega_{\rm pure}=\mathcal H\setminus\{0\}.
\]

The quotient by nonzero complex scalars is projective Hilbert space:

\[
\mathbb P(\mathcal H)
=
(\mathcal H\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb C^*.
\]

Equivalently, after normalization,

\[
\mathbb P(\mathcal H)
=
S(\mathcal H)/U(1).
\]

The invariant projection is

\[
I_{\rm QM}:\mathcal H\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb P(\mathcal H),
\]

\[
|\psi\rangle\mapsto[\psi].
\]

The physical pure state is the ray

\[
[\psi].
\]

---

### 5.3 Fubini–Study invariant geometry

The projective space \(\mathbb P(\mathcal H)\) carries the Fubini–Study metric:

\[
ds^2_{\rm FS}
=
\frac{
\langle d\psi|d\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\psi\rangle
-
|\langle\psi|d\psi\rangle|^2
}{
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle^2
}.
\]

This metric is invariant under phase transformations and unitary basis changes. It is therefore a natural geometric structure on the invariant description space of quantum mechanics.

---

### 5.4 Observables and Born probabilities

Let \(A\) be a self-adjoint operator. The expectation value

\[
\langle A\rangle_\psi
=
\frac{\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}
\]

is invariant under phase:

\[
\langle A\rangle_{e^{i\theta}\psi}=\langle A\rangle_\psi.
\]

Under a basis change \(U\),

\[
A\mapsto UAU^\dagger,
\]

\[
|\psi\rangle\mapsto U|\psi\rangle,
\]

and

\[
\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle
\mapsto
\langle\psi|U^\dagger UAU^\dagger U|\psi\rangle
=
\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle.
\]

For a spectral projection \(P_a\) of \(A\), the Born probability

\[
p(a)=\langle\psi|P_a|\psi\rangle
\]

is an invariant of the quantum admissibility structure.

For mixed states, the description space is the space of density operators \(\rho\). Under basis changes,

\[
\rho\mapsto U\rho U^\dagger.
\]

The invariant content includes:

1. eigenvalues of \(\rho\),
2. transition probabilities,
3. expectation values \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A)\),
4. entropies.

---

### 5.5 Schrödinger dynamics as quotient dynamics

The Schrödinger equation is

\[
i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle
=
\hat H|\psi(t)\rangle.
\]

If \(|\psi(t)\rangle\) is a solution, then

\[
e^{i\theta}|\psi(t)\rangle
\]

is also a solution. Therefore the dynamics commutes with the phase admissibility group.

It descends to projective Hilbert space:

\[
\frac{d[\psi]}{dt}=\bar X_{\rm QM}([\psi]).
\]

The projected flow is Hamiltonian with respect to the Fubini–Study symplectic form, with Hamiltonian function

\[
h([\psi])=\frac{\langle\psi|\hat H|\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}.
\]

Thus quantum mechanics is a quotient dynamics.

---

### 5.6 Symmetries and projective representations

A physical symmetry \(g\) acts on quantum states by a unitary or antiunitary transformation. For continuous symmetries connected to the identity, one has unitary operators \(U(g)\).

Because physical states are rays, the representation is projective:

\[
U(g)U(h)=e^{i\alpha(g,h)}U(gh).
\]

For the Galilei group, this central extension yields mass as a superselection charge. For the Poincaré group, it yields relativistic particle classifications.

If

\[
[\hat H,\hat Q]=0,
\]

then \(\hat Q\) is a conserved quantum Noether charge.

Thus quantum conservation laws arise from admissibility and symmetry in the same structural manner as classical conservation laws.

### 5.7 Algebraic quantum physics

More generally, quantum physics may be formulated using a \(C^*\)-algebra \(\mathcal A\). States are positive normalized linear functionals

\[
\rho:\mathcal A\to\mathbb C.
\]

An admissibility group \(G\) acts by automorphisms:

\[
\alpha_g:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A.
\]

Invariant observables satisfy

\[
\alpha_g(A)=A.
\]

The physical observable algebra is

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm phys}=\mathcal A^G.
\]

This algebraic formulation is especially important for quantum field theory, superselection sectors, and gauge theories.

Quantum mechanics therefore emerges as the relativity schema

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{\rm QM}=
(\Omega_{\rm QM},G_{\rm phase}\times G_{\rm basis},\triangleright,I_{\rm QM}).
}
\]

More invariantly,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{\rm QM}=
(\mathcal H\setminus\{0\},\mathbb C^*,\triangleright,\mathbb P(\mathcal H)).
}
\]

## 6. Quantum Field Theory as Gauge- and Spacetime-Relational Relativity Mechanics

Quantum field theory extends the invariant-description principle to fields, gauge redundancy, and spacetime symmetry.

### 6.1 Field descriptions

Let \(M\) be spacetime and let \(E\to M\) be a field bundle. A classical field configuration is a section

\[
\phi\in\Gamma(E).
\]

The field description space is

\[
\Omega_{\rm field}=\Gamma(E).
\]

For scalar fields,

\[
\Omega=C^\infty(M).
\]

For spinor fields,

\[
\Omega=\Gamma(S).
\]

For gauge fields,

\[
\Omega=\mathcal A,
\]

where \(\mathcal A\) is the affine space of connections on a principal bundle \(P\to M\).

---

### 6.2 Poincaré relativity

In relativistic quantum field theory on Minkowski spacetime, the admissibility group of inertial frames is the Poincaré group

\[
\mathcal P=ISO(1,3).
\]

An element \((\Lambda,a)\) acts on scalar fields by

\[
((\Lambda,a)\triangleright\phi)(x)
=
\phi(\Lambda^{-1}(x-a)).
\]

For fields with spin,

\[
((\Lambda,a)\triangleright\Phi)_\alpha(x)
=
D_\alpha{}^\beta(\Lambda)
\Phi_\beta(\Lambda^{-1}(x-a)),
\]

where \(D(\Lambda)\) is a finite-dimensional representation of the Lorentz group.

A relativistic field theory is defined by an action invariant under this action:

\[
S[(\Lambda,a)\triangleright\phi]=S[\phi].
\]

The corresponding quantum theory carries a unitary representation

\[
U:\mathcal P\to U(\mathcal H).
\]

The invariance of the \(S\)-matrix is

\[
U(g)SU(g)^{-1}=S.
\]

The Poincaré generators are

\[
P^\mu,\qquad M^{\mu\nu}.
\]

Their Casimir invariants are

\[
P_\mu P^\mu=-m^2,
\]

\[
W_\mu W^\mu=-m^2 s(s+1),
\]

where

\[
W^\mu=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}P_\nu M_{\rho\sigma}.
\]

Thus particle species are invariant labels of irreducible representations of spacetime admissibility symmetries.

---

### 6.3 Gauge theories as quotient theories

Let \(G_{\rm gauge}\) be a compact Lie group and \(P\to M\) a principal \(G_{\rm gauge}\)-bundle. The space of gauge connections is

\[
\Omega_{\rm YM}=\mathcal A.
\]

A connection is locally written as

\[
A=A_\mu^a T_a dx^\mu.
\]

The gauge group

\[
\mathcal G=\operatorname{Aut}(P)
\]

acts by

\[
A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu^u
=
uA_\mu u^{-1}
-
(\partial_\mu u)u^{-1},
\]

where \(u:M\to G_{\rm gauge}\).

The curvature is

\[
F_{\mu\nu}
=
\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu+[A_\mu,A_\nu].
\]

It transforms covariantly:

\[
F_{\mu\nu}\mapsto uF_{\mu\nu}u^{-1}.
\]

The Yang–Mills action is

\[
S_{\rm YM}[A]
=
-\frac{1}{2g^2}
\int_M \operatorname{tr}(F\wedge *F).
\]

It is invariant under gauge transformations:

\[
S_{\rm YM}[A^u]=S_{\rm YM}[A].
\]

The physical configuration space is the gauge orbit space

\[
\mathcal A/\mathcal G.
\]

The invariant projection is

\[
I_{\rm gauge}:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A/\mathcal G.
\]

Thus gauge theory is a direct realization of Relativity Mechanics.

---

### 6.4 Gauge-invariant observables

Local gauge-variant quantities such as

\[
A_\mu^a(x)
\]

are not physical observables. Physical observables must be gauge-invariant. Examples include:

1. local invariant polynomials:

\[
\operatorname{tr}(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}),
\]

2. Wilson loops:

\[
W_R(C)=\operatorname{Tr}_R
\mathcal P\exp\left(\oint_C A\right),
\]

3. dressed matter operators,
4. scattering amplitudes between gauge-invariant asymptotic states.

The physical observable algebra is

\[
\mathcal O_{\rm phys}=C^\infty(\mathcal A)^{\mathcal G}.
\]

In the quantum theory, it becomes the gauge-invariant operator algebra.

---

### 6.5 Constraints and Gauss law

In canonical gauge theory, the generators of gauge transformations are Gauss constraints:

\[
\mathcal G^a(x)=D_i E^{ia}(x)-\rho^a(x)\approx0.
\]

Physical states satisfy

\[
\hat{\mathcal G}^a(x)|\Psi_{\rm phys}\rangle=0.
\]

This is the quantum implementation of admissibility.

The physical Hilbert space is

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
=
\{
|\Psi\rangle\in\mathcal H
\mid
\hat{\mathcal G}^a(x)|\Psi\rangle=0
\}.
\]

Equivalently, one may use BRST quantization. Introduce ghosts \(c\), antighosts \(\bar c\), and Nakanishi–Lautrup fields \(B\). The BRST transformations are

\[
sA=Dc,
\]

\[
sc=-\frac12[c,c],
\]

\[
s\bar c=B,
\]

\[
sB=0.
\]

The BRST operator satisfies

\[
s^2=0.
\]

Physical operators are BRST-closed modulo BRST-exact operators:

\[
\mathcal O_{\rm phys}
=
\frac{\ker Q_{\rm BRST}}{\operatorname{im}Q_{\rm BRST}}.
\]

This is precisely an invariant-equivalence construction.

---

### 6.6 Path integral over equivalence classes

The gauge-theoretic path integral is formally

\[
Z=\int_{\mathcal A/\mathcal G}\mathcal D A\,
e^{iS_{\rm YM}[A]}.
\]

In practice one fixes a gauge, inserting a Faddeev–Popov determinant:

\[
Z=
\int\mathcal D A\,\mathcal D c\,\mathcal D\bar c\,
e^{iS_{\rm YM}+iS_{\rm gf}+iS_{\rm ghost}}.
\]

Gauge fixing is a choice of representative in each admissibility orbit. Physical results must be independent of that choice.

Anomalies are obstructions to maintaining admissibility invariance after quantization. A gauge anomaly indicates that the quantum measure fails to descend to the quotient. Anomaly cancellation is therefore a consistency condition for the quantum quotient.

---

### 6.7 Standard Model as Relativity Mechanics

The Standard Model is obtained by choosing

\[
G_{\rm gauge}=SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1).
\]

The description space contains:

1. gauge connections for each factor,
2. fermion sections in appropriate representations,
3. Higgs fields,
4. Yukawa and gauge interactions.

The physical content is the quotient by the gauge group and by spacetime frame transformations. Gauge bosons, fermions, and Higgs particles are interpreted as invariant or covariant structures in the quotient theory.

Thus quantum field theory is the schema

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{\rm QFT}=
(\Omega_{\rm field},
\mathcal G_{\rm gauge}\rtimes G_{\rm spacetime},
\triangleright,
I_{\rm QFT}).
}
\]

## 7. String Theory as Worldsheet-Diffeomorphism and Weyl Relativity Mechanics

String theory provides a particularly deep realization of Relativity Mechanics. The fundamental object is not a particle trajectory but a two-dimensional embedding, and the primary admissibility symmetries are worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations.

### 7.1 String description space

Let \(\Sigma\) be a two-dimensional worldsheet and \(M\) a target spacetime. A bosonic string description consists of:

1. an embedding

\[
X:\Sigma\to M,
\]

2. a worldsheet metric \(h_{ab}\),

3. possible worldsheet matter, ghosts, and background fields.

The description space is

\[
\Omega_{\rm str}
=
\{(\Sigma,h_{ab},X^\mu,\text{matter})\}.
\]

In local coordinates \(\sigma^a\), \(a=1,2\), the embedding is written

\[
X^\mu(\sigma).
\]

---

### 7.2 Polyakov action

The Polyakov action is

\[
S_P[X,h]
=
\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}
\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,
\sqrt{h}\,h^{ab}
\partial_a X^\mu\partial_b X^\nu
G_{\mu\nu}(X),
\]

where \(G_{\mu\nu}\) is the target-space metric.

Additional background couplings include the Kalb–Ramond field \(B_{\mu\nu}\) and the dilaton \(\Phi\):

\[
S_B=
\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}
\int_\Sigma X^*B,
\]

\[
S_\Phi=
\frac{1}{4\pi}
\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,
\sqrt{h}\,R^{(2)}\Phi(X).
\]

The full sigma-model action is

\[
S_{\sigma}=S_P+S_B+S_\Phi.
\]

---

### 7.3 Worldsheet admissibility group

The primary admissibility group is

\[
G_{\rm ws}
=
\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)\ltimes \operatorname{Weyl}(\Sigma).
\]

A worldsheet diffeomorphism \(f:\Sigma\to\Sigma\) acts by

\[
X\mapsto X\circ f^{-1},
\]

\[
h_{ab}\mapsto f_*h_{ab}.
\]

A Weyl transformation acts by

\[
h_{ab}\mapsto e^{2\omega(\sigma)}h_{ab},
\]

while

\[
X^\mu\mapsto X^\mu.
\]

The Polyakov action is classically invariant under both.

The invariant projection is

\[
I_{\rm str}:
\Omega_{\rm str}
\to
\Omega_{\rm str}/
\bigl(\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)\ltimes\operatorname{Weyl}(\Sigma)\bigr).
\]

The physical worldsheet geometry is not a particular metric \(h_{ab}\), but a conformal equivalence class

\[
[h_{ab}].
\]

The quotient of metrics by diffeomorphisms connected to the identity and Weyl transformations gives Teichmüller space. Including large diffeomorphisms gives moduli space \(\mathcal M_g\) of genus-\(g\) Riemann surfaces:

\[
\mathcal M_g=
\frac{\operatorname{Met}(\Sigma_g)}
{\operatorname{Diff}_0(\Sigma_g)\ltimes\operatorname{Weyl}(\Sigma_g)}.
\]

---

### 7.4 String amplitudes as quotient integrals

The string path integral is formally

\[
Z=
\sum_{g=0}^\infty
\int_{\Omega_{\rm str}/G_{\rm ws}}
\mathcal D X\,\mathcal D h\,
e^{-S_\sigma[X,h]}.
\]

After gauge fixing, this becomes an integral over moduli space:

\[
Z=
\sum_{g=0}^\infty
\int_{\mathcal M_g}
[d m]\,
\left\langle
\prod_i V_i
\right\rangle_g.
\]

The insertion points of vertex operators are also quotiented by worldsheet diffeomorphisms. Thus string scattering amplitudes are invariant equivalence-class integrals.

---

### 7.5 BRST cohomology and physical string states

Quantization of the worldsheet admissibility symmetries leads to BRST symmetry. The physical state condition is

\[
Q_{\rm BRST}|\psi_{\rm phys}\rangle=0,
\]

with equivalence

\[
|\psi_{\rm phys}\rangle
\sim
|\psi_{\rm phys}\rangle
+
Q_{\rm BRST}|\Lambda\rangle.
\]

Thus physical string states are elements of BRST cohomology:

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
=
H^\bullet(Q_{\rm BRST}).
\]

Vertex operators representing physical states must be BRST-closed and not BRST-exact:

\[
Q_{\rm BRST}V=0,
\]

\[
V\not\sim V+Q_{\rm BRST}\Lambda.
\]

For example, the closed-string graviton vertex operator in flat target space is

\[
V_{\rm grav}
=
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}
\partial X^\mu
\bar\partial X^\nu
e^{ik\cdot X},
\]

with conditions

\[
k^2=0,
\]

\[
k^\mu\epsilon_{\mu\nu}=0,
\]

and gauge equivalence

\[
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}
\sim
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}
+
k_\mu\xi_\nu
+
k_\nu\xi_\mu.
\]

The symmetric traceless part of \(\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\) corresponds to the graviton. Thus spacetime gauge invariance emerges from worldsheet admissibility symmetry.

---

### 7.6 Weyl invariance and emergence of target-space gravity

At the quantum level, Weyl invariance requires the vanishing of the beta functions of the worldsheet sigma model. For the bosonic string, to leading order in \(\alpha'\),

\[
\beta^G_{\mu\nu}
=
\alpha'
\left(
R_{\mu\nu}
-
\frac14 H_{\mu\rho\sigma}H_\nu{}^{\rho\sigma}
+
2\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\Phi
\right)
+
O(\alpha'^2)=0,
\]

\[
\beta^B_{\mu\nu}
=
\alpha'
\left(
-\frac12\nabla^\rho H_{\rho\mu\nu}
+
\nabla^\rho\Phi\,H_{\rho\mu\nu}
\right)
+
O(\alpha'^2)=0,
\]

\[
\beta^\Phi
=
\frac{D-26}{6}
+
\alpha'
\left(
4(\nabla\Phi)^2
-
4\nabla^2\Phi
-
R
+
\frac{1}{12}H^2
\right)
+
O(\alpha'^2)=0.
\]

For superstrings, the critical dimension becomes \(D=10\), and additional Ramond–Ramond fields appear.

These beta-function equations are the Euler–Lagrange equations of a target-space effective action. For the bosonic string,

\[
S_{\rm eff}
=
\frac{1}{2\kappa^2}
\int d^D X\sqrt{-G}\,
e^{-2\Phi}
\left(
R
+
4(\nabla\Phi)^2
-
\frac{1}{12}H_{\mu\nu\rho}H^{\mu\nu\rho}
\right).
\]

Thus target-space general relativity emerges as the consistency condition for worldsheet admissibility invariance.

In this sense, string theory does not escape Relativity Mechanics. It deepens it: spacetime gravitational dynamics arises from the requirement that the string description be independent of worldsheet coordinates and worldsheet scale.

---

### 7.7 Dualities as enlarged admissibility

String theory also contains dualities such as:

1. T-duality,
2. S-duality,
3. mirror symmetry,
4. U-duality.

From the viewpoint of Relativity Mechanics, dualities are further admissibility transformations. They identify apparently different target-space descriptions as representatives of the same physical equivalence class.

For example, T-duality identifies a string theory on a circle of radius \(R\) with one on radius

\[
R'=\frac{\alpha'}{R}.
\]

Thus the physical description space is enlarged by quotienting under duality groups:

\[
G_{\rm str}^{\rm full}
=
\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)\ltimes\operatorname{Weyl}(\Sigma)
\ltimes G_{\rm duality}.
\]

String theory is therefore the relativity schema

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{\rm string}=
(\Omega_{\rm str},
\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)\ltimes\operatorname{Weyl}(\Sigma)\ltimes G_{\rm duality},
\triangleright,
I_{\rm string}).
}
\]

## 8. Comparative Synthesis

The preceding sections show that the major theories of physics are not isolated constructions. They are different choices of description space, admissibility group, invariant projection, and quotient dynamics within the same formal architecture.

### 8.1 Table of emergences

| Theory | Description space \(\Omega\) | Admissibility group \(G\) | Quotient \(\Omega/G\) | Physical invariants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Newtonian mechanics | Particle trajectories in inertial frames | Galilei group | Galilean orbit space | Relative distances, accelerations, mass ratios, time intervals |
| Newton–Cartan gravity | Temporal metric, spatial metric, connection, matter | Structure-preserving diffeomorphisms | Geometrized Newtonian equivalence classes | Curvature, mass density, temporal intervals |
| General relativity | Metrics or tetrads plus matter | \(\operatorname{Diff}(M)\ltimes SO(1,3)_{\rm local}\) | Superspace / local-frame quotient | Diffeomorphism-invariant geometry and relational observables |
| Quantum mechanics | Hilbert-space vectors or basis-dependent components | \(U(1)\) phase, basis changes | Projective Hilbert space | Rays, expectation values, transition probabilities |
| Algebraic quantum theory | States and representations of operator algebras | Automorphism / gauge groups | Invariant state spaces | Spectra, probabilities, superselection sectors |
| Quantum field theory | Field configurations or connections | Gauge group, spacetime symmetry group | Gauge orbit space / representation space | Wilson loops, S-matrix, particle quantum numbers |
| String theory | Worldsheet embeddings, metrics, background fields | \(\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)\ltimes\operatorname{Weyl}(\Sigma)\), dualities | Moduli space / BRST cohomology | Conformal data, scattering amplitudes, target equations |

---

### 8.2 Structural universals

Despite their differences, all these theories share the same structural features.

#### 8.2.1 Description redundancy

Every theory contains auxiliary descriptive variables:

1. inertial frames in Newtonian mechanics,
2. coordinates in general relativity,
3. phases and bases in quantum mechanics,
4. gauge trivializations in quantum field theory,
5. worldsheet coordinates and metrics in string theory.

These variables are not physical.

#### 8.2.2 Admissibility group

Each theory has a group of transformations that preserve physical content:

1. Galilei group,
2. diffeomorphism group,
3. phase and basis transformations,
4. gauge group,
5. worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations.

These groups define equivalence relations.

#### 8.2.3 Invariant projection

Each theory possesses a map

\[
I:\Omega\to\Omega/G
\]

that forgets descriptive redundancy.

#### 8.2.4 Quotient dynamics

The true dynamics is not on arbitrary representatives but on equivalence classes:

\[
\frac{d[\omega]}{dt}=\bar X([\omega]).
\]

This condition ensures that physical evolution is independent of descriptive choices.

#### 8.2.5 Noether and constraint structures

Continuous admissibility transformations generate conserved quantities or constraints:

\[
J_\xi=\text{constant},
\]

or

\[
\phi_\xi\approx0.
\]

Global symmetries give conservation laws; local symmetries give constraints.

#### 8.2.6 Observables as invariants

Physical observables are invariant under admissibility transformations:

\[
\mathcal O_{\rm phys}=C^\infty(\Omega)^G,
\]

or, in quantum theory,

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm phys}=\mathcal A^G,
\]

or BRST cohomology.

---

### 8.3 Hierarchy of relativity

Relativity Mechanics also reveals a hierarchy of increasingly relational descriptions.

1. **Absolute description**: \(G=\{e\}\).  
   Every coordinate, frame, phase, and gauge choice is treated as physical.

2. **Galilean relativity**: quotient by inertial frame transformations.  
   Absolute uniform motion is removed.

3. **Special relativity**: quotient by Poincaré transformations.  
   Absolute simultaneity is removed.

4. **General relativity**: quotient by diffeomorphisms.  
   Absolute coordinate location is removed.

5. **Gauge theory**: quotient by local internal transformations.  
   Absolute internal orientation is removed.

6. **Quantum theory**: quotient by phase and basis presentation.  
   Absolute amplitude representation is removed.

7. **String theory**: quotient by worldsheet diffeomorphisms, Weyl transformations, and dualities.  
   Absolute worldsheet geometry and some target-space backgrounds are removed.

The direction of progress in fundamental physics is the progressive elimination of non-invariant descriptive structure.

## 9. Discussion

Relativity Mechanics is not a replacement for specific dynamical laws. It does not by itself determine whether the universe is Newtonian, relativistic, quantum, or stringy. Rather, it provides the universal architectural principle by which such laws become physically meaningful.

A physical theory is specified once one chooses:

1. a description space \(\Omega\),
2. an admissibility group \(G\),
3. an action \(\triangleright:G\times\Omega\to\Omega\),
4. invariant observables \(C^\infty(\Omega)^G\) or their quantum analogues,
5. a \(G\)-equivariant dynamics descending to \(\Omega/G\).

The same abstract schema yields:

\[
\mathcal R_{\rm Newton},
\quad
\mathcal R_{\rm GR},
\quad
\mathcal R_{\rm QM},
\quad
\mathcal R_{\rm QFT},
\quad
\mathcal R_{\rm string}.
\]

This demonstrates that the deep unity of modern theoretical physics is not primarily ontological but relational. The unifying object is not a particle, field, metric, wavefunction, or string. The unifying object is the equivalence class of admissible descriptions.

In quantum gravity, this viewpoint becomes especially important. One expects that spacetime itself may not be fundamental. If so, the correct description space \(\Omega\) may not consist of manifolds and metrics at all. Nevertheless, Relativity Mechanics suggests that the correct theory will still be organized by an admissibility group and an invariant quotient. The physical content will remain the invariant equivalence class.

The framework also clarifies several conceptual issues:

1. **Gauge freedom** is descriptive redundancy, not physical indeterminacy.
2. **Coordinates** are presentations, not observables.
3. **Quantum phases and bases** are representational, not absolute.
4. **Diffeomorphism invariance** is the statement that manifold points have no physical identity independent of relational structure.
5. **String dualities** are further admissibility identifications between apparently distinct backgrounds.
6. **Anomalies** are obstructions to the existence of a well-defined quantum quotient.

Thus Relativity Mechanics provides a systematic method for constructing and analyzing physical theories.

## 10. Conclusion

Relativity Mechanics proposes a universal foundational principle:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical reality is represented by invariant equivalence classes of admissible descriptions, not by individual descriptions themselves.}
}
\]

The formal schema

\[
\mathcal R=(\Omega,G,\triangleright,I)
\]

captures this principle mathematically. The description space \(\Omega\) contains all admissible presentations of a physical system. The admissibility group \(G\) identifies those presentations that differ only by descriptive redundancy. The invariant projection

\[
I:\Omega\to\Omega/G
\]

extracts the physical content. Observables are invariants. Dynamics must descend to the quotient. Conservation laws arise from the admissibility action. Quantization is the passage from commutative invariant algebras to noncommutative invariant algebras or BRST cohomologies.

Newtonian physics emerges when the admissibility group is the Galilei group. General relativity emerges when the admissibility group includes diffeomorphisms and local Lorentz transformations. Quantum mechanics emerges when phase and basis presentations are quotiented, yielding projective Hilbert space. Quantum field theory emerges when gauge transformations and spacetime symmetries define the admissibility structure. String theory emerges when worldsheet diffeomorphisms, Weyl transformations, and dualities define the admissibility equivalence.

Thus the major theories of physics are not disconnected edicts. They are realizations of one deeper architecture. Relativity Mechanics is therefore a candidate foundational framework for the systematic construction, comparison, and unification of physical theories.
