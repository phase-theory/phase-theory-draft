# Relativity 0.1 — Galilean Relativity  
## The First Historical Form of the Zeroth Principle

**White paper / academic preprint**

---

## Abstract

Galilean Relativity is the first great historical expression of Relativity 0.0: the principle that physical law must be formulated as invariant structure under transformations between frames. In its classical form, Galilean relativity states that the laws of mechanics are the same in all inertial frames moving uniformly relative to one another. Consider two inertial frames with relative velocity \(v\). The Galilean transformation is

\[
x'
=
x-vt,
\]

\[
t'
=
t.
\]

Velocities transform as

\[
u'
=
u-v,
\]

but acceleration is invariant:

\[
a'
=
a.
\]

Newton’s laws are invariant under Galilean transformations. Thus uniform motion is relative, but acceleration is not. This already contains the full structure of Relativity 0.0:

1. identify the frames: inertial frames;
2. identify the transformations: Galilean boosts, translations, and rotations;
3. identify the invariants: acceleration, mass, force laws, temporal intervals, simultaneous spatial distances, and the form of Newton’s equations.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is a special case of the zeroth principle.}
}
\]

This white paper reconstructs Galilean relativity as the first mature realization of relativistic method. It examines its historical origins, kinematics, dynamics, Lagrangian structure, conservation laws, spacetime geometry, limitations, and role as the low-velocity limit of special relativity. Galilean relativity is not merely a historical precursor to Einstein. It is the archetype of all later relativistic theories: the discovery that objectivity consists not in a privileged frame, but in invariant structure across frames.

## 1. Introduction

Before Einstein, there was Galileo.

Before Lorentz transformations, there were Galilean transformations.

Before spacetime geometry, there was the insight that uniform motion cannot be detected from within.

Galilean relativity begins with a simple but revolutionary idea:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Uniform motion is relative.}
}
\]

If a ship moves smoothly across the sea, the passengers below deck cannot determine, by internal mechanical experiments alone, whether the ship is at rest or moving uniformly.

Droplets fall vertically.

Fish swim normally.

Objects thrown upward return to the hand.

No mechanical experiment confined to the cabin can reveal the ship’s uniform velocity.

This is the birth of the relativistic frame.

The state of “being at rest” is not physically privileged over “moving uniformly.”

What is privileged, in Newtonian mechanics, is not rest but inertial motion.

Thus Galilean relativity establishes the first great relativistic distinction:

\[
\text{absolute rest is not observable;}
\]

\[
\text{uniform motion is frame-relative;}
\]

\[
\text{acceleration is invariant.}
\]

This is the first historical form of Relativity 0.0.

## 2. The Zeroth Principle in Galilean Form

Relativity 0.0 may be stated as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No frame is absolute; reality is the invariant under admissible transformations.}
}
\]

Galilean relativity instantiates this principle as follows.

### 2.1 Frames

The frames are inertial frames.

An inertial frame is a reference frame in which a free particle moves in a straight line at constant velocity.

### 2.2 Transformations

The transformations between inertial frames are Galilean transformations:

1. spatial translations,
2. time translations,
3. spatial rotations,
4. uniform boosts.

For two frames moving at relative velocity \(v\), the simplest transformation is:

\[
x'
=
x-vt,
\]

\[
t'
=
t.
\]

### 2.3 Invariants

The invariants include:

1. time intervals,
2. simultaneity,
3. spatial distances between simultaneous events,
4. acceleration,
5. mass,
6. force laws,
7. the form of Newton’s equations.

Thus Galilean relativity already contains the relativistic method:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Frame}
\rightarrow
\text{transformation}
\rightarrow
\text{invariant}.
}
\]

## 3. Historical Origins

### 3.1 Galileo’s Ship

Galileo’s famous ship argument appears in his *Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems*.

He asks us to imagine observers below deck on a smoothly moving ship.

If the ship moves uniformly, no internal experiment involving falling bodies, swimming fish, flying insects, or dripping water can reveal the ship’s motion.

The argument is not merely rhetorical.

It establishes an operational principle:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Uniform velocity has no internal mechanical signature.}
}
\]

This is the first clear statement of the relativity principle.

### 3.2 Newtonian Synthesis

Newton gave the principle mathematical form.

His laws of motion distinguish inertial motion from accelerated motion.

The first law states that a body remains at rest or in uniform rectilinear motion unless acted upon by a force.

Thus uniform motion is not dynamically special.

But acceleration is dynamically significant.

Acceleration requires force.

Thus Newtonian mechanics contains a subtle tension:

\[
\text{uniform motion is relative;}
\]

\[
\text{acceleration is absolute.}
\]

This tension becomes one of the driving problems of later physics.

## 4. Inertial Frames

An inertial frame is defined by the behavior of free particles.

In such a frame, a free particle satisfies:

\[
\frac{d^2 x}{dt^2}
=
0.
\]

Equivalently:

\[
x(t)
=
x_0
+
ut.
\]

The particle moves in a straight line at constant velocity.

If a frame is moving uniformly relative to an inertial frame, then it is also inertial.

If a frame accelerates or rotates relative to an inertial frame, then it is non-inertial.

Thus the class of inertial frames is closed under Galilean transformations.

## 5. The Galilean Transformation

Consider two inertial frames \(S\) and \(S'\).

Let \(S'\) move with velocity \(v\) relative to \(S\) along the \(x\)-axis.

The Galilean transformation is:

\[
x'
=
x-vt,
\]

\[
y'
=
y,
\]

\[
z'
=
z,
\]

\[
t'
=
t.
\]

The inverse transformation is:

\[
x
=
x'
+
vt',
\]

\[
t
=
t'.
\]

The essential feature is that time is unchanged:

\[
t'
=
t.
\]

Thus Galilean relativity assumes absolute time.

All observers agree on temporal order and duration.

They disagree only about positions and velocities.

---

## 6. Velocity Transformation

Differentiate the transformation:

\[
x'
=
x-vt.
\]

Then:

\[
\frac{dx'}{dt'}
=
\frac{dx}{dt}
-
v.
\]

Since \(t'=t\), we have:

\[
u'
=
u-v.
\]

This is the Galilean velocity-addition law.

If a particle moves with velocity \(u\) in frame \(S\), it moves with velocity \(u-v\) in frame \(S'\).

Thus velocity is relative.

There is no absolute velocity.

Only relative velocity between frames or objects has operational meaning.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Velocity is frame-relative.}
}
\]

---

## 7. Acceleration Invariance

Differentiate once more.

Since:

\[
u'
=
u-v,
\]

and \(v\) is constant, we obtain:

\[
a'
=
\frac{du'}{dt'}
=
\frac{du}{dt}
=
a.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
a'
=
a.
}
\]

Acceleration is invariant under Galilean boosts.

This is the crucial asymmetry of Galilean relativity.

Uniform motion is relative.

Acceleration is not.

If a frame accelerates, internal experiments can detect it.

Objects feel inertial forces.

Bodies are pressed against walls.

Pendulums deflect.

Springs stretch.

Thus acceleration has absolute status within Newtonian mechanics.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity relativizes velocity but not acceleration.}
}
\]

## 8. The Galilei Group

The full Galilei group includes:

1. time translations,
2. spatial translations,
3. spatial rotations,
4. boosts.

A general Galilean transformation may be written as:

\[
t'
=
t+s,
\]

\[
\mathbf{x}'
=
R\mathbf{x}
+
\mathbf{v}t
+
\mathbf{a},
\]

where:

- \(s\) is a time translation,
- \(\mathbf{a}\) is a spatial translation,
- \(R\in SO(3)\) is a rotation,
- \(\mathbf{v}\) is a boost velocity.

The transformation preserves:

1. temporal intervals,
2. simultaneity,
3. Euclidean spatial distances between simultaneous events.

The Galilei group is the symmetry group of Newtonian spacetime.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is invariance under the Galilei group.}
}
\]

## 9. Invariance of Newton’s First Law

Newton’s first law states that a free particle moves uniformly in a straight line.

In frame \(S\):

\[
\mathbf{x}(t)
=
\mathbf{x}_0
+
\mathbf{u}t.
\]

Under a Galilean boost:

\[
\mathbf{x}'(t)
=
\mathbf{x}(t)
-
\mathbf{v}t
=
\mathbf{x}_0
+
(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})t.
\]

This is still uniform rectilinear motion.

Thus if the first law holds in one inertial frame, it holds in every inertial frame.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The first law is Galilean-invariant.}
}
\]

## 10. Invariance of Newton’s Second Law

Newton’s second law is:

\[
m\mathbf{a}
=
\mathbf{F}.
\]

Under a Galilean boost:

\[
\mathbf{a}'
=
\mathbf{a}.
\]

If the force transforms as a spatial vector and does not depend on absolute velocity, then:

\[
\mathbf{F}'
=
\mathbf{F}.
\]

Therefore:

\[
m\mathbf{a}'
=
\mathbf{F}'.
\]

Thus the second law has the same form in all inertial frames.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The second law is Galilean-covariant.}
}
\]

This is the dynamical core of Galilean relativity.

## 11. Forces and Relative Quantities

Newtonian forces typically depend on relative positions and relative velocities, not on absolute velocity.

For two particles \(i\) and \(j\), the relative position is:

\[
\mathbf{r}_{ij}
=
\mathbf{x}_i
-
\mathbf{x}_j.
\]

Under a Galilean boost:

\[
\mathbf{x}_i'
=
\mathbf{x}_i
-
\mathbf{v}t,
\]

\[
\mathbf{x}_j'
=
\mathbf{x}_j
-
\mathbf{v}t.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\mathbf{r}_{ij}'
=
\mathbf{x}_i'
-
\mathbf{x}_j'
=
\mathbf{x}_i
-
\mathbf{x}_j
=
\mathbf{r}_{ij}.
\]

Relative positions are invariant under boosts.

Similarly, relative velocities are invariant:

\[
\mathbf{u}_{ij}'
=
\mathbf{u}_i'
-
\mathbf{u}_j'
=
(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{v})
-
(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{v})
=
\mathbf{u}_i
-
\mathbf{u}_j
=
\mathbf{u}_{ij}.
\]

Thus forces depending only on relative positions and relative velocities are automatically Galilean-invariant.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Newtonian dynamics depends on relational quantities.}
}
\]

## 12. Example: Newtonian Gravity

Newtonian gravitational force between masses \(m_i\) and \(m_j\) is:

\[
\mathbf{F}_{ij}
=
Gm_i m_j
\frac{
\mathbf{r}_j-\mathbf{r}_i
}{
|\mathbf{r}_j-\mathbf{r}_i|^3
}.
\]

Since relative position is invariant under Galilean boosts, the gravitational force is invariant.

Thus Newtonian gravity is Galilean-relativistic.

It does not select a preferred inertial frame.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Newtonian gravity respects Galilean relativity.}
}
\]

## 13. Lagrangian Formulation

The free-particle Lagrangian is:

\[
L
=
\frac{1}{2}
m|\mathbf{u}|^2.
\]

Under a Galilean boost:

\[
\mathbf{u}'
=
\mathbf{u}
-
\mathbf{v}.
\]

Thus:

\[
L'
=
\frac{1}{2}
m|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|^2.
\]

Expanding:

\[
L'
=
\frac{1}{2}
m|\mathbf{u}|^2
-
m\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}
+
\frac{1}{2}
m|\mathbf{v}|^2.
\]

The extra terms are a total time derivative:

\[
L'
=
L
+
\frac{d}{dt}
\left(
-
m\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}
+
\frac{1}{2}
m|\mathbf{v}|^2t
\right).
\]

The action changes only by a boundary term.

Therefore the equations of motion are unchanged.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The Lagrangian is invariant up to a total derivative.}
}
\]

This is the deeper variational expression of Galilean relativity.

## 14. Hamiltonian Transformation

The canonical momentum is:

\[
\mathbf{p}
=
m\mathbf{u}.
\]

Under a boost:

\[
\mathbf{p}'
=
m\mathbf{u}'
=
m(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})
=
\mathbf{p}
-
m\mathbf{v}.
\]

The kinetic energy transforms as:

\[
E'
=
\frac{|\mathbf{p}'|^2}{2m}
=
\frac{|\mathbf{p}-m\mathbf{v}|^2}{2m}.
\]

For a system of particles with total momentum \(\mathbf{P}\) and total mass \(M\), the total energy transforms as:

\[
E'
=
E
-
\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{P}
+
\frac{1}{2}
M|\mathbf{v}|^2.
\]

Thus energy is not invariant under boosts.

Momentum is not invariant under boosts.

But the form of the laws is invariant.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Energy and momentum are frame-dependent; dynamical law is frame-invariant.}
}
\]

## 16. What Is Relative and What Is Absolute?

Galilean relativity divides physical quantities into relative and absolute classes.

| Quantity | Status in Galilean Relativity |
|---|---|
| Position | Relative |
| Velocity | Relative |
| Acceleration | Absolute/invariant under boosts |
| Time interval | Absolute |
| Simultaneity | Absolute |
| Spatial distance at equal time | Absolute |
| Mass | Absolute |
| Force law | Invariant |
| Newton’s equations | Covariant |

Thus Galilean relativity is not universal relativization.

It is selective relativization.

It relativizes uniform motion.

It preserves acceleration, time, and simultaneity.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is the relativity of uniform motion, not of spacetime itself.}
}
\]

Time translation invariance gives conservation of energy:

\[
E

m|\mathbf{u}|^2
+

### 17.2 Spatial Translation Symmetry

Spatial translation invariance gives conservation of momentum:

\[
\mathbf{p}

\[
\mathbf{L}
=

For a free particle, the boost generator is:

\[
\mathbf{G}

-

This quantity is conserved.

Thus Galilean symmetry contains not only energy, momentum, and angular momentum, but also center-of-mass motion.

\boxed{

## 18. Galilean Spacetime Structure

Galilean relativity does not possess a spacetime metric like special relativity.

It possesses a split structure:

1. absolute time,
2. Euclidean space at each instant.

Temporal intervals are measured by:

\[
dt.
\]

Spatial distances on simultaneity slices are measured by:

\[
d\ell^2
=
dx^2
+
dy^2
+
dz^2.
\]

But there is no invariant spacetime interval combining space and time.

This is the key difference from Minkowski spacetime.

In special relativity, the invariant interval is:

\[
ds^2
=
-c^2dt^2
+
dx^2
+
dy^2
+
dz^2.
\]

In Galilean relativity, there is no such invariant.

There is only absolute time and relative space.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean spacetime is foliated by absolute simultaneity slices.}
}
\]

## 19. Non-Inertial Frames and Pseudo-Forces

Galilean relativity applies only to inertial frames.

If a frame accelerates or rotates, Newton’s laws do not retain their simple form unless pseudo-forces are introduced.

For an accelerating frame with acceleration \(\mathbf{A}\), one must add:

\[
\mathbf{F}_{\text{pseudo}}
=
-
m\mathbf{A}.
\]

For rotating frames, one must add:

1. centrifugal force,
2. Coriolis force,
3. Euler force.

These forces are not caused by material interactions.

They arise from the acceleration of the frame.

Thus non-inertial frames reveal the absolute status of acceleration in Newtonian mechanics.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Acceleration is detectable because it breaks inertial-frame equivalence.}
}
\]

## 20. Newton’s Bucket and Absolute Acceleration

Newton’s bucket argument attempted to show that acceleration is absolute.

A rotating bucket of water develops a concave surface.

The concavity cannot be explained by motion relative to the bucket alone.

It seems to require rotation relative to absolute space.

Galilean relativity handles boosts, but rotation and acceleration introduce dynamical effects.

This problem later influenced Mach and Einstein.

Einstein’s general relativity partially Machianizes the problem by making geometry dynamical.

But Galilean relativity itself remains clear:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Uniform motion is relative; accelerated motion is dynamically absolute.}
}
\]

## 21. Galilean Relativity and Electromagnetism

Galilean relativity works beautifully for Newtonian mechanics.

It fails for Maxwellian electromagnetism.

Maxwell’s equations predict electromagnetic waves traveling at speed:

\[
c
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}.
\]

Under Galilean velocity addition, light speed should transform as:

\[
c'
=
c-v.
\]

But experiments such as Michelson–Morley failed to detect motion relative to an ether.

The laws of electromagnetism did not fit Galilean symmetry.

The resolution was Lorentzian symmetry.

Thus Galilean relativity became the low-velocity limit of special relativity.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is exact for Newtonian mechanics but approximate for light.}
}
\]

## 22. Galilean Relativity as the Low-Velocity Limit of Special Relativity

The Lorentz transformation replaces the Galilean transformation.

For relative velocity \(v\):

\[
t'
=
\gamma
\left(
t-\frac{vx}{c^2}
\right),
\]

\[
x'
=
\gamma
\left(
x-vt
\right),
\]

where

\[
\gamma
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.
\]

For \(v\ll c\),

\[
\gamma
\approx
1,
\]

and

\[
\frac{vx}{c^2}
\approx
0.
\]

Thus:

\[
t'
\approx
t,
\]

\[
x'
\approx
x-vt.
\]

This recovers the Galilean transformation.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is the } c\to\infty \text{ limit of special relativity.}
}
\]

## 24. Philosophical Significance

Galilean relativity changed the meaning of motion.

Motion ceased to be an intrinsic state.

It became a relation between frames.

This had profound philosophical consequences.

It challenged Aristotelian natural places.

It undermined the idea that Earth must be absolutely at rest.

It prepared the way for Newtonian universal mechanics.

It also created a problem: if uniform motion is relative, what makes acceleration absolute?

This problem led through Mach to Einstein.

Thus Galilean relativity is not merely a technical result.

It is a transformation in the concept of physical reality.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is the birth of frame-thinking.}
}

## 25. Modern Mathematical Formulation

In modern terms, Galilean relativity is the invariance of Newtonian mechanics under the Galilei group.

Let \(G\) be the Galilei group.

Let \(q(t)\) be a trajectory.

A transformation \(g\in G\) maps the trajectory to:

\[
q(t)
\mapsto
gq(t).
\]

Newton’s equations are invariant if:

\[
\mathcal{E}[q]
=
0
\quad
\Longleftrightarrow
\quad
\mathcal{E}[gq]
=
0,
\]

where \(\mathcal{E}[q]=0\) denotes the Euler–Lagrange equations.

Equivalently, the action satisfies:

\[
S[gq]
=
S[q]
+
\text{boundary term}.
\]

Thus the dynamics is Galilean-covariant.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is symmetry of the equations of motion.}
}
\]

## 27. Newton–Cartan Geometry

General relativity geometrizes gravity using curved Lorentzian spacetime.

Newtonian gravity can also be geometrized using Newton–Cartan geometry.

In Newton–Cartan theory, spacetime has:

1. a temporal one-form \(\tau_\mu\),
2. a spatial metric \(h^{\mu\nu}\),
3. a compatible connection \(\nabla\).

These satisfy:

\[
\nabla_\mu\tau_\nu
=
0,
\]

\[
\nabla_\mu h^{\nu\rho}
=
0.
\]

Gravity appears as curvature of a Newtonian spacetime connection.

This shows that Galilean spacetime can be geometrized, just as Lorentzian spacetime is geometrized in general relativity.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Even Newtonian spacetime has geometric structure.}
}

## 28. Limitations of Galilean Relativity

Galilean relativity is powerful but limited.

Its limitations include:

1. It assumes absolute time.
2. It assumes absolute simultaneity.
3. It fails for electromagnetic waves.
4. It fails at velocities comparable to \(c\).
5. It cannot explain the invariant speed of light.
6. It cannot account for relativistic energy-momentum.
7. It cannot describe mass-energy equivalence.
8. It cannot describe gravitational time dilation.
9. It cannot describe spacetime curvature.
10. It cannot describe high-energy particle physics.

These limitations led to special and general relativity.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Galilean relativity is foundational but not final.}
}
\]

Despite its limitations, Galilean relativity remains foundational.

It teaches that some quantities are relative, while others are invariant.

It teaches that physical law must be formulated in terms of invariants.

It teaches that frames are part of the description.

Thus:

\boxed{

## 30. Axioms of Galilean Relativity

The framework may be organized around ten axioms.

### Axiom 1: Time Is Absolute

All inertial observers agree on time intervals.

### Axiom 2: Simultaneity Is Absolute

Events simultaneous in one inertial frame are simultaneous in all inertial frames.

### Axiom 3: Space Is Euclidean

Each simultaneity slice has Euclidean geometry.

### Axiom 4: Inertial Frames Exist

There exist frames in which free particles move uniformly.

### Axiom 5: Uniform Motion Is Relative

No inertial experiment detects absolute uniform velocity.

### Axiom 6: Acceleration Is Invariant

Acceleration is the same in all inertial frames.

### Axiom 7: Forces Depend on Relative Quantities

Fundamental Newtonian forces depend on relative positions and velocities.

### Axiom 8: Newton’s Laws Are Covariant

The form of Newton’s laws is preserved under Galilean transformations.

### Axiom 9: Mass Is Invariant

Mass does not change under boosts.

### Axiom 10: Galilean Symmetry Generates Conservation Laws

Time, space, rotation, and boost symmetries yield conservation laws.

## 32. What Einstein Would Think

Einstein would regard Galilean relativity as the necessary starting point.

Special relativity does not discard Galilean relativity.

It corrects and extends it.

Einstein would recognize that Galilean relativity discovered the key idea:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The laws of physics must not depend on arbitrary uniform motion.}
}
\]

But he would also insist that the Galilean structure is incomplete.

It must be replaced by Lorentz invariance when light is included.

Thus Galilean relativity is both foundational and provisional.

It is the first historical form of the relativistic method.

## 33. Conclusion

Galilean Relativity is the first great historical expression of Relativity 0.0.

It establishes the relativistic method:

1. identify frames,
2. identify transformations,
3. identify invariants.

In Galilean relativity:

- frames are inertial frames,
- transformations are Galilean boosts, translations, and rotations,
- velocities are relative,
- acceleration is invariant,
- Newton’s laws are covariant.

The central equations are:

\[
x'
=
x-vt,
\]

\[
t'
=
t,
\]

\[
u'
=
u-v,
\]

\[
a'
=
a.
\]

The central conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Uniform motion is relative; acceleration is invariant; law is objectivity under transformation.}
}
\]

Galilean relativity is not merely a historical curiosity.

It is the first explicit discovery that physical objectivity is not a privileged frame but invariant structure across frames.

This is why it remains the first historical form of the zeroth principle.

This is why it remains the root of all later relativity.

For a free particle:

## Appendix F: Low-Velocity Limit of Lorentz Transformations

Lorentz transformations are:

\[

=
\gamma
\left(
