## Abstract

The superconducting transmon qubit closes the open question left explicitly unresolved by the NMR/ESR framet (candidate #3, Appendix D): whether a bench-realizable system can demonstrate a genuinely **non-abelian** holonomy, rather than the abelian \(U(1)\) Berry phase available to the trapped-ion platform (candidate #2). This paper develops the transmon as an analogous framet in which the microwave in-phase/quadrature (IQ) control frame plays the role of the local frame, the qubit's two-level (or, in multi-level control, qutrit/qudit) manifold carries an engineered \(SU(2)\) representation, and — via established holonomic and non-adiabatic geometric gate protocols — a genuine non-abelian connection can be synthesized and its holonomy measured directly. Because the transmon is fabricated on a silicon (or sapphire) substrate using lithographic techniques closely related to those surveyed for the silicon MEMS and silicon photonic candidates elsewhere in this programme, this paper also serves as the bridge between the purely representation-theoretic analogous tier (candidates #1–3) and the fabrication-focused silicon design space (candidates #14, #22–24). As with all analogous-tier candidates, we are explicit throughout that no coupling to the gravitational tetrad or spin connection is present or claimed.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Why the transmon is the natural fourth candidate

Candidates #1–3 established a progression: a fixed lattice frame with axial invariance (NV center), a controllable field-defined frame with axial invariance and abelian holonomy (trapped ion), and a frame-independent full-\(SU(2)\)-invariant state realized within a controllable-frame apparatus (NMR/ESR singlet). None of the three, however, could realize a **non-abelian** holonomy — the trapped-ion Berry phase of candidate #2, Appendix C, is generated by a scalar (abelian) geometric phase proportional to solid angle, because the relevant transported states were non-degenerate. The gravitational connection \(\omega^{ab}{}_\mu\) of *Frame Theory* is a genuinely non-abelian \(\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\)-valued connection, and no analogous-tier candidate so far has realized a laboratory holonomy of the correct algebraic type — only the correct type of object (a holonomy of *some* connection under transport around a closed loop).

The transmon, controlled through engineered degenerate or near-degenerate multi-level manifolds and driven by microwave pulse sequences implementing holonomic quantum gates, closes this gap. Holonomic quantum computation is an established technique in which quantum gates are implemented as the non-abelian geometric phase (holonomy) acquired by an adiabatically or non-adiabatically transported degenerate subspace, and it has been demonstrated experimentally on superconducting qubit platforms including transmons. This gives the transmon framet access to a structurally correct (non-abelian, matrix-valued) holonomy, even though — as with every other analogous candidate — the connection being transported is a synthetic, microwave-engineered one rather than the gravitational spin connection itself.

### 1.2 Scope statement

Consistent with candidates #1–3, this paper treats the transmon strictly as a representation-theoretic and connection-formalism demonstration platform. No claim of gravitational coupling, torsion sensitivity, or frame-transport measurement is made. Full discussion in Section 7.

## 2. Physical System

### 2.1 The IQ control frame

A transmon is a weakly anharmonic superconducting artificial atom, typically fabricated from a Josephson junction shunted by a large capacitor, addressed and controlled via microwave pulses characterized by an in-phase (I) and quadrature (Q) amplitude pair at the qubit's transition frequency. The IQ plane defines a control frame in a sense structurally analogous to the field-defined frame of the trapped ion (candidate #2): the phase of the drive relative to a fixed local-oscillator reference sets the axis, on the qubit's Bloch sphere, about which a given pulse effects rotation. Unlike the trapped-ion field direction, which is a genuine three-dimensional spatial vector, the IQ frame is more precisely described as setting the *azimuthal* control axis on the Bloch sphere, with the polar (population-transfer) axis set independently by pulse duration and amplitude — giving the transmon full, arbitrary \(SU(2)\) single-qubit control via combinations of IQ-phase and amplitude/duration choices.

### 2.2 Two-level and extended multi-level manifolds

The computational subspace of a transmon is formed from its two lowest energy eigenstates, \(|0\rangle,|1\rangle\), which carry the same spin-1/2-type \(SU(2)\) representation used throughout candidates #1–3 for two-level control (contrasted with the spin-1 or \(J=0/1\) manifolds used where three- or four-dimensional structure was needed). The transmon's inherent weak anharmonicity also gives access to a third level \(|2\rangle\), routinely used for leakage characterization and, as developed in Section 4, for engineering the degenerate or near-degenerate subspaces required for non-abelian holonomic control.

### 2.3 Fabrication on silicon

Transmons are commonly fabricated on high-resistivity silicon or sapphire substrates using electron-beam lithography and shadow evaporation of aluminum (or, in some designs, niobium or tantalum) to form the Josephson junction and capacitor pads. The silicon substrate itself is not the active quantum element — unlike the silicon spin-qubit platforms considered elsewhere in this programme — but its low dielectric loss and compatibility with standard cleanroom microfabrication make it, alongside sapphire, one of the two dominant substrate choices in the field. This gives the transmon framet a direct fabrication-lineage connection to the silicon MEMS and silicon photonic candidates surveyed as genuine-tier frame-transport devices (#14–15), even though the transmon itself belongs to the analogous tier.

## 3. Correspondence to the Framet Formalism

### 3.1 The IQ frame as a synthetic local frame

Following the dictionaries built for candidates #1–3, the transmon's admissibility group is realized as the group of achievable single-qubit rotations generated by IQ-phase and amplitude control,

\[
G_{\mathrm{transmon}} = SU(2),
\]

realized synthetically through microwave engineering rather than through a crystallographic axis (candidate #1) or an applied static field (candidates #2–3). This is a further step in the "how is the frame realized" progression across the analogous tier: physical crystal axis, physical applied field, and now a purely control-theoretic, pulse-sequence-defined frame with no persistent physical directionality between pulses.

### 3.2 Invariant subspace under single-qubit control

For the ordinary two-level transmon, the invariant-subspace construction mirrors candidates #1–2 rather than candidate #3: a state invariant under rotation about a *fixed* control axis is axially invariant only, in the same sense as \(|m_s=0\rangle\) or \(|F,m_F=0\rangle\), since the bare two-level system does not carry a total-angular-momentum-zero sector analogous to the NMR/ESR singlet. Full-group invariance in the sense of candidate #3 requires either a multi-transmon entangled system (Section 3.3) or the engineered degenerate-subspace construction used for holonomic control (Section 4).

### 3.3 Multi-transmon singlet-like states

Two capacitively or resonator-coupled transmons can be prepared in maximally entangled Bell states, of which the singlet-like combination

\[
|\Psi^-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|01\rangle-|10\rangle\right)
\]

is, under the identification of \(|0\rangle,|1\rangle\) with spin-1/2 states, exactly the two-spin singlet of candidate #3, and inherits the same full-\(SU(2)\)-invariance property under *identical, simultaneous* rotation of both qubits' control frames:

\[
\left(U(g)\otimes U(g)\right)|\Psi^-\rangle = |\Psi^-\rangle,\qquad\forall g\in SU(2).
\]

This reproduces candidate #3's invariant-subspace result on a fully independent physical platform, using engineered rather than naturally-occurring spin coupling, and is a useful cross-platform consistency check in its own right — the same abstract invariance statement realized in NMR/ESR nuclear spins, in principle in ESR electron spins, and now in superconducting circuit qubits.

## 4. Non-Abelian Holonomy: Design and Protocol

### 4.1 Degenerate-subspace construction

Holonomic quantum computation requires a degenerate (or systematically near-degenerate, in the non-adiabatic variant used on fast superconducting timescales) subspace that can be adiabatically or rapidly transported around a closed loop in a control-parameter space, acquiring a matrix-valued (non-abelian, when the subspace dimension exceeds one) geometric phase independent of the transport rate, in contrast to the dynamical phase. In a transmon, this is engineered using a three-level \(\Lambda\)-type coupling scheme — typically involving the \(|0\rangle,|1\rangle\) computational states and the auxiliary \(|2\rangle\) level, driven by two microwave tones — in which a two-dimensional dark-state subspace remains degenerate throughout the control loop while an auxiliary bright state is transiently populated and returns to zero population at loop closure.

### 4.2 Protocol for holonomy measurement

1. Initialize the transmon in a known state within the engineered dark-state subspace.
2. Drive the two control tones through a closed loop in the relevant parameter space (relative phase and amplitude of the two tones), returning both to their initial values.
3. Perform full process tomography on the dark-state subspace to extract the acquired unitary — the holonomy matrix \(\mathcal H[\gamma]\in SU(2)\) (or a larger group, for a higher-dimensional engineered dark space).
4. Verify non-commutativity: perform two different loops \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\) and their composition in both orders, confirming \(\mathcal H[\gamma_1]\mathcal H[\gamma_2]\neq\mathcal H[\gamma_2]\mathcal H[\gamma_1]\) in general — the defining signature of a non-abelian holonomy, unavailable in principle to the abelian Berry-phase construction of candidate #2.

### 4.3 Structural significance relative to §7.3

Section 7.3 of *Frame Theory* defines the nonlocal invariant

\[
\mathcal H[\gamma] = \mathcal P\exp\left(\oint_\gamma\omega\right)
\]

for the \(\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\)-valued spin connection \(\omega\), explicitly noting its dependence on the frame bundle and connection rather than the metric alone. The holonomic transmon protocol of Section 4.2 produces an object of the *same algebraic type* — a path-ordered exponential of a non-abelian connection around a closed loop, valued in a genuine non-abelian Lie group — realized via engineered microwave control rather than gravitational parallel transport. This is, to our knowledge, the first candidate in this survey capable of demonstrating the non-commutative structure of §7.3's holonomy construction directly, rather than only its abelian special case (candidate #2) or its zero-curvature/trivial-holonomy special case (the invariant states of candidates #1 and #3).

## 6. What This Device Does Not Measure

Consistent with candidates #1–3:

- **No coupling to \(e^a{}_\mu\) or \(\omega^{ab}{}_\mu\).** The transmon's Hamiltonian is set by circuit quantum electrodynamics — Josephson junction nonlinearity, capacitive/inductive coupling, and applied microwave drives — with no term coupling to the gravitational tetrad or spin connection.
- **No torsion sensitivity.** There is no macroscopic spin-polarized source or spin-density current in a transmon circuit in the sense required by §5.3; the "spin" language used throughout this paper (Bloch sphere, \(SU(2)\)) is a mathematical identification with a two-level quantum system, not a physical electron or nuclear spin ensemble.
- **No frame-transport measurement.** The IQ control frame exists only during active microwave driving and has no relationship to inertial transport or gravitational parallel transport; the millikelvin operating environment and dilution-refrigerator mounting, if anything, actively isolate the device from the kind of inertial or gravitational signal a genuine-tier candidate (e.g. #4's namesake sibling in the broader survey, the Josephson-junction flux-qubit gyroscope, candidate #18) is designed to detect.
- **The holonomy of Section 4 is a synthetic, engineered connection** in a control-parameter space of the experimenter's own design, not a physical spacetime connection; its non-abelian character makes it the correct *algebraic type* of object for comparison to §7.3's construction, but the comparison remains structural, not physical.

## 7. Discussion: Completing the Representation-Theoretic Survey

With candidates #1–4, the analogous tier of this survey has now demonstrated, across four independent physical platforms: a fixed local frame (#1), a controllable local frame (#2), full-admissibility-group invariance independent of any frame choice (#3), and a non-abelian holonomy of the correct algebraic type (#4). No single platform demonstrates all four properties simultaneously, and it is worth noting explicitly that this is expected rather than a shortcoming to be engineered away — each platform's physical substrate (crystal defect, trapped atom, coupled nuclear/electron spins, superconducting circuit) imposes its own natural constraints on which parts of §7–8's formalism it can cleanly realize. Taken as a set, however, candidates #1–4 constitute a reasonably complete experimental syllabus for the representation-theoretic content of the framet formalism, independent of and prior to any question of gravitational coupling.

---

## 8. Conclusion

The superconducting transmon qubit realizes the terrestrial framet's engineered-frame case: a synthetic, microwave-defined control frame supporting arbitrary single-qubit \(SU(2)\) rotations, a full-\(SU(2)\)-invariant two-transmon singlet analog reproducing candidate #3's result on an independent platform, and — via established holonomic quantum computation protocols — a genuine non-abelian holonomy measurement, the first in this survey to match the algebraic type of §7.3's gravitational connection holonomy. Its silicon-substrate fabrication lineage also positions it as a natural bridge between the purely representation-theoretic analogous tier and the fabrication-focused silicon design space explored elsewhere in this programme. As with all analogous-tier candidates, this remains a structural and pedagogical demonstration with no coupling to the gravitational sector of the underlying theory.

## Appendix B: Non-Adiabatic Holonomic Gate Condition

For the non-adiabatic holonomic scheme used on superconducting timescales, the dark-state subspace \(\mathcal D\) must satisfy, at loop closure \(t=T\),

\[
\langle b(T)|d_i(0)\rangle = 0
\]

for the auxiliary bright state \(|b\rangle\) and each dark-subspace basis state \(|d_i\rangle\), ensuring the bright state returns to zero population and the acquired transformation on \(\mathcal D\) is purely geometric (holonomic) rather than containing an uncontrolled dynamical-phase contamination — the engineering condition underlying the protocol of Section 4.2.

## Appendix C: Two-Transmon Singlet Invariance

The invariance calculation of candidate #3, Appendix B applies without modification under the identification \(|\uparrow\rangle\to|0\rangle\), \(|\downarrow\rangle\to|1\rangle\): the two-transmon state \(|\Psi^-\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)\) is annihilated by all three generators of the collective \(SU(2)\) rotation \(U(g)\otimes U(g)\), by the identical algebraic argument, since the underlying representation theory does not depend on whether the two-level systems being combined are nuclear spins or superconducting circuit qubits.

## Appendix D: Open Questions for Future Transmon Framet Work

- Can the engineered dark-space holonomy of Section 4 be extended to a three-or-more-dimensional degenerate subspace, realizing a holonomy group larger than \(SU(2)\) and offering a closer structural match to the full \(\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\) group (six real generators) rather than its \(SU(2)\) subgroup?
- Can a multi-transmon lattice, coupled via a shared resonator bus, realize a spatially-extended synthetic connection with nontrivial curvature — i.e., an engineered analog of \(R^{ab}{}_{\mu\nu}\neq0\) — extending the single-loop holonomy measurement of Section 4.2 to a genuine curvature measurement in a control-parameter "space" rather than physical space?
- Is there a meaningful way to combine the non-abelian holonomy capability of the transmon with the full-group invariant-subspace capability of the two-transmon singlet (Section 3.3), e.g. by transporting an entangled singlet-like state around a non-abelian holonomic loop and verifying its invariance is preserved throughout transport rather than only at loop closure?
