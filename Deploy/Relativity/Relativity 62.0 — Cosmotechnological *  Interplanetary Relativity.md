# Relativity 62.0 — Cosmotechnological / Interplanetary Relativity  
## The Expansion of the Observer Beyond a Single Planet

**White paper / academic preprint**

---

## Abstract

Cosmotechnological / Interplanetary Relativity is the hypothesis that intelligence is not permanently planetary. Once a noosphere develops spacefaring capability, observation, memory, computation, governance, science, and culture begin to extend across multiple worlds. Earth’s noosphere may expand into a distributed cosmotechnological network spanning planets, moons, orbital habitats, spacecraft, probes, artificial ecosystems, and machine intelligences. Different worlds generate different embodied frames, different ecological constraints, and different civilizational perspectives. A multi-planetary observer may be represented formally as

\[
\mathcal{O}
=
\bigoplus_k
\mathcal{O}_k,
\]

where each \(\mathcal{O}_k\) is an observer-network located on a different world or platform, constrained by light-speed delay, local ecology, energy budgets, radiation environments, gravity, and resource limits. The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Intelligence is not bound to one planet; observation becomes interplanetary.}
}
\]

Interplanetary objectivity is not instantaneous consensus. It is slow, causal, and distributed verification across worlds separated by finite signal propagation, relativistic time structure, and ecological divergence. Cosmotechnological Relativity extends the relativistic program into the age of space civilization. It asserts that the future of knowledge is not merely global but interplanetary, and that objectivity itself must be reconceived as a causal, asynchronous, multi-world achievement.

## 1. Introduction

For most of history, intelligence was Earth-bound.

Observers were terrestrial.

Ecologies were terrestrial.

Science was terrestrial.

Memory was terrestrial.

Governance was terrestrial.

Even when humans looked at the stars, the observer remained on Earth.

Cosmotechnological / Interplanetary Relativity begins where this condition ends.

Once intelligence becomes spacefaring, the observer is no longer confined to a single planet. Observation becomes distributed across worlds. Memory becomes distributed across habitats. Computation becomes distributed across spacecraft and planetary networks. Governance becomes delayed by light-speed separation. Science becomes comparative across planets.

This changes the structure of knowledge.

A civilization spread across Earth, the Moon, Mars, orbital stations, asteroid habitats, and deep-space probes does not share a single instantaneous observational frame.

It inhabits a network of causal frames.

Each node sees locally.

Each node acts locally.

Each node communicates with delay.

Each node develops its own ecological pressures.

Each node may eventually develop its own culture, science, and identity.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Interplanetary civilization is not a larger nation-state. It is a relativistic network of observers.}
}
\]

## 4. Light-Speed Delay and Causal Structure

The most fundamental constraint on interplanetary observation is the finite speed of light.

If two nodes \(i\) and \(j\) are separated by distance \(d_{ij}\), the minimum communication delay is

\[
\tau_{ij}
=
\frac{d_{ij}}{c}.
\]

For Earth-Moon communication, this delay is about 1.3 seconds one way.

For Earth-Mars communication, it varies from about 3 to 22 minutes one way.

For outer planets, it becomes hours.

For interstellar distances, it becomes years.

This delay means that no interplanetary civilization can have real-time centralized awareness.

No global “now” exists in the strong sense.

Events must be ordered causally.

An event \(e_i\) at node \(i\) can influence event \(e_j\) at node \(j\) only if

\[
t_j - t_i
\geq
\tau_{ij}.
\]

Thus interplanetary knowledge is structured by a partial causal order.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Interplanetary objectivity is constrained by light cones.}
}
\]

## 5. Relativity of Simultaneity in Interplanetary Civilization

Special relativity already shows that simultaneity is frame-dependent.

For interplanetary civilization, this becomes practical.

Different habitats, spacecraft, and planets maintain different proper times.

High-velocity spacecraft experience time dilation:

\[
\Delta\tau
=
\Delta t
\sqrt{
1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}
}.
\]

Gravitational time dilation also matters near massive bodies:

\[
d\tau
=
dt
\sqrt{
1-\frac{2GM}{rc^2}
}.
\]

For most near-Earth and near-Mars operations, these effects are small but measurable.

For precision navigation, synchronization, and scientific observation, they must be included.

Thus an interplanetary civilization cannot rely on a single absolute time.

It must maintain a network of synchronized but locally autonomous time standards.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Interplanetary civilization requires relativistic time governance.}
}
\]

## 9. Distributed Observation and Asynchronous Fusion

Let each node \(k\) collect data \(D_k(t_k)\).

Because of delay, no node has immediate access to all data.

At local time \(t\), node \(k\) has access only to data within its past light cone:

\[
J_k(t)
=
\{
D_i(t_i)
\mid
t - t_i
\geq
\tau_{ik}
\}.
\]

The local posterior is therefore:

\[
P_k(\Theta\mid J_k(t)).
\]

As messages propagate, different nodes update their models.

The eventual integrated posterior is:

\[
P(\Theta\mid \{D_k(t_k)\}),
\]

but this posterior is not available everywhere at once.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Interplanetary knowledge converges asynchronously.}
}
\]

## 10. Slow Objectivity

Interplanetary objectivity is slow.

A claim verified on Earth may take minutes, hours, or years to verify elsewhere.

A consensus among worlds is not instantaneous agreement.

It is convergence after causal communication.

Let \(T\) be a time interval.

The verification horizon is the set of propositions that can be verified within time \(T\):

\[
\mathcal{V}(T)
=
\{
\theta
\mid
\theta
\text{ can be verified by signals within time }
T
\}.
\]

For small \(T\), only local verification is possible.

For large \(T\), interplanetary verification becomes possible.

Thus objectivity becomes time-scale dependent.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Objectivity has a light-speed latency.}
}
\]

\theta
