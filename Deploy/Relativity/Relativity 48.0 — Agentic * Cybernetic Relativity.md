# Relativity 48.0 — Agentic / Cybernetic Relativity  
## Agency as a Relational Control Structure Within Physics

**White paper / academic preprint**

---

## Abstract

Agentic / Cybernetic Relativity is the hypothesis that agents, goals, interventions, and control are not external additions to physics, nor metaphysical exceptions to physical law. They are effective physical frames defined by information, feedback, modeling, and causal influence. An agent is a physical system organized as a cybernetic loop:

\[
\text{agent}
=
\{
\text{sensor},
\text{model},
\text{policy},
\text{actuator}
\}.
\]

The effect of an intervention is written in causal notation as

\[
P(Y\mid \mathrm{do}(X)),
\]

distinguishing observation from intervention. Agency is emergent, but it is physically real insofar as it changes causal probabilities, regulates variables, and produces stable control structures. The central principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Agency is a relational control structure, not a metaphysical exception to physics.}
}
\]

Observers are not passive frames. They are cybernetic systems inside the physical process. Agentic / Cybernetic Relativity unifies control theory, causal inference, information theory, thermodynamics of computation, quantum measurement, biological regulation, artificial agency, and multi-agent coordination into a single relativistic framework. It asserts that agency is neither supernatural nor fundamental substance. It is a physically real, scale-relative, informationally constrained pattern of causal control.

## 1. Introduction

Physics has traditionally described the world as a system of states evolving under laws.

Observers were often treated as external spectators: idealized beings who measure without disturbing, record without cost, and intervene without being part of the dynamics.

This idealization is false.

Observers are physical systems. They sense, model, decide, act, and feedback. They consume energy. They store information. They erase records. They perturb what they measure. They are inside the physical process.

Agentic / Cybernetic Relativity makes this explicit.

It says that agency is not outside physics.

Agency is a physical organization of matter and information.

A thermostat is a simple agent. A bacterium is a more complex agent. An animal is a still more complex agent. A scientific laboratory is a distributed agent. A civilization is a network of agents.

In each case, agency consists of:

1. sensing,
2. modeling,
3. policy or control,
4. actuation,
5. feedback,
6. regulation toward goals or setpoints.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Agency is cybernetic control realized physically.}
}
\]

This does not mean that goals are fundamental cosmic principles.

It means that goals are internal control variables of certain physical systems.

Agency is real, but relational.

It is real when a system’s organization changes causal probabilities in a stable, goal-directed way.

## 3. What Is an Agent?

An agent is a physical system capable of a control loop.

A minimal agent may be represented as

\[
A
=
\left(
S,
M,
\pi,
\mathcal{A},
R
\right),
\]

where:

- \(S\) is a sensor or observation channel,
- \(M\) is an internal model,
- \(\pi\) is a policy or control law,
- \(\mathcal{A}\) is an actuator or intervention channel,
- \(R\) is a regulatory objective or setpoint.

The agent interacts with an environment \(W\).

At time \(t\), the environment has state \(w_t\).

The agent senses an observation,

\[
o_t
=
S(w_t).
\]

The agent updates its internal state,

\[
m_{t+1}
=
M(o_t,m_t).
\]

The agent selects an action,

\[
a_t
=
\pi(m_t).
\]

The environment evolves under the intervention,

\[
w_{t+1}
=
T(w_t,a_t).
\]

This is the cybernetic loop:

\[
w_t
\rightarrow
o_t
\rightarrow
m_t
\rightarrow
a_t
\rightarrow
w_{t+1}.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{An agent is a closed sensorimotor control loop embedded in physics.}
}
\]

## 4. Sensors and Observational Frames

A sensor is a physical transducer.

It maps environmental variables into internal signals.

For example:

\[
o
=
S(w).

It samples, filters, amplifies, and encodes.

Every sensor has:

1. a finite bandwidth,
2. a finite resolution,
3. noise,

6. a limited observable algebra.

Thus observation is not passive.

Observation is physical interaction.

An agent’s observational frame is its sensorimotor horizon.

## 5. Models and Internal Representations

An agent’s model is a physical structure that encodes regularities of the environment.

A model may be explicit or implicit.

It may be probabilistic:

\[
p(w\mid o).
\]

It may be predictive:

\[
p(o_{t+1}\mid o_{\leq t},a_t).

It may be causal:

\[
p(w\mid \mathrm{do}(a)).

The model allows the agent to predict the consequences of interventions.

A good model is not necessarily true in an absolute sense.

It is useful for control.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{An agent’s model is a control instrument, not a view from nowhere.}

## 6. Policies and Control Laws

A policy maps internal states to actions:

\[
a_t
=
\pi(m_t).
\]

In control theory, a simple feedback law is

\[
u_t
=
K(r_t-y_t),
\]

where:

- \(r_t\) is the reference or setpoint,
- \(y_t\) is the measured output,
- \(e_t=r_t-y_t\) is the error,
- \(K\) is the controller,
- \(u_t\) is the control signal.

The closed-loop system regulates the output toward the reference.

For a plant \(G\) and controller \(K\), the closed-loop transfer function is

\[
H
=
\frac{GK}{1+GK}.
\]

Negative feedback reduces error and rejects disturbances.

Thus agency is not mere action.

Agency is regulation.

\[
r,

\mathbb{E}

Goals are not metaphysical purposes.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A goal is an internal regulative constraint.}

## 8. Interventions and the do-Operator

Observation and intervention are different.

Observing \(X\) gives

\[
P(Y\mid X).
\]

Intervening on \(X\) gives

\[
P(Y\mid \mathrm{do}(X)).
\]

The do-operator represents an external manipulation that sets \(X\) independently of its usual causes.

In a structural causal model,

\[
X_i
=
f_i(\mathrm{PA}_i,U_i),
\]

an intervention

\[
\mathrm{do}(X=x)
\]

replaces the equation for \(X\) by

\[
X=x.
\]

The post-intervention distribution is

\[
P(v\mid \mathrm{do}(x))
=
\prod_{i:X_i\neq X}
P(v_i\mid \mathrm{pa}_i)
\bigg|_{X=x}.
\]

This is the mathematical core of interventionist causality.

Agents are systems capable of performing such interventions.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Agency is interventionist causality embodied in a physical system.}
}
\]

## 9. Causal Influence as Physical Reality of Agency

Agency is physically real when interventions change causal probabilities.

Let \(A\) be an agent’s action variable and \(Y\) an outcome variable.

The causal effect of \(A\) on \(Y\) is

\[
\Delta_Y(A)
=
P(Y\mid \mathrm{do}(A=a))
-
P(Y\mid \mathrm{do}(A=a')).
\]

If

\[
\Delta_Y(A)
\neq
0,
\]

then the agent’s intervention changes the causal distribution of outcomes.

This is the operational criterion of agency.

Agency is not a hidden soul.

It is a measurable causal capacity.

## 10. Cybernetics and Requisite Variety

Cybernetics is the science of communication and control.

A central principle is Ashby’s law of requisite variety:

\[
V(\text{controller})
\geq
V(\text{disturbance}),
\]

where \(V\) is variety, or the number of distinguishable states.

A regulator must possess enough internal variety to absorb or counter environmental disturbances.

Thus agency is constrained by information capacity.

An agent cannot control what it cannot distinguish.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Control is limited by information.}
}
\]

## 11. Information and Control

Control requires information.

The mutual information between an action \(A\) and a future state \(W'\) measures how much the action influences the future:

\[
I(A;W')
=
\sum_{a,w'}
P(a,w')
\ln
\frac{P(a,w')}{P(a)P(w')}.
\]

A useful measure of agency is empowerment:

\[
\mathcal{E}
=
\max_{\pi}
I(A_t;W_{t+1}),
\]

where the maximum is taken over available policies.

Empowerment measures the agent’s capacity to influence future states.

Thus agency can be quantified informationally.

## 12. Thermodynamics of Agency

Agents are physical systems and therefore obey thermodynamics.

Measurement, memory, computation, and erasure have energetic costs.

Landauer’s principle states that erasing one bit costs at least

\[
E
\geq
k_{\text{B}}T\ln 2.
\]

Maxwell’s demon appears to violate the second law by using information to extract work.

But when memory erasure is included, the second law is restored.

The generalized second law with information is

\[
\langle W\rangle
\leq
-\Delta F
+
k_{\text{B}}T I,
\]

where \(I\) is information gained or used.

Thus information is physical.

Agency is thermodynamically constrained.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Agents are information engines bound by entropy.}
}
\]

## 14. Simple Agents: The Thermostat

A thermostat is a minimal cybernetic agent.

Its sensor measures temperature \(T\).

Its setpoint is \(T_0\).

\[
e

Its policy is:

\[
u

\begin{cases}

It has no consciousness.

\{\mathcal{I}_k\},

\[
P(k)
=
\operatorname{Tr}

\mathcal{I}_k(\rho)
\right].

The post-intervention state is

\[
\rho_k
=
\frac{\mathcal{I}_k(\rho)}
{\operatorname{Tr}[\mathcal{I}_k(\rho)]}.
\]

An agent choosing an intervention chooses the instrument.

The agent does not stand outside quantum mechanics.

The agent is a physical system coupled to the measured system.

Thus quantum measurement is an intervention performed by a cybernetic system.
