Abstract

Fault tolerance is the defining requirement for scalable quantum computation. While quantum error correction protects information against noise, fault tolerance ensures that computational operations themselves remain reliable despite the presence of errors in physical hardware, measurements, control systems, and recovery mechanisms.

Within the Geometric Qubit framework, information is encoded in geometric degrees of freedom including curvature configurations, topological sectors, defect structures, boundary states, connectivity networks, and geometric excitations. These systems experience unique forms of geometric noise, geometric decoherence, geometric leakage, and geometric instability. Consequently, a specialized theory of fault tolerance is required.

This paper develops the theory of Geometric Fault Tolerance (GFT), establishing mathematical principles governing protected geometric computation, logical geometric operations, fault-tolerant geometric gates, geometric threshold theorems, geometric syndrome processing, distributed recovery architectures, self-correcting geometric memories, and large-scale geometric processors. The resulting framework defines the operational conditions under which geometry-encoded quantum information can support arbitrarily long computations with arbitrarily small logical error rates.
