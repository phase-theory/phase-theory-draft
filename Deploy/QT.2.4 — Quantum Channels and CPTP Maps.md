Part II — Mathematical Framework

Section 10. Quantum Channels and CPTP Maps

10.1 Introduction

Quantum computation is fundamentally a sequence of physical transformations acting upon quantum states. While idealized algorithms are represented by unitary operators,

[
\rho \mapsto U\rho U^\dagger,
]

real quantum computers perform a far broader class of operations including

* noisy quantum gates,
* state preparation,
* measurement,
* feedback control,
* qubit reset,
* error correction,
* thermalization.

The mathematically complete description of these processes is provided by quantum channels, namely completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps.

Within Quantum Computational Thermodynamics (QCT), every quantum channel is interpreted not merely as an information-processing operation but as a thermodynamic transformation that transports energy, entropy, and free energy through computational state space.

The purpose of this section is to construct a thermodynamic theory of CPTP maps and establish their role as the elementary dynamical operators of computational thermodynamics.

10.3 Complete Positivity

Ordinary positivity is insufficient because quantum systems may be entangled with external degrees of freedom.

A physically realizable operation must satisfy

[
\boxed{
I_n\otimes\Phi
\ge0
}
]

for every auxiliary Hilbert space.

This requirement defines complete positivity.

⸻

Proposition 10.1

Every physically realizable quantum computation must be represented by a completely positive map.

⸻

Proof

Suppose

[
\Phi
]

were merely positive.

Then an entangled extension

[
I\otimes\Phi
]

could transform a positive density operator into a non-positive operator,

violating probability conservation.

Therefore

[
\Phi
]

must be completely positive.

□

10.19 Summary

Quantum channels constitute the fundamental dynamical objects of Quantum Computational Thermodynamics. Every physically realizable operation—whether a gate, measurement, reset, or error-correction step—is represented by a CPTP map whose action extends beyond logical state transformation to include changes in energy, entropy, free energy, and exergy.

The principal results of this section are:

* a thermodynamic interpretation of CPTP maps as resource-transforming operations;
* the definition of channel entropy production and intrinsic thermodynamic cost functionals;
* a classification of channels according to dissipative behavior;
* generalized work and heat decompositions for arbitrary quantum operations;
* the introduction of computational channel capacity, thermodynamic channel distance, and the Computational Channel Tensor;
* and a thermodynamic channel algebra that elevates CPTP maps from purely informational objects to fundamental carriers of physical computational resources.

These results establish the operator framework required for Section 11, where quantum computation will be reformulated as a resource theory in which energy, entropy, coherence, and reversibility become explicit computational resources subject to monotonic transformations and thermodynamic constraints.
