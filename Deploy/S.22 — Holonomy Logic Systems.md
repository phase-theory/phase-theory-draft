Abstract

In differential geometry, a holonomy measures the transformation acquired by an object after parallel transport around a closed loop. Holonomies arise naturally in gauge theory, fiber bundles, geometric phases, gravitation, topological matter, and quantum field theory. Traditionally, holonomy is viewed as a geometric invariant or a physical phase factor. However, from an information-theoretic perspective, holonomies represent a powerful computational resource: they encode path-dependent transformations that are intrinsically geometric and often robust against local perturbations.

This paper develops the theory of Holonomy Logic Systems (HLS), a computational framework in which logical operations are implemented through controlled holonomies generated by geometric transport. Information is encoded into holonomy sectors, processed through loop operations, protected by topological and gauge structures, and manipulated through geometric control of connections and curvature.

The resulting architecture extends holonomic quantum computation into a broader geometry-native paradigm, providing a foundation for scalable, fault-tolerant computation based on the intrinsic properties of geometric transport.

The framework introduces holonomy qubits, loop encoding, path algebras, holonomy groups, geometric-phase logic, holonomic channels, stabilizers, fault-tolerant architectures, and photonic implementations. It extends the concepts of holonomic quantum computation into a broader geometry-native information theory.

Within the Structural Sector, Holonomy Logic Systems serve as the operational layer connecting Fiber-Bundle Quantum Computation with Geometric Gauge Structures. Together they demonstrate how connections, curvature, topology, and transport can be unified into a coherent computational architecture where logical operations emerge directly from the geometry of paths themselves.
