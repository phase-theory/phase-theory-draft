ABSTRACT

Contemporary mathematics generally begins with primitive entities whose existence is taken as given. Sets contain elements, numbers quantify magnitudes, spaces contain points, and structures are defined through relations among already-existing objects.

This white paper proposes a different foundation.

Resonant Mathematics is a proposed mathematical framework in which the primitive concept is not membership, quantity, distance, or location, but compatibility.

The central object of the theory is a resonance relation:

[
\mathcal{R}(x,y;\lambda),
]

which measures the degree to which configurations (x) and (y) can coexist, reinforce, transform, or persist relative to a contextual parameter (\lambda).

Within this framework, mathematical identity is not necessarily primitive. A configuration becomes mathematically significant when it forms a stable pattern of relational compatibility. Thus:

[
\boxed{
\text{Compatibility}
\rightarrow
\text{Resonance}
\rightarrow
\text{Persistence}
\rightarrow
\text{Identity}
\rightarrow
\text{Topology}
\rightarrow
\text{Geometry}
}
]

The theory proposes that topology may be generalized from neighborhood relations based on proximity to resonance neighborhoods based on compatibility. Geometry may then emerge as a special case of a more general resonant structure. Numbers may be interpreted as invariants of persistent relational configurations. Calculus may be generalized to describe deformation through resonance space rather than merely displacement through coordinate space.

The proposed framework introduces:

1. A primitive resonance relation.
2. Resonance spaces and resonance neighborhoods.
3. Stability-generated identity.
4. Resonant topology.
5. Resonant metrics.
6. Resonant algebra.
7. Resonant calculus.
8. Resonant curvature.
9. Resonant dynamics.
10. A theory of emergent discreteness.
11. Resonant information theory.
12. A proposed hierarchy of mathematical structures.
13. Candidate axioms and consistency requirements.
14. A program for formal mathematical development.

The theory is speculative and does not claim to replace established mathematics. Its purpose is to propose a new foundational direction: a mathematics in which structure is generated by persistent relational compatibility.
