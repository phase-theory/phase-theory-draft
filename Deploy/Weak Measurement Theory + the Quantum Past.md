# Abstract

Weak measurement theory introduced by Aharonov, Albert, and Vaidman (AAV) revealed a new layer of quantum description inaccessible to conventional projective measurements. In weak measurements, observables possess *weak values*

A_w = \frac{\langle \phi |A|\psi\rangle}{\langle \phi|\psi\rangle}

defined by both an initial state (|\psi\rangle) and a final post-selected state (|\phi\rangle).

Weak values can be:

* outside the eigenvalue spectrum,
* arbitrarily large,
* negative when eigenvalues are positive,
* complex-valued.

Although experimentally verified, their ontological status remains unclear.

This paper develops a new framework:

## Bidirectional Ontological Quantum Mechanics (BOQM)

where:

1. Quantum reality is jointly determined by forward and backward quantum information flows.
2. Weak values represent physically real conditional properties.
3. The quantum past is reconstructed from both temporal boundaries.
4. Quantum causality becomes a two-boundary variational principle.
5. Weak values of quantum fields define spacetime-dependent conditional field configurations.
6. Anomalous weak values become measures of contextual geometric curvature.
7. Post-selection becomes a thermodynamic resource.

The result is a mathematically coherent extension of weak measurement theory into QFT, quantum thermodynamics, and time-symmetric quantum foundations.
