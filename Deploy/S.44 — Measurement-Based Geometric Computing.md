Abstract

Traditional computational models define execution as controlled evolution generated by explicit operations. Circuit-based quantum computing extends this paradigm through ordered application of gates.

Measurement-based quantum computation introduced a different idea: prepare a highly structured resource state, then drive computation through sequences of measurements.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved from operators into geometry:

* gates became trajectories,
* circuits became manifolds,
* registers became accessibility structures,
* arithmetic became geometric transformation,
* universality became closure of computational geometry.

This paper develops Measurement-Based Geometric Computing (MBGC): a theoretical framework in which computation is represented as adaptive projection through structured state geometry.

Rather than treating measurement as termination of computation, the framework interprets measurement as the mechanism that generates computation.

The central proposal:

Computation is not performed before measurement.

Computation emerges through measurement.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally validated computational architecture.
