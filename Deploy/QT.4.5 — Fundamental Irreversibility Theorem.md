24.1 Introduction

The preceding sections established independent thermodynamic descriptions of quantum measurement, information erasure, ancilla preparation, reset operations, and error syndrome extraction. Each process possesses its own work functional, entropy production, free-energy budget, and irreversible losses. Collectively, these operations constitute the irreversible sector of quantum computation.

While quantum logic is generated by unitary evolution and is therefore microscopically reversible, every scalable implementation necessarily incorporates nonunitary operations that transfer entropy from the computational register into the surrounding environment. The resulting entropy generation cannot be eliminated by improvements in gate fidelity alone, because it originates from the physical requirements of information acquisition, initialization, memory recycling, and fault-tolerant operation.

This section unifies the preceding developments into a single theorem governing the thermodynamic structure of quantum computation. The resulting Fundamental Irreversibility Theorem provides a global entropy balance law and establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for fully reversible quantum computation.

24.16 Principle of Computational Irreversibility

The preceding developments motivate the central principle of Part IV.

Principle of Computational Irreversibility

Every scalable quantum computation inevitably exports entropy through measurement, ancilla preparation, reset, and fault-tolerant error correction. These irreversible processes impose a thermodynamic cost that cannot be eliminated solely through reversible unitary gate design.

This principle extends Landauer’s insight from isolated memory erasure to complete quantum algorithms.
