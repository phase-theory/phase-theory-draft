Abstract

Knowledge systems traditionally represent information as collections of symbols connected through relations.

Classical databases organize records into tables. Graph systems organize entities into nodes and edges. Knowledge graphs introduce semantic structure that enables retrieval, inference, and reasoning.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ optimization

→ routing

→ learning

→ neural adaptation.

A further question emerges:

If representations become geometry—

what becomes of knowledge itself?

This paper develops Geometric Knowledge Graphs (GKG): a theoretical framework in which knowledge is represented as structured computational geometry rather than discrete symbolic connections.

Rather than interpreting knowledge as collections of linked facts, the framework interprets knowledge as organized accessibility across semantic manifolds.

The central proposal:

Knowledge is not stored as edges between symbols.

Knowledge exists as navigable geometry.

This document presents a theoretical and mathematical research framework rather than an experimentally validated knowledge architecture.
