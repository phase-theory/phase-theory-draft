Abstract

Computation is traditionally represented through state evolution over registers, gates, and communication channels.

Quantum information extends this through superposition, entanglement, and nonclassical correlations.

Some research programs in mathematical physics explore whether geometry itself may be represented through discrete relational structures. Independently, information systems increasingly emphasize organization through networks and graph-like descriptions.

Across the Structural Sector, information evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ computation

→ security

→ memory

→ preservation

→ informational geometry

→ loop organization.

A further question emerges:

If information becomes organized through geometry—

can computation be represented through relational network structure itself?

This paper develops Spin-Network Computation (SNC): a speculative theoretical framework in which information processing is represented through transformations of discrete relational geometry.

This framework does not claim implementation of physical spin-network models or a theory of quantum gravity.

Instead, it studies an abstract computational language in which information is organized through structured connectivity.

The central proposal:

Computation is not only evolution of values.

Computation may also be represented as transformation of relationships.

This document presents speculative conceptual research rather than a realizable computational architecture.
