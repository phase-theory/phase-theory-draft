# Algebra of Structural Flows

**Preprint — August 2026**

**Mathematics Subject Classification (2020).** 18M05, 18D10, 15A72, 91B60, 37Cxx.

**Keywords.** structural flows, transformation algebra, object-free category, tensor calculus, monoidal category, input-output economics, dynamical systems, information flow.

---

## Abstract

This paper develops an algebraic theory in which transformations, not objects, are the primitive entities. The Algebra of Structural Flows (ASF) is a many-sorted algebra of typed flows equipped with sequential composition, parallel composition, and coherent structural symmetries. A flow is written as

\[
F:X\longrightarrow Y,
\]

where \(X\) and \(Y\) are interfaces, not point sets. Composition is governed by the law

\[
F_{3}=F_{2}\circ F_{1},
\]

and parallel composition is governed by a tensor operation

\[
F\otimes G:X\otimes U\longrightarrow Y\otimes V.
\]

No underlying elements, points, states, agents, or tuples are required for the theory to be well formed. Objects appear secondarily as identity flows, idempotent recirculations, or invariant substructures. The paper gives an equational axiomatization, a tensorial calculus, coherence and object-emergence theorems, and four principal application domains: economics, information systems, dynamical systems, and category theory.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical mathematical ontology usually proceeds from objects to maps. One first posits sets, spaces, agents, states, or types, and then studies functions, operators, relations, or transformations between them. The Algebra of Structural Flows reverses this order.

The primitive notion is not an object but a **flow**: a structured transformation between boundary specifications. Objects are not denied; they are demoted. They arise as stable units of composition, as identity channels, as idempotent self-maintaining processes, or as invariant subflows.

The central law is deceptively simple:

\[
F_{3}=F_{2}\circ F_{1}.
\]

If \(F_{1}\) carries an input interface into an intermediate interface, and \(F_{2}\) carries that intermediate interface into an output interface, then their composite \(F_{3}\) is again a flow. The law is required before any underlying point set is specified. In this sense, ASF is a point-free algebra of transformations. It is not anti-set-theoretic; rather, set-theoretic realizations are treated as models, not foundations.

The purpose of this paper is to give ASF a rigorous preprint-level formulation. The main contributions are:

1. an equational axiomatization of structural flows;
2. a tensorial component calculus for sequential and parallel composition;
3. an object-emergence theorem showing how objects arise as identity flows;
4. a coherence statement for free flow algebras;
5. applications to economics, information systems, dynamical systems, and category theory.

The guiding principle is:

\[
\textbf{Flows are primary. Objects are secondary.}
\]

## 2. Axiomatic Framework

### 2.1 Interfaces as boundary specifications

Let \(\Sigma\) be a set of atomic port types. Elements of \(\Sigma\) are not sets; they are labels for structural channels. Examples might include

\[
\Sigma=\{\text{commodity},\text{money},\text{data},\text{state},\text{signal}\},
\]

but the theory does not depend on any particular interpretation.

Let

\[
\operatorname{Int}(\Sigma)=\Sigma^{*}
\]

denote the free strict monoid generated by \(\Sigma\). Its elements are finite ordered interfaces

\[
X=A_{1}\otimes A_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{m},
\]

with unit interface \(\varepsilon\). We often write concatenation multiplicatively:

\[
XY:=X\otimes Y.
\]

An interface is a boundary specification, not a collection of points.

---

### 2.2 Flows

An Algebra of Structural Flows over \(\Sigma\) consists of a family of sets

\[
\mathcal A(X,Y),
\]

indexed by interfaces \(X,Y\in\operatorname{Int}(\Sigma)\). An element

\[
F\in\mathcal A(X,Y)
\]

is called a **flow from \(X\) to \(Y\)** and is written

\[
F:X\longrightarrow Y.
\]

The set \(\mathcal A(X,Y)\) is the set of transformations from boundary \(X\) to boundary \(Y\). No elements of \(X\) or \(Y\) are presupposed.

---

### 2.3 Sequential composition

For interfaces \(X,Y,Z\), ASF possesses a partial-free sequential composition

\[
\circ:\mathcal A(Y,Z)\times\mathcal A(X,Y)\longrightarrow\mathcal A(X,Z),
\]

written

\[
(G,F)\longmapsto G\circ F.
\]

Thus if

\[
F_{1}:X\longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
F_{2}:Y\longrightarrow Z,
\]

then

\[
F_{3}=F_{2}\circ F_{1}:X\longrightarrow Z.
\]

This is the fundamental composition law.

---

### 2.4 Identity flows

For every interface \(X\), there is a distinguished identity flow

\[
\mathbf 1_{X}\in\mathcal A(X,X).
\]

It represents the passive transmission of the interface \(X\) to itself.

---

### 2.5 Parallel composition

ASF also has a tensor or parallel composition

\[
\otimes:\mathcal A(X,Y)\times\mathcal A(U,V)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal A(X\otimes U,Y\otimes V).
\]

If

\[
F:X\longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
G:U\longrightarrow V,
\]

then

\[
F\otimes G:X\otimes U\longrightarrow Y\otimes V.
\]

This operation expresses simultaneous, independent, or parallel transformation.

---

### 2.6 Structural symmetries

For every pair of interfaces \(X,Y\), there is a symmetry flow

\[
\sigma_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\longrightarrow Y\otimes X.
\]

It reorders ports without altering their internal structure.

---

## 3. Equational Axioms

We state ASF in strict monoidal form for notational clarity. The non-strict version is obtained by inserting coherent associators.

Let

\[
F:X\to Y,\quad
G:Y\to Z,\quad
H:Z\to W,
\]

and let

\[
K:U\to V,\quad
L:V\to W.
\]

The axioms are as follows.

### Axiom ASF1: Associativity

\[
H\circ(G\circ F)=(H\circ G)\circ F.
\]

### Axiom ASF2: Units

\[
\mathbf 1_{Y}\circ F=F,
\qquad
F\circ\mathbf 1_{X}=F.
\]

### Axiom ASF3: Tensor unit

\[
\mathbf 1_{X}\otimes\mathbf 1_{Y}
=
\mathbf 1_{X\otimes Y}.
\]

### Axiom ASF4: Interchange law

Whenever the compositions are defined,

\[
(G\circ F)\otimes(L\circ K)
=
(G\otimes L)\circ(F\otimes K).
\]

This is the compatibility of sequential and parallel composition.

### Axiom ASF5: Symmetry involution

\[
\sigma_{Y,X}\circ\sigma_{X,Y}
=
\mathbf 1_{X\otimes Y}.
\]

### Axiom ASF6: Naturality of symmetry

For \(F:X\to Y\) and \(G:U\to V\),

\[
\sigma_{Y,V}\circ(F\otimes G)
=
(G\otimes F)\circ\sigma_{X,U}.
\]

### Axiom ASF7: Symmetry coherence

The symmetries satisfy the standard hexagonal coherence laws:

\[
\sigma_{X,Y\otimes Z}
=
(\mathbf 1_{Y}\otimes\sigma_{X,Z})
\circ
(\sigma_{X,Y}\otimes\mathbf 1_{Z}),
\]

and

\[
\sigma_{X\otimes Y,Z}
=
(\sigma_{X,Z}\otimes\mathbf 1_{Y})
\circ
(\mathbf 1_{X}\otimes\sigma_{Y,Z}).
\]

Together with the unit laws

\[
\sigma_{X,\varepsilon}=\mathbf 1_{X},
\qquad
\sigma_{\varepsilon,X}=\mathbf 1_{X}.
\]

These axioms define a strict symmetric monoidal algebra of flows.

---

### Proposition 3.1: Point-freeness

The axioms of ASF contain no membership relation \(x\in X\), no element variables ranging over interfaces, and no evaluation of flows at points. Therefore the theory is well formed without assigning point sets to interfaces.

**Proof.** The primitive sorted variables are flows \(F,G,H,\dots\), interfaces \(X,Y,Z,\dots\), and operations \(\circ,\otimes,\sigma,\mathbf 1\). All axioms are equations between flow terms. No axiom quantifies over elements of interfaces. Hence point-set semantics is optional, not foundational. \(\square\)

---

## 4. Linear Structural Flow Algebras

Many applications require addition and scalar multiplication of flows. Let \(R\) be a commutative ring.

### Definition 4.1: \(R\)-linear ASF

An \(R\)-linear ASF is an ASF such that each \(\mathcal A(X,Y)\) is an \(R\)-module, and the operations

\[
\circ:\mathcal A(Y,Z)\times\mathcal A(X,Y)\to\mathcal A(X,Z),
\]

\[
\otimes:\mathcal A(X,Y)\times\mathcal A(U,V)\to\mathcal A(X\otimes U,Y\otimes V)
\]

are \(R\)-bilinear.

In a linear ASF, if \(a,b\in R\), then

\[
G\circ(aF_{1}+bF_{2})
=
a(G\circ F_{1})+b(G\circ F_{2}),
\]

and

\[
(aG_{1}+bG_{2})\circ F
=
a(G_{1}\circ F)+b(G_{2}\circ F).
\]

Similarly,

\[
(aF_{1}+bF_{2})\otimes(cG_{1}+dG_{2})
=
ac(F_{1}\otimes G_{1})
+ad(F_{1}\otimes G_{2})
+bc(F_{2}\otimes G_{1})
+bd(F_{2}\otimes G_{2}).
\]

---

### Proposition 4.2: Endoflow algebras

For each interface \(X\), the set

\[
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal A}(X):=\mathcal A(X,X)
\]

is a unital associative algebra under composition. If \(\mathcal A\) is \(R\)-linear, then \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal A}(X)\) is a unital associative \(R\)-algebra.

**Proof.** Closure follows from composition

\[
\circ:\mathcal A(X,X)\times\mathcal A(X,X)\to\mathcal A(X,X).
\]

Associativity is ASF1. The unit is \(\mathbf 1_{X}\). In the linear case, bilinearity gives the \(R\)-algebra structure. \(\square\)

Thus every interface carries an algebra of self-transformations.

## 5. Tensor Calculus of Structural Flows

The tensor notation makes the algebraic structure explicit. It resembles classical tensor notation, but the indices label interfaces, not elements.

For a flow

\[
F:X\longrightarrow Y,
\]

write

\[
F^{Y}_{X}.
\]

If

\[
X=A_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{m},
\qquad
Y=B_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes B_{n},
\]

we may write

\[
F^{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}}.
\]

The lower indices denote input ports; the upper indices denote output ports.

---

### 5.1 Sequential composition as contraction

Let

\[
F^{Y}_{X}:X\to Y,
\qquad
G^{Z}_{Y}:Y\to Z.
\]

Then their composite is

\[
(G\circ F)^{Z}_{X}
=
G^{Z}_{Y}F^{Y}_{X}.
\]

The repeated interface symbol \(Y\) is contracted. This contraction is not necessarily numerical summation. Abstractly, it denotes sequential composition. In a linear realization, it may become summation or integration over an intermediate basis.

---

### 5.2 Associativity derivation

Let

\[
F:X\to Y,\qquad
G:Y\to Z,\qquad
H:Z\to W.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl((H\circ G)\circ F\bigr)^{W}_{X}
&=
(H\circ G)^{W}_{Y}F^{Y}_{X}
\\
&=
\bigl(H^{W}_{Z}G^{Z}_{Y}\bigr)F^{Y}_{X}
\\
&=
H^{W}_{Z}\bigl(G^{Z}_{Y}F^{Y}_{X}\bigr)
\\
&=
H^{W}_{Z}(G\circ F)^{Y}_{X}
\\
&=
\bigl(H\circ(G\circ F)\bigr)^{W}_{X}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus associativity is precisely the associativity of contracted tensorial composition.

---

### 5.3 Parallel composition

Let

\[
F:X\to Y,
\qquad
G:U\to V.
\]

Then

\[
(F\otimes G)^{Y V}_{X U}
=
F^{Y}_{X}G^{V}_{U}.
\]

Here juxtaposition denotes parallel placement.

---

### 5.4 Interchange derivation

Let

\[
F:X\to Y,\quad
G:Y\to Z,
\]

and

\[
K:U\to V,\quad
L:V\to W.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl((G\circ F)\otimes(L\circ K)\bigr)^{Z W}_{X U}
&=
(G\circ F)^{Z}_{X}(L\circ K)^{W}_{U}
\\
&=
\bigl(G^{Z}_{Y}F^{Y}_{X}\bigr)
  \bigl(L^{W}_{V}K^{V}_{U}\bigr)
\\
&=
(G\otimes L)^{Z W}_{Y V}
  (F\otimes K)^{Y V}_{X U}
\\
&=
\bigl((G\otimes L)\circ(F\otimes K)\bigr)^{Z W}_{X U}.
\end{aligned}
\]

If the port order is not already aligned, the symmetry flows \(\sigma_{X,Y}\) perform the necessary reordering.

---

### 5.5 Symmetry

The symmetry

\[
\sigma_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X
\]

is written

\[
\sigma^{Y X}_{X Y}.
\]

Its inverse property is

\[
\sigma^{X Y}_{Y X}\sigma^{Y X}_{X Y}
=
\delta^{X Y}_{X Y},
\]

where \(\delta^{X Y}_{X Y}\) denotes the identity flow \(\mathbf 1_{X\otimes Y}\).

---

### 5.6 Cyclic trace

In a linear ASF, one may introduce a cyclic trace operation

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{X}:\mathcal A(X,X)\to R,
\]

satisfying

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{Y}(G\circ F)
=
\operatorname{Tr}_{X}(F\circ G)
\]

for

\[
F:X\to Y,
\qquad
G:Y\to X.
\]

In tensor notation,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{Y}(G\circ F)
=
G^{Y}_{X}F^{X}_{Y}
=
F^{X}_{Y}G^{Y}_{X}
=
\operatorname{Tr}_{X}(F\circ G).
\]

This trace captures closed circulation, feedback, and aggregate recirculation.

## 6. Object Emergence

The central philosophical claim of ASF is that objects are secondary. We now make this precise.

### Theorem 6.1: Object-emergence theorem

Let \(\mathcal A\) be a small Algebra of Structural Flows. Define a category \(\mathsf{Cat}(\mathcal A)\) as follows.

1. The objects of \(\mathsf{Cat}(\mathcal A)\) are the identity flows \(\mathbf 1_{X}\).
2. The morphisms from \(\mathbf 1_{X}\) to \(\mathbf 1_{Y}\) are the flows in \(\mathcal A(X,Y)\).
3. Composition is inherited from \(\mathcal A\).
4. The tensor product is inherited from \(\mathcal A\).

Then \(\mathsf{Cat}(\mathcal A)\) is a strict symmetric monoidal category.

Conversely, every strict symmetric monoidal category whose object monoid is generated by \(\Sigma\) determines an Algebra of Structural Flows by taking its morphisms as flows.

**Proof.** For objects \(\mathbf 1_X,\mathbf 1_Y,\mathbf 1_Z\), define

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf 1_X,\mathbf 1_Y):=\mathcal A(X,Y).
\]

Given

\[
F\in\mathcal A(X,Y),
\qquad
G\in\mathcal A(Y,Z),
\]

composition in \(\mathsf{Cat}(\mathcal A)\) is

\[
G\circ F\in\mathcal A(X,Z).
\]

Associativity follows from ASF1. The identity morphism of \(\mathbf 1_X\) is \(\mathbf 1_X\in\mathcal A(X,X)\), and the unit laws follow from ASF2.

The tensor product on objects is

\[
\mathbf 1_X\otimes\mathbf 1_Y:=\mathbf 1_{X\otimes Y}.
\]

On morphisms it is

\[
F\otimes G.
\]

Axiom ASF3 gives the tensor unit. Axiom ASF4 gives functoriality of \(\otimes\). Axioms ASF5–ASF7 give the symmetric monoidal structure.

Conversely, given a strict symmetric monoidal category \(\mathcal C\), set

\[
\mathcal A(X,Y):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(X,Y).
\]

The categorical composition, monoidal product, and braiding supply the ASF operations. The two constructions are inverse up to the harmless identification of an interface \(X\) with its identity flow \(\mathbf 1_X\). \(\square\)

Thus objects are not absent; they are recovered as identity flows. The primitive ontology remains transformational.

---

### 6.2 Idempotents and persistent structures

A more refined notion of objecthood arises from idempotent flows.

### Definition 6.2

An endoflow

\[
p:X\to X
\]

is idempotent if

\[
p\circ p=p.
\]

In tensor notation,

\[
p^{X}_{Y}p^{Y}_{X}=p^{X}_{X}.
\]

Idempotents represent self-stabilizing processes.

---

### Proposition 6.3: Idempotent objectification

Suppose \(\mathcal A\) is idempotent complete. If \(p\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal A}(X)\) satisfies \(p^{2}=p\), then there exist an interface \(Y\) and flows

\[
i:Y\to X,
\qquad
r:X\to Y
\]

such that

\[
r\circ i=\mathbf 1_{Y},
\qquad
i\circ r=p.
\]

Thus the idempotent flow \(p\) splits into an object-like component \(Y\).

**Proof.** This is the standard splitting of idempotents, interpreted flow-theoretically. In a linear realization, one may take \(Y\) to be the image of \(p\), \(i\) the inclusion, and \(r\) the projection. Abstractly, idempotent completion freely adjoins such \(Y,i,r\). \(\square\)

Therefore an object may be understood as a flow that has become idempotently stable.

---

### 6.3 Invariant subflows

Given an endoflow

\[
T:X\to X,
\]

an idempotent \(p:X\to X\) defines an invariant substructure if

\[
T\circ p=p\circ T.
\]

Then \(T\) preserves the stabilized component represented by \(p\). In tensor notation,

\[
T^{X}_{Y}p^{Y}_{Z}
=
p^{X}_{Y}T^{Y}_{Z}.
\]

This gives a flow-theoretic replacement for the classical notion of an invariant subset or invariant subspace.

---

## 7. Free Structural Flow Algebras and Coherence

Let \(\Gamma\) be a directed graph whose vertices are interfaces and whose edges are primitive flows

\[
g:X\to Y.
\]

The free ASF generated by \(\Gamma\), denoted

\[
\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma),
\]

is obtained by closing \(\Gamma\) under composition, tensor product, identities, and symmetries, modulo the ASF axioms.

---

### Theorem 7.1: Normal-form coherence

Every well-typed flow expression in \(\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma)\) is equal, modulo the ASF axioms, to a string diagram with input interface \(X\) and output interface \(Y\). Two such expressions are equal in \(\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma)\) precisely when their string diagrams are equivalent under the coherence laws for strict symmetric monoidal categories.

**Proof sketch.** Translate each primitive generator \(g:X\to Y\) into a box with input wire \(X\) and output wire \(Y\). Sequential composition is vertical composition of diagrams. Tensor product is horizontal juxtaposition. Symmetries are wire crossings. The ASF axioms are exactly the equational laws for strict symmetric monoidal categories. By the Joyal–Street–Mac Lane coherence theorem for symmetric monoidal categories, every diagram reduces to a unique normal form up to planar isotopy and symmetry coherence. \(\square\)

Thus ASF expressions may be manipulated as tensor networks or flow diagrams without reference to points.

## 8. Application I: Economics

In classical economics, one often begins with agents, firms, households, or sectors as objects. ASF instead begins with flows of commodities, money, obligations, and information. Objects such as firms or markets are reconstructed as stable flow patterns.

Let

\[
S=s_{1}\otimes s_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes s_{n}
\]

be a sector interface. Each port \(s_i\) represents a commodity or productive channel.

Let

\[
A:S\to S
\]

be an intermediate-consumption flow. In components,

\[
A^{i}{}_{j}
\]

denotes the flow of sector-\(i\) output required by sector-\(j\) production.

Let

\[
d:\varepsilon\to S
\]

be final demand, and let

\[
x:\varepsilon\to S
\]

be total output. The basic input-output equation is

\[
x=d+A\circ x.
\]

In components,

\[
x^{i}=d^{i}+A^{i}{}_{j}x^{j}.
\]

Here the repeated index \(j\) is structurally contracted.

---

### 8.1 Leontief inverse as a flow series

Iterating,

\[
\begin{aligned}
x
&=d+A\circ x
\\
&=d+A\circ(d+A\circ x)
\\
&=d+A\circ d+A^{2}\circ x
\\
&=d+A\circ d+A^{2}\circ d+\cdots+A^{n}\circ d+A^{n+1}\circ x.
\end{aligned}
\]

If the endoflow algebra is a Banach algebra and the spectral radius satisfies

\[
\rho(A)<1,
\]

then

\[
A^{n+1}\circ x\to 0.
\]

Hence

\[
x
=
\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A^{k}\right)\circ d.
\]

Define the Leontief flow

\[
L:=( \mathbf 1_{S}-A)^{-1}
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A^{k}.
\]

Then

\[
x=L\circ d.
\]

In components,

\[
x^{i}=L^{i}{}_{j}d^{j}.
\]

The multiplier tensor is

\[
M^{i}{}_{j}=L^{i}{}_{j}.
\]

This derivation uses only composition, addition, and convergence in an endoflow algebra. No underlying set of firms or households is required.

---

### 8.2 Circular flow and trace

Let

\[
M:S\to C
\]

be a monetary flow, where \(C\) is a claims or money interface, and let

\[
B:C\to S
\]

be an expenditure flow returning money to sectors. The composite

\[
T:=B\circ M:S\to S
\]

is a closed circular flow.

A purely circular economy with no external demand satisfies

\[
x=T\circ x.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
(\mathbf 1_{S}-T)\circ x=0.
\]

In a finite-dimensional linear realization, a nonzero self-sustaining flow exists precisely when

\[
\det(\mathbf 1_{S}-T)=0.
\]

The aggregate circulation intensity may be measured by the trace

\[
\operatorname{Circ}(T):=\operatorname{Tr}_{S}(T).
\]

In components,

\[
\operatorname{Circ}(T)=T^{i}{}_{i}.
\]

Thus macroeconomic circulation becomes a trace over an endoflow.

---

### 8.3 Firms as idempotent flow structures

A firm is not primitive. It may be modeled as a stabilized subnetwork

\[
E:X\to X
\]

satisfying approximately or exactly

\[
E\circ E=E.
\]

The firm then appears as an idempotent objectification of flows of labor, capital, commodities, and money. Its “boundary” is the interface \(X\); its persistence is the idempotent law \(E^{2}=E\).

---

## 9. Application II: Information Systems

Information systems are naturally flow-theoretic. Schemas, message types, APIs, databases, and services are less fundamental than the transformations between them.

Let

\[
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})
\]

and

\[
B=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
\]

be schema interfaces. A flow

\[
F:A\to B
\]

may represent an ETL process, a query, a view, a protocol adapter, or a service transformation.

If

\[
F:(\text{user},\text{cart})\to\text{order},
\]

\[
G:\text{order}\to\text{payment},
\]

\[
H:\text{payment}\to\text{receipt},
\]

then the full pipeline is

\[
H\circ G\circ F:
(\text{user},\text{cart})
\to
\text{receipt}.
\]

In components,

\[
(H\circ G\circ F)^{r}_{u,c}
=
H^{r}_{p}G^{p}_{o}F^{o}_{u,c}.
\]

Again, the repeated indices \(o,p\) denote structural contraction.

---

### 9.1 Relational and probabilistic realizations

If flows are realized as relations, composition becomes existential contraction:

\[
(G\circ F)^{z}_{x}
=
\exists y\,
\bigl(G^{z}_{y}\wedge F^{y}_{x}\bigr).
\]

If flows are stochastic matrices or Markov kernels, composition becomes summation or integration:

\[
(G\circ F)^{z}_{x}
=
\sum_{y}G^{z}_{y}F^{y}_{x},
\]

or

\[
(G\circ F)^{z}_{x}
=
\int G^{z}_{y}F^{y}_{x}\,dy.
\]

But ASF does not require these realizations. They are models of the same underlying flow algebra.

---

### 9.2 Parallel services

If

\[
F:A\to B
\]

and

\[
G:C\to D
\]

are independent services, their parallel composition is

\[
F\otimes G:A\otimes C\to B\otimes D.
\]

This represents simultaneous execution.

For example,

\[
F:\text{order}\to\text{invoice},
\qquad
G:\text{user}\to\text{profile},
\]

gives

\[
F\otimes G:
\text{order}\otimes\text{user}
\to
\text{invoice}\otimes\text{profile}.
\]

The symmetry

\[
\sigma_{\text{order},\text{user}}
\]

reorders message channels.

---

### 9.3 Schema migration compatibility

Let

\[
v_A:A_{1}\to A_{2},
\qquad
v_B:B_{1}\to B_{2}
\]

be version-migration flows. Let

\[
F_{1}:A_{1}\to B_{1},
\qquad
F_{2}:A_{2}\to B_{2}
\]

be transformations at two versions.

Compatibility is the flow equation

\[
v_B\circ F_{1}=F_{2}\circ v_A.
\]

In tensor notation,

\[
(v_B)^{B_{2}}_{B_{1}}
(F_{1})^{B_{1}}_{A_{1}}
=
(F_{2})^{B_{2}}_{A_{2}}
(v_A)^{A_{2}}_{A_{1}}.
\]

In a linear ASF, the migration defect is

\[
D:=v_B\circ F_{1}-F_{2}\circ v_A.
\]

A system is migration-coherent when

\[
D=0.
\]

Thus schema evolution becomes an equation in the algebra of structural flows.

---

## 10. Application III: Dynamical Systems

Dynamical systems are usually presented as maps on a state space. ASF instead treats evolution itself as primary.

Let \(S\) be a state interface. A non-autonomous dynamical flow is a family

\[
\Phi_{t,s}:S\to S,
\qquad s\leq t,
\]

satisfying the cocycle law

\[
\Phi_{u,t}\circ\Phi_{t,s}
=
\Phi_{u,s}.
\]

In components,

\[
(\Phi_{u,t})^{i}{}_{j}
(\Phi_{t,s})^{j}{}_{k}
=
(\Phi_{u,s})^{i}{}_{k}.
\]

The intermediate state interface \(S\) is contracted.

---

### 10.1 Autonomous semigroups

For an autonomous system, write

\[
\Phi_t:=\Phi_{t,0}.
\]

Then

\[
\Phi_{t+s}=\Phi_t\circ\Phi_s,
\qquad
\Phi_0=\mathbf 1_S.
\]

Thus the dynamics is a one-parameter submonoid of \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal A}(S)\).

---

### 10.2 Infinitesimal generator

Assume \(\mathcal A\) is a Banach ASF and \(t\mapsto\Phi_t\) is differentiable. Since

\[
\Phi_{t+h}=\Phi_h\circ\Phi_t,
\]

we have

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_t
=
\lim_{h\to 0}
\frac{\Phi_h-\mathbf 1_S}{h}\circ\Phi_t.
\]

Define the generator

\[
L:=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_t\right|_{t=0}
=
\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\Phi_h-\mathbf 1_S}{h}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_t
=
L\circ\Phi_t.
\]

Equivalently, using the other semigroup ordering,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_t
=
\Phi_t\circ L.
\]

The solution is the flow exponential

\[
\Phi_t
=
\exp(tL)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}L^{n}.
\]

This is an entirely endoflow-algebraic expression.

---

### 10.3 Tensor product dynamics

Let a composite system have interface

\[
S=S^{(1)}\otimes S^{(2)}.
\]

If the subsystems evolve independently, then

\[
\Phi_t
=
\Phi^{(1)}_t\otimes\Phi^{(2)}_t.
\]

At the infinitesimal level,

\[
L
=
L_{1}\otimes\mathbf 1
+
\mathbf 1\otimes L_{2}.
\]

With interaction, one adds an interaction flow

\[
\Lambda:S^{(1)}\otimes S^{(2)}
\to
S^{(1)}\otimes S^{(2)},
\]

so that

\[
L
=
L_{1}\otimes\mathbf 1
+
\mathbf 1\otimes L_{2}
+
\Lambda.
\]

The full flow is

\[
\Phi_t=\exp\bigl(t(L_{1}\otimes\mathbf 1+\mathbf 1\otimes L_{2}+\Lambda)\bigr).
\]

If \(\Lambda\) is small, perturbation theory gives the Dyson-type expansion

\[
\Phi_t
=
e^{tL_0}
+
\int_{0}^{t}
e^{(t-s)L_0}
\circ
\Lambda
\circ
e^{sL_0}
\,ds
+
\cdots,
\]

where

\[
L_0:=L_{1}\otimes\mathbf 1+\mathbf 1\otimes L_{2}.
\]

Again, the formalism requires only flows and their compositions.

---

### 10.4 Koopman and transfer flows

In Koopman theory, one studies the induced action on observables. Let \(\mathcal O\) be an observable interface. The Koopman flow is

\[
U_t:\mathcal O\to\mathcal O,
\]

satisfying

\[
U_{t+s}=U_t\circ U_s.
\]

In a point-set realization,

\[
(U_t f)(x)=f(\varphi_t(x)),
\]

but ASF does not require the state space \(X\) or the point map \(\varphi_t\). The operator flow \(U_t\) is primary.

Similarly, a Perron–Frobenius or Markov transfer flow

\[
P_t:\mathcal M\to\mathcal M
\]

acts on measures, densities, or distributional channels. Its composition law is

\[
P_{t+s}=P_t\circ P_s.
\]

Thus deterministic, stochastic, quantum, and observable-level dynamics all fit the same flow-algebraic pattern.

## 11. Application IV: Category Theory

ASF may be viewed as a recentering of category theory.

A conventional category consists of:

1. a collection of objects;
2. a collection of morphisms;
3. source and target assignments;
4. composition;
5. identities.

ASF begins instead with:

1. interfaces as boundary specifications;
2. flows as primitive transformations;
3. sequential composition;
4. parallel composition;
5. identities as passive flows.

Objects are then recovered as identity flows.

---

### 11.1 Special cases

Several standard structures appear as special cases of ASF.

#### Monoids

If there is only one interface \(\varepsilon\), then

\[
\mathcal A(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
\]

is a monoid under composition. If the ASF is \(R\)-linear, it is an \(R\)-algebra.

#### Categories

If tensor product is ignored, an ASF is essentially a category whose objects are interfaces.

#### Strict symmetric monoidal categories

With tensor and symmetry included, an ASF is the morphism-level algebra of a strict symmetric monoidal category.

#### PROPs and operads

If the interface monoid is generated by a single sort and tensor product corresponds to addition of natural numbers, one obtains a PROP-like structure. Restricting to one-output interfaces recovers operadic composition.

Thus ASF does not reject category theory; it extracts its transformational core.

---

### 11.2 Higher flows

A natural extension introduces 2-flows:

\[
\alpha:F\Rightarrow G,
\]

where \(F,G:X\to Y\). These represent transformations between transformations. Iterating yields higher structural flow algebras, related to 2-categories, bicategories, and \(\infty\)-categories, but with the same principle: transformations precede objects.

---

## 12. Model Realizations

Although ASF does not require point sets, many familiar mathematical structures provide models.

| Model | Interface interpretation | Flow realization | Composition |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(\mathbf{Set}\) | lists of sets | functions | ordinary composition |
| \(\mathbf{Rel}\) | lists of sets | relations | relational composition |
| \(\mathbf{Mat}_R\) | finite free \(R\)-modules | matrices | matrix multiplication |
| \(\mathbf{Stoch}\) | measurable spaces | Markov kernels | Chapman–Kolmogorov composition |
| \(\mathbf{Ban}\) | Banach spaces | bounded operators | operator composition |
| \(\mathbf{Hilb}\) | Hilbert spaces | bounded linear operators | operator composition |
| \(\mathbf{Proc}\) | input-output ports | processes | process composition |

In \(\mathbf{Mat}_R\), for example, a flow

\[
F:X\to Y
\]

is represented by a matrix

\[
F^{i}{}_{j},
\]

sequential composition is matrix multiplication,

\[
(G\circ F)^{i}{}_{k}
=
G^{i}{}_{j}F^{j}{}_{k},
\]

and parallel composition is the Kronecker product,

\[
(F\otimes G)^{(i,a)}{}_{(j,b)}
=
F^{i}{}_{j}G^{a}{}_{b}.
\]

These are useful semantics, but they are not the foundation of ASF.

## 13. Discussion: The Ontology of Flows

The Algebra of Structural Flows proposes a processual mathematical ontology. An object is not a substance underlying change; it is a stable pattern within change.

Three mechanisms of objectification are especially important:

1. **Identity objectification.**  
   An object is the identity flow \(\mathbf 1_X\).

2. **Idempotent objectification.**  
   An object is a self-stabilizing flow \(p\) with \(p^2=p\).

3. **Invariant objectification.**  
   An object is a substructure preserved by a flow \(T\), expressed by \(T\circ p=p\circ T\).

This does not eliminate objects. It explains how objects can arise from transformational laws.

The classical picture is:

\[
\text{objects} \longrightarrow \text{maps}.
\]

The ASF picture is:

\[
\text{flows} \longrightarrow \text{stable interfaces} \longrightarrow \text{objects}.
\]

The equation

\[
F_3=F_2\circ F_1
\]

is therefore not merely compositional notation. It is the primary structural law.

---

## 14. Conclusion

The Algebra of Structural Flows provides a rigorous framework for reasoning about transformations without first committing to underlying point sets. Its axioms are equational, its calculus is tensorial, and its semantics are flexible. Sequential composition gives the basic law

\[
F_3=F_2\circ F_1.
\]

Parallel composition gives the tensor law

\[
F\otimes G:X\otimes U\to Y\otimes V.
\]

Objects emerge as identities, idempotents, and invariant subflows. The framework applies uniformly to economic circulation, information pipelines, dynamical evolution, and categorical structure.

The central claim is therefore established:

\[
\boxed{\text{Flows are primary; objects are secondary.}}
\]

## Appendix A: Axioms in Compact Form

An Algebra of Structural Flows consists of:

\[
\mathcal A(X,Y),
\]

with operations

\[
\circ:\mathcal A(Y,Z)\times\mathcal A(X,Y)\to\mathcal A(X,Z),
\]

\[
\otimes:\mathcal A(X,Y)\times\mathcal A(U,V)\to\mathcal A(XU,YV),
\]

\[
\mathbf 1_X\in\mathcal A(X,X),
\]

\[
\sigma_{X,Y}\in\mathcal A(XY,YX),
\]

satisfying:

\[
H\circ(G\circ F)=(H\circ G)\circ F,
\]

\[
\mathbf 1_Y\circ F=F=F\circ\mathbf 1_X,
\]

\[
\mathbf 1_X\otimes\mathbf 1_Y=\mathbf 1_{XY},

\[
(G\circ F)\otimes(L\circ K)=(G\otimes L)\circ(F\otimes K),

\sigma_{Y,X}\circ\sigma_{X,Y}=\mathbf 1_{XY},

\[
\sigma_{Y,V}\circ(F\otimes G)=(G\otimes F)\circ\sigma_{X,U},

together with the standard symmetry coherence laws.
