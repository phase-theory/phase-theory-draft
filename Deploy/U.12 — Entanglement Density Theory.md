# Abstract

Quantum entanglement is one of the defining features of quantum theory. In conventional quantum field theory, entanglement is typically treated as a property of states, subsystems, or correlation functions. Entanglement entropy measures information shared between regions, while mutual information and related quantities characterize nonlocal quantum structure.

In finite-density quantum field theory, however, entanglement acquires additional significance. Density, temperature, chemical potential, and many-body interactions continuously reshape the organization of quantum correlations. Existing approaches generally treat these variables as external parameters influencing entanglement.

Unified Finite-Density Quantum Field Theory (UFD-QFT) proposes a stronger principle: entanglement itself is a dynamical field defined over density space. Rather than merely measuring quantum correlations, entanglement becomes a geometric object with its own density-space dynamics, conservation laws, curvature structure, and phase organization.

This document develops Entanglement Density Theory (EDT), providing a framework in which entanglement forms a continuous field over thermodynamic geometry and acts as a fundamental degree of freedom governing dense quantum matter.
