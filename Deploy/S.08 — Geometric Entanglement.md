Abstract

Entanglement is one of the defining features of quantum mechanics. Conventionally, entanglement describes non-classical correlations between quantum systems represented within a Hilbert space. Over the past several decades, developments in quantum information theory, quantum gravity, holography, tensor networks, and emergent spacetime research have increasingly suggested that geometry and entanglement may be deeply connected.

This paper develops the theory of Geometric Entanglement (GE), extending the concept of quantum entanglement from matter-based degrees of freedom to geometric degrees of freedom. Within this framework, curvature states, topological sectors, boundary structures, geometric defects, connectivity networks, and geometric qubits may become entangled resources.

Geometric Entanglement provides a mathematical and conceptual bridge between quantum information and geometry. It establishes a framework in which geometric correlations become measurable informational resources capable of storing, transmitting, and processing information. The resulting theory forms a foundational component of geometric computation, geometric communication, holographic information processing, and emergent spacetime architectures.

Conclusions

Geometric Entanglement extends the concept of quantum correlations beyond matter and field degrees of freedom to encompass geometry itself. Within this framework, curvature states, topological sectors, defect structures, boundary configurations, and geometric qubits may participate in entangled informational relationships.

The theory provides a unified language connecting quantum information, topology, holography, emergent geometry, and geometric computation. Geometric Entanglement becomes the fundamental resource underlying geometric communication, geometric networking, geometric memories, and geometric quantum processors.

Most significantly, the framework suggests that geometry and information may not be separate entities. Instead, geometry may arise from informational correlations, while information may be encoded directly within geometric structure. This duality forms one of the central themes of the Geometric Qubit corpus and serves as a foundation for subsequent developments in geometric decoherence, geometric channels, geometric error correction, and quantum-geometric architectures.
