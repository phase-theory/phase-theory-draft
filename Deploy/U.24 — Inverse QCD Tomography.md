# Abstract

The central challenge of finite-density Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) is not merely predicting the properties of dense matter, but determining the actual structure realized in nature. Direct laboratory access to neutron-star core densities remains impossible, leaving astrophysical observations as the primary source of information about the high-density QCD landscape.

Traditional approaches seek to infer an equation of state from observations of neutron-star masses, radii, tidal deformabilities, cooling histories, and merger remnants. While successful, such methods typically reconstruct only thermodynamic functions rather than the deeper geometric organization underlying dense matter.

Unified Finite-Density Quantum Field Theory (UFD-QFT) introduces a broader framework: **Inverse QCD Tomography**. The objective is not simply to infer an equation of state, but to reconstruct the density manifold itself. Observable astrophysical signals become projections of thermodynamic geometry, information geometry, topological structure, attractor basins, and phase transitions.

This document develops the mathematical foundations of inverse density-manifold reconstruction and establishes a formal framework for recovering the internal architecture of dense QCD from astronomical data.
