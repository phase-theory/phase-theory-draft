Abstract

The Einstein field equations admit exact solutions sourced by vacuum, perfect fluids, electromagnetic fields, scalar fields, and isolated radiative systems. Pure null radiation occupies a singular position among these sectors: its stress-energy tensor possesses rank one, vanishing invariant norm, and propagation constrained to null characteristics. Existing exact solutions overwhelmingly rely upon symmetry assumptions, most notably spherical symmetry in radiating Vaidya geometries or plane-wave reductions.

The objective of this work is to formulate the unrestricted Einstein–null-fluid problem:

[
G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi T_{ab},
]

with matter content constrained exclusively to pure radiation,

[
T_{ab}=\rho,k_a k_b,
\qquad
g_{ab}k^a k^b=0,
]

without symmetry assumptions.

Part I develops the variational foundations of the theory. Beginning from a constrained action principle, the null-fluid stress tensor is derived directly from metric variation. Conservation laws are obtained from diffeomorphism invariance and shown to imply propagation of radiation along null geodesic congruences. Local existence conditions are formulated as a characteristic initial-value problem posed on null hypersurfaces.

Throughout, units (c=G=1) and metric signature ((-+++)) are used.
