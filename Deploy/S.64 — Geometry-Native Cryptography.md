Abstract

Cryptography determines how information remains protected while remaining usable.

Classical cryptography organizes security through keys, algorithms, computational hardness, authentication, and controlled disclosure. Quantum cryptography extends this framework using quantum states, uncertainty, entanglement, and information-theoretic guarantees.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ information

→ communication

→ distributed computation

→ consensus.

A further question emerges:

If information becomes geometry—

what becomes of secrecy?

This paper develops Geometry-Native Cryptography (GNC): a speculative theoretical framework in which cryptographic protection is represented as controlled accessibility across computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting cryptography as concealment through symbolic transformation, the framework interprets security as regulation of admissible access paths across organized computational manifolds.

The central proposal:

Secrets are not hidden objects.

Secrets are inaccessible regions.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than a practical cryptographic replacement or a proposal to supersede modern security standards.
