Abstract

Processing units traditionally accelerate computation through specialization.

Classical CPUs optimize sequential execution.

Conventional GPUs optimize massively parallel execution.

A further question emerges:

If computation becomes organized geometry—

This paper develops Geometric Processing Units (GPUs): a speculative theoretical framework in which computational acceleration is represented through structured accessibility, coordinated parallel organization, and execution geometry.

This framework does not propose a replacement for graphics processors, existing accelerators, or practical quantum hardware.

Instead, it studies abstract computational organization in which throughput emerges from geometry.

The central proposal:

Acceleration is not only faster execution.

Acceleration may also be represented as improved accessibility organization.

This document presents conceptual research rather than a realizable processor architecture.
