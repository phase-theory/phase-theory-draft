Abstract

Archives determine whether information survives beyond immediate computation.

Classical archival systems preserve information through replication, indexing, media durability, redundancy, migration, and recoverability. Digital archives extend this through distributed storage, integrity verification, metadata systems, and fault tolerance. Quantum information introduces additional considerations including coherence, state reconstruction, and persistent encoding.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ communication

→ computation

→ security

→ memory

→ defect stabilization.

A further question emerges:

If memory becomes persistent geometry—

how is information preserved across arbitrarily long organizational timescales?

This paper develops Topological Archives (TA): a speculative theoretical framework in which archival preservation is represented as maintenance of recoverable accessibility through persistent computational topology.

Rather than interpreting archives as collections of stored records, the framework interprets archives as stabilized organizational structures designed for long-term reconstruction.

The central proposal:

Archives are not collections.

Archives are persistent recoverability.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an archival technology proposal or experimentally validated storage method.
