# Carry Geometry: A Tensorial Number Theory

**Preprint — July 30, 2026**

**Mathematics Subject Classification.** 11A05, 11A41, 11D85, 53B05, 39A12.  
**Keywords.** prime valuations, carry tensor, discrete connection, arithmetic curvature, successor field, \(p\)-adic residues.

---

## Abstract

This paper introduces **carry geometry**, a new tensorial number theory in which positive integers are treated as points of a discrete arithmetic manifold whose canonical coordinates are their prime valuations. In this framework, multiplication becomes a flat translation on a free prime-exponent lattice, while addition becomes a nonlinear map whose failure to be coordinatewise linear is encoded by a **carry tensor**. We define a successor field \(S^p(n)=v_p(n+1)\), a discrete torsion-free arithmetic connection
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}(n)=D_qD_r S^p(n),
\]
and an associated curvature tensor. The central structural result is that the linear part of arithmetic curvature vanishes identically, so that all curvature arises from the noncommutativity of carry-propagation matrices. We prove the carry decomposition theorem, the finite-jet theorem, the associativity cocycle, and the curvature-commutator theorem. The resulting formalism recasts classical divisibility phenomena in geometric language and suggests a curvature-based program for studying primes, gaps, and additive irregularity.

## 1. Introduction

Classical number theory is built upon two incompatible operations. Multiplication is transparent after prime factorization:
\[
n=\prod_{p}p^{x^p(n)},
\qquad
x^p(n)=v_p(n),
\]
so that
\[
v_p(nm)=v_p(n)+v_p(m).
\]
Addition, however, is not coordinatewise in the prime-exponent coordinates:
\[
v_p(n+m)\neq v_p(n)+v_p(m)
\]
in general. The obstruction is the **carry**. In ordinary arithmetic, carries are treated as algorithmic artifacts of a chosen base. In this paper, we promote carries to primary geometric objects.

The central thesis is:

> **Addition is a nonlinear transport on the prime-exponent manifold, and carries are its curvature.**

This produces a new number theory, distinct from classical analytic number theory, algebraic number theory, and \(p\)-adic analysis. Those subjects study numbers through external analytic or algebraic structures. Carry geometry studies the internal tensorial obstruction generated by the interaction of addition and prime factorization.

The basic objects are:

1. The **arithmetic manifold** \(\mathcal A\), whose points are positive integers.
2. The **prime-coordinate chart**
   \[
   n\longmapsto x(n)=(x^p(n))_{p\in\mathcal P},
   \qquad
   x^p(n)=v_p(n).
   \]
3. The **addition map**
   \[
   A^p(n,m)=x^p(n+m).
   \]
4. The **carry tensor**
   \[
   C^p(n,m)=A^p(n,m)-\min\{x^p(n),x^p(m)\}.
   \]
5. The **successor field**
   \[
   S^p(n)=v_p(n+1).
   \]
6. The **arithmetic connection**
   \[
   \Gamma^p{}_{qr}(n)=D_qD_rS^p(n),
   \]
   where \(D_q f(n)=f(qn)-f(n)\).
7. The **curvature tensor**
   \[
   R^p{}_{qrs}
   =
   D_r\Gamma^p{}_{sq}
   -
   D_s\Gamma^p{}_{rq}
   +
   \Gamma^m{}_{sq}\Gamma^p{}_{rm}
   -
   \Gamma^m{}_{rq}\Gamma^p{}_{sm}.
   \]

The main theorem is that the linear part of \(R^p{}_{qrs}\) vanishes identically:
\[
D_r\Gamma^p{}_{sq}-D_s\Gamma^p{}_{rq}=0,
\]
and therefore
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}
=
\Gamma^m{}_{sq}\Gamma^p{}_{rm}
-
\Gamma^m{}_{rq}\Gamma^p{}_{sm}.
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
(M_r)^p{}_m=\Gamma^p{}_{rm},
\]
then
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}=(M_rM_s-M_sM_r)^p{}_q.
\]
Thus arithmetic curvature is precisely the noncommutativity of carry propagation.

This paper develops the axioms, fundamental theorems, examples, and first applications of the theory.

## 2. Axiomatic Foundations

Let \(\mathbb N^+=\{1,2,3,\dots\}\). Let \(\mathcal P\) denote the set of rational primes. We write \(p,q,r,s\) as indices ranging over \(\mathcal P\), and we use Einstein summation over repeated prime indices whenever the relevant tensors have finite support.

### Axiom 1: Arithmetic substrate

The primitive structure is
\[
(\mathbb N^+, +, \times, 1).
\]

### Axiom 2: Prime valuation coordinates

For each prime \(p\), define
\[
v_p:\mathbb N^+\to \mathbb N
\]
by
\[
v_p(n)=\max\{k\in\mathbb N:p^k\mid n\}.
\]
The fundamental theorem of arithmetic gives a bijection
\[
\nu:\mathbb N^+\longrightarrow \Lambda,
\qquad
\nu(n)=(v_p(n))_{p\in\mathcal P},
\]
where
\[
\Lambda=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}\mathbb N e_p
\]
is the free commutative monoid of finite-support prime-exponent vectors.

### Axiom 3: Unit jets

For each prime \(p\) and integer \(k\ge 1\), define the \(p\)-adic unit jet
\[
u_{p,k}(n)
=
p^{-v_p(n)}n
\pmod{p^k}.
\]
When \(v_p(n)=0\), this is simply
\[
u_{p,k}(n)\equiv n\pmod{p^k}.
\]
The full unit jet is the compatible system
\[
u_p(n)=(u_{p,k}(n))_{k\ge 1}\in\mathbb Z_p^\times.
\]

### Axiom 4: Prime-direction difference operators

For each prime \(q\), define a discrete difference operator on functions
\[
f:\mathbb N^+\to \mathbb Z
\]
by
\[
(D_q f)(n)=f(qn)-f(n).
\]
For composite \(d\), define
\[
D_d f(n)=f(dn)-f(n).
\]

### Axiom 5: Tensor convention

The tangent module at \(n\) is the free \(\mathbb Z\)-module
\[
T_n\mathcal A=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}\mathbb Z\,\partial_p.
\]
The coordinate functions satisfy
\[
D_q x^p=\delta_q^p,
\]
where
\[
\delta_q^p=
\begin{cases}
1,&p=q,\\
0,&p\neq q.
\end{cases}
\]

We use upper indices for contravariant components and lower indices for covariant or directional indices.

## 3. The Arithmetic Manifold

### 3.1 Prime-exponent coordinates

Define
\[
x^p(n)=v_p(n).
\]
Then
\[
n=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{x^p(n)},
\]
with only finitely many nonzero \(x^p(n)\).

Multiplication is linear in these coordinates:
\[
x^p(nm)=x^p(n)+x^p(m).
\]
Thus the multiplicative monoid \((\mathbb N^+,\times)\) is identified with the flat lattice monoid \((\Lambda,+)\).

Addition, however, induces a nonlinear operation
\[
\boxplus:\Lambda\times\Lambda\to\Lambda
\]
defined by
\[
x(n)\boxplus x(m)=x(n+m).
\]
The entire difficulty of additive number theory is encoded in the nonlinearity of \(\boxplus\).

### 3.2 Tangent module and metric

The prime directions \(\partial_p\) form a global frame. A discrete vector field is a finite-support expression
\[
X=X^p(n)\partial_p.
\]

We equip \(T_n\mathcal A\) with the canonical diagonal metric
\[
g_{pq}=(\log p)^2\delta_{pq},
\]
with inverse
\[
g^{pq}=\frac{\delta^{pq}}{(\log p)^2}.
\]
This metric measures multiplicative size: if \(n=\prod p^{x^p}\), then
\[
\log n=x^p\log p.
\]
The metric is not the main object of the theory; its role is to permit index raising and lowering in the usual tensorial manner.

For example,
\[
\Gamma_{pqr}=g_{pm}\Gamma^m{}_{qr}.
\]

## 4. The Carry Tensor

Define the coordinate components of addition by
\[
A^p(n,m)=x^p(n+m)=v_p(n+m).
\]
The ultrametric inequality gives
\[
v_p(n+m)\ge \min\{v_p(n),v_p(m)\}.
\]
Therefore we may decompose
\[
A^p(n,m)=\min\{x^p(n),x^p(m)\}+C^p(n,m),
\]
where
\[
C^p(n,m)
=
v_p(n+m)-\min\{v_p(n),v_p(m)\}.
\]

We call \(C^p(n,m)\) the **carry tensor** of the pair \((n,m)\) in the prime direction \(p\).

### Theorem 4.1: Carry decomposition

Let
\[
\alpha=v_p(n),
\qquad
\beta=v_p(m),
\]
and write
\[
n=p^\alpha u,
\qquad
m=p^\beta v,
\]
where \(u,v\in\mathbb Z_p^\times\). Then
\[
C^p(n,m)=
\begin{cases}
0, & \alpha\neq\beta,\\[4pt]
v_p(u+v), & \alpha=\beta.
\end{cases}
\]

Equivalently, if \(\alpha=\beta\),
\[
C^p(n,m)
=
\sum_{k\ge 1}
\mathbf 1_{\,u+v\equiv 0\pmod{p^k}}.
\]

#### Proof

If \(\alpha<\beta\), then
\[
n+m=p^\alpha\left(u+p^{\beta-\alpha}v\right).
\]
Since \(\beta-\alpha\ge 1\), the term \(p^{\beta-\alpha}v\) is divisible by \(p\), while \(u\) is a \(p\)-adic unit. Hence
\[
u+p^{\beta-\alpha}v\in\mathbb Z_p^\times,
\]
so
\[
v_p(n+m)=\alpha.
\]
Thus \(C^p(n,m)=0\). The case \(\alpha>\beta\) is identical.

If \(\alpha=\beta\), then
\[
n+m=p^\alpha(u+v),
\]
so
\[
v_p(n+m)=\alpha+v_p(u+v).
\]
Therefore
\[
C^p(n,m)=v_p(u+v).
\]
The indicator formula follows from the identity
\[
v_p(w)=\sum_{k\ge 1}\mathbf 1_{\,p^k\mid w}.
\]
∎

### Corollary 4.2: Carry locality

The value of \(C^p(n,m)\) is determined by the relative size of \(v_p(n)\) and \(v_p(m)\), together with the \(p\)-adic unit jets of \(n\) and \(m\). It is not determined by the exponent coordinates \(x^p(n),x^p(m)\) alone.

This is the first structural distinction between carry geometry and ordinary lattice geometry.

## 5. The Successor Field and Arithmetic Connection

The simplest additive translation is the successor map
\[
\sigma(n)=n+1.
\]
Its coordinate components are
\[
S^p(n)=x^p(n+1)=v_p(n+1).
\]
We call \(S^p\) the **successor field**.

Since \(v_p(1)=0\), we have
\[
S^p(n)=C^p(n,1).
\]
Thus the successor field is the elementary carry field.

### 5.1 Discrete derivatives

For primes \(q,r\), the difference operators commute:
\[
D_qD_r f(n)=D_rD_q f(n).
\]
Indeed,
\[
D_qD_r f(n)
=
f(qrn)-f(qn)-f(rn)+f(n),
\]
which is symmetric in \(q,r\).

Define the first successor gradient
\[
S^p_q(n)=D_qS^p(n)
=
v_p(qn+1)-v_p(n+1).
\]

Define the second successor Hessian
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}(n)
=
D_qD_rS^p(n).
\]
Explicitly,
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}(n)
=
v_p(qrn+1)
-
v_p(qn+1)
-
v_p(rn+1)
+
v_p(n+1).
\]

We call \(\Gamma^p{}_{qr}\) the **arithmetic connection**.

### Theorem 5.1: Symmetry

The arithmetic connection is symmetric in its lower indices:
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}=\Gamma^p{}_{rq}.
\]

#### Proof

This follows immediately from the commutativity of \(D_q\) and \(D_r\):
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}
=
D_qD_rS^p
=
D_rD_qS^p
=
\Gamma^p{}_{rq}.
\]
∎

Thus the connection is torsion-free in the prime frame.

### 5.2 Congruence form of the connection

Using
\[
v_p(N)=\sum_{k\ge 1}\mathbf 1_{\,p^k\mid N},
\]
we obtain the congruence representation
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}(n)
=
\sum_{k\ge 1}
\left[
\mathbf 1_{\,p^k\mid qrn+1}
-
\mathbf 1_{\,p^k\mid qn+1}
-
\mathbf 1_{\,p^k\mid rn+1}
+
\mathbf 1_{\,p^k\mid n+1}
\right].
\]

This expression exhibits \(\Gamma^p{}_{qr}\) as an inclusion-exclusion tensor of congruence conditions.

### 5.3 Connection and torsion

Define a discrete connection on the prime frame by
\[
\nabla_{\partial_q}\partial_r
=
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}\partial_p.
\]
For a vector field \(Y=Y^p\partial_p\), set
\[
\nabla_{\partial_q}Y
=
(D_qY^p)\partial_p
+
Y^r\Gamma^p{}_{qr}\partial_p.
\]

The torsion tensor is
\[
T^p{}_{qr}
=
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}-\Gamma^p{}_{rq}.
\]
By Theorem 5.1,
\[
T^p{}_{qr}=0.
\]

Thus the arithmetic connection is torsion-free.

### 5.4 Curvature

Define the curvature tensor by the commutator of covariant differences:
\[
R(\partial_r,\partial_s)\partial_q
=
\nabla_r\nabla_s\partial_q
-
\nabla_s\nabla_r\partial_q.
\]
In components,
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}
=
D_r\Gamma^p{}_{sq}
-
D_s\Gamma^p{}_{rq}
+
\Gamma^m{}_{sq}\Gamma^p{}_{rm}
-
\Gamma^m{}_{rq}\Gamma^p{}_{sm}.
\]

### Theorem 5.2: Linear flatness and curvature-commutator theorem

The linear part of arithmetic curvature vanishes identically:
\[
D_r\Gamma^p{}_{sq}
-
D_s\Gamma^p{}_{rq}
=
0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}
=
\Gamma^m{}_{sq}\Gamma^p{}_{rm}
-
\Gamma^m{}_{rq}\Gamma^p{}_{sm}.
\]

Equivalently, define the carry-propagation matrix
\[
(M_r)^p{}_m=\Gamma^p{}_{rm}.
\]
Then
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}
=
(M_rM_s-M_sM_r)^p{}_q.
\]

#### Proof

Since
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{sq}=D_sD_qS^p,
\]
we have
\[
D_r\Gamma^p{}_{sq}
=
D_rD_sD_qS^p.
\]
Similarly,
\[
D_s\Gamma^p{}_{rq}
=
D_sD_rD_qS^p.
\]
The operators \(D_r,D_s,D_q\) commute, hence
\[
D_rD_sD_qS^p
=
D_sD_rD_qS^p.
\]
Therefore the linear part vanishes.

The remaining quadratic terms are exactly
\[
\Gamma^m{}_{sq}\Gamma^p{}_{rm}
-
\Gamma^m{}_{rq}\Gamma^p{}_{sm}.
\]
Using
\[
(M_r)^p{}_m=\Gamma^p{}_{rm},
\qquad
(M_s)^m{}_q=\Gamma^m{}_{sq},
\]
we obtain
\[
\Gamma^m{}_{sq}\Gamma^p{}_{rm}
=
(M_rM_s)^p{}_q,
\]
and
\[
\Gamma^m{}_{rq}\Gamma^p{}_{sm}
=
(M_sM_r)^p{}_q.
\]
Thus
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}
=
(M_rM_s-M_sM_r)^p{}_q.
\]
∎

This is the central structural theorem of carry geometry.

### 5.5 Ricci tensor and scalar curvature

Define the arithmetic Ricci tensor by contraction:
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_{qs}
=
R^p{}_{qps}.
\]
The scalar curvature is
\[
\mathcal R
=
g^{qs}\operatorname{Ric}_{qs}.
\]
Because all relevant tensors have finite support at each fixed \(n\), these contractions are finite.

## 6. Higher Carries and the Associativity Cocycle

### 6.1 Higher carry tensor

For a finite family \(n_1,\dots,n_N\), define
\[
C^p(n_1,\dots,n_N)
=
v_p\!\left(\sum_{a=1}^N n_a\right)
-
\min_{1\le a\le N}v_p(n_a).
\]

Let
\[
m_p=\min_a v_p(n_a),
\]
and let
\[
I_p=\{a:v_p(n_a)=m_p\}.
\]
For \(a\in I_p\), write
\[
n_a=p^{m_p}u_a,
\qquad
u_a\in\mathbb Z_p^\times.
\]
For \(b\notin I_p\), write
\[
n_b=p^{m_p+\lambda_b}w_b,
\qquad
\lambda_b\ge 1.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{a=1}^N n_a
=
p^{m_p}
\left(
\sum_{a\in I_p}u_a
+
pW
\right)
\]
for some \(W\in\mathbb Z_p\). Hence
\[
C^p(n_1,\dots,n_N)
=
v_p
\left(
\sum_{a\in I_p}u_a+pW
\right).
\]

In particular,
\[
C^p(n_1,\dots,n_N)=0
\]
if and only if
\[
\sum_{a\in I_p}u_a\not\equiv 0\pmod p.
\]

### Theorem 6.1: First-order carry criterion

Let \(n_1,\dots,n_N\in\mathbb N^+\). For a fixed prime \(p\), the sum \(\sum_a n_a\) has \(p\)-adic valuation strictly larger than \(\min_a v_p(n_a)\) if and only if the residues of the minimally valued terms cancel modulo \(p\).

#### Proof

This follows immediately from the decomposition above. The leading term modulo \(p\) is
\[
\sum_{a\in I_p}u_a.
\]
If this is nonzero modulo \(p\), the valuation is exactly \(m_p\). If it vanishes modulo \(p\), the valuation is at least \(m_p+1\). ∎

### 6.2 Associativity cocycle

Let
\[
F^p(n,m)=v_p(n+m).
\]
Then
\[
F^p(n,m)=\min\{x^p(n),x^p(m)\}+C^p(n,m).
\]

Associativity of integer addition implies
\[
(n+m)+\ell=n+(m+\ell).
\]
Taking \(v_p\) of both sides gives the cocycle identity
\[
\min\{F^p(n,m),x^p(\ell)\}
+
C^p(n+m,\ell)
=
\min\{x^p(n),F^p(m,\ell)\}
+
C^p(n,m+\ell).
\]

This is the **carry associativity cocycle**. It expresses the compatibility of the carry tensor with the associativity of addition.

## 7. Finite-Jet Theorem

For practical computation, one rarely needs the full \(p\)-adic unit. The following theorem gives the finite information required.

### Theorem 7.1: Finite-jet determination

Fix \(K\ge 1\). The truncated carry
\[
C^p_K(n,m)=\min\{C^p(n,m),K\}
\]
is determined by the unit jets
\[
u_{p,K}(n),
\qquad
u_{p,K}(m),
\]
together with the valuations \(v_p(n),v_p(m)\).

#### Proof

If \(v_p(n)\neq v_p(m)\), then \(C^p(n,m)=0\), so the claim is trivial.

If \(v_p(n)=v_p(m)=\alpha\), then
\[
C^p(n,m)=v_p(u+v),
\]
where \(u,v\in\mathbb Z_p^\times\). The truncated valuation \(\min\{v_p(u+v),K\}\) depends only on whether
\[
u+v\equiv 0\pmod{p^k}
\]
for \(1\le k\le K\). These congruences are determined by \(u\bmod p^K\) and \(v\bmod p^K\). ∎

Thus carry geometry is locally finite: every finite carry computation depends on finitely many residue data.

## 8. Examples

### Example 8.1: The carry tensor of \(5+7\)

Let \(n=5\), \(m=7\). Then
\[
5+7=12=2^2\cdot 3.
\]

For \(p=2\):
\[
v_2(5)=0,
\qquad
v_2(7)=0.
\]
The units are \(5\equiv 1\pmod 2\) and \(7\equiv 1\pmod 2\), so
\[
1+1\equiv 0\pmod 2.
\]
Indeed,
\[
v_2(12)=2,
\]
so
\[
C^2(5,7)=2.
\]

For \(p=3\):
\[
v_3(5)=0,
\qquad
v_3(7)=0.
\]
Modulo \(3\),
\[
5\equiv 2,
\qquad
7\equiv 1,
\]
so
\[
2+1\equiv 0\pmod 3.
\]
Since
\[
v_3(12)=1,
\]
we have
\[
C^3(5,7)=1.
\]

For \(p=5\):
\[
v_5(5)=1,
\qquad
v_5(7)=0.
\]
The valuations differ, so
\[
C^5(5,7)=0.
\]

For all \(p\notin\{2,3\}\),
\[
C^p(5,7)=0.
\]

Thus the carry vector is supported at \(2\) and \(3\):
\[
C(5,7)=2\,e_2+1\,e_3.
\]

### Example 8.2: Successor Hessian at \(n=1\)

Let \(n=1\). Consider \(p=2\), \(q=r=2\). Then
\[
\Gamma^2{}_{22}(1)
=
v_2(4\cdot 1+1)
-
2v_2(2\cdot 1+1)
+
v_2(1+1).
\]
Thus
\[
\Gamma^2{}_{22}(1)
=
v_2(5)-2v_2(3)+v_2(2)
=
0-0+1
=
1.
\]

Now take \(p=3\), \(q=r=2\). Then
\[
\Gamma^3{}_{22}(1)
=
v_3(5)-2v_3(3)+v_3(2)
=
0-2\cdot 1+0
=
-2.
\]

Thus the connection coefficients may be negative. This is natural: \(\Gamma\) is a second difference, not a valuation itself.

### Example 8.3: A mixed Hessian

Let \(n=5\), \(p=2\), \(q=2\), \(r=3\). Then
\[
\Gamma^2{}_{23}(5)
=
v_2(2\cdot 3\cdot 5+1)
-
v_2(2\cdot 5+1)
-
v_2(3\cdot 5+1)
+
v_2(5+1).
\]
Compute:
\[
2\cdot 3\cdot 5+1=31,
\qquad
2\cdot 5+1=11,
\]
\[
3\cdot 5+1=16,
\qquad
5+1=6.
\]
Thus
\[
v_2(31)=0,
\qquad
v_2(11)=0,
\]
\[
v_2(16)=4,
\qquad
v_2(6)=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Gamma^2{}_{23}(5)
=
0-0-4+1
=
-3.
\]

## 9. Structural Consequences

### 9.1 Multiplicative scaling invariance

For any \(a,n,m\in\mathbb N^+\),
\[
C^p(an,am)=C^p(n,m).
\]

#### Proof

We have
\[
v_p(an+am)=v_p(a(n+m))=v_p(a)+v_p(n+m).
\]
Also,
\[
\min\{v_p(an),v_p(am)\}
=
v_p(a)+\min\{v_p(n),v_p(m)\}.
\]
Subtracting gives
\[
C^p(an,am)=C^p(n,m).
\]
∎

Thus common multiplicative translation preserves carry. This is the geometric statement that carries are invariant under radial scaling in the prime-exponent lattice.

### 9.2 Multiplication as flat transport

For multiplication,
\[
x^p(nm)=x^p(n)+x^p(m).
\]
There is no multiplicative carry. The multiplicative structure is flat. Addition is the sole source of curvature.

### 9.3 GCD and LCM as lattice operations

In prime coordinates,
\[
v_p(\gcd(n,m))=\min\{v_p(n),v_p(m)\},
\]
and
\[
v_p(\operatorname{lcm}(n,m))=\max\{v_p(n),v_p(m)\}.
\]
Thus \(\gcd\) and \(\operatorname{lcm}\) are coordinatewise lattice operations. The carry tensor measures exactly the failure of addition to reduce to these lattice operations.

Indeed,
\[
v_p(n+m)
=
v_p(\gcd(n,m))
+
C^p(n,m)
\]
whenever \(v_p(n)=v_p(m)\), while if the valuations differ, the carry vanishes.

### 9.4 Prime basis theorem

Let \(e_p\in\Lambda\) denote the vector with a \(1\) in the \(p\)-coordinate and \(0\) elsewhere. Then
\[
n \text{ is prime}
\]
if and only if
\[
x(n)=e_p
\]
for some prime \(p\), with \(n=p\).

Thus primes are precisely the basis vectors of the arithmetic lattice.

### 9.5 Composite numbers as lattice superpositions

A composite number corresponds to a lattice vector of norm greater than one:
\[
x(n)=\sum_p x^p(n)e_p,
\qquad
\sum_p x^p(n)\ge 2.
\]
Multiplication is vector addition; primality is basis purity.

## 10. Relation to Classical Carry Phenomena

In elementary arithmetic, carries are usually defined relative to a base \(b\). For example, in base \(10\), adding digits produces carries across powers of \(10\). Carry geometry refines this by decomposing carries into prime directions.

A base-\(b\) carry is a projection of the full prime carry tensor onto the prime factors of \(b\). If
\[
b=\prod_{p\mid b}p^{a_p},
\]
then the usual base-\(b\) digit carry is a coarse-grained observable of the family
\[
\{C^p(n,m):p\mid b\}.
\]

Thus classical carrying is not eliminated but embedded into a finer prime-tensorial structure.

## 11. \(p\)-adic Interpretation

For fixed \(p\), the successor field
\[
S^p(n)=v_p(n+1)
\]
extends naturally to a function on \(\mathbb Z_p\setminus\{-1\}\):
\[
S^p(x)=v_p(x+1).
\]
This function is locally constant away from \(-1\). The singular behavior at \(x=-1\) corresponds to infinite carry.

The discrete operators
\[
D_q f(n)=f(qn)-f(n)
\]
also extend to \(p\)-adic difference operators when \(q\) is viewed as an element of \(\mathbb Z_p^\times\) for \(q\neq p\). Thus carry geometry admits a local \(p\)-adic analytic interpretation while remaining fundamentally arithmetic.

## 12. Curvature and Prime Irregularity

The distribution of primes is governed by the interaction between additive translation and multiplicative coordinates. In carry geometry, the successor of \(n\) is encoded by
\[
S^p(n)=v_p(n+1).
\]
A number \(n+1\) is prime exactly when its coordinate vector is a basis vector:
\[
x(n+1)=e_p.
\]
Thus primes are points at which the successor field lands on the prime basis.

The connection
\[
\Gamma^p{}_{qr}(n)
\]
measures how the successor carry changes under multiplicative perturbations \(n\mapsto qn\), \(n\mapsto rn\), and \(n\mapsto qrn\). Its curvature measures the noncommutativity of these perturbations.

This suggests a new viewpoint: prime-free intervals are regions in which the successor field repeatedly fails to land on basis vectors, and this failure should be reflected in the statistics of \(\Gamma^p{}_{qr}\) and \(R^p{}_{qrs}\).

We therefore propose the following conjectural program.

### Conjecture 12.1: Curvature suppression and prime gaps

There exists a natural scalar functional \(\mathcal R(n)\), constructed from the arithmetic curvature, such that large prime gaps correspond to intervals of anomalously low or highly degenerate curvature.

More precisely, one expects that if an interval
\[
[N,N+H]
\]
contains no primes, then the averaged curvature
\[
\frac1H\sum_{n=N}^{N+H}\mathcal R(n)
\]
satisfies a structural constraint not satisfied by typical intervals.

This conjecture is not a replacement for known analytic results; it is a proposed new invariant for studying additive irregularity.

### Conjecture 12.2: Equidistribution of carry jets

For fixed distinct primes \(p_1,\dots,p_k\) and fixed \(K\), the joint distribution of the truncated unit jets
\[
u_{p_i,K}(n)
\]
along \(n\le X\) becomes equidistributed in the product of unit groups as \(X\to\infty\), subject to the usual Chinese remainder constraints. Consequently, the truncated carry tensors
\[
C^{p_i}_K(n,m)
\]
should admit a probabilistic model governed by independent local residue cancellations.

This would give a geometric probabilistic model for carries, analogous in spirit but not identical to the Cramér model for primes.

## 13. Open Problems

### Problem 13.1: Classification of flat arithmetic neighborhoods

Determine all finite sets \(U\subset\mathbb N^+\) such that
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}(n)=0
\]
for all \(n\in U\) and all relevant primes \(p,q,r,s\). Equivalently, classify local regions where all carry-propagation matrices commute.

### Problem 13.2: Carry cohomology

Define a cochain complex using the carry associativity cocycle. Investigate whether the carry tensor defines a nontrivial cohomology class on the arithmetic semiring.

### Problem 13.3: Curvature bounds and additive functions

Relate arithmetic curvature to classical additive functions such as
\[
\omega(n),\qquad \Omega(n),\qquad \log n.
\]
For example, determine whether bounds on
\[
\sum_{n\le X}\mathcal R(n)^2
\]
imply nontrivial information about the distribution of \(v_p(n+1)\).

### Problem 13.4: Global field equations

Investigate whether a meaningful arithmetic analogue of a geometric field equation exists, for example an equation of the form
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_{qr}
-
\frac12 g_{qr}\mathcal R
=
T_{qr},
\]
where \(T_{qr}\) is a tensor constructed from prime-counting or additive convolution data.

## 14. Conclusion

Carry geometry reinterprets the most elementary operation of arithmetic, addition, as a nonlinear geometric transport on the prime-exponent manifold. The carry tensor measures the failure of addition to be linear in prime coordinates. The successor field encodes addition by one. The arithmetic connection measures second-order carry interaction under multiplicative perturbations. The curvature tensor measures the noncommutativity of carry propagation.

The central result,
\[
R^p{}_{qrs}
=
(M_rM_s-M_sM_r)^p{}_q,
\]
shows that arithmetic curvature is purely nonlinear: its linear part vanishes identically. All curvature arises from the self-interaction of carries.

This theory does not replace classical number theory. It adds a new structural layer. In the same way that Riemannian geometry reinterprets distance through curvature, carry geometry reinterprets addition through carry curvature. The resulting language is tensorial, local, \(p\)-adically refined, and naturally adapted to the central tension between addition and multiplication.
