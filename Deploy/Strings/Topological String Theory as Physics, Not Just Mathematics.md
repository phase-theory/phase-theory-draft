Abstract

Topological String Theory emerged through the work of Edward Witten as a mathematically simplified sector of superstring theory. The A-model and B-model topological strings were developed primarily as computational tools for enumerative geometry, mirror symmetry, Gromov–Witten theory, Donaldson–Thomas invariants, and Calabi–Yau compactification analysis.

Historically, the flow of information has been one-directional:

Physical string theory → topological reduction → mathematical invariants.

This paper investigates the neglected inverse question:

Can topological string theory itself describe a physically real sector of nature?

We examine whether topological strings represent an independent phase of string dynamics whose observables are purely topological rather than metric-dependent. We argue that existing evidence from black hole entropy, holography, quantum gravity, spacetime emergence, and nonperturbative string theory suggests that the topological sector may be more fundamental than previously assumed.

A central hypothesis is developed:

Physical spacetime may emerge from an underlying topological string phase whose observables are global topological structures rather than local geometric quantities.

The resulting framework predicts new classes of phenomena arising purely from topological sector dynamics and proposes experimental and theoretical tests.

Conclusion

Topological String Theory has traditionally been treated as a mathematical reduction of physical string theory. Yet some of the deepest results in modern theoretical physics—including black-hole entropy, mirror symmetry, holography, and protected state counting—suggest that the topological sector repeatedly captures the essential physical information while geometric details become secondary.

This white paper advances the possibility that the relationship should be reversed. Rather than viewing topological strings as simplified shadows of physical strings, physical spacetime itself may emerge from a deeper topological phase governed by A-model and B-model dynamics. In this view, topology is not merely a computational convenience but a genuine sector of reality, with its own vacuum structure, excitations, phase transitions, cosmological relics, and gravitational implications.

While speculative, the hypothesis is grounded in existing structures already present throughout string theory. If correct, it would imply that the deepest layer of nature is not geometric but topological, and that spacetime, gravity, and matter are emergent manifestations of an underlying topological string phase. Such a shift would transform topological string theory from a mathematical toolkit into a candidate foundation for fundamental physics.
