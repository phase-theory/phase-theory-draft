Abstract

Ambitwistor string theory, introduced by Lionel Mason and David Skinner in 2013, represents one of the most radical reformulations of string theory since the advent of the original dual resonance models. Unlike conventional strings, ambitwistor strings are chiral, tensionless, and propagate not in spacetime itself but in ambitwistor space—the space of null geodesics.

Their principal achievement is extraordinary: they reproduce the complete tree-level S-matrix of gravity and gauge theories through the Cachazo–He–Yuan scattering equations and extend naturally to loop amplitudes. Yet despite these successes, ambitwistor strings are typically regarded merely as computational tools for perturbative amplitudes rather than candidates for a fundamental theory.

This white paper develops a comprehensive physical interpretation of ambitwistor strings as an independent theory of nature. We propose that ambitwistor strings describe a fundamentally null-geometric universe whose primary ontology consists not of particles moving through spacetime but of networks of null trajectories. Conventional spacetime, massive particles, quantum fields, and even standard strings emerge as collective excitations of this deeper null structure.

A central objective is resolving the longstanding ambiguity surrounding the massive sector. We develop a physically intuitive picture in which mass emerges from compactification, self-intersection, topological winding, and null-network condensation. Massive particles are interpreted not as elementary objects but as bound states of fundamentally massless ambitwistor excitations.

The resulting framework suggests that ambitwistor strings may define a genuinely distinct ultraviolet-complete theory rather than merely a tensionless limit of conventional string theory.

Conclusion

Ambitwistor string theory has traditionally been regarded as a remarkable mathematical framework for calculating scattering amplitudes. However, its structure suggests something far more ambitious. By placing null trajectories rather than strings, particles, or spacetime points at the foundation of physics, ambitwistor theory offers a radically different ontology in which geometry, matter, and mass emerge from collective organization within a universal network of lightlike relations.

The unresolved massive sector can be interpreted as a hierarchy of bound, knotted, or condensed configurations of fundamentally massless ambitwistor degrees of freedom. In this picture, ordinary string theory is not the parent theory but a condensed phase of a deeper null-geometric substrate.

If this perspective is correct, ambitwistor strings are not merely a tensionless limit of string theory. They define a distinct candidate for fundamental physics: a theory in which the universe is ultimately composed of interacting null trajectories whose collective behavior gives rise to spacetime, gravity, quantum fields, and the massive world we observe.
