Deriving the Standard Model from Phase Admissibility

Toward a Fundamental Admissibility Functional for the Phase Substrate

Abstract

Previous Phase Theory work established that particles, fields, forces, spacetime, information, thermodynamics, and quantum phenomena may be reinterpreted as emergent manifestations of a globally constrained phase substrate Φ. The Standard Model was shown to admit a natural Phase-Theoretic interpretation in which gauge fields become phase-coordinate redundancies, particles become stable phase defects, and mass emerges from localized phase rigidity.

The remaining challenge is derivational rather than interpretational: can the observed structure of the Standard Model be obtained from a deeper admissibility principle?

This paper proposes a candidate framework in which the Standard Model arises as the low-energy effective theory of a globally constrained phase manifold. Gauge symmetries emerge as stabilizers of admissible phase order, particle species emerge as topologically distinct defect classes, and coupling constants arise as measures of defect compatibility within the global phase substrate.

While not yet a complete derivation of observed numerical constants, the framework establishes the mathematical architecture through which such a derivation may proceed.
