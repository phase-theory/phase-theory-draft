Abstract

The Internet is one of the largest engineered information systems ever constructed.

Classical Internet architectures organize communication through hosts, addresses, routers, protocols, layered abstractions, and packet delivery. Modern systems extend these foundations through software-defined networking, distributed infrastructure, cloud systems, edge coordination, and increasingly adaptive routing.

Across the Structural Sector, computation evolved through:

operators

→ geometry

→ routing

→ networks

→ repeaters

→ channels

→ topology

→ holographic reconstruction.

A further question emerges:

If communication becomes organized accessibility—

what becomes of the Internet itself?

This paper develops Geometric Internet Architectures (GIA): a speculative theoretical framework in which internetworking is represented as structured accessibility across distributed computational geometry.

Rather than interpreting the Internet as interconnected endpoints exchanging packets, the framework interprets global communication as evolving accessibility across organized computational manifolds.

The central proposal:

The Internet is not a collection of connected machines.

It is an evolving geometry of accessible information.

This document presents a speculative theoretical research framework rather than an implementation proposal or replacement for existing Internet standards.
