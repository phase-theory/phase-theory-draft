# Abstract

Black holes represent the most severe conflict between quantum mechanics and general relativity. Classical general relativity predicts smooth event horizons and spacetime interiors terminating at singularities. Quantum mechanics demands unitary evolution and information preservation. String theory offers the only known ultraviolet-complete framework capable of addressing both simultaneously.

The fuzzball program, pioneered by Samir Mathur, proposes that black hole microstates are horizon-scale string/brane configurations with no traditional interior whatsoever. While successful for special supersymmetric systems, the program has never been extended into a complete computational theory of generic black holes.

Simultaneously, the ER=EPR proposal of Juan Maldacena and Leonard Susskind identifies spacetime connectivity with quantum entanglement. However, the conjecture remains largely phenomenological because the entanglement structures have never been systematically derived from string worldsheet dynamics.

This white paper develops a unified computational framework in which:

1. Black hole microstates are explicit worldsheet condensates.
2. Fuzzballs become computable statistical geometries.
3. ER=EPR emerges from string interaction topology.
4. Interior geometry is reconstructed from entanglement networks.
5. Singularities disappear through topological phase transitions.
6. The black hole interior emerges as a coarse-grained collective variable rather than a fundamental spacetime region.

The resulting theory suggests that black hole interiors are not fundamental geometric regions but emergent entanglement manifolds generated by worldsheet connectivity among exponentially many fuzzball microstates.
