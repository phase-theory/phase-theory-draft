# 17. Three-Pion Systems

# 20. Efimov Physics

## 20.1 Introduction

One of the most remarkable phenomena in few-body physics is the appearance of infinitely many three-body bound states despite the absence of corresponding two-body bound states.

This is known as Efimov physics.

It represents a purely three-body quantum effect.

---

[

]

[

\infty.

# Conclusions of Part V

The applications developed in this part demonstrate that finite-volume three-body quantum field theory is not merely a formal framework but a practical tool for extracting physical observables across hadronic, nuclear, and universal few-body systems.

Three-pion systems provide the cleanest laboratory for testing relativistic three-body quantization. Baryon resonances reveal the importance of spin, coupled channels, and resonant substructures. Nuclear systems establish the connection between QCD and few-body nuclear forces. Efimov physics exposes the emergence of universal scale structures generated purely by three-body dynamics.

Together these examples illustrate the central thesis of this volume: finite-volume spectra constitute complete physical observables. Through the exact quantization and reconstruction framework developed in earlier parts, they provide direct access to scattering amplitudes, resonance poles, nuclear forces, and universal three-body phenomena. The next volume extends these principles beyond the three-body sector and develops the general theory of finite-volume many-body quantum field dynamics.
