Traditional computation separates systems into distinct classes:

and adaptive systems.

Across the Structural Sector, these distinctions progressively transformed into increasingly abstract organizational layers:

→ execution environments

→ acceleration fabrics

→ adaptive organization.

A further question emerges:

If information organization becomes sufficiently general—

can processing itself become substrate-independent?

This paper develops Universal Geometric Information Processors (UGIP): a speculative theoretical framework in which information processing is represented as a unified organizational process independent of specialized execution roles.

This framework does not propose a universal computer beyond established computation theory, nor claim physical universality.

Instead, it studies abstract coordination principles in which execution, storage, adaptation, and communication are represented within a common organizational language.

The central proposal:

Processing is not only execution.

Processing may also be represented as structured transformation of accessibility.

This document presents conceptual research rather than a realizable architecture.
