**Phase Field Theory: A Field-Theoretic Foundation for Phase Physics**

*Abstract*

Physical reality is not any single effective description but the full phase diagram: the set of dominant saddles, their transitions, and the universal data invariant across them. Phase Physics is the phase in which a continuous global phase substrate is the dominant effective ontology, giving rise to spacetime geometry, gauge structure, and matter as emergent content. Phase Field Theory formalizes this phase as a field theory proper: an action-level, Lagrangian formulation of the substrate, its gauge structure, its induced coupling to gravity, and its topological sector identified with matter. It treats the substrate dynamically — field quantization on its configuration space, propagators and interaction vertices for its excitations, and a renormalization-group treatment of admissibility constraints across scales. Phase Field Theory is the effective Lagrangian of its saddle: one entry, not the whole, of the invariant phase diagram. Its central open problem is the one common to any such construction — defining a consistent path-integral measure over admissible configurations.

*A phase substrate's laws are the effective Lagrangian of its saddle; the invariant is the full phase diagram in which that saddle sits.*
