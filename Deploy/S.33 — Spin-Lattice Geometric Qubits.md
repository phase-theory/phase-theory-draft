Abstract

Quantum information is often described using localized two-level systems: trapped ions, superconducting circuits, spins, photons, and semiconductor structures. Yet many physical systems naturally organize into interacting spin lattices whose collective behavior generates emergent geometry, topology, protected phases, and distributed information structures.

Spin lattices occupy a central position across condensed matter physics, quantum simulation, magnetic materials, and quantum many-body theory. Rather than viewing the lattice as merely a support for qubits, this paper develops a different perspective:

the lattice itself becomes the information substrate.

This paper introduces Spin-Lattice Geometric Qubits (SLGQ): a theoretical framework in which logical states are encoded into collective spin geometry, lattice organization, topological sectors, and structured many-body evolution.

Logical information is stored not in individual spins but in protected geometric subspaces of lattice configuration space.

This document presents a theoretical research architecture rather than an experimentally established qubit platform.
