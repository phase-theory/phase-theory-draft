# Information Topos Theory

**Preprint**

---

## Abstract

Information Topos Theory (ITT) is a categorical generalization of topos theory in which logical universes are replaced by *information universes*. In ITT, the primitive semantic entities are not truth values but *consistent information states*. Objects are information contexts; morphisms are consistency-preserving transformations; and propositions are interpreted as information-stable subobjects classified by an information classifier \(\Omega_I\) rather than by the ordinary truth-value object \(\Omega\) alone.

This paper develops ITT from first principles. We introduce **information bases**, their states, consistency-preserving approximable morphisms, and a tensorial calculus of independent information contexts. We then define **information sites**, Grothendieck topologies generated by informational covers, and prove that the category of sheaves on an information site is an **information topos**. Its subobject classifier is the sheaf of consistent closed sieves. The internal logic of an information topos is an intuitionistic, tensorially structured logic equipped with an information modality expressing stability under refinement.

Applications are developed in four domains: foundations of mathematics, quantum logic, distributed computation, and knowledge systems. In each case, ITT provides a unified semantic principle: *local consistency glues to global structure precisely when descent holds*.

---

## 1. Introduction

A Grothendieck topos is often described as a generalized universe of sets, and an elementary topos as a categorical universe of logic. In both cases, the subobject classifier

\[
\Omega
\]

plays the role of the universe of truth values. A proposition over an object \(X\) is a morphism

\[
\chi : X \to \Omega,
\]

and logical operations are induced by the internal Heyting algebra structure of \(\Omega\).

Information Topos Theory modifies this picture at its root. Instead of taking truth values as primitive, we take **information states** as primitive. A proposition is not initially something that is true or false; it is something that may be supported, refuted, refined, or rendered inconsistent by further information. Thus the primary semantic object is an **information universe**

\[
\Omega_I,
\]

whose elements are stable, consistent information predicates.

The central principle of ITT is:

> **Logical truth is replaced by informational consistency under refinement.**

This leads to the following conceptual shift.

| Classical topos theory | Information topos theory |
|---|---|
| Objects are generalized spaces or sets | Objects are information contexts |
| Morphisms are structure-preserving maps | Morphisms preserve informational consistency |
| \(\Omega\) classifies subobjects | \(\Omega_I\) classifies consistent information subobjects |
| Logic is internal truth-value semantics | Logic is internal consistency-stability semantics |
| Sheaves glue local truth | Sheaves glue local information |

The theory is motivated by four domains.

1. **Foundations of mathematics.** Mathematical objects are interpreted as stable information structures rather than as static sets.
2. **Quantum logic.** Quantum contexts are maximal compatible families of observables; noncommutativity becomes a failure of global informational consistency.
3. **Distributed computation.** Local node states are information contexts; sheaf descent is precisely the condition under which local consistency yields global consistency.
4. **Knowledge systems.** Belief, revision, and multi-agent knowledge are modeled by consistency-preserving maps between epistemic information bases.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 develops the algebraic theory of information bases and information morphisms. Section 3 introduces information sites and information toposes. Section 4 gives an elementary axiomatization. Section 5 presents the internal tensorial logic. Sections 6–9 develop applications. Section 10 discusses open directions.

## 2. Information Universes

### 2.1 Information bases

The basic algebraic structure underlying ITT is an **information base**. It formalizes the idea that information is given by finite observations, subject to consistency and entailment constraints.

#### Definition 2.1 — Information base

An **information base** is a triple

\[
\mathcal{A} = (|\mathcal{A}|, \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}, \vdash_{\mathcal{A}})
\]

where:

1. \(|\mathcal{A}|\) is a set of **tokens**, or atomic observations;
2. \(\operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}} \subseteq \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(|\mathcal{A}|)\) is a collection of finite **consistent sets**;
3. \(\vdash_{\mathcal{A}} \subseteq \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}} \times |\mathcal{A}|\) is an **entailment relation**.

These data satisfy the following axioms.

For \(X,Y \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(|\mathcal{A}|)\) and \(a \in |\mathcal{A}|\):

1. **Empty consistency**
   \[
   \varnothing \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}.
   \]

2. **Hereditary consistency**
   \[
   X \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}},\; Y \subseteq X
   \implies
   Y \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}.
   \]

3. **Reflexive entailment**
   \[
   X \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}},\; a \in X
   \implies
   X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a.
   \]

4. **Conservative entailment**
   \[
   X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a
   \implies
   X \cup \{a\} \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}.
   \]

5. **Cut**
   If \(X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} b\) for all \(b \in Y\), and
   \[
   X \cup Y \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a,
   \]
   then
   \[
   X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a.
   \]

6. **Monotonicity**
   \[
   X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a,\; X \subseteq Y \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}
   \implies
   Y \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a.
   \]

We write

\[
X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} Y
\]

when \(X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} y\) for every \(y \in Y\).

---

### 2.2 Information states

An information state is a set of tokens that is finitely consistent and closed under entailment.

#### Definition 2.2 — State

A **state** of an information base \(\mathcal{A}\) is a subset

\[
x \subseteq |\mathcal{A}|
\]

such that:

1. every finite subset of \(x\) is consistent:
   \[
   X \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x \implies X \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}};
   \]

2. \(x\) is closed under entailment:
   \[
   X \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x,\; X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a
   \implies
   a \in x.
   \]

The set of states is denoted

\[
\operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A}).
\]

It is ordered by inclusion:

\[
x \leq y \iff x \subseteq y.
\]

Thus larger states contain more information.

For \(X \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}\), define

\[
\overline{X}
=
\{a \in |\mathcal{A}| \mid X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a\}.
\]

#### Proposition 2.3 — States form an algebraic domain

For any information base \(\mathcal{A}\), the poset \(\operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A})\) is an algebraic directed-complete partial order. Its compact elements are the states \(\overline{X}\) for finite consistent \(X\), and every state \(x\) is the directed join

\[
x
=
\bigcup_{X \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x} \overline{X}.
\]

**Proof.**

Let \(x\) be a state. Every finite \(X \subseteq x\) is consistent, so \(\overline{X}\) is defined. By monotonicity and cut, \(\overline{X}\) is a state. The family

\[
\{\overline{X} \mid X \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x\}
\]

is directed because for finite \(X,Y \subseteq x\),

\[
\overline{X}, \overline{Y} \subseteq \overline{X \cup Y}.
\]

Finally, if \(a \in x\), then \(\{a\} \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x\), and reflexivity gives

\[
a \in \overline{\{a\}} \subseteq x.
\]

Conversely, every element of every \(\overline{X}\) is entailed by a finite subset of \(x\), hence belongs to \(x\) by closure. Therefore

\[
x = \bigcup_{X \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x} \overline{X}.
\]

Directed completeness follows because the union of a directed family of states is again a state. \(\square\)

Thus information bases present domains of information states.

### 2.3 Information morphisms

A morphism of information bases is a relation describing how finite information in one context determines information in another. The defining requirement is preservation of consistency.

#### Definition 2.4 — Approximable information morphism

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(\mathcal{B}\) be information bases. An **information morphism**

\[
f : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}
\]

is a relation

\[
f \subseteq \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}} \times |\mathcal{B}|
\]

satisfying the following axioms.

For finite consistent \(X,Y \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}\) and tokens \(b,c \in |\mathcal{B}|\):

1. **Consistency preservation**
   \[
   X f b,\; Y f c,\; X \cup Y \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}
   \implies
   \{b,c\} \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{B}}.
   \]

2. **Monotonicity in the input**
   \[
   X f b,\; X \subseteq Y \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}
   \implies
   Y f b.
   \]

3. **Closure under output entailment**
   \[
   X f b,\; b \vdash_{\mathcal{B}} c
   \implies
   X f c.
   \]

4. **Cut in the input**
   If
   \[
   X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} Y
   \]
   and
   \[
   Y f b,
   \]
   then
   \[
   X f b.
   \]

We write

\[
X f Y
\]

when \(X f y\) for every \(y \in Y\).

---

#### Definition 2.5 — Action on states

Given an information morphism \(f : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\), define a function

\[
\operatorname{Pt}(f) : \operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A}) \to \operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{B})
\]

by

\[
\operatorname{Pt}(f)(x)
=
\{b \in |\mathcal{B}| \mid \exists X \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x,\; X f b\}.
\]

#### Proposition 2.6 — Morphisms preserve states

For every information morphism \(f : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\), the map \(\operatorname{Pt}(f)\) is well-defined, monotone, and Scott-continuous.

**Proof.**

Let \(x \in \operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A})\). We verify that \(\operatorname{Pt}(f)(x)\) is a state of \(\mathcal{B}\).

First, let \(F \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} \operatorname{Pt}(f)(x)\). For each \(b_i \in F\), choose finite \(X_i \subseteq x\) with \(X_i f b_i\). Since \(x\) is a state,

\[
X = \bigcup_i X_i \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} x
\]

is consistent. By monotonicity,

\[
X f b_i
\]

for all \(i\). Iterating consistency preservation yields

\[
F \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{B}}.
\]

Second, suppose \(F \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} \operatorname{Pt}(f)(x)\) and \(F \vdash_{\mathcal{B}} b\). Choose finite \(X \subseteq x\) with \(X f c\) for all \(c \in F\). Closure under output entailment gives \(X f b\). Hence \(b \in \operatorname{Pt}(f)(x)\).

Monotonicity and continuity follow because the definition uses only finite approximations. \(\square\)

---

### 2.4 The category of information bases

#### Definition 2.7 — Identity and composition

For an information base \(\mathcal{A}\), define the identity morphism

\[
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}} \subseteq \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}} \times |\mathcal{A}|
\]

by

\[
X \,\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\, a
\iff
X \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a.
\]

For morphisms

\[
f : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B},
\qquad
g : \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{C},
\]

define their composite

\[
g \circ f : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{C}
\]

by

\[
X (g \circ f) c
\iff
\exists Y \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{B}}
\text{ such that }
X f Y
\text{ and }
Y g c.
\]

#### Proposition 2.8 — \(\mathbf{Info}\) is a category

Information bases and information morphisms form a category, denoted

\[
\mathbf{Info}.
\]

**Proof.**

We check that \(g \circ f\) satisfies the axioms of Definition 2.4.

Let

\[
X (g \circ f) c
\]

via \(Y\), and

\[
X' (g \circ f) c'
\]

via \(Y'\). Suppose \(X \cup X' \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}\). By consistency preservation for \(f\),

\[
Y \cup Y' \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{B}}.
\]

By consistency preservation for \(g\),

\[
\{c,c'\} \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{C}}.
\]

The remaining axioms follow from monotonicity and cut for \(f\) and \(g\). Associativity is immediate from the existential definition of composition. The identity laws follow from the cut axiom. \(\square\)

### 2.5 Tensor product of information contexts

Information contexts can be combined independently. This gives ITT a tensorial structure.

#### Definition 2.9 — Tensor product

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(\mathcal{B}\) be information bases. Define

\[
\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}
\]

as follows.

1. Tokens:
   \[
   |\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}|
   =
   |\mathcal{A}| \times |\mathcal{B}|.
   \]

2. Consistency:
   A finite set
   \[
   F \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} |\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}|
   \]
   is consistent iff
   \[
   \pi_{\mathcal{A}}(F) \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{A}}
   \]
   and
   \[
   \pi_{\mathcal{B}}(F) \in \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{B}}.
   \]

3. Entailment:
   \[
   F \vdash_{\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}} (a,b)
   \]
   iff
   \[
   \pi_{\mathcal{A}}(F) \vdash_{\mathcal{A}} a
   \]
   and
   \[
   \pi_{\mathcal{B}}(F) \vdash_{\mathcal{B}} b.
   \]

The unit object \(I\) is the terminal information base with a single token \(*\), with

\[
\varnothing \vdash_I *
\]

and all finite sets consistent.

For morphisms

\[
f : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}',
\qquad
g : \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{B}',
\]

define

\[
f \otimes g : \mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{A}' \otimes \mathcal{B}'
\]

by

\[
F (f \otimes g) (a',b')
\iff
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(F) f a'
\quad \text{and} \quad
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(F) g b'.
\]

#### Proposition 2.10 — Symmetric monoidal structure

The category \(\mathbf{Info}\) is symmetric monoidal under \(\otimes\), with symmetry

\[
\sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(a,b) = (b,a).
\]

Moreover, on states,

\[
\operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B})
\cong
\operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A}) \times \operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{B}).
\]

**Proof.**

Given states \(x \in \operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A})\) and \(y \in \operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{B})\), define

\[
x \otimes y = x \times y.
\]

Finite subsets of \(x \times y\) project to finite subsets of \(x\) and \(y\), hence are consistent. Closure under entailment follows componentwise.

Conversely, any state \(z \in \operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B})\) determines states

\[
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(z), \quad \pi_{\mathcal{B}}(z),
\]

and one verifies

\[
z = \pi_{\mathcal{A}}(z) \otimes \pi_{\mathcal{B}}(z).
\]

The monoidal laws hold strictly up to canonical bijection. \(\square\)

For complete information bases, the tensor structure is closed. The internal hom \([\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}]\) is the information base whose states are information morphisms

\[
\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}.
\]

Thus, in the complete case,

\[
\mathbf{Info}(\mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C})
\cong
\mathbf{Info}(\mathcal{A}, [\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}]).
\]

This justifies the tensorial notation for independent contexts:

\[
\Gamma \otimes \Delta.
\]

## 3. Information Sites and Information Toposes

We now pass from individual information contexts to universes of sheaves over them.

### 3.1 Informational covers

In a topological space, an open cover expresses that a global region is exhaustively described by local regions. In ITT, a cover expresses that an information context is exhaustively approximated by consistent refinements.

Let \(\mathbf{C}\) be a small category of information universes, with morphisms given by information morphisms. For an object \(\Gamma\), a sieve \(S\) on \(\Gamma\) is a set of arrows into \(\Gamma\) closed under precomposition.

#### Definition 3.1 — Informational covering sieve

A sieve \(S\) on \(\Gamma\) is an **informational cover** if for every finite consistent approximation

\[
X \in \operatorname{Con}_{\Gamma},
\]

there exists an arrow

\[
f : \Delta \to \Gamma
\]

in \(S\) and a finite consistent approximation

\[
Y \in \operatorname{Con}_{\Delta}
\]

such that

\[
X \subseteq f_*(Y),
\]

where

\[
f_*(Y)
=
\{a \in |\Gamma| \mid Y f a\}.
\]

Intuitively, every finite piece of information in \(\Gamma\) is refined by some context appearing in the sieve.

The collection of such sieves generates a Grothendieck topology

\[
J_I
\]

on \(\mathbf{C}\).

#### Definition 3.2 — Information site

An **information site** is a pair

\[
(\mathbf{C}, J_I)
\]

where:

1. \(\mathbf{C}\) is a small category of information contexts and consistency-preserving morphisms;
2. \(J_I\) is the Grothendieck topology generated by informational covering sieves.

---

### 3.2 Sheaves over information sites

A presheaf on \(\mathbf{C}\) is a functor

\[
F : \mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}.
\]

For an arrow

\[
f : \Delta \to \Gamma,
\]

the restriction map is

\[
F(f) : F(\Gamma) \to F(\Delta).
\]

A sheaf is a presheaf satisfying descent with respect to informational covers.

#### Definition 3.3 — Information sheaf

A presheaf \(F\) is an **information sheaf** if for every informational cover

\[
\{f_i : \Gamma_i \to \Gamma\}_{i \in I},
\]

the diagram

\[
F(\Gamma)
\longrightarrow
\prod_i F(\Gamma_i)
\rightrightarrows
\prod_{i,j} F(\Gamma_i \times_{\Gamma} \Gamma_j)
\]

is an equalizer.

Equivalently, given local sections

\[
s_i \in F(\Gamma_i)
\]

such that

\[
F(p_{ij})(s_i) = F(q_{ij})(s_j)
\]

on all overlaps

\[
p_{ij} : \Gamma_i \times_{\Gamma} \Gamma_j \to \Gamma_i,
\qquad
q_{ij} : \Gamma_i \times_{\Gamma} \Gamma_j \to \Gamma_j,
\]

there exists a unique global section

\[
s \in F(\Gamma)
\]

with

\[
F(f_i)(s) = s_i.
\]

This is the fundamental local-to-global principle of ITT.

---

### 3.3 Information toposes

#### Definition 3.4 — Grothendieck information topos

Given an information site \((\mathbf{C}, J_I)\), the category

\[
\operatorname{Sh}(\mathbf{C}, J_I)
\]

of information sheaves is called a **Grothendieck information topos**.

#### Theorem 3.5 — Information sheaves form a topos

For every information site \((\mathbf{C}, J_I)\), the category

\[
\operatorname{Sh}(\mathbf{C}, J_I)
\]

is a Grothendieck topos.

**Proof.**

The topology \(J_I\) is a Grothendieck topology by construction. The category of sheaves on any small site is a Grothendieck topos. Therefore \(\operatorname{Sh}(\mathbf{C}, J_I)\) has finite limits, finite colimits, exponentials, and a subobject classifier. \(\square\)

However, ITT refines the usual subobject classifier by requiring that classified subobjects be informationally consistent.

---

### 3.4 The information classifier

In an ordinary sheaf topos, the subobject classifier is the sheaf of closed sieves. In ITT, we restrict to **consistent closed sieves**.

#### Definition 3.6 — Informational sieve consistency

A sieve \(S\) on \(\Gamma\) is **informationally consistent** if every finite family of arrows in \(S\) admits a common refinement in which all pulled-back observations remain consistent.

A sieve is **informationally closed** if it is closed under the topology \(J_I\): whenever an arrow \(f : \Delta \to \Gamma\) pulls back \(S\) to a covering sieve of \(\Delta\), then \(f \in S\).

#### Definition 3.7 — Information classifier

Define a sheaf

\[
\Omega_I
\]

by

\[
\Omega_I(\Gamma)
=
\{S \mid S \text{ is an informationally consistent, } J_I\text{-closed sieve on } \Gamma\}.
\]

For \(f : \Delta \to \Gamma\), restriction is pullback of sieves:

\[
\Omega_I(f)(S)
=
f^* S.
\]

The global truth arrow

\[
\top : 1 \to \Omega_I
\]

sends the terminal object to the maximal sieve.

#### Theorem 3.8 — \(\Omega_I\) classifies consistent subobjects

In \(\operatorname{Sh}(\mathbf{C}, J_I)\), the object \(\Omega_I\) classifies information-consistent subobjects.

That is, for every sheaf \(F\), there is a natural bijection

\[
\operatorname{Sub}_I(F)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(F, \Omega_I),
\]

where \(\operatorname{Sub}_I(F)\) denotes the poset of information-consistent subobjects of \(F\).

**Proof.**

Let \(A \hookrightarrow F\) be a consistent subobject. For a section \(s \in F(\Gamma)\), define

\[
\chi_A(s)
=
\{f : \Delta \to \Gamma \mid F(f)(s) \in A(\Delta)\}.
\]

This is a sieve on \(\Gamma\). Since \(A\) is a subobject, \(\chi_A(s)\) is closed under precomposition. Since \(A\) satisfies descent, \(\chi_A(s)\) is \(J_I\)-closed. Since \(A\) is informationally consistent, \(\chi_A(s)\) is informationally consistent.

Conversely, given a morphism

\[
\chi : F \to \Omega_I,
\]

define \(A \subseteq F\) by

\[
s \in A(\Gamma)
\iff
\chi(s) \text{ is the maximal sieve on } \Gamma.
\]

Descent for \(\Omega_I\) ensures that \(A\) is a subobject, and consistency of sieves ensures that \(A\) is informationally consistent. These constructions are inverse. \(\square\)

Thus ITT replaces the ordinary logical classifier \(\Omega\) by the information classifier \(\Omega_I\).

## 4. Elementary Information Toposes

The sheaf-theoretic construction gives a broad class of examples. We now isolate the elementary structure.

### 4.1 Information subobjects

Let \(\mathcal{E}\) be a category with finite limits.

#### Definition 4.1 — Information subobject structure

An **information subobject structure** on \(\mathcal{E}\) assigns to each object \(X\) a subset

\[
\operatorname{Sub}_I(X) \subseteq \operatorname{Sub}(X)
\]

such that:

1. **Pullback stability**  
   If \(A \in \operatorname{Sub}_I(X)\) and \(f : Y \to X\), then
   \[
   f^*A \in \operatorname{Sub}_I(Y).
   \]

2. **Finite intersection stability**  
   If \(A,B \in \operatorname{Sub}_I(X)\), then
   \[
   A \cap B \in \operatorname{Sub}_I(X).
   \]

3. **Local character**  
   If \(\{f_i : X_i \to X\}\) is an epimorphic informational cover and each \(f_i^*A\) is informational, then \(A\) is informational.

An object \(X\) is **informationally consistent** if its maximal subobject belongs to \(\operatorname{Sub}_I(X)\).

---

### 4.2 Elementary information topos

#### Definition 4.2 — Elementary information topos

An **elementary information topos** is a category \(\mathcal{E}\) with finite limits, exponentials, and an object

\[
\Omega_I
\]

such that for each object \(X\) there is a natural bijection

\[
\operatorname{Sub}_I(X)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(X, \Omega_I).
\]

The object \(\Omega_I\) is called the **information classifier**.

When every subobject is informational, this reduces to the definition of an elementary topos with subobject classifier \(\Omega_I = \Omega\). Thus elementary information toposes generalize elementary toposes.

---

### 4.3 Information modalities

In many examples, the information classifier arises from a Lawvere-Tierney-style modality.

Let \(\Omega\) be the ordinary subobject classifier of an ambient topos. An **information modality** is a map

\[
j : \Omega \to \Omega
\]

satisfying:

1. **Inflationarity**
   \[
   p \leq j(p).
   \]

2. **Meet preservation**
   \[
   j(p \wedge q) = j(p) \wedge j(q).
   \]

3. **Idempotence**
   \[
   j(j(p)) = j(p).
   \]

The fixed points

\[
\Omega_I = \{p \in \Omega \mid j(p) = p\}
\]

form the information classifier.

The internal operations on \(\Omega_I\) are:

\[
p \wedge_I q = p \wedge q,
\]

\[
p \vee_I q = j(p \vee q),
\]

\[
p \Rightarrow_I q = j(p \Rightarrow q),
\]

\[
\neg_I p = j(p \Rightarrow \bot).
\]

Thus the logic of information is obtained by forcing ordinary logical operations to be information-stable.

## 5. Internal Tensorial Logic of ITT

The internal language of an information topos is a typed logic with contexts, tensorial composition, and information-stable propositions.

### 5.1 Judgments

We use the following judgments.

1. Context formation:
   \[
   \Gamma \;\mathrm{ctx}.
   \]

2. Term judgment:
   \[
   \Gamma \vdash t : A.
   \]

3. Information proposition:
   \[
   \Gamma \vdash \varphi : \Omega_I.
   \]

4. Consistency judgment:
   \[
   \Gamma \vdash \operatorname{Con}(\varphi).
   \]

5. Entailment:
   \[
   \Gamma \vdash \varphi \leq_I \psi.
   \]

### 5.2 Tensorial context combination

If

\[
\Gamma \vdash \varphi : \Omega_I
\]

and

\[
\Delta \vdash \psi : \Omega_I,
\]

then

\[
\Gamma \otimes \Delta \vdash \varphi \otimes \psi : \Omega_I.
\]

The tensor product expresses independent combination of information.

For morphisms

\[
f : \Gamma \to \Gamma',
\qquad
g : \Delta \to \Delta',
\]

there is a tensor product morphism

\[
f \otimes g : \Gamma \otimes \Delta \to \Gamma' \otimes \Delta'.
\]

Consistency preservation is expressed by the rule

\[
\frac{
\Gamma' \vdash \operatorname{Con}(\varphi)
\quad
f : \Gamma \to \Gamma'
}{
\Gamma \vdash \operatorname{Con}(f^*\varphi)
}.
\]

In tensorial form:

\[
\frac{
\Gamma' \otimes \Delta' \vdash \operatorname{Con}(\Phi)
\quad
f \otimes g : \Gamma \otimes \Delta \to \Gamma' \otimes \Delta'
}{
\Gamma \otimes \Delta \vdash \operatorname{Con}((f \otimes g)^*\Phi)
}.
\]

---

### 5.3 Descent rule

The sheaf condition is expressed logically as a descent rule.

Suppose

\[
\{f_i : \Gamma_i \to \Gamma\}_{i \in I}
\]

is an informational cover. If for each \(i\) we have local sections

\[
\Gamma_i \vdash s_i : \varphi
\]

and on overlaps

\[
\Gamma_i \times_{\Gamma} \Gamma_j \vdash s_i = s_j,
\]

then there exists a unique global section

\[
\Gamma \vdash s : \varphi
\]

such that

\[
f_i^* s = s_i.
\]

This is the logical expression of local-to-global consistency.

---

### 5.4 Informational entailment

Entailment is defined internally by

\[
\varphi \leq_I \psi
\iff
\top = (\varphi \Rightarrow_I \psi).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\varphi \leq_I \psi
\iff
j(\varphi \Rightarrow \psi) = \top.
\]

Thus \(\psi\) follows from \(\varphi\) not merely classically, but in an information-stable manner.

---

## 6. Foundations of Mathematics

In classical topos theory, sets are objects of a topos and logic is interpreted through \(\Omega\). In ITT, mathematical objects are interpreted as stable information structures.

### 6.1 Sets as information sheaves

Let \((\mathbf{C}, J_I)\) be an information site. A **set** in ITT is a sheaf

\[
X \in \operatorname{Sh}(\mathbf{C}, J_I).
\]

A term of type \(X\) over context \(\Gamma\) is a section

\[
s \in X(\Gamma).
\]

Equality of terms is not absolute but contextual: two terms \(s,t \in X(\Gamma)\) are equal in context \(\Gamma\) if they agree as sections. They are locally equal if there exists an informational cover \(\{f_i : \Gamma_i \to \Gamma\}\) such that

\[
f_i^* s = f_i^* t
\]

for all \(i\). By sheaf descent, local equality implies global equality.

---

### 6.2 Propositions as information predicates

A proposition over \(X\) is a morphism

\[
\varphi : X \to \Omega_I.
\]

Its extension is the consistent subobject

\[
\{x \in X \mid \varphi(x)\}.
\]

A proposition is **known** in context \(\Gamma\) if its characteristic map restricts to truth:

\[
\Gamma \vdash \varphi
\iff
\varphi \circ s = \top
\]

for the relevant section \(s : 1 \to X\), or more generally if the associated subobject contains the context.

---

### 6.3 Informational foundations

ITT suggests a foundational principle:

> A mathematical assertion is valid when it is invariant under all consistent refinements of the information context in which it is formulated.

Thus mathematical truth is not primitive. It is the stable limit of consistent information.

This leads to an information-theoretic reinterpretation of the usual structural foundations of mathematics:

- Objects are information contexts.
- Morphisms are consistency-preserving transformations.
- Types are sheaves.
- Propositions are maps into \(\Omega_I\).
- Proofs are constructions stable under descent.
- Equality is contextual identification.
- Set-theoretic membership is replaced by sectionhood in an information sheaf.

In this sense, ITT provides an alternative to material set theory and to type-theoretic foundations.

---

## 7. Quantum Logic

Quantum theory is a natural domain for ITT because quantum observables are contextual: only compatible observables can be jointly assigned consistent values.

### 7.1 Quantum information contexts

Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a Hilbert space. Define the quantum context category

\[
\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{H})
\]

as follows.

Objects are unital commutative \(C^*\)-subalgebras

\[
C \subseteq \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).
\]

Morphisms are inclusions

\[
C \hookrightarrow D.
\]

Each context \(C\) represents a maximal compatible classical perspective inside quantum theory.

For finite-dimensional \(\mathcal{H}\), each such \(C\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb{C}^n\), and its projections form a Boolean algebra

\[
\operatorname{Proj}(C).
\]

---

### 7.2 Quantum information bases

For each context \(C\), define an information base

\[
\mathcal{Q}_C
\]

as follows.

Tokens are nontrivial projections

\[
p \in \operatorname{Proj}(C).
\]

A finite set \(F \subseteq \operatorname{Proj}(C)\) is consistent iff

\[
\bigwedge F \neq 0.
\]

Entailment is defined by

\[
F \vdash_C p
\iff
\bigwedge F \leq p.
\]

For an inclusion \(C \hookrightarrow D\), the induced information morphism sends a projection \(p \in C\) to the same projection regarded in \(D\). Since a nonzero finite meet in \(C\) remains nonzero in \(D\), consistency is preserved.

---

### 7.3 The quantum information topos

The quantum information site is

\[
(\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{H}), J_Q),
\]

where covers express jointly consistent refinements of contexts.

The associated information topos is

\[
\operatorname{Sh}(\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{H}), J_Q).
\]

The spectral presheaf

\[
\Sigma
\]

is defined by

\[
\Sigma(C)
=
\operatorname{Hom}_{C^*}(C, \mathbb{C}).
\]

Equivalently, \(\Sigma(C)\) is the Gelfand spectrum of \(C\).

A global section

\[
1 \to \Sigma
\]

would assign to every context \(C\) a character

\[
\chi_C : C \to \mathbb{C}
\]

naturally with respect to inclusions.

---

### 7.4 Kochen-Specker obstruction

#### Theorem 7.1 — No global quantum valuation

If \(\dim(\mathcal{H}) \geq 3\), the spectral presheaf \(\Sigma\) has no global section:

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(1,\Sigma) = \varnothing.
\]

**Proof.**

Suppose \(s : 1 \to \Sigma\) existed. For each projection \(p\), choose a context \(C_p\) containing \(p\). Define

\[
v(p) = \chi_{C_p}(p) \in \{0,1\}.
\]

Naturality of \(s\) implies that \(v(p)\) is independent of the choice of context. Functional calculus implies that \(v\) preserves Boolean operations inside every context. Thus \(v\) is a Kochen-Specker valuation. The Kochen-Specker theorem forbids such a valuation in dimension at least three. Contradiction. \(\square\)

In ITT, this says that quantum reality admits local consistent valuations but no global classical truth assignment.

---

### 7.5 Quantum propositions

A quantum proposition is a subobject

\[
P \hookrightarrow \Sigma
\]

in the information topos. The information classifier \(\Omega_I\) assigns to each context \(C\) the set of consistent downsets of projections in \(C\).

Thus quantum logic becomes contextual, intuitionistic, and information-stable. Noncommutativity is not represented by a non-distributive global propositional lattice; instead, it appears as the impossibility of gluing all local Boolean perspectives into one global Boolean valuation.

The tensor product of independent quantum systems is expressed by

\[
\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B,
\]

and contextual independence by

\[
C_A \otimes C_B.
\]

Morphisms preserve consistency exactly when they preserve compatibility of observables.

---

## 8. Distributed Computation

Distributed systems are intrinsically informational. Nodes possess partial views, and global correctness is the possibility of gluing those views consistently.

### 8.1 Distributed information contexts

Let \(N\) be a set of nodes. A distributed context is a finite family of local logs

\[
\Gamma = (\Gamma_i)_{i \in N}
\]

together with a consistency relation expressing absence of conflict.

A token may represent an event, message, or update. A finite set of tokens is consistent if it contains no conflicting events and respects causal order.

A morphism of distributed contexts is a simulation preserving causal order and conflict-freedom. Thus morphisms preserve informational consistency.

---

### 8.2 Covers and local views

For a distributed context \(\Gamma\), the projection maps

\[
\pi_i : \Gamma \to \Gamma_i
\]

form an informational cover. More generally, covers correspond to collections of local views whose combination exhausts the global context.

The sheaf condition says that a global distributed state is precisely a compatible family of local states.

Let \(F\) be a sheaf representing a data type or protocol state space. Given local states

\[
s_i \in F(\Gamma_i),
\]

if

\[
F(p_{ij})(s_i) = F(q_{ij})(s_j)
\]

on overlaps, then there exists a unique global state

\[
s \in F(\Gamma)
\]

such that

\[
F(\pi_i)(s) = s_i.
\]

This is exactly the mathematical formulation of strong eventual consistency.

---

### 8.3 Tensorial composition of protocols

If \(P\) and \(Q\) are protocols over contexts \(\Gamma\) and \(\Delta\), their parallel composition is

\[
P \otimes Q.
\]

For morphisms

\[
f : \Gamma \to \Gamma',
\qquad
g : \Delta \to \Delta',
\]

the composite parallel protocol is

\[
f \otimes g : \Gamma \otimes \Delta \to \Gamma' \otimes \Delta'.
\]

Consistency preservation means:

\[
\operatorname{Con}_{\Gamma \otimes \Delta}(S)
\implies
\operatorname{Con}_{\Gamma' \otimes \Delta'}((f \otimes g)_* S).
\]

Thus ITT gives a categorical semantics for safe parallel composition.

---

### 8.4 Consensus and descent

Consensus protocols can be understood as attempts to construct global sections of a sheaf from local proposals.

The descent rule says that consensus is possible precisely when local proposals agree on overlaps. If they do not, the obstruction is cohomological: the family of local states forms a cocycle that fails to cobound a global section.

Thus ITT connects distributed computing with sheaf cohomology. In particular:

- Safety is preservation of consistency.
- Liveness is existence of covers refining partial states.
- Agreement is descent.
- Impossibility is nontrivial cohomology.

This provides a geometric interpretation of classic distributed computing constraints.

---

## 9. Knowledge Systems

Knowledge systems are systems of information states closed under inference and revision. ITT gives them a categorical semantics.

### 9.1 Epistemic information bases

An epistemic information base is an information base

\[
\mathcal{K} = (|\mathcal{K}|, \operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{K}}, \vdash_{\mathcal{K}})
\]

where tokens are beliefs, observations, or assertions.

A state is a consistent belief set. Entailment is inference. Consistency is compatibility with the underlying ontology.

A belief revision operator is a morphism

\[
* : \mathcal{K} \otimes \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{K}
\]

where \(\mathcal{I}\) is an information base of new inputs, subject to the condition that if the input is consistent with the current context, the output remains consistent.

---

### 9.2 Multi-agent epistemic sites

For a set of agents \(A\), an epistemic site contains contexts \(\Gamma\) and accessibility morphisms

\[
\rho_a : \Delta \to \Gamma
\]

representing the epistemic alternatives available to agent \(a\).

The knowledge operator \(K_a\) is interpreted by universal quantification over accessible refinements:

\[
\Gamma \vdash K_a \varphi
\]

iff for every accessible morphism

\[
\rho_a : \Delta \to \Gamma,
\]

we have

\[
\Delta \vdash \rho_a^* \varphi.
\]

Categorically, \(K_a\) is a right adjoint to reindexing along the accessibility fibration.

It preserves finite meets:

\[
K_a(\varphi \wedge \psi)
=
K_a \varphi \wedge K_a \psi.
\]

If accessibility is reflexive, then

\[
K_a \varphi \leq \varphi.
\]

If accessibility is transitive, then

\[
K_a \varphi \leq K_a K_a \varphi.
\]

Thus ITT recovers S4-style epistemic logic in a sheaf-theoretic setting, while allowing richer non-classical behavior when information is partial or inconsistent.

---

### 9.3 Belief revision as consistency preservation

A revision map

\[
R : \mathcal{K} \to \mathcal{K}'
\]

is rational in the ITT sense if it preserves consistency:

\[
\operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
\implies
\operatorname{Con}_{\mathcal{K}'}(R(X)).
\]

In categorical terms, belief revision is a morphism in \(\mathbf{Info}\). The sheaf condition ensures that if revised beliefs are locally compatible across a cover of evidence contexts, they glue to a globally coherent belief state.

---

## 10. Main Structural Results

We collect the principal theoretical results.

### Theorem 10.1 — Information bases form a monoidal category

The category \(\mathbf{Info}\) of information bases and consistency-preserving approximable morphisms is symmetric monoidal under \(\otimes\). In the complete case, it is monoidal closed.

---

### Theorem 10.2 — Information sites yield toposes

For every information site \((\mathbf{C}, J_I)\), the category

\[
\operatorname{Sh}(\mathbf{C}, J_I)
\]

is a Grothendieck topos.

---

### Theorem 10.3 — The information classifier classifies consistent subobjects

In any Grothendieck information topos, the sheaf

\[
\Omega_I
\]

of informationally consistent closed sieves classifies consistent subobjects.

---

### Theorem 10.4 — Information logic is stable under refinement

If

\[
f : \Gamma \to \Delta
\]

is an information morphism and \(\varphi\) is an information predicate on \(\Delta\), then

\[
\Delta \vdash \operatorname{Con}(\varphi)
\implies
\Gamma \vdash \operatorname{Con}(f^*\varphi).
\]

Thus consistency is invariant under pullback along consistency-preserving morphisms.

---

### Theorem 10.5 — Descent is local-to-global consistency

Let

\[
\{f_i : \Gamma_i \to \Gamma\}
\]

be an informational cover. A global section of an information sheaf exists uniquely precisely when local sections agree on overlaps.

Equivalently, in the internal logic:

\[
\frac{
\Gamma_i \vdash s_i : \varphi
\quad
\Gamma_i \times_{\Gamma} \Gamma_j \vdash s_i = s_j
}{
\Gamma \vdash s : \varphi
}.
\]

## 11. Comparison with Related Frameworks

### 11.1 Topos theory

Classical topos theory generalizes set theory and logic. ITT generalizes topos theory by replacing truth-value classification with information-consistency classification. Every topos is an information topos in which every subobject is informational and \(\Omega_I = \Omega\).

### 11.2 Locale theory

Locales are pointfree spaces given by frames. Information bases generalize locales by explicitly incorporating finite consistency and entailment relations. The state space functor

\[
\operatorname{Pt} : \mathbf{Info} \to \mathbf{DCPO}
\]

relates ITT to domain theory.

### 11.3 Domain theory

Scott information systems are a special case of information bases. ITT extends domain theory by adding sheaf-theoretic and logical structure, and by treating information contexts as objects of a topos.

### 11.4 Quantum toposes

Topos approaches to quantum theory usually use presheaves over contexts of commutative subalgebras. ITT adds an explicit consistency classifier and tensorial context calculus, making the treatment closer to operational information flow.

### 11.5 Epistemic logic

Traditional epistemic logic uses Kripke semantics. ITT replaces possible worlds with information contexts and accessibility relations with consistency-preserving morphisms. This allows partial, contextual, and revisable knowledge.

---

## 12. Open Problems

Several directions arise.

### 12.1 Higher information toposes

Develop \(\infty\)-categorical ITT, where information contexts are higher contexts and consistency is coherent up to higher equivalence.

### 12.2 Informational univalence

Formulate an information-theoretic analogue of univalence:

\[
(\Gamma \simeq \Delta)
\simeq
(\Gamma =_{\Omega_I} \Delta).
\]

That is, equivalence of information contexts should correspond to indistinguishability inside the information classifier.

### 12.3 Quantitative information

Introduce probabilistic and metric information topologies, where consistency is relaxed to consistency up to noise, and \(\Omega_I\) is a sheaf of quantitative truth values.

### 12.4 Quantum field theory

Extend ITT to type III von Neumann algebras and local quantum field theory, using information sites of local observable algebras.

### 12.5 Distributed verification

Use ITT as a semantic foundation for verified distributed systems, with sheaf cohomology measuring obstruction to consensus.

---

## 13. Conclusion

Information Topos Theory replaces the classical logical universe of a topos with an information universe. Its primitive notion is not truth but consistency under refinement. Objects are information contexts, morphisms preserve informational consistency, and propositions are classified by an information classifier \(\Omega_I\).

The central structural insight is that local information can be glued into global structure exactly when descent holds. This principle unifies foundations of mathematics, quantum contextuality, distributed computation, and knowledge systems.

In classical topos theory, the fundamental question is:

\[
\text{What is true?}
\]

In Information Topos Theory, the fundamental question becomes:

\[
\text{What is consistently supported by the available information?}
\]

This shift provides a unified categorical foundation for systems in which information is partial, contextual, distributed, or quantum.
