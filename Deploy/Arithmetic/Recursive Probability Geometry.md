# Recursive Probability Geometry

**Preprint — White Paper**

---

## Abstract

We develop **Recursive Probability Geometry** (RPG), a framework in which probability distributions are not passive objects living on a fixed statistical manifold, but active generators of the geometry itself. Given a recursive probability law

\[
P_{n+1}=\mathcal{R}_n(P_n),
\]

one associates to each state \(P_n\) a metric tensor

\[
g_{ij}^{(n)}=\Gamma(P_n),
\]

where \(\Gamma\) is a metric-generation operator. In this setting, probability updates induce metric evolution, curvature dynamics, and geodesic structure. We formulate RPG axiomatically, construct canonical metric generators, derive tensorial recursion laws for Bayesian updating, establish contraction and stability results for Markov recursions, and develop local precision geometry for recursive inference. Applications are given to information geometry, Bayesian inference, sequential statistics, and artificial intelligence, including natural-gradient learning, Bayesian neural networks, and recursive kernel geometry.

**Keywords:** recursive probability, information geometry, Fisher metric, Bayesian inference, statistical manifolds, natural gradient, curvature, machine learning.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical information geometry studies a statistical manifold \(\mathcal{M}\) of probability distributions endowed with a fixed metric, typically the Fisher information metric. One begins with a parametric family

\[
\mathcal{M}=\{p(x;\theta):\theta\in\Theta\}
\]

and assigns to each point \(\theta\) the Fisher metric

\[
g_{ij}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
\!\left[
\partial_i \log p(X;\theta)\,
\partial_j \log p(X;\theta)
\right].
\]

The geometry is therefore a static structure imposed on probability.

Recursive Probability Geometry reverses this relationship. Rather than treating probability as a coordinate system on a pre-existing geometry, RPG treats probability as the engine that generates geometry recursively. The basic objects are a sequence of probability distributions

\[
P_0,\;P_1,\;P_2,\dots
\]

generated by a recursion

\[
P_{n+1}=\mathcal{R}_n(P_n),
\]

and a metric-generation operator \(\Gamma\) producing a sequence of metric tensors

\[
g^{(n)}=\Gamma(P_n),
\qquad
g_{ij}^{(n)}=\Gamma_{ij}(P_n).
\]

Thus geometry becomes a dynamical consequence of probabilistic evolution.

The central equation of RPG is therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_{n+1}=\mathcal{R}_n(P_n),
\qquad
g_{ij}^{(n)}=\Gamma(P_n).
}
\]

The operator \(\mathcal{R}_n\) may represent Bayesian updating, stochastic filtering, Markov transport, online learning, variational reweighting, or any probabilistic recursion. The operator \(\Gamma\) may be the Fisher-Rao generator, a Hessian precision generator, a kernel generator, or a history-dependent metric generator. The resulting object is a recursively evolving Riemannian structure.

The purpose of this paper is to provide a systematic foundation for RPG. We introduce the basic axioms, derive metric recursion formulas, analyze curvature evolution, and show how Bayesian inference, sequential estimation, and learning algorithms acquire a natural geometric formulation.

## 2. Probability Manifolds and Tangent Tensors

### 2.1 Space of probability densities

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)\) be a \(\sigma\)-finite measure space. Let

\[
\mathcal{P}_+(\Omega)
=
\left\{
p:\Omega\to(0,\infty)
\;\middle|\;
\int_\Omega p\,d\mu=1
\right\}
\]

denote a smooth space of strictly positive probability densities. A tangent vector at \(p\in\mathcal{P}_+(\Omega)\) is a signed density \(u\) satisfying

\[
\int_\Omega u\,d\mu=0.
\]

Thus

\[
T_p\mathcal{P}_+(\Omega)
=
\left\{
u\in L^1(\Omega,\mu):
\int_\Omega u\,d\mu=0
\right\}.
\]

In finite-dimensional parametric models, this tangent space is spanned by derivatives of the density with respect to parameters.

### 2.2 Parametric statistical manifolds

Let \(p(x;\theta)\) be a smooth parametric family with coordinates

\[
\theta=(\theta^1,\dots,\theta^d)\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d.
\]

Define the score fields

\[
s_i(x;\theta)
=
\partial_i \log p(x;\theta),
\qquad
\partial_i=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^i}.
\]

The tangent vector associated with \(\theta^i\) is

\[
\partial_i p(x;\theta)
=
p(x;\theta)s_i(x;\theta).
\]

The Fisher-Rao metric is

\[
g_{ij}(\theta)
=
\int_\Omega
\frac{\partial_i p(x;\theta)\,\partial_j p(x;\theta)}
{p(x;\theta)}
\,d\mu(x)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[s_i s_j].
\]

Equivalently,

\[
g_{ij}(\theta)
=
-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
\left[
\partial_i\partial_j \log p(X;\theta)
\right],
\]

under standard regularity conditions.

### 2.3 Infinite-dimensional Fisher-Rao metric

On the full density manifold, the Fisher-Rao metric is the weak Riemannian metric

\[
g_p^{\mathrm{FR}}(u,v)
=
\int_\Omega \frac{u(x)v(x)}{p(x)}\,d\mu(x).
\]

In amplitude coordinates

\[
\psi(x)=\sqrt{p(x)},
\]

one has

\[
g_p^{\mathrm{FR}}(u,v)
=
4\int_\Omega \delta\psi_u(x)\,\delta\psi_v(x)\,d\mu(x),
\]

where \(u=2\psi\,\delta\psi_u\). The Fisher-Rao geometry of the probability simplex is thereby identified with the geometry of the positive part of the unit sphere in \(L^2(\Omega,\mu)\).

## 3. Recursive Probability Systems

### 3.1 Basic definition

A **Recursive Probability Geometry system** is a tuple

\[
(\mathcal{P},\{\mathcal{R}_n\},\Gamma,P_0),
\]

where:

1. \(\mathcal{P}\) is a smooth manifold of probability measures or densities;
2. \(\mathcal{R}_n:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}\) is a smooth recursion map;
3. \(\Gamma\) is a metric-generation operator;
4. \(P_0\in\mathcal{P}\) is an initial distribution.

The probability recursion is

\[
P_{n+1}=\mathcal{R}_n(P_n).
\]

The associated geometry is

\[
g^{(n)}=\Gamma(P_n),
\qquad
g_{ij}^{(n)}=\Gamma_{ij}(P_n).
\]

The map \(\mathcal{R}_n\) may depend on data, time, or external control parameters. We often write

\[
P_{n+1}=\mathcal{R}_n(P_n;D_{n+1}),
\]

where \(D_{n+1}\) denotes new data.

### 3.2 Metric-generation operator

A metric-generation operator is a map

\[
\Gamma:\mathcal{P}\to \Gamma\bigl(S^2T^*\mathcal{P}\bigr),
\]

assigning to each probability distribution \(P\) a symmetric covariant two-tensor \(\Gamma(P)\). In local coordinates,

\[
\Gamma(P)
=
\Gamma_{ij}[P]\,d\theta^i\otimes d\theta^j.
\]

The essential RPG relation is

\[
g_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\Gamma_{ij}[P_n].
\]

When \(\Gamma\) is positive definite, each \(g^{(n)}\) is a Riemannian metric.

### 3.3 Axioms for metric generation

We impose the following structural axioms.

#### Axiom 1: Positivity

For every \(P\in\mathcal{P}\),

\[
\Gamma_P(u,u)\ge 0,
\]

with equality only when \(u=0\).

#### Axiom 2: Reparametrization covariance

For any diffeomorphism \(\phi:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}\),

\[
\Gamma_{\phi_*P}(\phi_*u,\phi_*v)
=
\Gamma_P(u,v).
\]

Thus the geometry is independent of coordinate representation.

#### Axiom 3: Contrast generation

There exists a statistical contrast \(D(P,Q)\) such that

\[
\Gamma_P(u,v)
=
\left.
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial s\,\partial t}
D(P+s u,Q+t v)
\right|_{s=t=0}
\]

or an analogous symmetric second-order construction.

For the Fisher-Rao metric, one may take \(D\) to be the Kullback-Leibler divergence.

#### Axiom 4: Markov monotonicity

If \(K:\Omega\to\Omega'\) is a Markov kernel, then the induced pushforward map \(K_*\) satisfies

\[
g^{\mathrm{FR}}_{K_*P}(K_*u,K_*v)
\le
g^{\mathrm{FR}}_P(u,v).
\]

This is the Chentsov monotonicity property and is fundamental for statistical naturalness.

### 3.4 Recursive strain tensor

Suppose the recursion induces a coordinate map

\[
F_n:\Theta\to\Theta,
\qquad
\theta_{n+1}=F_n(\theta_n).
\]

Then the pullback of the next metric is

\[
(F_n^*g^{(n+1)})_{ij}
=
\partial_i F_n^a\,
\partial_j F_n^b\,
g^{(n+1)}_{ab}.
\]

Define the **recursive strain tensor**

\[
B_{ij}^{(n)}
=
(F_n^*g^{(n+1)})_{ij}
-
g_{ij}^{(n)}.
\]

If

\[
B^{(n)}=0,
\]

the recursion is locally isometric. If \(B^{(n)}\) is positive definite, the recursion expands local information geometry; if negative definite, it contracts it.

### 3.5 Categorical viewpoint

One may regard RPG as a functorial assignment

\[
\Gamma:\mathbf{Prob}\longrightarrow \mathbf{Riem},
\]

where objects are probability measures and morphisms are Markov kernels or measurable transformations. A recursive probability system is then an orbit

\[
P_0
\mapsto
P_1
\mapsto
P_2
\mapsto
\cdots
\]

under an endomorphism or sequence of endomorphisms, and RPG is the induced orbit of Riemannian structures.

## 4. Canonical Metric Generators

### 4.1 Fisher-Rao generator

The canonical generator is

\[
\Gamma^{\mathrm{FR}}_P(u,v)
=
\int_\Omega
\frac{u(x)v(x)}{P(x)}
\,d\mu(x).
\]

For a parametric family \(P_\theta\), this gives

\[
g_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\int_\Omega
P_n(x)
\partial_i \log P_n(x)
\partial_j \log P_n(x)
\,d\mu(x).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
g_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\mathbb{E}_{P_n}
\left[
\partial_i \log P_n\,
\partial_j \log P_n
\right].
}
\]

This is the most natural RPG generator because it is reparametrization invariant and monotone under Markov maps.

### 4.2 Precision or Hessian generator

Let \(U_n\) be the negative log-density,

\[
U_n(\theta)=-\log P_n(\theta).
\]

Given a reference connection \(\nabla^0\), define the precision tensor

\[
g_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\nabla^0_i\nabla^0_j U_n.
\]

In Euclidean coordinates this reduces to

\[
g_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\partial_i\partial_j U_n.
\]

When \(U_n\) is strictly convex, this tensor is positive definite and defines a local Riemannian metric.

For Bayesian recursion, this generator becomes especially powerful because multiplication of densities corresponds to addition of potentials, hence addition of metrics.

### 4.3 Kernel generator

Let \(K_P:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\) be a positive-definite kernel depending on \(P\). Define

\[
\Gamma_P^K(u,v)
=
\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}
u(x)K_P(x,y)v(y)
\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y).
\]

The Fisher-Rao metric is recovered formally by

\[
K_P(x,y)=\frac{\delta(x-y)}{P(x)}.
\]

Kernel generators are useful in machine learning, where \(K_P\) may be a neural tangent kernel, a covariance kernel, or a reproducing kernel associated with a model class.

### 4.4 History-dependent recursive generator

A purely instantaneous metric \(\Gamma(P_n)\) may ignore the trajectory by which \(P_n\) was reached. A recursive history-sensitive generator can be defined as follows.

Let

\[
\Delta_n=\log\frac{P_n}{P_{n-1}}
\]

be the logarithmic increment. In a parametric model, define

\[
s_i^{(n)}=\partial_i \log P_n,
\qquad
r_i^{(n)}=\partial_i \Delta_n.
\]

Then for a weight \(\beta_n\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{
g_{ij}^{(n)}
=
(1-\beta_n)
\mathbb{E}_{P_n}
\left[
s_i^{(n)}s_j^{(n)}
\right]
+
\beta_n
\mathbb{E}_{P_n}
\left[
r_i^{(n)}r_j^{(n)}
\right].
}
\]

The first term measures instantaneous statistical information; the second term measures recursive displacement. This produces a geometry sensitive to learning dynamics rather than only to the current distribution.

## 5. Bayesian Recursive Geometry

Bayesian inference is the prototypical recursive probability system.

Let \(\pi_0(\theta)\) be a prior density on a parameter space \(\Theta\). Given data \(x_{n+1}\) with likelihood \(L_{n+1}(\theta)=p(x_{n+1}\mid\theta)\), define

\[
\pi_{n+1}(\theta)
=
\frac{
\pi_n(\theta)L_{n+1}(\theta)
}{
Z_{n+1}
},
\]

where

\[
Z_{n+1}
=
\int_\Theta
\pi_n(\vartheta)L_{n+1}(\vartheta)
\,d\vartheta.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal{B}_{L_{n+1}}(\pi_n)
=
\frac{L_{n+1}\pi_n}{Z_{n+1}}.
\]

This is a recursive probability map.

---

### 5.1 Potential recursion and precision geometry

Define the posterior potential

\[
U_n(\theta)
=
-\log \pi_n(\theta).
\]

Then Bayesian updating gives

\[
U_{n+1}(\theta)
=
U_n(\theta)
+
V_{n+1}(\theta)
-
\log Z_{n+1},
\]

where

\[
V_{n+1}(\theta)
=
-\log L_{n+1}(\theta).
\]

Since \(\log Z_{n+1}\) is constant in \(\theta\), its Hessian vanishes. Therefore, using the precision generator,

\[
g_{ij}^{(n+1)}
=
g_{ij}^{(n)}
+
\partial_i\partial_j V_{n+1}.
\]

More invariantly,

\[
\boxed{
g^{(n+1)}
=
g^{(n)}
+
\nabla^0 d V_{n+1}.
}
\]

Thus Bayesian inference recursively accumulates geometric precision.

If \(V_k\) is the negative log-likelihood of observation \(x_k\), then

\[
g^{(n)}
=
g^{(0)}
+
\sum_{k=1}^n
\nabla^0 d V_k.
\]

This is a tensorial version of recursive information accumulation.

---

### 5.2 Laplace approximation and observed information

Let \(\widehat{\theta}_n\) be the posterior mode,

\[
\widehat{\theta}_n
=
\arg\max_\theta \pi_n(\theta).
\]

The local posterior precision tensor is

\[
H_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\left.
\partial_i\partial_j U_n(\theta)
\right|_{\theta=\widehat{\theta}_n}.
\]

The Laplace approximation to the posterior covariance is

\[
\Sigma_n
=
\left(H^{(n)}\right)^{-1}.
\]

Because

\[
U_n
=
U_0
+
\sum_{k=1}^n V_k
-
\sum_{k=1}^n \log Z_k,
\]

we obtain

\[
H^{(n)}
=
H^{(0)}
+
\sum_{k=1}^n
\left.
\partial_i\partial_j V_k
\right|_{\theta=\widehat{\theta}_n}.
\]

Thus, asymptotically,

\[
H^{(n)}
\approx
n I(\theta_*),
\]

where \(I(\theta_*)\) is the Fisher information at the true parameter. Consequently,

\[
\Sigma_n
\approx
\frac{1}{n}I(\theta_*)^{-1}.
\]

RPG recovers Bayesian Bernstein-von Mises behavior as recursive metric concentration.

---

### 5.3 Fisher metric recursion under Bayesian reweighting

Let \(\pi_\alpha(\theta)\) be a smooth parametric perturbation of the prior \(\pi_0\), with scores

\[
s_i(\theta)
=
\left.
\partial_i \log \pi_\alpha(\theta)
\right|_{\alpha=0}.
\]

Apply the Bayesian map

\[
\mathcal{B}_L(\pi)
=
\frac{L\pi}{Z}.
\]

The posterior density corresponding to \(\pi_\alpha\) is

\[
q_\alpha(\theta)
=
\frac{L(\theta)\pi_\alpha(\theta)}
{Z_\alpha},
\qquad
Z_\alpha
=
\int L(\vartheta)\pi_\alpha(\vartheta)\,d\vartheta.
\]

Its score is

\[
\partial_i\log q_\alpha
=
\partial_i\log\pi_\alpha
-
\partial_i\log Z_\alpha.
\]

At \(\alpha=0\),

\[
\partial_i\log Z_\alpha
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}[s_i],
\]

where

\[
\pi'(\theta)
=
\frac{L(\theta)\pi_0(\theta)}{Z}.
\]

Therefore the posterior score is the centered prior score:

\[
s_i'
=
s_i-\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}[s_i].
\]

The induced posterior Fisher metric is

\[
g_{ij}^{\mathrm{post}}
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
s_i's_j'
\right].
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
g_{ij}^{\mathrm{post}}
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
[
s_i s_j
]
-
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}[s_i]
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}[s_j].
}
\]

Writing

\[
r(\theta)
=
\frac{L(\theta)}{Z},
\]

so that \(\pi'=r\pi_0\), we obtain the recursive reweighting formula

\[
\boxed{
g_{ij}^{\mathrm{post}}
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}
[
r s_i s_j
]
-
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}
[
r s_i
]
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}
[
r s_j
].
}
\]

This is a tensorial covariance recursion. Bayesian updating reweights the prior Fisher information by the likelihood ratio and subtracts the induced posterior mean score.

For sequential data, if

\[
r_n
=
\frac{
\prod_{k=1}^n L_k
}{
\prod_{k=1}^n Z_k
},
\]

then

\[
g_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_0}
\bigl(
r_n s_i,\,
r_n s_j
\bigr).
\]

Thus recursive Bayesian inference produces a recursive covariance geometry.

### 5.4 Exponential-family recursion

Let

\[
p_\eta(x)
=
\exp\left(
\eta^a T_a(x)-\psi(\eta)
\right)
\]

be an exponential family, with natural parameters \(\eta^a\), sufficient statistics \(T_a\), and log-partition function \(\psi(\eta)\).

The Fisher metric in natural coordinates is

\[
g_{ab}(\eta)
=
\partial_a\partial_b\psi(\eta).
\]

Suppose the recursive probability update is an exponential tilt,

\[
P_{n+1}(x)
\propto
P_n(x)
\exp\left(
\lambda_{n+1}^a T_a(x)
\right).
\]

Then

\[
\eta_{n+1}
=
\eta_n+\lambda_{n+1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
g_{ab}^{(n+1)}
=
\partial_a\partial_b\psi(\eta_{n+1}).
\]

Expanding,

\[
g_{ab}^{(n+1)}
=
g_{ab}^{(n)}
+
\lambda_{n+1}^c
\partial_c g_{ab}^{(n)}
+
\frac12
\lambda_{n+1}^c
\lambda_{n+1}^d
\partial_c\partial_d g_{ab}^{(n)}
+
O(\|\lambda_{n+1}\|^3).
\]

Thus the recursive metric evolution is governed by higher-order cumulants of the exponential family.

## 6. Tensor Calculus of Recursive Metrics

Given a sequence of metrics \(g^{(n)}\), each metric defines its own Levi-Civita connection.

### 6.1 Recursive Christoffel symbols

The Christoffel symbols of \(g^{(n)}\) are

\[
\Gamma_{ij}^{(n)k}
=
\frac12
g_{(n)}^{k\ell}
\left(
\partial_i g_{j\ell}^{(n)}
+
\partial_j g_{i\ell}^{(n)}
-
\partial_\ell g_{ij}^{(n)}
\right),
\]

where \(g_{(n)}^{k\ell}\) is the inverse of \(g_{ij}^{(n)}\).

### 6.2 Recursive curvature

The Riemann curvature tensor is

\[
R_{j k\ell}^{(n)i}
=
\partial_k \Gamma_{\ell j}^{(n)i}
-
\partial_\ell \Gamma_{k j}^{(n)i}
+
\Gamma_{k m}^{(n)i}
\Gamma_{\ell j}^{(n)m}
-
\Gamma_{\ell m}^{(n)i}
\Gamma_{k j}^{(n)m}.
\]

The Ricci tensor is

\[
R_{ij}^{(n)}
=
R_{i k j}^{(n)k},
\]

and the scalar curvature is

\[
R^{(n)}
=
g_{(n)}^{ij}R_{ij}^{(n)}.
\]

Curvature in RPG measures the non-commutativity of local probabilistic displacements. In statistical terms, it encodes higher-order interactions among parameters, model nonlinearity, and identifiability defects.

### 6.3 First-order curvature evolution

Let

\[
h_{ij}^{(n)}
=
g_{ij}^{(n+1)}
-
g_{ij}^{(n)}.
\]

Assume \(h^{(n)}\) is small. Let \(\nabla^{(n)}\) be the Levi-Civita connection of \(g^{(n)}\), and let

\[
h
=
g_{(n)}^{ij}h_{ij}^{(n)}.
\]

The first variation of the Ricci tensor is

\[
\delta R_{ij}^{(n)}
=
\frac12
\left(
\nabla^k\nabla_i h_{jk}^{(n)}
+
\nabla^k\nabla_j h_{ik}^{(n)}
-
\nabla^k\nabla_k h_{ij}^{(n)}
-
\nabla_i\nabla_j h
\right)
+
O(\|h\|^2).
\]

Thus recursive probability updates generate curvature flow through the differential structure of the metric increment.

### 6.4 Metric velocity and Lie transport

If the recursion is generated by a vector field \(V\) on parameter space,

\[
\theta_{n+1}
=
\theta_n+\varepsilon V(\theta_n)+O(\varepsilon^2),
\]

and if the metric generator is stationary, then

\[
F_\varepsilon^*g
=
g+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_V g+O(\varepsilon^2),
\]

where

\[
(\mathcal{L}_V g)_{ij}
=
V^k\partial_k g_{ij}
+
g_{kj}\partial_i V^k
+
g_{ik}\partial_j V^k.
\]

Thus a probability flow induces a metric transport equation. In continuous time,

\[
\partial_t g_{ij}
=
\mathcal{L}_V g_{ij}
+
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}
\Gamma_{ij}(P_s),
\]

where the second term accounts for intrinsic change in the metric-generation operator.

## 7. Recursive Geodesics and Inference Flows

### 7.1 Discrete geodesic recursion

A natural RPG inference scheme minimizes a recursive action

\[
S[\theta_0,\dots,\theta_N]
=
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
\left[
\frac12
d_{g^{(n)}}^2(\theta_{n+1},\theta_n)
+
\lambda_n \Phi_n(\theta_{n+1})
\right],
\]

where \(d_{g^{(n)}}\) is the geodesic distance induced by \(g^{(n)}\), and \(\Phi_n\) is a penalty, loss, or regularization potential.

The first-order optimality condition may be written using the exponential map:

\[
\exp_{\theta_{n+1}}^{-1}(\theta_n)
=
\lambda_n
\nabla^{(n)}\Phi_n(\theta_{n+1}).
\]

This is a recursive geodesic proximal step.

### 7.2 Natural-gradient recursion

Given a loss \(\mathcal{L}_n(\theta)\), define the recursive natural-gradient update

\[
\theta_{n+1}^i
=
\theta_n^i
-
\eta_n
g_{(n)}^{ij}(\theta_n)
\partial_j \mathcal{L}_n(\theta_n).
\]

In continuous time,

\[
\dot{\theta}^i
=
-
g^{ij}(\theta,t)
\partial_j\Phi(\theta,t).
\]

Because the metric itself depends on \(P_n\), this is not merely a gradient flow on a fixed manifold but a flow on a recursively generated geometry.

### 7.3 Fisher-Rao geodesics between recursive states

In amplitude coordinates

\[
\psi_n=\sqrt{P_n},
\]

the Fisher-Rao geodesic connecting \(P_n\) and \(P_{n+1}\) is given by spherical interpolation. Let

\[
\Omega_n
=
\arccos
\int_\Omega
\psi_n(x)\psi_{n+1}(x)\,d\mu(x).
\]

Then

\[
\psi_n(t)
=
\frac{
\sin((1-t)\Omega_n)
}{
\sin\Omega_n
}
\psi_n
+
\frac{
\sin(t\Omega_n)
}{
\sin\Omega_n
}
\psi_{n+1},
\qquad
t\in[0,1].
\]

The interpolated probability density is

\[
P_n(t)
=
\psi_n(t)^2.
\]

Thus each recursive probability step determines a canonical geodesic segment in the instantaneous Fisher-Rao geometry.

## 8. Stability, Contraction, and Convergence

### 8.1 Markov contraction

Let \(K:\Omega\to\Omega'\) be a Markov kernel. For a density \(p\), define

\[
(K_*p)(y)
=
\int_\Omega K(y\mid x)p(x)\,d\mu(x).
\]

For a tangent vector \(u\), define

\[
(K_*u)(y)
=
\int_\Omega K(y\mid x)u(x)\,d\mu(x).
\]

Then

\[
g_{K_*p}^{\mathrm{FR}}(K_*u,K_*u)
=
\int_{\Omega'}
\frac{(K_*u)(y)^2}{(K_*p)(y)}
\,d\nu(y).
\]

Define the conditional expectation

\[
m(y)
=
\mathbb{E}_p
\left[
\frac{u(X)}{p(X)}
\;\middle|\;
Y=y
\right].
\]

Then

\[
K_*u(y)
=
(K_*p)(y)m(y).
\]

Therefore

\[
g_{K_*p}^{\mathrm{FR}}(K_*u,K_*u)
=
\mathbb{E}_{K_*p}[m(Y)^2].
\]

By Jensen’s inequality,

\[
\mathbb{E}_{K_*p}[m(Y)^2]
\le
\mathbb{E}_p
\left[
\left(\frac{u(X)}{p(X)}\right)^2
\right]
=
g_p^{\mathrm{FR}}(u,u).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
g_{K_*p}^{\mathrm{FR}}(K_*u,K_*u)
\le
g_p^{\mathrm{FR}}(u,u).
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
d_{\mathrm{FR}}(K_*p,K_*q)
\le
d_{\mathrm{FR}}(p,q).
\]

Thus Markov recursions are non-expansive in Fisher geometry.

### 8.2 Convergence of recursive geometry

Suppose

\[
P_{n+1}=K_*P_n
\]

for a Markov operator \(K_*\) with unique stationary distribution \(P_\infty\). If \(K_*\) is strictly contractive with contraction coefficient \(\rho<1\), then

\[
d_{\mathrm{FR}}(P_n,P_\infty)
\le
\rho^n d_{\mathrm{FR}}(P_0,P_\infty).
\]

If \(\Gamma\) is Lipschitz with respect to Fisher distance, then

\[
\|g^{(n)}-g^{(\infty)}\|
\le
L\,d_{\mathrm{FR}}(P_n,P_\infty),
\]

and therefore

\[
g^{(n)}\to g^{(\infty)}.
\]

Thus stable probability recursion induces stable geometry.

### 8.3 Bayesian concentration as geometric concentration

Under regular iid Bayesian updating, the posterior precision satisfies

\[
\frac{1}{n}H^{(n)}
\to
I(\theta_*),
\]

almost surely, where \(I(\theta_*)\) is the Fisher information at the true parameter. Hence the posterior covariance satisfies

\[
\Sigma_n
\sim
\frac{1}{n}I(\theta_*)^{-1}.
\]

The Riemannian volume element

\[
dV^{(n)}
=
\sqrt{\det g^{(n)}}\,d\theta
\]

therefore expands in directions of increasing information, while posterior mass concentrates into shrinking geometric cells.

## 9. Examples

### 9.1 Finite categorical simplex

Let

\[
P_n=(p_n^1,\dots,p_n^m),
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^m p_n^i=1.
\]

A Bayesian or multiplicative recursion has the form

\[
p_{n+1}^i
=
\frac{
p_n^i L_i
}{
\sum_{j=1}^m p_n^j L_j
}.
\]

The Fisher-Rao metric on the simplex is

\[
g^{(n)}(u,v)
=
\sum_{i=1}^m
\frac{u^i v^i}{p_n^i},
\qquad
\sum_i u^i=\sum_i v^i=0.
\]

The Fisher-Rao distance is

\[
d_{\mathrm{FR}}(P_n,P_{n+1})
=
2\arccos
\sum_{i=1}^m
\sqrt{p_n^i p_{n+1}^i}.
\]

In square-root coordinates

\[
\psi_n^i=\sqrt{p_n^i},
\]

the simplex becomes a portion of the sphere

\[
\sum_{i=1}^m (\psi_n^i)^2=1.
\]

Thus recursive categorical updating is a recursive motion on the positive orthant of the unit sphere, with geometry generated at each step by the current probability vector.

---

### 9.2 Gaussian location model with known variance

Let

\[
x_k\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2),
\]

with known \(\sigma^2\). Let the prior be

\[
\mu\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_0,\sigma_0^2).
\]

The posterior after \(n\) observations is Gaussian with precision

\[
g_n
=
\sigma_0^{-2}
+
n\sigma^{-2}.
\]

The posterior mean is

\[
\mu_n
=
\frac{
\sigma_0^{-2}\mu_0
+
\sigma^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^n x_k
}{
g_n
}.
\]

The recursive update is

\[
g_{n+1}
=
g_n+\sigma^{-2},
\]

and

\[
\mu_{n+1}
=
\mu_n
+
\frac{\sigma^{-2}}{g_{n+1}}
\left(
x_{n+1}-\mu_n
\right).
\]

Here RPG reduces to recursive precision accumulation. The posterior variance is

\[
\Sigma_n
=
g_n^{-1}.
\]

This is the simplest nontrivial instance of recursive probability generating recursive geometry.

### 9.3 Gaussian location-scale geometry

For a Gaussian distribution

\[
\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2),
\]

with coordinates \((\mu,\sigma)\), the Fisher metric is

\[
g_{\mu\mu}
=
\frac{1}{\sigma^2},
\qquad
g_{\sigma\sigma}
=
\frac{2}{\sigma^2},
\qquad
g_{\mu\sigma}=0.
\]

Equivalently, using variance \(v=\sigma^2\),

\[
g_{\mu\mu}
=
\frac{1}{v},
\qquad
g_{vv}
=
\frac{1}{2v^2}.
\]

A recursive estimator \((\mu_n,v_n)\) induces a recursive metric

\[
g^{(n)}_{\mu\mu}
=
\frac{1}{v_n},
\qquad
g^{(n)}_{vv}
=
\frac{1}{2v_n^2}.
\]

Thus uncertainty estimation in Gaussian models becomes a recursive geometric flow on the half-plane \(v>0\).

### 9.4 Online exponential-family update

Let

\[
p_\eta(x)=
\exp\left(
\eta^aT_a(x)-\psi(\eta)
\right).
\]

An online recursive update may be written

\[
\eta_{n+1}
=
\eta_n
+
\alpha
\left(
T(x_{n+1})
-
\nabla\psi(\eta_n)
\right).
\]

The metric is

\[
G_n
=
\nabla^2\psi(\eta_n).
\]

Expanding,

\[
G_{n+1}
=
G_n
+
\alpha
\nabla^3\psi(\eta_n)
\left[
T(x_{n+1})-\nabla\psi(\eta_n)
\right]
+
O(\alpha^2).
\]

Taking expectation under the current model gives zero first-order drift, while the second-order drift is governed by cumulants. Hence recursive learning induces stochastic metric evolution whose noise structure is encoded by higher-order information tensors.

## 10. Applications

### 10.1 Information geometry

RPG generalizes classical information geometry by replacing a fixed statistical metric with a recursively generated family

\[
g^{(0)},g^{(1)},g^{(2)},\dots
\]

This allows one to study:

1. time-dependent Fisher information;
2. recursive \(\alpha\)-connections;
3. curvature evolution under Bayesian updating;
4. geometric strain induced by probabilistic transformations;
5. history-dependent statistical manifolds.

In particular, one may define recursive \(\alpha\)-connections by

\[
\Gamma_{ij}^{(n,\alpha)k}
=
\Gamma_{ij}^{(n,0)k}
+
\frac{\alpha}{2}
g_{(n)}^{k\ell}
T_{\ell ij}^{(n)},
\]

where

\[
T_{ijk}^{(n)}
=
\mathbb{E}_{P_n}
[
\partial_i\log P_n\,
\partial_j\log P_n\,
\partial_k\log P_n
].
\]

Thus the full hierarchy of information-geometric structures becomes recursive.

---

### 10.2 Bayesian inference

RPG provides a geometric interpretation of sequential Bayesian inference.

The posterior sequence

\[
\pi_{n+1}
\propto
L_{n+1}\pi_n
\]

induces:

1. a potential recursion;
2. a precision recursion;
3. a Fisher covariance recursion;
4. a curvature recursion;
5. a volume-element recursion.

Jeffreys-type priors at stage \(n\) become

\[
\pi_J^{(n)}(\theta)
\propto
\sqrt{\det g^{(n)}(\theta)}.
\]

Thus the prior itself may evolve recursively as the geometry generated by posterior data changes.

---

### 10.3 Sequential statistics and experimental design

In sequential experimental design, one chooses the next observation to optimize future geometry. Natural criteria include:

\[
D_{n+1}
=
\arg\max_D
\det g^{(n+1)}(D),
\]

for D-optimality;

\[
D_{n+1}
=
\arg\min_D
\operatorname{tr}\left(g^{(n+1)}(D)^{-1}\right),
\]

for A-optimality;

\[
D_{n+1}
=
\arg\max_D
R^{(n+1)}(D),
\]

for curvature-based model discrimination.

RPG turns experimental design into recursive geometric control.

### 10.4 Artificial intelligence and machine learning

RPG is especially natural in learning systems where the model’s predictive distribution evolves recursively.

#### Natural-gradient learning

For parameters \(\theta\), let \(P_\theta\) be the model distribution. The Fisher matrix is

\[
F(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{P_\theta}
[
\nabla_\theta\log P_\theta
\nabla_\theta\log P_\theta^\top
].
\]

A recursive natural-gradient update is

\[
\theta_{n+1}
=
\theta_n
-
\eta_n
F(\theta_n)^{-1}
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_n(\theta_n).
\]

In RPG language,

\[
g^{(n)}=F(\theta_n),
\]

so learning occurs on a recursively changing information manifold.

#### Bayesian neural networks

Let \(f(x;\theta)\) be a neural network. In a Laplace approximation, the posterior precision after data \(\{(x_k,y_k)\}\) is

\[
H_n
=
H_0
+
\sum_{k=1}^n
J_k^\top \Lambda_k J_k,
\]

where

\[
J_k
=
\nabla_\theta f(x_k;\theta_n)
\]

is the Jacobian and \(\Lambda_k\) is the likelihood precision. This is a recursive metric recursion:

\[
g^{(n+1)}
=
g^{(n)}
+
J_{n+1}^\top \Lambda_{n+1}J_{n+1}.
\]

Thus Bayesian deep learning is naturally formulated as RPG.

#### Recursive kernel geometry

Let

\[
K_n(x,x')
=
\mathbb{E}_{P_n}
[
\nabla_\theta f(x;\theta)
\cdot
\nabla_\theta f(x';\theta)
].
\]

This kernel induces a parameter-space metric

\[
G_n
=
\int
K_n(x,x)\,dP_n(x).
\]

As the data distribution or model distribution changes, \(K_n\) changes, and the geometry is recursively regenerated.

#### Reinforcement learning

For a policy \(\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\), the Fisher metric is

\[
g_{ij}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_\theta}
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_\theta}
[
\partial_i\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)
\partial_j\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)
].
\]

The state distribution \(d_\theta\) itself changes recursively under policy updates. Hence reinforcement learning induces a doubly recursive geometry:

\[
\pi_\theta\mapsto \pi_{\theta_{n+1}},
\qquad
d_{\theta_n}\mapsto d_{\theta_{n+1}}.
\]

Trust-region methods are therefore discrete geodesic or metric-proximal methods in recursive probability geometry.

## 11. Discussion and Open Problems

Recursive Probability Geometry proposes a shift in perspective: probability is not merely represented by geometry; probability actively generates geometry.

The core recursion

\[
P_{n+1}=\mathcal{R}_n(P_n),
\qquad
g_{ij}^{(n)}=\Gamma(P_n),
\]

unifies several phenomena:

1. Bayesian posterior precision accumulation;
2. Fisher information evolution;
3. natural-gradient learning;
4. Markov contraction;
5. geometric concentration of uncertainty;
6. recursive kernel and neural tangent geometry.

Several open problems arise.

### 11.1 Infinite-dimensional foundations

A rigorous theory of RPG on Banach, Fréchet, or Orlicz manifolds is needed, particularly for singular measures, discrete-continuous mixtures, and neural network function spaces.

### 11.2 Optimal metric generators

Given a learning task, one may ask which \(\Gamma\) optimizes stability, generalization, or inferential efficiency. This leads to variational principles over metric generators.

### 11.3 Curvature regularization

Recursive curvature may be used as a diagnostic for model collapse, overfitting, or identifiability loss. Controlling scalar curvature or Ricci curvature during training is a promising direction.

### 11.4 Noncommutative and quantum extensions

Probability measures may be replaced by density matrices, and recursive updates by quantum channels. The corresponding RPG framework would connect information geometry with quantum information theory.

### 11.5 Geometric learning algorithms

RPG suggests new algorithms based on recursive geodesic stepping, curvature-aware regularization, and metric-adaptive stochastic optimization.

---

## 12. Conclusion

Recursive Probability Geometry provides a unified formalism in which probability distributions generate their own geometric environment. The central relation

\[
g_{ij}^{(n)}=\Gamma(P_n)
\]

elevates probability from a static object of inference to a dynamic geometric engine. Bayesian updating becomes metric accumulation, learning becomes motion through recursively generated geometry, and uncertainty becomes curvature and volume.

The framework is broad enough to encompass Fisher-Rao geometry, Hessian precision geometry, kernel methods, and recursive learning systems, while remaining specific enough to yield concrete tensorial recursion laws. RPG therefore offers a natural language for modern problems in information geometry, statistical inference, and artificial intelligence.
