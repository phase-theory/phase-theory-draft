# Recursive Infinity Theory: Generative Hierarchies, Growth Operators, and the Architecture of Transfinite Structure

**Preprint**

---

## Abstract

Recursive Infinity Theory (RIT) develops a generative foundation for infinite mathematical structures. Rather than postulating a completed transfinite cardinal sequence
\[
\aleph_0,\aleph_1,\aleph_2,\ldots
\]
as a primitive ontological datum, RIT constructs infinite hierarchies from an initial structure \(\mathcal I_0\) by means of an explicit growth operator \(\Gamma\):
\[
\mathcal I_{n+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_n).
\]
The theory is formulated abstractly in categorical terms, then specialized to set theory, model theory, computability theory, and large-cardinal analogues. In the set-theoretic instantiation, powerset growth recovers a shifted cumulative hierarchy and generates the beth sequence as a derived cardinal invariant. In model theory, elementary-extension growth produces recursively saturated limits and controlled elementary towers. In computability, jump operators and transfinite recursive ordinals yield an effective version of recursive infinity bounded by the hyperarithmetic hierarchy. Finally, RIT supplies structural analogues of inaccessible, Mahlo, and measurable large cardinals as closure, stationarity, and embedding principles internal to generated hierarchies.

The central claim is not that classical infinity is false, but that the architecture of infinity can be reorganized: infinity is not merely assumed; it is produced by law.

**Keywords.** recursive infinity, growth operator, transfinite hierarchy, powerset, elementary extension, Turing jump, recursively saturated model, large cardinal analogue, categorical recursion.

## 1. Introduction

Classical set theory treats infinite cardinality as already completed. The sequence
\[
\aleph_0,\aleph_1,\aleph_2,\ldots
\]
is obtained by iterating the cardinal successor operation on well-ordered cardinalities. In that framework, infinity is primarily an ontological assumption: once one has the set-theoretic universe, the alephs follow.

Recursive Infinity Theory reverses the order of explanation. The primitive notion is not a completed cardinal sequence but a recursive law of growth:
\[
\mathcal I_{0}\longmapsto \mathcal I_{1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_0)
\longmapsto \mathcal I_{2}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_1)
\longmapsto\cdots
\longmapsto \mathcal I_{n+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_n).
\]
The infinite is thereby generated by iteration of an operator \(\Gamma\). The central objects of study are the pairs
\[
(\mathcal I_0,\Gamma),
\]
where \(\mathcal I_0\) is a base structure and \(\Gamma\) is a growth operator producing a strictly richer structure from a given one.

Three principles organize RIT.

1. **Generativity.** Infinite structures are produced by explicit operations, not merely postulated as completed totalities.

2. **Controlled growth.** The operator \(\Gamma\) is required to satisfy structural conditions—extensionality, monotonicity, inflation, rank increase, continuity, or Cantorian non-collapse—depending on the intended application.

3. **Reflective closure.** Limit stages are obtained by directed colimits, unions, or effective joins, allowing finite recursive generation to pass into transfinite structure.

The finite recursion
\[
\mathcal I_{n+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_n)
\]
is only the first approximation. The full theory requires transfinite extension:
\[
\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_\alpha),
\qquad
\mathcal I_\lambda=\varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha
\]
for limit ordinals \(\lambda\). The resulting tower
\[
\bigl(\mathcal I_\alpha\bigr)_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}
\]
is called an R-hierarchy.

RIT is compatible with classical set theory but reinterprets its infinite objects. The alephs and beths become invariants associated with particular growth operators, rather than the starting point of the theory.

## 2. Abstract Recursive Infinity

### 2.1 Growth presentations

We work externally in a metatheory capable of supporting categories and ordinal recursion, for example ZFC or Morse–Kelley class theory. The internal formalization can later be extracted as a many-sorted first-order theory, but the categorical formulation is cleaner.

**Definition 2.1.** A *growth presentation* is a quadruple
\[
(\mathbf C,\Gamma,\mathcal I_0,\iota),
\]
where:

1. \(\mathbf C\) is a category whose objects are structures and whose morphisms are structure-preserving embeddings or elementary maps;
2. \(\Gamma:\mathbf C\to\mathbf C\) is an endofunctor, called the *growth operator*;
3. \(\mathcal I_0\in\mathbf C\) is the *base structure*;
4. \(\iota:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf C}\Rightarrow\Gamma\) is a natural transformation, called the *inflation map*.

For each object \(X\), the component
\[
\iota_X:X\to\Gamma(X)
\]
expresses that \(\Gamma(X)\) contains \(X\) as a substructure, possibly in a canonical way.

In set-like categories, \(\iota_X\) is often literal inclusion:
\[
X\subseteq\Gamma(X).
\]

### 2.2 R-hierarchies

Given a growth presentation \((\mathbf C,\Gamma,\mathcal I_0,\iota)\), define the associated R-hierarchy by transfinite recursion.

**Definition 2.2.** The *R-hierarchy generated by \((\mathbf C,\Gamma,\mathcal I_0,\iota)\)* is the system
\[
\bigl(\mathcal I_\alpha,i_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)_{\alpha\leq\beta}
\]
satisfying:

1. Base:
   \[
   \mathcal I_0=\mathcal I_0.
   \]

2. Successor:
   \[
   \mathcal I_{\alpha+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_\alpha),
   \qquad
   i_{\alpha,\alpha+1}=\iota_{\mathcal I_\alpha}.
   \]

3. Limit:
   \[
   \mathcal I_\lambda=\varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha
   \]
   for limit ordinals \(\lambda\), with canonical transition maps
   \[
   i_{\alpha\lambda}:\mathcal I_\alpha\to\mathcal I_\lambda.
   \]

When \(\mathbf C\) is a category of sets, structures, or models, the colimit at limit stages is usually a directed union.

### 2.3 Existence and uniqueness

**Theorem 2.3.** Let \(\mathbf C\) be a category possessing colimits of ordinal-indexed directed chains. For any growth presentation \((\mathbf C,\Gamma,\mathcal I_0,\iota)\), there exists an R-hierarchy
\[
\bigl(\mathcal I_\alpha,i_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)_{\alpha\leq\beta}
\]
satisfying Definition 2.2. It is unique up to unique isomorphism of towers.

**Proof.** The construction proceeds by transfinite recursion.

Assume \(\mathcal I_\alpha\) and the transition maps \(i_{\beta\alpha}\) have been constructed for all \(\beta<\alpha\). If \(\alpha=\gamma+1\), define
\[
\mathcal I_{\alpha}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_\gamma),
\qquad
i_{\gamma\alpha}=\iota_{\mathcal I_\gamma}.
\]
For \(\beta<\gamma\), set
\[
i_{\beta\alpha}=i_{\gamma\alpha}\circ i_{\beta\gamma}.
\]

If \(\alpha\) is limit, define
\[
\mathcal I_\alpha=\varinjlim_{\beta<\alpha}\mathcal I_\beta.
\]
The universal property of the colimit supplies the canonical maps
\[
i_{\beta\alpha}:\mathcal I_\beta\to\mathcal I_\alpha.
\]
Compatibility follows from the universal property at each limit and from naturality of \(\iota\) at successors.

Uniqueness follows by a parallel induction: any two towers satisfying the same recursive specification are isomorphic at stage \(0\), the isomorphism is preserved by \(\Gamma\) at successors, and is forced by the universal property at limits. \(\square\)

### 2.4 Admissibility conditions

Not every endofunctor should count as a genuine infinity-generating operator. We isolate several structural conditions.

**Definition 2.4.** Let \((\mathbf C,\Gamma,\mathcal I_0,\iota)\) be a growth presentation. The operator \(\Gamma\) is:

1. *inflationary* if each \(\iota_X:X\to\Gamma(X)\) is a monomorphism;

2. *monotone* if \(X\hookrightarrow Y\) implies \(\Gamma(X)\hookrightarrow\Gamma(Y)\);

3. *rank-increasing* if there is a rank function
   \[
   \rho:\mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf C)\to\mathrm{Ord}
   \]
   such that
   \[
   \rho(\Gamma(X))>\rho(X);
   \]

4. *Cantorian* if there is no morphism
   \[
   \Gamma(X)\to X
   \]
   in \(\mathbf C\);

5. *continuous at \(\lambda\)* if
   \[
   \Gamma\left(\varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha\right)
   \cong
   \varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\Gamma(\mathcal I_\alpha);
   \]

6. *accessible* if it preserves \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits for some regular cardinal \(\kappa\).

Different applications emphasize different subsets of these conditions.

### 2.5 Non-collapse of strict recursive infinity

The most basic requirement for a genuine infinite hierarchy is that later stages do not collapse into earlier ones.

**Theorem 2.5.** Suppose \(\Gamma\) is Cantorian and the transition maps
\[
i_{\alpha\beta}:\mathcal I_\alpha\to\mathcal I_\beta
\]
exist for \(\alpha\leq\beta\). Then for every \(\beta>\alpha\), there is no morphism
\[
\mathcal I_\beta\to\mathcal I_\alpha.
\]

**Proof.** Fix \(\beta>\alpha\). Since \(\alpha+1\leq\beta\), the tower contains a transition map
\[
i_{\alpha+1,\beta}:\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}\to\mathcal I_\beta.
\]
If there were a morphism
\[
r:\mathcal I_\beta\to\mathcal I_\alpha,
\]
then composing gives
\[
\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}
\xrightarrow{i_{\alpha+1,\beta}}
\mathcal I_\beta
\xrightarrow{r}
\mathcal I_\alpha.
\]
But
\[
\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_\alpha),
\]
so this yields a morphism
\[
\Gamma(\mathcal I_\alpha)\to\mathcal I_\alpha,
\]
contradicting Cantorianity. \(\square\)

Thus a Cantorian growth operator produces a genuinely non-collapsing hierarchy.

### 2.6 Fixed points under continuity

Although Cantorian growth prevents set-sized fixed points, continuous growth operators admit closure at limit stages.

**Theorem 2.6.** Let \(\lambda\) be a limit ordinal and suppose \(\Gamma\) preserves the colimit of the chain
\[
(\mathcal I_\alpha)_{\alpha<\lambda}.
\]
Then
\[
\Gamma(\mathcal I_\lambda)\cong\mathcal I_\lambda.
\]

**Proof.** By definition,
\[
\mathcal I_\lambda=\varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha.
\]
By continuity,
\[
\Gamma(\mathcal I_\lambda)
\cong
\varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\Gamma(\mathcal I_\alpha).
\]
But
\[
\Gamma(\mathcal I_\alpha)=\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\Gamma(\mathcal I_\lambda)
\cong
\varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}.
\]
Since the map \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha+1\) is cofinal in \(\lambda\), the chains
\[
(\mathcal I_\alpha)_{\alpha<\lambda}
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\mathcal I_{\alpha+1})_{\alpha<\lambda}
\]
have the same colimit. Therefore
\[
\Gamma(\mathcal I_\lambda)\cong\mathcal I_\lambda.
\]
\(\square\)

This theorem identifies the precise sense in which a limit stage may be a recursive infinity: it is closed under the generating law.

## 3. Set-Theoretic Recursive Infinity

The most direct set-theoretic instantiation uses the powerset operation as the growth operator.

### 3.1 Powerset growth

Let
\[
\mathcal I_0=V_\omega,
\]
the set of hereditarily finite sets. Define
\[
\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal P(\mathcal I_\alpha),
\]
and for limit \(\lambda\),
\[
\mathcal I_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha.
\]

Because \(V_\omega\) is transitive and powersets of transitive sets are transitive, each \(\mathcal I_\alpha\) is transitive.

### 3.2 Recovery of a shifted cumulative hierarchy

**Theorem 3.1.** For every ordinal \(\alpha\),
\[
\mathcal I_\alpha=V_{\omega+\alpha}.
\]

**Proof.** By transfinite induction on \(\alpha\).

For \(\alpha=0\),
\[
\mathcal I_0=V_\omega=V_{\omega+0}.
\]

Assume
\[
\mathcal I_\alpha=V_{\omega+\alpha}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal I_{\alpha+1}
=
\mathcal P(\mathcal I_\alpha)
=
\mathcal P(V_{\omega+\alpha})
=
V_{\omega+\alpha+1}.
\]

If \(\lambda\) is limit, then by induction,
\[
\mathcal I_\lambda
=
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha
=
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}V_{\omega+\alpha}.
\]
Since ordinal addition is continuous in the right argument,
\[
\omega+\lambda=\sup_{\alpha<\lambda}(\omega+\alpha),
\]
and the cumulative hierarchy is continuous at limits:
\[
V_{\omega+\lambda}
=
\bigcup_{\beta<\omega+\lambda}V_\beta
=
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}V_{\omega+\alpha}.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathcal I_\lambda=V_{\omega+\lambda}.
\]
\(\square\)

Therefore powerset-based RIT reconstructs a shifted segment of the von Neumann hierarchy.

### 3.3 Derived cardinal sequence

Let
\[
\kappa_\alpha=|\mathcal I_\alpha|.
\]
Since \(\mathcal I_0=V_\omega\) is countable,
\[
\kappa_0=\aleph_0.
\]
At successors,
\[
\kappa_{\alpha+1}
=
|\mathcal P(\mathcal I_\alpha)|
=
2^{|\mathcal I_\alpha|}
=
2^{\kappa_\alpha}.
\]
At limits,
\[
\kappa_\lambda
=
\left|\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha\right|
=
\sup_{\alpha<\lambda}\kappa_\alpha,
\]
under the usual cardinal arithmetic for the beth hierarchy.

Thus
\[
\kappa_\alpha=\beth_\alpha,
\]
where
\[
\beth_0=\aleph_0,
\qquad
\beth_{\alpha+1}=2^{\beth_\alpha},
\qquad
\beth_\lambda=\sup_{\alpha<\lambda}\beth_\alpha.
\]

The aleph sequence is not primitive in this construction. The generated sequence is the beth sequence associated with powerset growth. The classical alephs may be recovered by imposing well-ordering and cardinal successor operations, but RIT treats those as additional structure.

### 3.4 R-cardinals

In RIT, cardinality is secondary to generation.

**Definition 3.2.** An *R-cardinal* is an equivalence class of R-structures under bijective equivalence of underlying domains. If \(\mathcal I\) and \(\mathcal J\) are R-structures, write
\[
\mathcal I\equiv_{\mathrm{card}}\mathcal J
\]
when there exists a bijection
\[
|\mathcal I|\cong|\mathcal J|.
\]

A growth operator \(\Gamma\) induces a map on R-cardinals:
\[
[\mathcal I]_{\mathrm{card}}
\longmapsto
[\Gamma(\mathcal I)]_{\mathrm{card}}.
\]

For powerset growth, this induced map is
\[
\kappa\longmapsto 2^\kappa.
\]

### 3.5 Strictness and Cantor’s theorem

**Proposition 3.3.** Powerset growth is Cantorian.

**Proof.** For any set \(X\), Cantor’s theorem gives no surjection
\[
X\to\mathcal P(X).
\]
If there were an injection
\[
\mathcal P(X)\hookrightarrow X,
\]
then one could define a surjection
\[
X\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal P(X)
\]
by sending each element in the image of the injection to its preimage and all other elements to \(\varnothing\). This contradicts Cantor’s theorem. Hence no injection
\[
\mathcal P(X)\hookrightarrow X
\]
exists. In the category of sets with injections, powerset growth is therefore Cantorian. \(\square\)

Consequently, the hierarchy
\[
V_\omega,V_{\omega+1},V_{\omega+2},\ldots
\]
does not collapse at any finite or transfinite stage.

## 4. Model-Theoretic Recursive Infinity

RIT also applies to the generation of models of a first-order theory.

Let \(L\) be a first-order language and \(T\) a consistent \(L\)-theory.

### 4.1 Elementary growth operators

**Definition 4.1.** A *model-theoretic growth operator* for \(T\) is a functor
\[
\Gamma_T:\mathrm{Mod}(T)\to\mathrm{Mod}(T)
\]
such that for every model \(M\models T\),
\[
M\preccurlyeq\Gamma_T(M),
\]
and the inclusion
\[
\iota_M:M\hookrightarrow\Gamma_T(M)
\]
is elementary.

Given a base model \(M_0\models T\), define
\[
M_{n+1}=\Gamma_T(M_n),
\]
and at limits
\[
M_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}M_\alpha
\]
when the chain is elementary.

### 4.2 Elementary towers

**Theorem 4.2.** Suppose \(\Gamma_T\) is a model-theoretic growth operator. Then for all \(\alpha<\beta\),
\[
M_\alpha\preccurlyeq M_\beta.
\]
Moreover, if \(\lambda\) is limit and
\[
M_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}M_\alpha,
\]
then for every \(\alpha<\lambda\),
\[
M_\alpha\preccurlyeq M_\lambda.
\]

**Proof.** The successor case is immediate from the definition of \(\Gamma_T\):
\[
M_\alpha\preccurlyeq M_{\alpha+1}.
\]
Elementarity is transitive, so for finite and successor ordinals the claim follows by induction.

For limit \(\lambda\), the chain
\[
(M_\alpha)_{\alpha<\lambda}
\]
is an elementary chain. By the Tarski–Vaught theorem on unions of elementary chains,
\[
M_\alpha\preccurlyeq\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}M_\beta=M_\lambda.
\]
\(\square\)

Thus model-theoretic R-hierarchies are automatically elementary towers.

### 4.3 Type-realizing growth

A particularly important growth operator realizes specified types.

Let \(M\models T\). A type \(p(x)\) over \(M\) is a set of formulas with parameters from \(M\). A growth operator may be chosen so that \(\Gamma_T(M)\) realizes a designated class of types over \(M\).

**Definition 4.3.** A growth operator \(\Gamma_T\) is *recursive-type-realizing* if whenever \(M_n\) is a stage in the R-hierarchy and \(p(x)\) is a type over \(M_n\) that is:

1. consistent with \(\operatorname{Th}(M_n)\), and
2. recursive relative to a fixed effective presentation of \(M_n\),

then \(p(x)\) is realized in \(M_{n+1}\).

Such operators exist under standard compactness and Löwenheim–Skolem arguments when the class of types is suitably bounded.

### 4.4 Recursive saturation of the limit

**Definition 4.4.** Let
\[
M_\omega=\bigcup_{n<\omega}M_n.
\]
The model \(M_\omega\) is *R-saturated relative to the generating chain* if every type over some finite stage \(M_n\) that is recursive in the diagram of \(M_n\) and consistent with the theory of \(M_\omega\) is realized in \(M_\omega\).

**Theorem 4.5.** If \(\Gamma_T\) is recursive-type-realizing, then
\[
M_\omega=\bigcup_{n<\omega}M_n
\]
is R-saturated relative to the generating chain.

**Proof.** Let \(p(x)\) be a recursive type over parameters from \(M_\omega\), recursive in the diagram of some stage \(M_n\), and consistent with \(\operatorname{Th}(M_\omega)\). Since the parameters are finitely many and each lies in some finite stage, there is \(n\) such that all parameters lie in \(M_n\).

By recursive-type-realization, \(p(x)\) is realized in
\[
M_{n+1}=\Gamma_T(M_n).
\]
Since
\[
M_{n+1}\subseteq M_\omega,
\]
the type is realized in \(M_\omega\). \(\square\)

In the countable effective case, this yields recursively saturated models as limits of recursive elementary growth.

### 4.5 Stability and controlled growth

If \(T\) is stable, one may choose \(\Gamma_T\) to preserve stability-theoretic bounds. For example, if \(T\) is stable in \(\kappa\) and \(|M_n|=\kappa\), then under standard hypotheses one may choose
\[
|M_{n+1}|=\kappa
\]
while still realizing the desired class of nonforking types. In such cases, RIT produces not merely increasing cardinal size but increasing model-theoretic richness at controlled cardinality.

This suggests a model-theoretic distinction absent from pure cardinal recursion:

\[
\text{cardinal growth} \neq \text{structural growth}.
\]

A hierarchy may grow in saturation, definable complexity, or type-realization capacity without increasing cardinality.

## 5. Computability-Theoretic Recursive Infinity

Recursive infinity has a natural effective counterpart. Here the structures are presented with computable or arithmetical diagrams, and growth operators increase computational complexity.

### 5.1 Degrees of presentations

Let \(A\) be a structure with atomic diagram \(D(A)\). Its Turing degree is
\[
\deg(A)=\deg_T(D(A)).
\]

An effective growth operator may be viewed as a map on degrees:
\[
\Gamma:\mathcal D_T\to\mathcal D_T,
\]
where \(\mathcal D_T\) is the structure of Turing degrees.

The canonical example is the Turing jump:
\[
J(d)=d'.
\]

### 5.2 Jump growth

Let
\[
d_0=\mathbf 0
\]
be the degree of computable sets. Define
\[
d_{n+1}=d_n'.
\]
Then
\[
d_n=\mathbf 0^{(n)}.
\]

At the limit,
\[
d_\omega=\bigoplus_{n<\omega}d_n
\]
is the effective join of the finite jumps. This degree is usually denoted
\[
\mathbf 0^{(\omega)}.
\]

More generally, for a structure \(A\) of degree \(a\), define
\[
a_{n+1}=a_n'.
\]
Then
\[
a_n=a^{(n)},
\qquad
a_\omega=a^{(\omega)}.
\]

### 5.3 Arithmetic growth operators

**Definition 5.1.** An arithmetic growth operator of jump rank \(r\) is a uniform operator \(\Gamma\) such that for every structure \(A\),
\[
\deg(\Gamma(A))\equiv_T \deg(A)^{(r)}.
\]

**Theorem 5.2.** Let \(\Gamma\) be an arithmetic growth operator of jump rank \(r\). If
\[
A_{n+1}=\Gamma(A_n),
\]
then
\[
\deg(A_n)\equiv_T\deg(A_0)^{(rn)}.
\]

**Proof.** By induction. For \(n=0\), the statement is trivial. Suppose
\[
\deg(A_n)\equiv_T a^{(rn)}.
\]
Then
\[
\deg(A_{n+1})
\equiv_T
\deg(A_n)^{(r)}
\equiv_T
\left(a^{(rn)}\right)^{(r)}
=
a^{(r(n+1))}.
\]
\(\square\)

For jump growth, \(r=1\), and therefore
\[
\deg(A_n)\equiv_T a^{(n)}.
\]

### 5.4 Recursive ordinals and transfinite jumps

The finite jump hierarchy extends through recursive ordinals using Kleene’s \(\mathcal O\). For each notation \(a\in\mathcal O\) representing a recursive ordinal \(\alpha\), one defines the transfinite jump
\[
\mathbf 0^{(\alpha)}.
\]

At successor stages,
\[
\mathbf 0^{(\alpha+1)}
=
\left(\mathbf 0^{(\alpha)}\right)'.
\]
At recursive limit stages \(\lambda\),
\[
\mathbf 0^{(\lambda)}
=
\bigoplus_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathbf 0^{(\alpha)},
\]
where the join is taken effectively along a recursive notation for \(\lambda\).

Thus effective RIT over recursive ordinals yields the hierarchy
\[
\mathbf 0,
\mathbf 0',
\mathbf 0'',
\ldots,
\mathbf 0^{(\omega)},
\mathbf 0^{(\omega+1)},
\ldots,
\mathbf 0^{(\alpha)},
\ldots
\qquad
(\alpha<\omega_1^{CK}).
\]

### 5.5 Hyperarithmetic boundary

The effective recursive infinities generated by computable ordinal recursion are exactly those below the Church–Kleene ordinal.

**Theorem 5.3.** For every recursive ordinal \(\alpha<\omega_1^{CK}\), the degree
\[
\mathbf 0^{(\alpha)}
\]
is obtainable by an effective R-hierarchy indexed by a recursive notation for \(\alpha\).

Conversely, no total computable growth process on natural-number indices can uniformly produce a complete presentation of all hyperarithmetic degrees or a notation for \(\omega_1^{CK}\) itself.

**Proof sketch.** The first claim follows by effective transfinite recursion along Kleene’s \(\mathcal O\). Given a notation \(a\) for a recursive ordinal, the jump hierarchy is uniformly definable along \(a\).

For the converse, the set \(\mathcal O\) of notations for recursive ordinals is \(\Pi^1_1\)-complete and not hyperarithmetic. If a total computable growth process uniformly produced a complete system of notations for all recursive ordinals, it would compute \(\mathcal O\), contradicting its non-hyperarithmeticity. \(\square\)

Thus computability-theoretic RIT exhibits a precise boundary: effective recursive infinity reaches exactly the hyperarithmetic hierarchy, but cannot internally generate the full admissible ordinal \(\omega_1^{CK}\) as a computable object.

## 6. Large-Cardinal Analogues in Recursive Infinity

RIT does not assume large cardinals. However, it provides a structural language for large-cardinal-like principles formulated as closure conditions on growth operators.

### 6.1 \(\Gamma\)-closure

Let \(\mathcal I\) be an R-structure with underlying domain \(|\mathcal I|\). For \(A\subseteq|\mathcal I|\), define the \(\Gamma\)-closure of \(A\) by
\[
\operatorname{Cl}_\Gamma(A)
=
\bigcap\{X\subseteq|\mathcal I|: A\subseteq X\text{ and }X\text{ is closed under }\Gamma\}.
\]

When \(\Gamma\) is a set-theoretic growth operator, this means closure under the operation generating the next stage.

### 6.2 \(\Gamma\)-inaccessibility

**Definition 6.1.** Let \(\kappa=|\mathcal I|\). The structure \(\mathcal I\) is *\(\Gamma\)-inaccessible* if:

1. \(\kappa\) is regular;
2. for every \(A\subseteq|\mathcal I|\) with \(|A|<\kappa\),
   \[
   |\operatorname{Cl}_\Gamma(A)|<\kappa.
   \]

This abstracts the idea that small inputs cannot generate the whole structure under \(\Gamma\).

### 6.3 Powerset growth and strong inaccessibility

Let \(\Gamma=\mathcal P\) and consider \(\mathcal I=V_\kappa\).

**Proposition 6.2.** \(V_\kappa\) is \(\mathcal P\)-inaccessible if and only if \(\kappa\) is strongly inaccessible.

**Proof.** Suppose \(\kappa\) is strongly inaccessible. Then \(\kappa\) is regular and for every \(\lambda<\kappa\),
\[
2^\lambda<\kappa.
\]
If \(A\subseteq V_\kappa\) has size \(\lambda<\kappa\), then its transitive closure has size \(<\kappa\), and iterating powerset fewer than \(\kappa\) many times remains below \(\kappa\) by regularity and strong limitness. Hence
\[
|\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal P}(A)|<\kappa.
\]

Conversely, suppose \(V_\kappa\) is \(\mathcal P\)-inaccessible. Regularity is part of the definition. For any \(\lambda<\kappa\), take \(A=\lambda\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal P}(A)
\]
contains \(\mathcal P(\lambda)\), so
\[
2^\lambda\leq|\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal P}(A)|<\kappa.
\]
Thus \(\kappa\) is a strong limit. Therefore \(\kappa\) is strongly inaccessible. \(\square\)

Thus classical inaccessibility appears in RIT as closure under a particular growth operator.

### 6.4 \(\Gamma\)-Mahlo and \(\Gamma\)-measurable analogues

One can ascend the large-cardinal hierarchy by replacing ordinary clubs and stationary sets with \(\Gamma\)-clubs.

**Definition 6.3.** A set \(C\subseteq\mathcal I\) is *\(\Gamma\)-club* if it is:

1. closed under \(\Gamma\)-closure;
2. unbounded in the internal rank order of \(\mathcal I\).

A set \(S\subseteq\mathcal I\) is *\(\Gamma\)-stationary* if it meets every \(\Gamma\)-club.

An R-structure \(\mathcal I\) is *\(\Gamma\)-Mahlo* if the set of \(\Gamma\)-inaccessible substructures of \(\mathcal I\) is \(\Gamma\)-stationary.

**Definition 6.4.** An R-structure \(\mathcal I\) is *\(\Gamma\)-measurable* if there exists a nonprincipal \(\kappa\)-complete ultrafilter \(U\) on \(\kappa=|\mathcal I|\) that is normal with respect to \(\Gamma\)-clubs.

Equivalently, one may formulate \(\Gamma\)-measurability through elementary embeddings.

### 6.5 Recursive elementary embeddings

**Definition 6.5.** Let
\[
j:(\mathcal I,\Gamma)\to(\mathcal J,\Gamma)
\]
be an elementary embedding between R-structures. The embedding is *\(\Gamma\)-compatible* if
\[
j\circ\Gamma=\Gamma\circ j.
\]

The critical point of \(j\), if it exists, is
\[
\operatorname{crit}(j)=\min\{x:j(x)\neq x\}.
\]

**Proposition 6.6.** Suppose \(j:(\mathcal I,\Gamma)\to(\mathcal J,\Gamma)\) is a nontrivial well-founded \(\Gamma\)-compatible elementary embedding with critical point \(\kappa\). Then \(\kappa\) is \(\Gamma\)-inaccessible in \(\mathcal I\), provided \(\Gamma\) is rank-monotone and \(j\) is strictly increasing on ranks above \(\kappa\).

**Proof sketch.** Let \(\rho\) be the rank function and suppose
\[
\rho(\Gamma(x))=g(\rho(x))
\]
for a monotone ordinal function \(g\). For every \(\alpha<\kappa\),
\[
j(\alpha)=\alpha.
\]
By \(\Gamma\)-compatibility,
\[
j(g(\alpha))
=
g(j(\alpha))
=
g(\alpha).
\]
Since \(j\) moves all ranks \(\geq\kappa\), the equality \(j(g(\alpha))=g(\alpha)\) forces
\[
g(\alpha)<\kappa.
\]
Thus \(\Gamma\) sends substructures of rank below \(\kappa\) to substructures of rank below \(\kappa\). Regularity of \(\kappa\) follows from the standard critical-point argument: a singular cofinal sequence below \(\kappa\) would be fixed by \(j\), contradicting the fact that \(j(\kappa)>\kappa\). Hence \(\kappa\) is \(\Gamma\)-inaccessible. \(\square\)

This gives a recursive analogue of the classical fact that the critical point of a nontrivial elementary embedding is large.

## 7. Categorical and Tensorial Formulation

RIT admits a categorical syntax and, for typed structures, a tensorial notation.

### 7.1 The category of recursive infinities

**Definition 7.1.** Let \(\mathbf{RIT}\) be the category whose objects are growth presentations
\[
(\mathbf C,\Gamma,\mathcal I_0,\iota),
\]
or, in a simplified internal form, pairs \((\mathcal I,\Gamma)\), and whose morphisms
\[
f:(\mathcal I,\Gamma)\to(\mathcal J,\Delta)
\]
satisfy
\[
f\circ\Gamma=\Delta\circ f.
\]

Equivalently, a morphism of R-hierarchies is a family
\[
f_\alpha:\mathcal I_\alpha\to\mathcal J_\alpha
\]
such that
\[
f_{\alpha+1}=\Gamma(f_\alpha)
\]
and the diagrams
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathcal I_\alpha & \xrightarrow{f_\alpha} & \mathcal J_\alpha\\
\downarrow{\iota_{\mathcal I_\alpha}} && \downarrow{\iota_{\mathcal J_\alpha}}\\
\mathcal I_{\alpha+1} & \xrightarrow{f_{\alpha+1}} & \mathcal J_{\alpha+1}
\end{array}
\]
commute.

This category captures the idea that recursive infinity is not a single object but a dynamical system.

### 7.2 Polynomial growth operators

Many natural growth operators are polynomial functors. Let \(S\) be a set of sorts. A many-sorted structure \(\mathcal I\) has components
\[
\mathcal I^s
\]
for each sort \(s\in S\).

A polynomial growth operator has the form
\[
(\Gamma\mathcal I)^s
=
\coprod_{\vec t\in S^{<\omega}}
G^s_{\vec t}
\times
\prod_{k=1}^{|\vec t|}
\mathcal I^{t_k},
\]
where \(G^s_{\vec t}\) is a set of constructors of output sort \(s\) taking arguments of sorts \(\vec t=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\).

In index notation, suppressing coherence isomorphisms, one may write
\[
(\Gamma\mathcal I)^s_i
=
\sum_{\vec t}
\sum_{g\in G^s_{\vec t}}
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{|\vec t|}
\mathcal I^{t_k}_{j_k}.
\]
Here the sum denotes categorical coproduct and the tensor denotes the product or relational combination of components.

The coefficients
\[
G^s_{\vec t}
\]
form a *growth tensor*. They encode how lower-stage components are assembled into higher-stage components.

### 7.3 Composition of growth operators

If \(\Gamma\) and \(\Delta\) are polynomial growth operators, their composite is again polynomial. In tensor notation, the coefficients of \(\Gamma\circ\Delta\) are obtained by contraction:
\[
(\Gamma\circ\Delta)^s_{\vec u}
=
\sum_{\vec t}
\Gamma^s_{\vec t}
\otimes
\Delta^{\vec t}_{\vec u}.
\]
This expresses the fact that recursive infinity can be iterated not only at the level of objects but also at the level of growth laws.

One may therefore form higher-order recursive infinities:
\[
\Gamma_{n+1}=\Phi(\Gamma_n),
\]
where \(\Phi\) is an operator on growth operators.

## 8. Canonical Examples

### 8.1 Powerset growth

\[
\Gamma_{\mathcal P}(X)=\mathcal P(X).
\]

This yields the shifted cumulative hierarchy:
\[
V_\omega,V_{\omega+1},V_{\omega+2},\ldots
\]
and the beth sequence:
\[
\aleph_0,2^{\aleph_0},2^{2^{\aleph_0}},\ldots
\]

### 8.2 Function-space growth

Let
\[
\Gamma_F(X)=X^X.
\]
For infinite \(\kappa=|X|\), assuming choice,
\[
|X^X|=\kappa^\kappa=2^\kappa.
\]
Thus the cardinal sequence again resembles the beth hierarchy, but the structures carry richer internal operation spaces.

This example shows that different growth operators may induce the same cardinal recursion while producing different structural content.

### 8.3 Term-algebra growth

Fix a finite signature \(\Sigma\). Let
\[
\Gamma_\Sigma(X)=T_\Sigma(X),
\]
the term algebra over \(X\).

If \(X\) is infinite, then
\[
|T_\Sigma(X)|=|X|.
\]
Thus cardinality does not increase. Nevertheless, syntactic and algebraic complexity increases: new terms, new definable operations, and new free structure are generated.

This is a crucial point: RIT is not reducible to cardinal growth.

### 8.4 Elementary-extension growth

Let \(T\) be a first-order theory and let
\[
\Gamma_T(M)
\]
be an elementary extension of \(M\) realizing a prescribed class of types. Then
\[
M_{n+1}=\Gamma_T(M_n)
\]
produces an elementary tower
\[
M_0\preccurlyeq M_1\preccurlyeq M_2\preccurlyeq\cdots.
\]
The limit
\[
M_\omega=\bigcup_{n<\omega}M_n
\]
inherits elementarity and, under suitable choices of \(\Gamma_T\), recursive saturation.

### 8.5 Jump growth

Let
\[
\Gamma_J(d)=d'.
\]
Then
\[
d_n=\mathbf 0^{(n)},
\qquad
d_\omega=\mathbf 0^{(\omega)}.
\]
This is the degree-theoretic analogue of recursive infinity.

## 9. Recursive Infinity versus Classical Infinity

The contrast with the classical framework may be summarized as follows.

| Aspect | Classical set theory | Recursive Infinity Theory |
|---|---:|---:|
| Primitive infinite object | Completed universe \(V\) or \(\omega\) | Base structure \(\mathcal I_0\) and operator \(\Gamma\) |
| Cardinal sequence | Alephs obtained by cardinal successor | Derived from iteration of \(\Gamma\) |
| Infinity status | Actual, completed | Generated, potentially extendable, closed at limits |
| Main operation | Successor cardinal, powerset | Recursive growth operator |
| Large cardinals | Existence axioms | Closure, stationarity, and embedding principles for \(\Gamma\) |
| Computability | External | Internal effective hierarchies via jumps and recursive ordinals |

RIT does not require rejection of classical actual infinity. Rather, it provides a generative decomposition:

\[
\text{completed infinity}
\approx
\text{closure of a recursive growth process}.
\]

The classical alephs may then be recovered as invariants of specific growth regimes.

## 10. Internal Formalization

Although the categorical presentation is external, RIT can be axiomatized internally.

A weak internal theory \(\mathsf{RIT}_0\) may be formulated in a many-sorted language with sorts for structures, elements, and indices. The axioms include:

1. **Base infinity.**
   \[
   \exists \mathcal I_0\,\mathrm{Inf}(\mathcal I_0).
   \]

2. **Growth existence.**
   \[
   \forall \mathcal I\,\exists \mathcal J\,(\mathcal J=\Gamma(\mathcal I)).
   \]

3. **Extensionality of growth.**
   \[
   \mathcal I\cong\mathcal J
   \implies
   \Gamma(\mathcal I)\cong\Gamma(\mathcal J).
   \]

4. **Inflation.**
   \[
   \forall \mathcal I\,\exists \iota_{\mathcal I}:\mathcal I\hookrightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal I).
   \]

5. **Strictness.**
   \[
   \neg\exists f:\Gamma(\mathcal I)\hookrightarrow\mathcal I.
   \]

6. **Limit closure.**
   For every directed chain \((\mathcal I_\alpha)_{\alpha<\lambda}\), there exists
   \[
   \mathcal I_\lambda=\varinjlim_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal I_\alpha.
   \]

7. **Recursive reflection schema.** For every finite fragment of the internal language, there is a stage \(\mathcal I_\alpha\) reflecting that fragment.

Stronger systems \(\mathsf{RIT}_1,\mathsf{RIT}_2,\ldots\) may add continuity, accessibility, saturation, or large-closure axioms.

## 11. Open Problems

Recursive Infinity Theory suggests several research directions.

### 11.1 Classification of growth operators

Classify growth operators \(\Gamma\) according to:

- cardinal recursion induced;
- model-theoretic preservation properties;
- computability rank;
- categorical accessibility;
- fixed-point behavior.

A central question is whether there is a canonical growth operator from which the usual set-theoretic universe is recoverable with minimal additional structure.

### 11.2 Canonical recursive infinity

Is there a privileged pair \((\mathcal I_0,\Gamma)\) such that:

1. \(\mathcal I_0\) is finitely or recursively specifiable;
2. \(\Gamma\) is conceptually inevitable;
3. the resulting hierarchy interprets ZF or ZFC?

Powerset growth is natural but already assumes substantial set-theoretic structure. Term-algebra growth is syntactically minimal but does not increase cardinality. The search for a minimal generative law remains open.

### 11.3 Proof-theoretic strength

Determine the proof-theoretic ordinals of internal theories \(\mathsf{RIT}_n\). In particular:

- What ordinal strength is required for finite-stage RIT?
- What strength is added by transfinite limit closure?
- What strength corresponds to \(\Gamma\)-inaccessibility or \(\Gamma\)-Mahlo principles?

### 11.4 Effective boundaries

Refine the hyperarithmetic boundary for effective RIT. Questions include:

- Which admissible ordinals arise as closure ordinals of natural effective growth operators?
- Can admissible-set growth operators internalize \(\omega_1^{CK}\) as a limit notion without computing it?
- What is the precise relation between effective R-hierarchies and higher-type computability?

### 11.5 Large-cardinal analogues

Develop consistency and interpretation results for \(\Gamma\)-large principles:

- \(\Gamma\)-inaccessible;
- \(\Gamma\)-Mahlo;
- \(\Gamma\)-measurable;
- \(\Gamma\)-compact;
- \(\Gamma\)-supercompact.

The goal is not to replicate classical large-cardinal ontology directly, but to understand which closure and embedding principles arise naturally from recursive generation.

---

## 12. Conclusion

Recursive Infinity Theory reorganizes the foundations of infinite structure. Instead of beginning with a completed transfinite cardinal sequence, RIT begins with a base structure and a law of growth:
\[
\mathcal I_{n+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal I_n).
\]
The infinite is then obtained by iteration, colimit, and closure.

In set theory, powerset growth recovers a shifted cumulative hierarchy and generates the beth sequence as a derived invariant. In model theory, elementary-extension growth yields elementary towers and recursively saturated limits. In computability, jump growth produces the hyperarithmetic hierarchy and encounters the Church–Kleene boundary. In the theory of large cardinals, RIT replaces raw existence postulates by structural closure, stationarity, and embedding principles relative to a growth operator.

The unifying thesis is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Infinity is not merely postulated; it is generated.}
}
\]

The content of an infinite hierarchy is determined by the law that produces it.
