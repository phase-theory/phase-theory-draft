Abstract

Silicon photonic geometric qubits establish a framework where quantum information is encoded into photonic geometry: paths, phases, modes, curvature, topology, and holonomic transformations. However, the physical substrate strongly influences achievable coherence, loss, nonlinear interaction strength, and scalability.

Silicon nitride (Si₃N₄) emerges as a uniquely suitable material platform for geometric quantum processors due to its combination of:

* Ultra-low optical loss
* Wide transparency window
* High optical quality factors
* Strong nonlinear photonic capabilities
* CMOS-compatible fabrication pathways
* Compatibility with heterogeneous quantum light sources

This paper develops Silicon Nitride Geometric Processors (SNGP): an integrated architecture where silicon nitride photonic circuits implement geometric qubits, holonomic logic, synthetic gauge fields, topological transport, and geometry-native quantum computation.

The processor is not designed as a conventional photonic circuit. It is a programmable quantum geometry substrate where waveguide topology, resonator manifolds, and phase landscapes define the computational space.

The architecture extends the Structural Sector from abstract geometric computation into a realistic integrated photonic processor platform.
