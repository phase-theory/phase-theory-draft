## Abstract

We derive a physical theory from Structural Genesis Theory (SGT). The central claim is that physical spacetime, gravitational curvature, gauge interactions, matter fields, and quantum superposition are not primitive ingredients of nature but stabilized outcomes of genesis operations acting on nascent structures. We introduce a pre-geometric genesis field \(S^{A}\), a structural connection \(\Gamma^{A}{}_{B\mu}\), a stabilization projector \(P^{A}{}_{B}\), and an abstraction map \(Q^{\alpha}{}_{A}\). Physical spacetime is obtained as the stabilized abstraction quotient of the genesis manifold. The spacetime metric is the pullback of an invariant proto-metric on the stable subspace. Structural curvature projects to Riemannian curvature and yields the Einstein–Hilbert action as the lowest-order closed-cycle invariant. The interaction operator of SGT generates gauge fields as connection components along the automorphism algebra of the stabilized interaction tensor. Differentiation produces matter multiplets; stabilization yields Dirac dynamics; abstraction produces the path-integral superposition principle. The resulting effective action contains general relativity, Yang–Mills theory, spinor matter, a scalar abstraction field, torsion-mediated four-fermion interactions, and higher-genesis corrections suppressed by a fundamental genesis length \(\ell_{\mathrm{G}}\). Novel consequences include a minimal lineage length, modified dispersion relations, residual torsion couplings to spin, abstraction-driven dark energy, cosmological birefringence through \(\theta F\widetilde F\), lineage-counting corrections to black-hole entropy, and hidden gauge sectors arising from unbroken interaction automorphisms.

**Keywords:** Structural Genesis Theory, pre-geometric physics, emergent spacetime, gauge fields from interaction, quantum abstraction, genesis curvature, stabilization projector, minimal length, torsion, dark sector.

## 5. Gauge Fields from the Interaction Operator

\[

\mathfrak{aut}(M).

\[
[t_{i},t_{j}]
=
f^{k}{}_{ij}t_{k}.
