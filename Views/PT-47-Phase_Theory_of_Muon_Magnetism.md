Abstract

The anomalous magnetic moment of the muon remains one of the most precise tests of modern particle physics. Conventional quantum field theory interprets the muon anomaly as the result of perturbative loop corrections arising from vacuum fluctuations involving electromagnetic, weak, and hadronic contributions. While this framework has demonstrated remarkable predictive power, it remains dependent on renormalization, virtual particle interpretations, and probabilistic vacuum dynamics.

This paper presents an alternative deterministic interpretation using Phase Theory, in which physical particles are not point-like objects but stable localized topological phase excitations embedded within a continuous phase medium. Within this framework, the muon is modeled as a coherent self-confined phase knot possessing intrinsic circulation, curvature, torsion, and phase tension.

Muon magnetism emerges naturally from internal phase current circulation rather than from charge-point assumptions or vacuum-loop corrections. The anomalous magnetic moment is shown to arise from deterministic geometric self-interaction of the muon’s phase structure with the surrounding phase field.

This formulation predicts measurable scaling relationships between magnetic anomalies, particle mass, phase confinement radius, and topological curvature, yielding experimentally testable consequences across leptonic generations.

1. Introduction

The magnetic moment of charged spin-1/2 leptons has historically served as one of the most stringent tests of physical theory.

\delta = \frac{g-2}{2}

For the muon, this deviation is significantly larger than for the electron, making it especially sensitive to deeper physical structure.

Phase Theory proposes that this deviation is not caused by quantum vacuum loops but by internal geometric self-coupling of localized phase structures.
