# Mediator-Free Correlation Persistence (MFCP)

## A Quantitative Theory of Correlation Survival During Interaction-Free Evolution

Research classification: Foundational theoretical framework and proposed quantitative research programme\
Primary domains: Quantum information theory, open quantum systems, decoherence, relativistic quantum theory, quantum networks, and nonequilibrium dynamics

## Abstract

Quantum correlations can persist over a finite interval during which two subsystems have no direct interaction. Their continued existence does not, by itself, imply that a signal, particle, field excitation, or other physical influence continuously travels between them. The persistence of a relational property of a joint quantum state must therefore be distinguished from the dynamical transmission of a new influence.

This paper develops Mediator-Free Correlation Persistence (MFCP) as a quantitative framework for investigating that distinction. The central object is a correlation witness \\(W(t)\\), evaluated over an experimentally specified interval \\([t_0,t_1]\\), during which the direct interaction between the subsystems is independently characterized. A candidate persistence functional is defined by

\\[ \Pi_W[t_0,t_1] = \frac{1}{t_1-t_0} \int\_{t_0}^{t_1}W(t)\\,dt. \\]

The framework distinguishes correlation persistence from entanglement persistence, Bell-nonlocality persistence, quantum-memory survival, and causal communication. These are related but non-equivalent phenomena. In particular, a nonzero connected correlation does not certify entanglement, and entanglement does not automatically imply Bell inequality violation for a given measurement configuration.

A general dynamical model is developed for two subsystems undergoing local or approximately local evolution, environmental decoherence, residual direct coupling, and correlated environmental noise. The ideal interaction-free case is formulated using product quantum channels. Under such evolution, the joint state may retain correlations even though the subsystems undergo no direct interaction. The paper establishes that local trace-preserving operations cannot increase standard entanglement monotones on average across the bipartition, while also demonstrating that local unitary evolution can preserve entanglement indefinitely in ideal models. The persistence of classical correlations, entanglement, and Bell nonlocality is then analyzed separately.

The principal research target is a universal or universality-class-specific persistence law relating environmental coupling, initial state structure, subsystem separation, and witness decay. Candidate exponential and non-Markovian decay models are introduced, together with dimensionless persistence parameters and operational bounds. The proposed experimental programme varies the interaction-free interval, separation, local noise, and environmental correlations while measuring the same independently calibrated witness.

The established foundation is that correlations can survive without continuous direct interaction. The proposed contribution is a predictive law governing how selected correlation resources persist under specified dynamical conditions. MFCP does not posit a new nonlocal mechanism, prove the absence of every possible mediator, or establish that correlations are literally timeless. Its objective is to quantify the difference between preserving an existing relational state and transmitting a new causal influence.

Central proposition: A joint quantum state can preserve measurable correlations during evolution generated by local operations alone; the persistence of those correlations is not equivalent to continuous inter-subsystem signaling.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The persistence problem

Consider two quantum subsystems, \\(A\\) and \\(B\\), initially prepared in a correlated state:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(t_0). \\]

After preparation, the subsystems are separated and their direct interaction is removed or suppressed. They then evolve for a finite interval:

\\[ t_0\leq t\leq t_1. \\]

During this interval, each subsystem may undergo local evolution, environmental coupling, thermal fluctuations, measurement, or other forms of dynamical disturbance.

A correlation witness is measured at successive times:

\\[ W(t_0),W(t_1),\ldots,W(t_n). \\]

The central question is whether the correlation survives despite the absence of direct interaction between the subsystems.

A positive answer does not require a continuous signal traveling from one subsystem to the other. It may instead reflect the fact that the joint state established during preparation continues to evolve under local dynamics.

This distinction is elementary in the mathematics of composite quantum systems, but it has important operational consequences.

### 1.2 Three physically different processes

The following processes must be separated.

Preparation: A joint state is created through an interaction, common source, or other preparation procedure.

Persistence: The resulting joint state evolves while the subsystems have no direct interaction.

Communication: An intervention at one subsystem produces a controllable change in the statistics of the other subsystem.

These processes are not interchangeable.

A state may be prepared through an interaction and subsequently preserve correlations after that interaction ends. Conversely, a state may lose its entanglement even though the subsystems remain physically close.

The persistence of correlations therefore depends on the state and subsequent dynamics, not simply on whether a direct interaction remains active.

### 1.3 The MFCP hypothesis

Let:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(t) \\]

denote the joint state of two subsystems.

Let:

\\[ W[\rho\_{AB}(t)] \\]

be a specified correlation witness.

Let:

\\[ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}(t) \\]

denote the independently characterized direct interaction Hamiltonian.

The ideal mediator-free persistence regime is defined by:

\\[ \boxed{ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}(t)=0 \qquad \forall t\in[t_0,t_1]. } \\]

The corresponding operational proposition is:

\\[ \boxed{ W[\rho\_{AB}(t)]\neq0 \quad \text{for some or all }t\in[t_0,t_1], } \\]

despite the absence of direct interaction.

The term mediator-free is used here in a restricted operational sense: no direct inter-subsystem interaction is required during the specified persistence interval. It does not establish that no shared environmental degree of freedom, common field, background interaction, or deeper microscopic description exists.

### 1.4 Research objectives

The present work develops six objectives:

1. Define an experimentally meaningful correlation-persistence functional.
2. Establish the quantum dynamical conditions under which correlations survive without direct coupling.
3. Distinguish persistence of classical correlations, entanglement, and Bell nonlocality.
4. Derive candidate decay laws for selected witnesses under local environmental noise.
5. Identify the conditions required for a universal persistence bound.
6. Formulate experiments capable of distinguishing genuine interaction-free persistence from residual coupling or environmental mediation.

The intended contribution is not the observation that entanglement can survive after separation. That is established physics. The research target is a quantitative law governing the survival of carefully specified correlation resources under independently characterized local dynamics.

## 2. Mathematical foundations

### 2.1 Composite quantum systems

Let the two subsystems have Hilbert spaces:

\\[ \mathcal H_A,\qquad\mathcal H_B. \\]

The joint system occupies:

\\[ \mathcal H\_{AB} = \mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B. \\]

Its density operator satisfies:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}\geq0, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\rho\_{AB}=1. \\]

The reduced states are:

\\[ \rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}\_B\rho\_{AB}, \qquad \rho_B=\operatorname{Tr}\_A\rho\_{AB}. \\]

The product state with the same local marginals is:

\\[ \rho_A\otimes\rho_B. \\]

The joint state is uncorrelated across the bipartition if:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}=\rho_A\otimes\rho_B. \\]

Otherwise, it contains correlations of some kind.

This definition includes both classical and quantum correlations.

### 2.2 Correlation witnesses

A correlation witness is a real-valued functional:

\\[ W:\mathcal D(\mathcal H\_{AB})\rightarrow\mathbb R, \\]

where \\(\mathcal D\\) denotes the set of density operators.

The witness must be selected according to the physical property under investigation.

Examples include:

- connected covariance;
- mutual information;
- entanglement witnesses;
- negativity;
- Bell inequality violation;
- steering inequalities;
- quantum discord measures;
- state fidelity with a target correlated state.

These quantities are not equivalent.

A nonzero covariance can arise from classical correlations. Mutual information detects total correlations but does not distinguish their classical and quantum components. An entanglement witness can certify entanglement under its stated assumptions. A Bell inequality violation certifies a stronger operational form of nonclassical correlation.

MFCP must therefore be formulated around a declared witness rather than an unspecified notion of correlation.

### 2.3 Connected correlation witness

For local Hermitian observables \\(A\\) and \\(B\\), define:

\\[ \boxed{ W\_{\mathrm{cov}}(\rho\_{AB}) = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho\_{AB}A\otimes B) - \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A A) \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_B B). } \\]

This is the connected covariance.

It vanishes for product states, but a nonzero value does not prove entanglement.

Its advantages are:

- direct experimental accessibility;
- clear statistical interpretation;
- compatibility with continuous measurement;
- and straightforward time-dependent reconstruction.

Its limitation is that it detects only correlations visible to the chosen observable pair.

### 2.4 Entanglement witness

An entanglement witness is a Hermitian operator \\(E\\) satisfying:

\\[ \operatorname{Tr}(E\sigma)\geq0 \\]

for all states \\(\sigma\\) separable across the specified bipartition, while there exists an entangled state \\(\rho\\) for which:

\\[ \operatorname{Tr}(E\rho)<0. \\]

Define:

\\[ \boxed{ W_E(\rho) = -\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho). } \\]

Then:

\\[ W_E(\rho)>0 \\]

certifies entanglement.

The value is witness-dependent and generally does not equal an entanglement monotone. It should not be interpreted as an absolute measure of entanglement without further justification.

### 2.5 Bell witness

For two parties with binary measurement settings, define the CHSH expression:

\\[ S = \langle A_0B_0\rangle + \langle A_0B_1\rangle + \langle A_1B_0\rangle - \langle A_1B_1\rangle. \\]

Local hidden-variable models satisfy:

\\[ |S|\leq2. \\]

Quantum theory permits:

\\[ |S|\leq2\sqrt2. \\]

A Bell-violation witness may be defined as:

\\[ \boxed{ W\_{\mathrm{CHSH}} = |S|-2. } \\]

A positive value indicates a violation of the corresponding CHSH local bound, subject to the usual experimental assumptions.

This witness is more demanding than a covariance measurement. It requires a suitable set of measurement settings and a valid Bell-test protocol.

### 2.6 Mutual information

The quantum mutual information is:

\\[ \boxed{ I(A:B)\_\rho = S(\rho_A)+S(\rho_B)-S(\rho\_{AB}), } \\]

where:

\\[ S(\rho)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log\rho). \\]

It is nonnegative and vanishes if and only if the state is a product state in finite dimensions.

Mutual information measures total correlations. It does not, by itself, certify entanglement or Bell nonlocality.

It is nevertheless a useful persistence witness when the research question concerns the survival of all correlations rather than specifically quantum entanglement.

## 3. Interaction-free evolution

### 3.1 Hamiltonian decomposition

Write the total Hamiltonian as:

\\[ H(t) = H_A(t)\otimes I_B + I_A\otimes H_B(t) + H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}(t) + H_E + H\_{AE}(t) + H\_{BE}(t) + H\_{\mathrm{other}}(t). \\]

The terms represent:

- local subsystem dynamics;
- direct inter-subsystem interaction;
- environmental dynamics;
- local environmental couplings;
- and any additional interactions.

The ideal direct-interaction-free condition is:

\\[ \boxed{ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}(t)=0. } \\]

This does not require the environmental terms to vanish.

In particular, a shared environment may couple to both subsystems and mediate effective correlations or interactions.

The distinction between direct coupling and environmental coupling is therefore essential.

### 3.2 Product-channel evolution

If the subsystems evolve independently and there is no shared environmental influence, the evolution map factorizes:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathcal E\_{AB}(t,t_0) = \mathcal E_A(t,t_0) \otimes \mathcal E_B(t,t_0). } \\]

The state evolves as:

\\[ \boxed{ \rho\_{AB}(t) = [ \mathcal E_A(t,t_0) \otimes \mathcal E_B(t,t_0) ] \rho\_{AB}(t_0). } \\]

This is the central ideal MFCP model.

It permits each subsystem to undergo arbitrary local quantum-channel evolution while forbidding direct dynamical coupling between them.

The joint state can remain correlated because the initial density operator need not factorize.

### 3.3 Preservation under local unitary evolution

For local unitary operators \\(U_A(t)\\) and \\(U_B(t)\\):

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(t) = (U_A\otimes U_B) \rho\_{AB}(t_0) (U_A^\dagger\otimes U_B^\dagger). \\]

Local unitaries preserve the Schmidt coefficients of pure bipartite states and preserve standard entanglement measures.

For a pure state:

\\[ |\psi\rangle = \sum_i\sqrt{\lambda_i} |i\rangle_A|i\rangle_B, \\]

local unitary evolution changes the local bases but not the Schmidt coefficients:

\\[ \lambda_i(t)=\lambda_i(t_0). \\]

Thus entanglement can persist exactly in the absence of direct interaction.

This is a direct mathematical consequence of the tensor-product structure of local evolution.

### 3.4 Preservation under local nonunitary evolution

When local noise is present, correlations may decay.

For local completely positive trace-preserving maps:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(t) = (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B)\rho\_{AB}(t_0). \\]

Local operations cannot increase entanglement on average under the standard LOCC framework.

For deterministic local channels, an appropriate entanglement monotone \\(E\\) satisfies:

\\[ \boxed{ E[ (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B)(\rho) ] \leq E(\rho). } \\]

This does not mean every correlation witness decreases monotonically. A covariance can increase under local transformations because the observable basis and local state structure can change. Mutual information and entanglement monotones have their own distinct data-processing properties.

The witness must therefore be interpreted according to its mathematical properties.

### 3.5 Interaction-free is not environment-free

A critical distinction is:

\\[ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}=0 \\]

does not imply:

\\[ H\_{AE}=H\_{BE}=0. \\]

Nor does it imply:

\\[ \mathcal E\_{AB}=\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B. \\]

If both subsystems couple to a shared environment, tracing out that environment can produce correlated dynamics.

For example, a common fluctuating field may generate collective dephasing even when no direct subsystem–subsystem Hamiltonian is present.

A strong MFCP experiment must therefore characterize both direct coupling and common-environment effects.

### 3.6 Operational definition of the interval

Let:

\\[ [t_0,t_1] \\]

be the declared interaction-free interval.

The interval is operationally specified by:

1. the time at which the preparation interaction is terminated;
2. the time at which the final measurement begins;
3. the subsystem separation throughout the interval;
4. the direct-coupling calibration;
5. the residual-coupling upper bound;
6. and the environmental coupling model.

The claim of interaction-free persistence is always relative to the resolution and sensitivity of these characterizations.

A residual interaction below the experimental detection limit is not mathematically identical to zero interaction.

## 4. The MFCP persistence functional

### 4.1 Time-averaged persistence

Let \\(W(t)\\) be a specified real-valued witness.

The proposed persistence functional is:

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi_W[t_0,t_1] = \frac{1}{t_1-t_0} \int\_{t_0}^{t_1}W(t)\\,dt. } \\]

This is the time-averaged witness over the persistence interval.

It measures the average value of the selected witness, not the probability that correlation exists throughout the interval.

The functional has the same units as \\(W\\).

### 4.2 Normalized persistence

For comparisons across experiments, define a reference witness scale \\(W\_\*>0\\):

\\[ \boxed{ \widetilde\Pi_W = \frac{1}{(t_1-t_0)W\_\*} \int\_{t_0}^{t_1}W(t)\\,dt. } \\]

If the witness is nonnegative and bounded by \\(W\_{\max}\\), a natural choice is:

\\[ W\_\*=W\_{\max}. \\]

Then:

\\[ 0\leq\widetilde\Pi_W\leq1. \\]

For a signed witness, the normalization and interpretation must be specified separately.

### 4.3 Integrated survival

A complementary quantity is the integrated survival time:

\\[ \boxed{ \tau_W = \int\_{t_0}^{t_1} \frac{W(t)}{W(t_0)}\\,dt, } \\]

assuming \\(W(t_0)\neq0\\).

For a nonnegative witness, this measures the accumulated normalized witness strength.

Unlike the time average, it has units of time.

The relation is:

\\[ \tau_W = (t_1-t_0) \frac{\Pi_W}{W(t_0)}. \\]

### 4.4 Threshold persistence

A time average can conceal temporary disappearance and reappearance of a witness.

Define the persistence fraction:

\\[ \boxed{ P_W(\varepsilon) = \frac{1}{t_1-t_0} \int\_{t_0}^{t_1} \mathbf 1[W(t)>\varepsilon]\\,dt. } \\]

Here \\(\mathbf 1\\) is the indicator function.

This measures the fraction of the interval during which the witness exceeds a declared threshold.

For an entanglement witness, the threshold should account for statistical uncertainty and the witness's separable-state bound.

### 4.5 Monotonicity and sign conventions

The witness must be chosen so that larger values correspond to the property whose persistence is being studied.

For example:

- covariance may have either sign;
- CHSH violation can be represented by \\(|S|-2\\);
- negativity is nonnegative;
- mutual information is nonnegative;
- a conventional entanglement witness may be negative on entangled states.

For witnesses whose favorable region is negative, one should either reverse the sign or define a positive violation margin.

A time average of a signed witness can otherwise suffer cancellations that do not represent physical loss of correlation.

### 4.6 Time-dependent measurement settings

If the witness requires optimized measurement settings, distinguish two procedures.

Fixed-setting persistence:

\\[ W\_{\mathrm{fixed}}(t) = W[\rho(t);\mathcal M_0], \\]

where \\(\mathcal M_0\\) is held constant.

Reoptimized persistence:

\\[ W\_{\mathrm{opt}}(t) = \sup\_{\mathcal M\in\mathcal M\_{\mathrm{allowed}}} W[\rho(t);\mathcal M]. \\]

These quantities answer different questions.

Fixed-setting persistence measures survival under a fixed operational protocol. Reoptimized persistence measures the best accessible witness at each time, but requires additional measurement resources and may hide the degradation of the original measurement configuration.

Both may be useful, but they must not be conflated.

## 5. Exact examples of mediator-free persistence

The strongest foundational examples are those in which the entire evolution can be calculated analytically.

### 5.1 Bell-state persistence under local unitary evolution

Consider two qubits prepared in:

\\[ |\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2} (|00\rangle+|11\rangle). \\]

Their initial density operator is:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(0) = |\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|. \\]

Suppose the direct interaction is switched off after preparation:

\\[ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}=0. \\]

Let each qubit undergo independent local unitary evolution:

\\[ U(t)=U_A(t)\otimes U_B(t). \\]

Then:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(t) = U(t)\rho\_{AB}(0)U^\dagger(t). \\]

The state remains pure and maximally entangled.

Its entanglement entropy remains:

\\[ S(\rho_A(t)) = S(\rho_B(t)) = \ln2. \\]

Its concurrence remains:

\\[ \boxed{C(t)=1.} \\]

For an interval of duration \\(T=t_1-t_0\\), using concurrence as the witness:

\\[ \Pi_C = \frac1T\int\_{t_0}^{t_1}1\\,dt = 1. \\]

This is exact persistence without direct interaction.

The result requires no continuous communication between the qubits. The correlations are properties of the joint state and survive because local unitary transformations preserve entanglement.

### 5.2 Persistence under independent dephasing

The ideal unitary case is structurally simple but does not include environmental decoherence.

Consider independent local dephasing channels. For each qubit, define:

\\[ \mathcal E_j(\rho) = \frac{1+\eta_j}{2}\rho + \frac{1-\eta_j}{2} Z\rho Z, \\]

where:

\\[ 0\leq\eta_j\leq1. \\]

The parameter \\(\eta_j\\) describes the survival of local phase coherence.

For the initial Bell state:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(0) = \frac12 \left( |00\rangle\langle00| + |00\rangle\langle11| + |11\rangle\langle00| + |11\rangle\langle11| \right). \\]

After independent dephasing:

\\[ \boxed{ \rho\_{AB}(t) = \frac12 \begin{pmatrix} 1&0&0&\eta_A\eta_B\\\ 0&0&0&0\\\ 0&0&0&0\\\ \eta_A\eta_B&0&0&1 \end{pmatrix}. } \\]

The concurrence is:

\\[ \boxed{ C(t)=\eta_A(t)\eta_B(t). } \\]

For identical Markovian dephasing:

\\[ \eta_A(t)=\eta_B(t)=e^{-\gamma t}, \\]

so:

\\[ \boxed{ C(t)=e^{-2\gamma t}. } \\]

The time-averaged concurrence over an interval of duration \\(T\\), beginning at \\(t=0\\), is:

\\[ \Pi_C(T) = \frac1T\int_0^T e^{-2\gamma t}\\,dt. \\]

For \\(\gamma>0\\):

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi_C(T) = \frac{1-e^{-2\gamma T}}{2\gamma T}. } \\]

In the zero-dephasing limit:

\\[ \lim\_{\gamma\rightarrow0}\Pi_C(T)=1. \\]

This result provides a controlled baseline for MFCP: entanglement persists under local noise even when no direct interaction operates between the subsystems, but its magnitude decays according to the local coherence dynamics.

### 5.3 Bell-nonlocality persistence in the same model

For the dephased Bell state, the correlation tensor in the Pauli basis is:

\\[ T= \operatorname{diag} \left( \eta_A\eta_B, -\eta_A\eta_B, 1 \right). \\]

The maximal CHSH value is determined by the two largest eigenvalues of \\(T^{\mathsf T}T\\). Hence:

\\[ \boxed{ S\_{\max}(t) = 2\sqrt{1+[\eta_A(t)\eta_B(t)]^2}. } \\]

For identical exponential dephasing:

\\[ S\_{\max}(t) = 2\sqrt{1+e^{-4\gamma t}}. \\]

The CHSH violation margin is:

\\[ W\_{\mathrm{CHSH}}(t) = 2\left[ \sqrt{1+e^{-4\gamma t}}-1 \right]. \\]

It remains positive at every finite time in the ideal mathematical model, although it approaches zero asymptotically.

This example is particularly instructive because concurrence and Bell violation are different witnesses of the same evolving state. Their decay profiles are not identical.

A practical experiment will eventually lose statistically significant Bell violation at a finite observation time because of finite sampling and detector imperfections, even though the ideal theoretical expression remains positive.

### 5.4 Persistence of classical correlations

Entanglement is not necessary for MFCP.

Consider the classically correlated state:

\\[ \rho\_{\mathrm{cl}} = \frac12|00\rangle\langle00| + \frac12|11\rangle\langle11|. \\]

It is separable, since it is a convex mixture of product states.

Its connected \\(ZZ\\) covariance is:

\\[ \langle Z\otimes Z\rangle - \langle Z\rangle\langle Z\rangle = 1. \\]

Thus:

\\[ W\_{\mathrm{cov}}=1. \\]

Under independent local \\(Z\\)-dephasing, the state remains unchanged:

\\[ (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B) (\rho\_{\mathrm{cl}}) = \rho\_{\mathrm{cl}}. \\]

Consequently:

\\[ \boxed{ W\_{\mathrm{cov}}(t)=1, \qquad \Pi\_{\mathrm{cov}}=1. } \\]

The state retains perfect classical correlation without direct interaction and without entanglement.

This example establishes a crucial interpretive rule:

MFCP describes persistence of a selected correlation property. Whether that property is classical, quantum-entangled, or Bell-nonlocal must be determined independently.

### 5.5 What these examples establish

The three examples establish distinct persistence regimes:

| Initial state and evolution                     | Persistent property   | Direct interaction required during persistence? |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Bell state, local unitaries                     | Maximal entanglement  | No                                              |
| Bell state, independent dephasing               | Decaying entanglement | No                                              |
| Classically correlated mixture, local dephasing | Classical correlation | No                                              |

They demonstrate the mathematical possibility of mediator-free persistence under the operational definition adopted here.

They do not establish a universal decay law. The dynamics depend on the channel, initial state, witness, and environmental structure.

## 6. General dynamical theory of persistence

### 6.1 Local Lindblad evolution

For Markovian local open-system dynamics, the joint state may satisfy:

\\[ \frac{d\rho\_{AB}}{dt} = \mathcal L_A(\rho\_{AB}) + \mathcal L_B(\rho\_{AB}), \\]

where:

\\[ \mathcal L_j(\rho) = -i[H_j,\rho] + \sum\_\alpha \left( L\_{j,\alpha}\rho L\_{j,\alpha}^\dagger - \frac12 \\{L\_{j,\alpha}^\dagger L\_{j,\alpha},\rho\\} \right). \\]

The operators \\(L\_{j,\alpha}\\) act locally on subsystem \\(j\\), with identities on the other subsystem understood.

There is no direct interaction term:

\\[ -i[H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}},\rho\_{AB}]. \\]

The resulting dynamics are local and trace preserving.

### 6.2 Witness evolution equation

For a time-independent Hermitian witness operator \\(O\\), define:

\\[ W_O(t)=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\_{AB}(t)O]. \\]

Then:

\\[ \frac{dW_O}{dt} = \operatorname{Tr} [ \dot\rho\_{AB}(t)O ]. \\]

Using the adjoint generator:

\\[ \boxed{ \frac{dW_O}{dt} = \operatorname{Tr} \left[ \rho\_{AB}(t) \left( \mathcal L_A^\dagger + \mathcal L_B^\dagger \right)(O) \right]. } \\]

This equation provides the general starting point for deriving witness-specific persistence laws.

For a connected covariance, \\(O=A\otimes B\\) is not by itself sufficient because the one-point terms also evolve. The full derivative must include:

\\[ \frac{d}{dt} \left[ \langle A\otimes B\rangle - \langle A\otimes I\rangle \langle I\otimes B\rangle \right]. \\]

### 6.3 Local noise does not imply universal witness decay

The generator determines the evolution, but the sign of:

\\[ \frac{dW}{dt} \\]

depends on the state and the observable.

An entanglement monotone may be nonincreasing under local operations, while an individual covariance may increase because local rotations or noise redistribute correlations between measurement directions.

A proposed MFCP decay law must therefore specify whether the witness is:

- an entanglement monotone;
- a Bell violation margin;
- a mutual-information measure;
- a fixed-observable covariance;
- or a witness optimized over measurement settings.

No general monotonicity theorem applies to every possible correlation witness.

### 6.4 Local channels and data processing

Quantum mutual information obeys data processing under local quantum channels:

\\[ \boxed{ I(A:B)\_{(\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B)(\rho)} \leq I(A:B)\_\rho. } \\]

This follows from its representation as quantum relative entropy:

\\[ I(A:B)\_\rho = D( \rho\_{AB}\\| \rho_A\otimes\rho_B ), \\]

and contractivity of relative entropy under completely positive trace-preserving maps.

This provides a general upper bound on total-correlation growth under independent local channels.

It does not imply that every correlation witness decreases monotonically, nor that mutual information decays exponentially.

### 6.5 Entanglement monotonicity

For a bipartite entanglement monotone \\(E\\), deterministic local channels satisfy:

\\[ \boxed{ E[ (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B)(\rho) ] \leq E(\rho). } \\]

For LOCC protocols with classical communication and selective outcomes, the appropriate statement is average monotonicity:

\\[ \sum_i p_i E(\rho_i) \leq E(\rho). \\]

The deterministic local-channel form is particularly relevant to MFCP because it permits the subsystems to evolve independently without communication.

The theorem constrains entanglement growth, not its decay rate. It does not supply a universal function of time.

### 6.6 Residual direct interaction

In a real experiment:

\\[ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}(t) \neq0 \\]

may hold at a small but nonzero level.

A more realistic model is:

\\[ \frac{d\rho}{dt} = \left[ \mathcal L_A+ \mathcal L_B+ \mathcal L\_{AB}^{\mathrm{res}} \right]\(\rho). \\]

The residual generator can create, maintain, or modify correlations.

The experimental problem is to establish an upper bound on its contribution.

A claim of interaction-free persistence must therefore be expressed with a calibrated residual-coupling bound:

\\[ \\|H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}(t)\\| \leq \epsilon_H. \\]

The value of \\(\epsilon_H\\) must be determined independently of the measured correlation witness.

## 7. Environmental dynamics and correlation decay

### 7.1 Independent environments

Let the subsystems couple to distinct environments \\(E_A\\) and \\(E_B\\), initially uncorrelated with one another and with the system.

The total initial state is:

\\[ \rho\_{AB E_A E_B}(0) = \rho\_{AB}(0) \otimes\rho\_{E_A} \otimes\rho\_{E_B}. \\]

If the total evolution factorizes into independent local system-environment unitaries:

\\[ U(t) = U\_{AE_A}(t)\otimes U\_{BE_B}(t), \\]

then tracing out the environments produces product-channel evolution:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(t) = [ \mathcal E_A(t)\otimes\mathcal E_B(t) ]\rho\_{AB}(0). \\]

This is the cleanest microscopic realization of interaction-free persistence.

### 7.2 Shared environments

If both subsystems interact with a common environment \\(E\\), the total Hamiltonian may contain:

\\[ H\_{AE}+H\_{BE}. \\]

Even when:

\\[ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}=0, \\]

the reduced evolution can be correlated.

The environment may induce:

- collective dephasing;
- correlated amplitude fluctuations;
- effective coherent coupling;
- bath-mediated entanglement;
- or common-mode noise.

The reduced map need not factorize:

\\[ \mathcal E\_{AB} \neq \mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B. \\]

Such a system may still satisfy the operational condition of no direct subsystem interaction, but it does not satisfy the stronger condition of independent local evolution.

MFCP should therefore distinguish:

1. direct-interaction-free persistence;
2. product-channel persistence;
3. shared-environment persistence.

These are three different experimental regimes.

### 7.3 Environmental correlation functions

For a shared bosonic environment, write:

\\[ H\_{\mathrm{int}} = A\otimes B_E^{(A)} + B\otimes B_E^{(B)}. \\]

The environmental cross-correlation function is:

\\[ C\_{AB}^{E}(t,t') = \operatorname{Tr}\_E[ \rho_E B_E^{(A)}(t) B_E^{(B)}(t') ]. \\]

If this function is nonzero, the environmental fluctuations sampled by the two subsystems may be correlated.

Such correlations can influence the reduced system dynamics even without a direct \\(A\\)-\\(B\\) Hamiltonian.

A rigorous experiment should therefore characterize, bound, or control the environmental cross-correlations.

### 7.4 Decoherence rates

For a dephasing model, define a local coherence factor:

\\[ \eta_j(t)=e^{-\Gamma_j(t)}. \\]

For the Bell-state example:

\\[ C(t) = e^{-\Gamma_A(t)-\Gamma_B(t)}. \\]

The functions \\(\Gamma_j(t)\\) may be nonlinear in time.

For Markovian exponential dephasing:

\\[ \Gamma_j(t)=\gamma_j t. \\]

For non-Markovian evolution, the decay may include plateaus, revivals, or nonexponential tails.

A general persistence law should therefore not assume a constant decay rate from the outset.

### 7.5 Non-Markovian persistence

A non-Markovian environment can produce temporary recovery of coherence.

For a dephasing model:

\\[ \eta_j(t)=e^{-\Gamma_j(t)} \\]

may exhibit intervals in which:

\\[ \frac{d\Gamma_j}{dt}<0. \\]

Then:

\\[ \frac{d|\eta_j|}{dt}>0. \\]

The corresponding entanglement witness may temporarily increase even though no direct inter-subsystem interaction is present.

This does not imply that the environment has ceased to exist or that correlation has been created from nothing. Information and coherence may be returning from environmental degrees of freedom.

The distinction between direct subsystem interaction and environmental memory is therefore essential to interpreting apparent revivals.

## 8. Candidate persistence laws

The central theoretical objective is to identify quantitative relations between witness survival and independently measurable physical parameters.

### 8.1 Exponential persistence model

The simplest candidate is:

\\[ \boxed{ W(t) = W_0e^{-\Gamma t}, } \\]

where:

- \\(W_0\\) is the initial witness;
- \\(\Gamma\geq0\\) is an effective decay rate.

The corresponding time average over duration \\(T\\) is:

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi_W(T) = W_0 \frac{1-e^{-\Gamma T}}{\Gamma T}. } \\]

For \\(\Gamma=0\\):

\\[ \Pi_W(T)=W_0. \\]

The normalized average is:

\\[ \boxed{ \widetilde\Pi_W(T) = \frac{1-e^{-\Gamma T}}{\Gamma T}. } \\]

This expression is a baseline model, not a universal law.

It applies when the selected witness follows a single exponential decay over the measured regime.

### 8.2 Multi-rate persistence

If different physical processes contribute independent exponential decay channels, a candidate model is:

\\[ W(t) = W_0 \exp\left[ -\sum_j\Gamma_j t \right]. \\]

Thus:

\\[ \Gamma\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sum_j\Gamma_j. \\]

For independent local dephasing of the Bell state:

\\[ \Gamma\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \gamma_A+\gamma_B. \\]

This decomposition is physically useful because it separates local contributions.

However, independent decay rates do not automatically add for arbitrary witnesses or correlated noise models.

### 8.3 Nonexponential persistence

A more general law is:

\\[ \boxed{ W(t) = W_0e^{-\Phi(t)}, } \\]

where \\(\Phi(t)\\) is a nonnegative decay functional.

For Markovian exponential decay:

\\[ \Phi(t)=\Gamma t. \\]

For a Gaussian decay profile:

\\[ \Phi(t)=\frac12\sigma^2t^2. \\]

For power-law decay:

\\[ W(t) = W_0 \left(1+\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{-\alpha}. \\]

The persistence average becomes:

\\[ \Pi_W(T) = \frac{W_0}{T} \int_0^T e^{-\Phi(t)}dt. \\]

The functional \\(\Phi(t)\\) is the principal object to derive from the environment's spectral density and coupling structure.

### 8.4 Spectral-density dependence

For a pure-dephasing spin-boson model, the coherence factor can be expressed in terms of the environmental spectral density \\(J(\omega)\\).

Under common conventions and a thermal bosonic bath:

\\[ \Gamma(t) = \int_0^\infty d\omega\\, J(\omega) \coth\left(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}\right) \frac{1-\cos(\omega t)}{\omega^2}, \\]

up to convention-dependent normalization factors.

This formula shows how persistence depends on:

- the bath spectral density;
- temperature;
- interaction strength;
- and observation time.

For two independently dephased subsystems, the corresponding exponents add.

This provides a concrete route from environmental physics to an MFCP persistence profile.

### 8.5 Distance dependence

If the dominant environmental coupling depends on subsystem separation \\(r\\), one may write:

\\[ \Gamma\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Gamma\_{\mathrm{eff}}(r,\beta,\lambda,\ldots), \\]

where \\(\lambda\\) denotes a coupling scale.

A candidate law is:

\\[ \boxed{ W(t,r) = W_0 \exp[ -\Gamma\_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)t ]. } \\]

The dependence on \\(r\\) may reflect environmental cross-correlations, residual direct coupling, thermal gradients, or spatially varying noise.

It must be derived for the specific physical model.

### 8.6 A dimensionless persistence parameter

Let \\(\tau\_{\mathrm{env}}\\) be a characteristic environmental coherence or relaxation time.

Define:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathcal P_W = \frac{\tau_W}{\tau\_{\mathrm{env}}} } \\]

when both times are operationally defined.

Alternatively, for a constant exponential rate:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathcal P\_{\mathrm{rate}} = \frac{1}{\Gamma\_{\mathrm{eff}}\tau\_{\mathrm{env}}}. } \\]

These parameters can be useful for comparing systems with different absolute timescales.

They are not necessarily universal. Their value depends on the definition of the witness and environmental timescale.

### 8.7 Candidate universal law

The principal research conjecture is that, within a specified class of environments and initial states, the normalized persistence profile may take a universal form:

\\[ \boxed{ \frac{W(t)}{W(0)} = \mathcal F \left( \frac{t}{\tau\_{\mathrm{env}}}, \frac{r}{\ell\_{\mathrm{env}}}, \frac{k_BT}{E\_\*}, \mathfrak S\_{\rho_0} \right). } \\]

Here:

- \\(\tau\_{\mathrm{env}}\\) is an environmental timescale;
- \\(\ell\_{\mathrm{env}}\\) is a characteristic environmental correlation length;
- \\(E\_\*\\) is a declared energy scale;
- \\(\mathfrak S\_{\rho_0}\\) describes the relevant initial-state structure.

The research objective is to determine whether \\(\mathcal F\\) becomes independent of microscopic details within a meaningful universality class.

Status: Conjecture. No universal function is established by the definitions alone.

## 9. Persistence versus causal communication

The distinction between maintaining an existing correlation and transmitting a new influence is the conceptual foundation of MFCP.

### 9.1 Local intervention

Suppose an experimenter performs a local trace-preserving quantum operation \\(\mathcal E_A\\) on subsystem \\(A\\).

If the operation is local, the unconditional joint state transforms as:

\\[ \rho'\_{AB} = (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal I_B)(\rho\_{AB}). \\]

The reduced state of \\(B\\) becomes:

\\[ \rho'\_B = \operatorname{Tr}\_A[ (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal I_B)(\rho\_{AB}) ]. \\]

For a trace-preserving local operation:

\\[ \boxed{ \rho'\_B=\rho_B. } \\]

This is the no-signaling property of local quantum operations.

The local operation may change the global state and its correlations, but it cannot change the unconditional reduced statistics of the remote subsystem.

### 9.2 Proof of local no-signaling

Write the local channel in Kraus form:

\\[ \mathcal E_A(\rho) = \sum\_\alpha K\_\alpha\rho K\_\alpha^\dagger, \\]

with:

\\[ \sum\_\alpha K\_\alpha^\dagger K\_\alpha=I_A. \\]

Then:

\\[ \rho'\_B = \sum\_\alpha \operatorname{Tr}\_A[ (K\_\alpha\otimes I) \rho\_{AB} (K\_\alpha^\dagger\otimes I) ]. \\]

Using cyclicity of the partial trace over subsystem \\(A\\):

\\[ \rho'\_B = \operatorname{Tr}\_A \left[ \rho\_{AB} \left( \sum\_\alpha K\_\alpha^\dagger K\_\alpha \otimes I_B \right) \right]. \\]

Therefore:

\\[ \rho'\_B = \operatorname{Tr}\_A[ \rho\_{AB}(I_A\otimes I_B) ] = \rho_B. \\]

Hence:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathcal E_A\text{ trace preserving} \Longrightarrow \rho'\_B=\rho_B. } \\]

The result does not require the initial state to be separable.

### 9.3 Conditional statistics

If the operation at \\(A\\) is selective, the conditional state of \\(B\\) associated with a particular outcome may change.

For an outcome represented by Kraus operator \\(K_a\\):

\\[ \rho\_{B|a} = \frac{ \operatorname{Tr}\_A[ (K_a\otimes I)\rho\_{AB} (K_a^\dagger\otimes I) ] }{ p(a) }. \\]

This conditional state can differ from \\(\rho_B\\).

However, the remote observer does not know which outcome occurred until the result is communicated through an ordinary causal channel.

A change in conditional description is not, by itself, a controllable signal.

### 9.4 Persistence does not require continuous signaling

During the interaction-free interval, the state may retain a nonzero witness:

\\[ W[\rho\_{AB}(t)]\neq0. \\]

But a local intervention at \\(A\\) cannot change the unconditional reduced state at \\(B\\) when the dynamics are strictly local and trace preserving.

Therefore:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Correlation persistence} \not\Rightarrow \text{continuous causal communication}. } \\]

This is not an additional postulate. It follows from the tensor-product structure of local quantum operations.

### 9.5 Persistence and causal response are complementary measurements

An MFCP experiment should measure two different quantities:

\\[ W(t) \\]

for the survival of a correlation witness, and:

\\[ R\_{B\leftarrow A}(t) = \frac{\delta\langle B(t)\rangle} {\delta j_A(t_0)} \\]

for the response to a controlled intervention at \\(A\\).

The first measures whether a selected correlation property remains. The second tests whether a local perturbation produces a response at the other subsystem.

This distinction connects MFCP with the broader separation between correlation and causal-response support, while adding an explicit temporal persistence dimension.

## 10. Correlation persistence and quantum information resources

### 10.1 Entanglement survival

For a bipartite state, define an entanglement measure \\(E(\rho\_{AB})\\).

Examples include:

- concurrence for two qubits;
- negativity;
- logarithmic negativity;
- entanglement of formation;
- relative entropy of entanglement.

The entanglement-persistence functional is:

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi_E = \frac1T \int\_{t_0}^{t_1} E[\rho\_{AB}(t)]\\,dt. } \\]

This is a special case of MFCP.

Its interpretation is stronger than that of an arbitrary covariance witness because \\(E\\) quantifies entanglement according to a declared measure.

However, different entanglement measures can produce different persistence profiles.

### 10.2 Bell-nonlocality survival

Define the maximal Bell violation accessible within a measurement family \\(\mathcal M\\):

\\[ \mathcal B\_{\mathcal M}(\rho) = \sup\_{M\in\mathcal M} \left[ \langle\mathcal B\rangle\_\rho - \beta\_{\mathrm{LHV}} \right], \\]

where \\(\beta\_{\mathrm{LHV}}\\) is the local-hidden-variable bound.

The Bell-persistence functional is:

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi\_{\mathrm{Bell}} = \frac1T \int\_{t_0}^{t_1} \mathcal B\_{\mathcal M}[\rho(t)]\\,dt. } \\]

The measurement family must be fixed or explicitly reoptimized at each time.

Entanglement can persist after Bell inequality violation disappears. Therefore:

\\[ \Pi_E>0 \\]

does not imply:

\\[ \Pi\_{\mathrm{Bell}}>0. \\]

The two persistence resources must be reported separately.

### 10.3 Quantum-memory survival

An initial correlated state can be viewed as a stored relational resource.

The persistence of that resource can be evaluated by asking how well the state at time \\(t\\) retains its initial structure.

One possible measure is the fidelity:

\\[ F(t) = \left[ \operatorname{Tr} \sqrt{ \sqrt{\rho_0}\rho(t)\sqrt{\rho_0} } \right]^2. \\]

But fidelity measures state preservation, not correlation specifically.

A state can evolve substantially while preserving its entanglement, or remain close to its initial state while having only weak entanglement.

Therefore, fidelity should be used as a supplementary diagnostic rather than as a universal correlation witness.

### 10.4 Relational-state survival

A more targeted quantity compares the correlated state with the product of its instantaneous marginals:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathcal R(\rho\_{AB}) = D( \rho\_{AB}\\| \rho_A\otimes\rho_B ), } \\]

where \\(D\\) is quantum relative entropy.

This equals quantum mutual information:

\\[ \mathcal R(\rho\_{AB})=I(A:B)\_\rho. \\]

It quantifies total departure from statistical independence.

Under independent local channels, it cannot increase.

It is therefore a natural measure of relational information persistence, although it does not distinguish classical and quantum correlations.

### 10.5 Witness hierarchy

An MFCP study should use a declared witness hierarchy rather than treating one witness as universally representative.

| Persistence target     | Candidate quantity                                      | What it certifies                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Statistical dependence | Connected covariance                                    | Correlation in selected observables  |
| Total correlation      | Mutual information                                      | Departure from product state         |
| Entanglement           | Negativity or concurrence                               | Quantum entanglement                 |
| Bell nonlocality       | CHSH violation margin                                   | Violation of a specified local bound |
| State preservation     | Fidelity                                                | Similarity to a target state         |
| Relational memory      | Mutual information or a specified relational functional | Survival of total correlations       |

The strongest experimental design measures more than one level. This reveals whether different forms of correlation decay at different rates.

## 11. Dependence on separation and geometry

### 11.1 Separation as an experimental variable

Let the subsystem separation be:

\\[ r(t)=|\mathbf x_A(t)-\mathbf x_B(t)| \\]

in a specified laboratory frame.

For relativistic experiments, the relevant spacetime separation should also be characterized through the invariant interval:

\\[ s^2 = c^2\Delta t^2-\Delta r^2. \\]

The distance \\(r\\) is not itself a universal measure of causal separation. The full spacetime localization of the subsystems and their interactions must be considered.

### 11.2 Separation-dependent persistence

A candidate persistence model is:

\\[ \boxed{ W(t,r) = W_0 \exp[ -\Phi(t,r) ]. } \\]

The decay functional may contain contributions from:

\\[ \Phi(t,r) = \Phi_A(t) + \Phi_B(t) + \Phi\_{\mathrm{env}}(t,r) + \Phi\_{\mathrm{res}}(t,r). \\]

Here:

- \\(\Phi_A\\) and \\(\Phi_B\\) describe local decoherence;
- \\(\Phi\_{\mathrm{env}}\\) describes correlated environmental effects;
- \\(\Phi\_{\mathrm{res}}\\) describes residual direct coupling.

This decomposition is a model ansatz. Its terms must be derived from the microscopic dynamics or estimated independently.

### 11.3 Distance-dependent environmental correlations

For a spatially correlated environment, the cross-correlation may decay with distance:

\\[ C_E(r,\tau) = \langle B_E(\mathbf x_A,t) B_E(\mathbf x_B,t+\tau)\rangle. \\]

A characteristic environmental correlation length \\(\ell_E\\) may be defined through an asymptotic form such as:

\\[ C_E(r,0) \sim C_0e^{-r/\ell_E}. \\]

This can influence the degree to which the two subsystems experience common fluctuations.

The resulting dependence of MFCP on separation is not necessarily monotonic. Increasing separation may reduce common-mode noise, but it can also change local coupling strengths, residual interactions, and propagation delays.

### 11.4 Geometry-dependent persistence

In curved spacetime or accelerated frames, local detector trajectories and environmental couplings may depend on geometry.

A covariant treatment should represent subsystem worldlines as:

\\[ x_A^\mu(\tau_A), \qquad x_B^\mu(\tau_B). \\]

The persistence functional can then be written:

\\[ \Pi_W = \frac{1}{\Delta\tau} \int\_{\tau_0}^{\tau_1} W[ \rho\_{AB}(\tau) ]\\,d\tau, \\]

where \\(\tau\\) is a declared reference proper-time parameter.

For nonidentical proper times, the synchronization convention must be specified.

No general prediction that gravitational separation either enhances or suppresses MFCP follows from this definition alone.

## 12. A quantitative distinction between persistence and mediation

The term mediator-free needs careful operational definition.

A correlation that survives without direct interaction is not necessarily free from all environmental or field-mediated processes.

This section defines a hierarchy of claims.

### 12.1 Direct-interaction-free persistence

The weakest and most experimentally accessible claim is:

\\[ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}(t)=0 \\]

within the declared model and calibrated uncertainty.

The subsystems may still share an environment.

This establishes that continued direct interaction is unnecessary for the observed persistence.

### 12.2 Product-channel persistence

A stronger claim is:

\\[ \mathcal E\_{AB} = \mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B. \\]

This means the reduced dynamics factorize into independent local channels.

It excludes correlated reduced dynamics during the interval.

It is a more stringent test of persistence under independent local evolution.

### 12.3 Shared-environment persistence

A third regime has:

\\[ H\_{AB}^{\mathrm{direct}}=0, \qquad \mathcal E\_{AB}\neq\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B. \\]

Correlations persist while shared environmental degrees of freedom remain relevant.

This is direct-interaction-free but not environment-independent.

### 12.4 Causal-response characterization

A further test measures whether an intervention at one subsystem changes the unconditional statistics of the other.

Define:

\\[ \mathcal C\_{A\rightarrow B} = \sup\_{\mathcal E_A\in\mathfrak I_A} D\left[ \rho_B^{(\mathcal E_A)}, \rho_B \right], \\]

where \\(D\\) is a state distinguishability measure and \\(\mathfrak I_A\\) is a specified set of allowed local trace-preserving interventions.

Under ideal local product-channel dynamics:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathcal C\_{A\rightarrow B}=0. } \\]

This quantity tests operational influence rather than merely measuring a correlation.

### 12.5 A persistence–response pair

Define the MFCP diagnostic pair:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak M_W = (\Pi_W,\mathcal C\_{A\rightarrow B}). } \\]

Its interpretation is:

- large \\(\Pi_W\\), negligible \\(\mathcal C\\): persistent correlation without detectable controllable remote response;
- decreasing \\(\Pi_W\\), negligible \\(\mathcal C\\): local environmental degradation without remote signaling;
- large \\(\Pi_W\\), nonzero \\(\mathcal C\\): possible residual coupling, common-environment effects, or an incorrectly specified intervention model;
- small \\(\Pi_W\\), nonzero \\(\mathcal C\\): causal response may exist even when the selected correlation witness is weak.

The diagnostic is protocol-relative. A zero response for one intervention family does not prove zero response for every conceivable intervention.

### 12.6 Why this distinction matters

The principal conceptual error MFCP is designed to prevent is the inference:

\\[ \text{correlation remains} \Longrightarrow \text{something must continuously travel between subsystems}. \\]

That inference is not generally valid.

The correct analysis asks separately:

1. Was the joint state initially correlated?
2. What local and environmental dynamics operated afterward?
3. Did the correlation witness persist?
4. Was a direct interaction present?
5. Did a controlled intervention produce a remote response?

Only the combined answers characterize the physical mechanism.

## 13. Proposed theorems and conjectures

The framework permits several precise mathematical results. Some are established consequences of quantum information theory; others are open research targets.

### 13.1 Theorem I — Local-unitary invariance of bipartite entanglement

Statement. Let \\(\rho\_{AB}\\) evolve under local unitary transformations:

\\[ \rho\_{AB}(t) = (U_A(t)\otimes U_B(t)) \rho\_{AB}(t_0) (U_A^\dagger(t)\otimes U_B^\dagger(t)). \\]

Then every entanglement measure invariant under local unitaries satisfies:

\\[ \boxed{ E[\rho\_{AB}(t)] = E[\rho\_{AB}(t_0)]. } \\]

Proof. Local unitaries are reversible local operations. They preserve the Schmidt coefficients of pure bipartite states and map separable states to separable states in both directions. Standard bipartite entanglement measures are invariant under local unitary transformations. Therefore the entanglement measure remains unchanged.

Status: Established result.

MFCP implication: Entanglement can persist indefinitely under ideal independent local unitary evolution.

### 13.2 Theorem II — Local-channel monotonicity of entanglement

Statement. Let \\(E\\) be a bipartite entanglement monotone. For deterministic local trace-preserving channels:

\\[ \rho'\_{AB} = (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B)(\rho\_{AB}), \\]

one has:

\\[ \boxed{ E(\rho'\_{AB})\leq E(\rho\_{AB}). } \\]

Status: Established under the stated definition of entanglement monotone.

MFCP implication: Independent local decoherence cannot increase the chosen entanglement monotone, although it need not destroy entanglement immediately.

### 13.3 Theorem III — Local no-signaling during product-channel persistence

Statement. Suppose the joint dynamics factorize into local trace-preserving channels:

\\[ \mathcal E\_{AB} = \mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B. \\]

A subsequent local trace-preserving operation at \\(A\\) cannot change the reduced state of \\(B\\).

Thus:

\\[ \boxed{ \rho'\_B=\rho_B. } \\]

Proof. This follows from trace preservation and the partial-trace identity derived in Section 9.2.

Status: Established.

MFCP implication: A nonzero persistence witness does not imply that a local intervention can communicate information to the other subsystem.

### 13.4 Theorem IV — Exponential persistence average

Statement. If a nonnegative witness follows:

\\[ W(t)=W_0e^{-\Gamma(t-t_0)}, \\]

then:

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi_W(T) = W_0 \frac{1-e^{-\Gamma T}}{\Gamma T} } \\]

for \\(\Gamma>0\\), where \\(T=t_1-t_0\\).

Proof. Direct integration:

\\[ \Pi_W = \frac{W_0}{T} \int_0^T e^{-\Gamma u}du = \frac{W_0}{T} \frac{1-e^{-\Gamma T}}{\Gamma}. \\]

Status: Proven conditional result.

Limitation: The theorem establishes the average given an exponential decay law. It does not derive exponential decay from general quantum dynamics.

### 13.5 Theorem V — Monotonicity of mutual information under independent local channels

Statement. For local CPTP maps:

\\[ \rho'\_{AB} = (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B)(\rho\_{AB}), \\]

the quantum mutual information satisfies:

\\[ \boxed{ I(A:B)\_{\rho'} \leq I(A:B)\_\rho. } \\]

Proof. Express mutual information as quantum relative entropy:

\\[ I(A:B)\_\rho = D(\rho\_{AB}\\|\rho_A\otimes\rho_B). \\]

The local channels map the two arguments to:

\\[ (\mathcal E_A\otimes\mathcal E_B)(\rho\_{AB}) \\]

and:

\\[ \mathcal E_A(\rho_A)\otimes \mathcal E_B(\rho_B). \\]

Contractivity of quantum relative entropy under CPTP maps gives the inequality.

Status: Established.

MFCP implication: Under independent local channels, total correlations cannot increase. Their survival is bounded by the initial mutual information.

### 13.6 Conjecture VI — Universal persistence scaling

For a restricted class of local environments with a common low-frequency spectral exponent, the normalized persistence function may approach:

\\[ \boxed{ \frac{W(t)}{W(0)} \rightarrow \mathcal F\_\alpha \left( \frac{t}{\tau\_{\mathrm{env}}} \right) } \\]

in an appropriate scaling limit.

Here \\(\alpha\\) characterizes the environmental spectral behavior.

Status: Conjecture.

Required result: Derive the scaling function from the environmental spectral density and show its stability across different microscopic implementations.

### 13.7 Conjecture VII — Separation-dependent persistence bound

For a specified family of independently coupled environments, there may exist a bound:

\\[ \boxed{ E[\rho\_{AB}(t)] \geq E[\rho\_{AB}(0)] - \mathcal B( t,r,J_A,J_B,C_E ), } \\]

where \\(C_E\\) denotes an independently characterized environmental correlation function.

A useful bound would remain valid across a specified family of initial states and would be tighter than a generic worst-case channel bound.

Status: Open problem.

### 13.8 Conjecture VIII — Witness-dependent persistence hierarchy

Different correlation resources may exhibit ordered decay times:

\\[ \tau\_{\mathrm{Bell}} \leq \tau\_{\mathrm{ent}} \leq \tau\_{\mathrm{corr}} \\]

for a particular model and witness family.

This ordering is plausible in some noise models because Bell nonlocality is a stronger resource than entanglement, while total correlation includes classical dependence.

It is not a universal theorem. The ordering of witness thresholds and decay times can depend on the state, measurement family, and noise model.

A major research opportunity is to identify precisely which classes of dynamics support such a hierarchy and where it fails.

### 13.9 Conjecture IX — Residual-interaction robustness

Suppose the actual evolution differs from a product-channel model by a small generator:

\\[ \mathcal L = \mathcal L_A+\mathcal L_B+\delta\mathcal L\_{AB}. \\]

For a bounded observable witness, one may seek a perturbative bound of the form:

\\[ \boxed{ |W\_{\mathrm{actual}}(t)-W\_{\mathrm{local}}(t)| \leq C_W \int\_{t_0}^{t} \\|\delta\mathcal L\_{AB}(s)\\|\\,ds } \\]

for an appropriate superoperator norm and a constant \\(C_W\\).

Status: Proposed theorem target.

Importance: Such a result would quantify how much observed persistence can be attributed to residual direct coupling rather than the local evolution of the initial state.

A rigorous derivation must specify the norm, state space, generator regularity, and witness operator.

The experimental programme must establish two facts independently:

1. A selected correlation witness survives during the interval.
2. The direct interaction between the subsystems is absent or bounded below a declared experimental sensitivity.

The first is a quantum-state measurement problem. The second is a dynamical characterization problem.

Where full state tomography is impractical, use a validated preparation characterization and report its uncertainty.

### 14.3 Interaction termination

At \\(t_0\\), terminate the preparation coupling.

For a tunable coupling Hamiltonian:

\\[ H\_{AB}(t) = g(t)A\otimes B, \\]

the target condition is:

\\[ g(t)=0 \qquad t\in[t_0,t_1]. \\]

Measure the residual coupling independently.

Possible characterization methods include:

- spectroscopy;
- calibrated dynamical decoupling;
- cross-talk measurements;
- system identification;
- independent response measurements;
- and Hamiltonian parameter estimation.

The shutdown transient must be included in the analysis. A coupling that decays slowly after the control signal is removed may continue to influence the subsystems.

### 14.4 Persistence measurement

At selected times \\(t_i\\), measure the same witness:

\\[ W(t_i). \\]

The measurement protocol should remain fixed unless the experiment explicitly studies reoptimized persistence.

For a concurrence or negativity experiment, use an estimator with validated statistical properties.

For a Bell test, collect the full setting-dependent outcome distributions and calculate the prespecified Bell expression.

For mutual information, reconstruct sufficient state information or use a validated direct-estimation procedure.

### 14.5 Interaction calibration

The direct coupling should be bounded independently:

\\[ |g(t)|\leq g\_{\max}. \\]

The upper bound should be translated into an estimated maximum effect on the witness over the experimental interval.

Where possible, compare the observed witness trajectory with:

1. a product-channel model;
2. a model including the calibrated residual coupling;
3. a shared-environment model.

This separates genuine local-channel persistence from effects that could be attributed to residual interactions.

### 14.6 Separation sweep

Repeat the experiment at multiple separations:

\\[ r_1\<r_2<\cdots\<r_n. \\]

For each separation, record:

\\[ W(t,r_i). \\]

The environmental coupling and residual direct interaction should be calibrated at every separation.

The experiment should not assume that increasing distance automatically decreases every relevant interaction. A shared environmental mode may have spatial correlations that do not follow the same distance law as the direct coupling.

### 14.7 Environmental-noise sweep

Vary a controlled environmental parameter, such as:

- temperature;
- magnetic-field noise;
- electric-field noise;
- phonon occupation;
- photon occupation;
- engineered dephasing;
- or bath spectral density.

Measure:

\\[ W(t;\lambda_E) \\]

for a set of environmental conditions \\(\lambda_E\\).

This enables the extraction of an empirical persistence law.

### 14.8 Response control

In a separate trial, apply a local perturbation to subsystem \\(A\\) and measure the unconditional statistics at \\(B\\).

Define:

\\[ R\_{B\leftarrow A} = \left. \frac{\partial\langle B\rangle} {\partial\lambda_A} \right|\_{\lambda_A=0}. \\]

The response experiment must be independent of the correlation measurement and must avoid postselection that could create an apparent conditional effect.

The goal is not merely to demonstrate that the subsystems remain correlated. It is to determine whether their persistence is accompanied by an operationally detectable causal influence.

### 14.9 Measurement schedule

A useful schedule includes:

| Phase                    | Measurement                                | Purpose                                    |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Preparation              | Initial witness and state characterization | Establish \\(W_0\\)                        |
| Shutdown                 | Coupling transient                         | Bound residual interaction                 |
| Early persistence        | \\(W(t)\\)                                 | Detect immediate loss                      |
| Intermediate persistence | \\(W(t)\\)                                 | Determine decay profile                    |
| Late persistence         | \\(W(t)\\)                                 | Identify threshold and asymptotic behavior |
| Response control         | Local intervention and remote statistics   | Test causal response                       |
| Environmental sweep      | \\(W(t;\lambda_E)\\)                       | Identify noise dependence                  |
| Separation sweep         | \\(W(t;r)\\)                               | Identify spatial dependence                |

## 15. Data analysis and statistical inference

### 15.1 Discrete persistence estimator

For measurements at times \\(t_i\\), estimate:

\\[ \Pi_W = \frac1T \int\_{t_0}^{t_1}W(t)\\,dt \\]

using numerical quadrature.

For equally spaced sampling, the trapezoidal estimator is:

\\[ \boxed{ \widehat\Pi_W = \frac1T \sum\_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{W_i+W\_{i+1}}{2} (t\_{i+1}-t_i). } \\]

This estimator assumes that the sampling interval is sufficiently small to resolve the witness dynamics.

Rapid oscillations, sudden death, or revival require denser sampling.

### 15.2 Uncertainty propagation

If the measured witness values have covariance matrix:

\\[ \Sigma\_{ij} = \operatorname{Cov}(W_i,W_j), \\]

then the uncertainty in the numerical persistence integral should account for correlations between time samples.

Writing the quadrature as:

\\[ \widehat\Pi_W=\sum_i a_iW_i, \\]

its variance is:

\\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\Pi_W) = \sum\_{i,j}a_i\Sigma\_{ij}a_j. } \\]

Treating all time samples as statistically independent can underestimate uncertainty when the same apparatus, calibration, or environmental realization is used across the interval.

### 15.3 Exponential-model estimation

For a proposed exponential model:

\\[ W(t)=W_0e^{-\Gamma t}, \\]

estimate \\(\Gamma\\) through a likelihood fit to the original measurement data.

A logarithmic linear fit:

\\[ \ln W(t)=\ln W_0-\Gamma t \\]

is appropriate only when the witness is strictly positive and the measurement noise model justifies the transformation.

If the witness can approach zero or become negative, fitting the original observable is preferable.

### 15.4 Model comparison

Compare at least the following candidate models:

\\[ W_1(t)=W_0e^{-\Gamma t}, \\]

\\[ W_2(t)=W_0e^{-(t/\tau)^2}, \\]

\\[ W_3(t)=W_0(1+t/\tau)^{-\alpha}, \\]

and, where justified:

\\[ W_4(t)=W_0e^{-\Phi(t)} \\]

with a physically derived non-Markovian \\(\Phi(t)\\).

Model selection should account for parameter count, uncertainty, and out-of-sample predictive performance.

A more complicated decay law should not be preferred merely because it fits the observed data more closely.

### 15.5 Witness-threshold analysis

Define a persistence threshold \\(\varepsilon\\).

The threshold-crossing time is:

\\[ \boxed{ \tau\_\varepsilon = \inf\\{t\geq t_0:W(t)\leq\varepsilon\\}. } \\]

For a Bell witness, \\(\varepsilon=0\\) corresponds to the theoretical local bound, while a positive threshold may represent the minimum violation considered experimentally resolvable.

For entanglement witnesses, the threshold must be interpreted relative to the separable-state boundary.

The reported persistence time should therefore distinguish:

- theoretical witness disappearance;
- loss of statistically significant certification;
- and loss of operational usefulness.

These times need not coincide.

### 15.6 Survival curves

For repeated preparations, define a binary survival indicator:

\\[ S_i(t) = \mathbf 1[ W_i(t)>\varepsilon ]. \\]

An ensemble persistence probability can be estimated as:

\\[ \boxed{ P\_{\mathrm{surv}}(t) = \frac1N\sum\_{i=1}^{N}S_i(t). } \\]

This is useful when environmental fluctuations or preparation variability cause different trials to exhibit different persistence times.

It must not be confused with the average witness:

\\[ \langle W(t)\rangle. \\]

The survival probability measures how often the threshold is exceeded; the mean witness measures average strength.

### 15.7 Falsification criteria

A proposed MFCP persistence law should be rejected or revised if:

1. it fails to predict a held-out time interval;
2. its fitted parameters vary systematically with detector settings that should be irrelevant;
3. it cannot account for independently measured environmental noise;
4. it requires residual direct coupling inconsistent with the calibration;
5. it fails under repeated separation sweeps;
6. or its claimed universal scaling collapses when the microscopic implementation changes.

A valid discovery should predict new data rather than merely describe an observed decay curve.

## 16. Novelty assessment

### 16.1 Established scientific foundations

The following are established subjects:

- persistence of entanglement after subsystem separation;
- entanglement dynamics under local channels;
- decoherence and dephasing;
- quantum memory;
- open-system dynamics;
- quantum mutual information;
- Bell nonlocality and its degradation under noise;
- environmental spectral-density models;
- non-Markovian information backflow;
- and local no-signaling.

The fact that a correlated quantum state can survive after its preparation interaction ends is not new.

### 16.2 What MFCP contributes as a framework

MFCP organizes these established subjects around a specific operational question:

> How much of a selected correlation resource survives during an interval in which direct subsystem interaction is independently bounded or absent?

The framework adds a common measurement structure:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Initial state} \rightarrow \text{interaction termination} \rightarrow \text{local/environmental evolution} \rightarrow \text{witness trajectory} \rightarrow \text{persistence functional}. } \\]

This is a useful unifying architecture, but a formalism alone is not a new physical discovery.

### 16.3 Potentially novel results

A substantive new contribution could take one of the following forms.

A. Universal persistence scaling law

A normalized witness trajectory converges to a common function across physically distinct systems within a declared environmental universality class.

B. Sharp persistence bound

A bound relates entanglement or total-correlation survival to independently measured environmental spectral density, separation, and coupling strength.

C. Residual-interaction certification theorem

A theorem bounds the maximum witness change attributable to calibrated residual direct coupling.

D. Witness hierarchy theorem

A rigorous result identifies conditions under which entanglement, Bell nonlocality, and total correlations must disappear in a particular order.

E. Non-Markovian persistence invariant

A new quantity characterizes witness revival and environmental memory independently of the particular time parametrization or measurement scale.

F. Separation-dependent environmental law

A reproducible scaling relation connects persistence to spatial environmental correlations and detector separation.

### 16.4 What would not establish novelty

The following would be insufficient:

- demonstrating that a Bell pair remains entangled after its preparation interaction ends;
- showing that local unitary evolution preserves entanglement;
- measuring exponential dephasing;
- defining the time average of a witness without deriving a new result;
- demonstrating that classical correlations persist;
- or observing that no direct coupling is required in a known product-channel model.

These are demonstrations of established quantum dynamics.

### 16.5 The principal obstacle

The largest obstacle is witness dependence.

A persistence law for concurrence need not be a persistence law for mutual information. A Bell violation may disappear while entanglement remains. A covariance can vanish because of measurement-basis rotation even when the underlying state remains entangled.

Therefore, a universal MFCP law cannot be asserted without defining the class of witnesses to which it applies.

A strong result should either:

1. derive a law for a mathematically well-defined class of correlation measures; or
2. identify a particular operational witness whose behavior has universal significance.

### 16.6 Novelty criterion

The decisive test is whether a proposed law can be written:

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi_W = \mathcal F( \rho_0, J_E, \beta, r, T, \mathfrak W ) } \\]

with all inputs independently measurable or theoretically specified, and with the function \\(\mathcal F\\) derived rather than fitted arbitrarily.

Here:

- \\(\rho_0\\) is the initial state;
- \\(J_E\\) is the environmental spectral density;
- \\(\beta\\) is inverse temperature;
- \\(r\\) is subsystem separation;
- \\(T\\) is persistence duration;
- \\(\mathfrak W\\) identifies the witness.

A universal result would require demonstrating that the relation remains valid across a declared model class and survives changes in microscopic implementation.

## 17. Limitations and unresolved problems

### 17.1 The absence of direct interaction is model-relative

A Hamiltonian decomposition depends on the chosen system-environment partition.

An interaction term may be moved between subsystem and environmental descriptions under changes of model or representation.

The experimental definition must therefore specify the physical subsystem boundaries and the operational meaning of direct coupling.

### 17.2 Shared environmental degrees of freedom

Even if direct coupling vanishes, a common environment can create effective interactions or correlated noise.

The phrase mediator-free cannot mean that no environmental degree of freedom exists unless the experiment independently establishes that stronger claim.

The defensible initial claim is that continued direct interaction is unnecessary.

### 17.3 Witness incompleteness

A single witness probes only a restricted aspect of the joint state.

A zero covariance does not imply a product state. A zero Bell violation does not imply separability. A small mutual information does not necessarily imply that a specific entanglement witness is small.

The witness hierarchy must remain explicit.

### 17.4 Dependence on initial preparation

The persistence profile depends on how the initial correlations were generated.

Two states with the same initial concurrence may have different responses to local noise because their local marginals, coherences, and environmental coupling structures differ.

A universal law may therefore require more state information than one scalar entanglement measure.

### 17.5 Environmental memory

Non-Markovian dynamics can produce revivals.

A monotonically decaying persistence law will fail for such systems even when the model is physically correct.

A general MFCP theory must accommodate nonmonotonic witness trajectories.

### 17.6 Residual coupling

A small direct interaction can become important over a long interval.

A coupling that is negligible instantaneously may produce an accumulated effect.

The relevant error bound must therefore depend on the time integral of the residual generator, not only on its instantaneous magnitude.

### 17.7 Measurement back-action

Repeatedly measuring a quantum witness can itself disturb the state.

A time-resolved persistence experiment should use a measurement architecture that either:

- performs weak measurements with calibrated back-action;
- uses repeated identically prepared trials at different observation times;
- or reconstructs the trajectory through a validated quantum process model.

Repeated projective measurement of the same individual pair may alter the very persistence being measured.

### 17.8 No ontological conclusion from persistence alone

The survival of a correlation witness does not prove:

- that correlations exist independently of physical states;
- that they are transmitted through a non-temporal mechanism;
- that every mediator is absent;
- or that quantum correlations constitute a new fundamental interaction.

MFCP is initially a dynamical and operational framework.

Any deeper ontological interpretation must be supported independently.

## 18. Research roadmap

The strongest route to a predictive MFCP theory is to establish a sequence of increasingly demanding results.

### Phase I — Exact baseline

Use two qubits initially prepared in a Bell state.

Implement independent local dephasing.

Derive and measure:

\\[ C(t)=e^{-(\gamma_A+\gamma_B)t} \\]

for the chosen phase-damping convention.

Verify:

\\[ \Pi_C(T) = \frac{ 1-e^{-(\gamma_A+\gamma_B)T} }{ (\gamma_A+\gamma_B)T }. \\]

This is the first reproducible reference model.

### Phase II — Independent witness comparison

Measure:

\\[ C(t),\qquad S\_{\max}(t),\qquad I(A:B)\_t. \\]

Determine how entanglement, Bell nonlocality, and total correlation decay relative to one another.

This tests whether a meaningful witness hierarchy exists for the selected model.

### Phase III — Environmental characterization

Measure or reconstruct the local environmental spectral densities:

\\[ J_A(\omega),\qquad J_B(\omega). \\]

Then predict the decay functional:

\\[ \Phi(t) \\]

from the independently measured bath parameters.

A successful prediction should not require fitting a separate arbitrary decay constant to every persistence curve.

### Phase IV — Shared-environment comparison

Compare independent and common-environment configurations.

Determine whether the observed persistence differs because of environmental cross-correlations rather than direct subsystem interaction.

### Phase V — Separation scaling

Repeat the experiment across a range of subsystem separations.

Test whether the normalized persistence curve depends on:

\\[ \frac{t}{\tau\_{\mathrm{env}}}, \qquad \frac{r}{\ell\_{\mathrm{env}}}. \\]

### Phase VI — Universal law

Compare physically distinct implementations.

A genuine universal law should predict the same normalized behavior within a declared universality class, after the appropriate rescaling.

### Phase VII — Interaction-error theorem

Develop a rigorous bound connecting residual direct coupling to the maximum deviation from product-channel persistence.

This would make experimental claims of mediator-free persistence quantitatively defensible.

## 19. A stronger mathematical result: robustness against residual interaction

An important advance would be to quantify how closely an experimentally realized system follows the ideal product-channel model.

This section develops a useful perturbative bound.

### 19.1 Ideal and actual generators

Let the ideal local evolution satisfy:

\\[ \frac{d\rho_0}{dt} = \mathcal L_0(t)\rho_0, \\]

where:

\\[ \mathcal L_0(t) = \mathcal L_A(t)+\mathcal L_B(t). \\]

Let the actual evolution contain an additional residual interaction:

\\[ \frac{d\rho}{dt} = [\mathcal L_0(t)+\delta\mathcal L(t)]\rho. \\]

Assume both evolutions are completely positive and trace preserving, and that they begin with the same initial state:

\\[ \rho(t_0)=\rho_0(t_0). \\]

### 19.2 Trace-distance deviation

Define:

\\[ \Delta\rho(t)=\rho(t)-\rho_0(t). \\]

Let \\(\mathcal U_0(t,s)\\) denote the propagator generated by \\(\mathcal L_0\\).

Variation of constants gives:

\\[ \Delta\rho(t) = \int\_{t_0}^{t} \mathcal U_0(t,s) \delta\mathcal L(s)\rho(s)\\,ds. \\]

Because the ideal propagator is CPTP, it is contractive in trace norm for Hermitian operators:

\\[ \\|\mathcal U_0(t,s)X\\|\_1 \leq \\|X\\|\_1. \\]

Consequently:

\\[ \\|\Delta\rho(t)\\|\_1 \leq \int\_{t_0}^{t} \\| \delta\mathcal L(s)\rho(s) \\|\_1\\,ds. \\]

Using the induced trace norm:

\\[ \\|\delta\mathcal L(s)\rho(s)\\|\_1 \leq \\|\delta\mathcal L(s)\\|\_{1\rightarrow1} \\|\rho(s)\\|\_1, \\]

and:

\\[ \\|\rho(s)\\|\_1=1, \\]

we obtain:

\\[ \boxed{ \\|\rho(t)-\rho_0(t)\\|\_1 \leq \int\_{t_0}^{t} \\|\delta\mathcal L(s)\\|\_{1\rightarrow1}\\,ds. } \\]

### 19.3 Witness-deviation bound

For a bounded Hermitian witness operator \\(O\\):

\\[ W(t)=\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho(t)], \\]

and:

\\[ W_0(t)=\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho_0(t)]. \\]

Then:

\\[ |W(t)-W_0(t)| \leq \\|O\\|\_\infty \\|\rho(t)-\rho_0(t)\\|\_1. \\]

Therefore:

\\[ \boxed{ |W(t)-W_0(t)| \leq \\|O\\|\_\infty \int\_{t_0}^{t} \\|\delta\mathcal L(s)\\|\_{1\rightarrow1}\\,ds. } \\]

This is a useful robustness inequality.

It bounds the deviation of an observed witness from its ideal local-evolution prediction in terms of the accumulated residual generator.

### 19.4 Bound on the persistence functional

Integrating the witness-deviation inequality over the observation interval gives:

\\[ |\Pi_W-\Pi_W^{(0)}| \leq \frac{\\|O\\|\_\infty}{T} \int\_{t_0}^{t_1} \int\_{t_0}^{t} \\|\delta\mathcal L(s)\\|\_{1\rightarrow1} \\,ds\\,dt. \\]

Changing the order of integration:

\\[ \boxed{ |\Pi_W-\Pi_W^{(0)}| \leq \frac{\\|O\\|\_\infty}{T} \int\_{t_0}^{t_1} (t_1-s) \\|\delta\mathcal L(s)\\|\_{1\rightarrow1}\\,ds. } \\]

This result has a useful physical interpretation: residual interaction at earlier times can influence the persistence integral for a longer remaining duration.

It is therefore not sufficient to characterize residual coupling only at the final measurement time.

Status: Proven under the stated generator, norm, and CPTP assumptions.

Research significance: This inequality supplies a quantitative correction budget for real MFCP experiments. It does not require residual coupling to be exactly zero.

A stronger future result would replace the induced superoperator norm with experimentally measurable Hamiltonian and environmental parameters, yielding a directly usable laboratory bound.

## 20. Final synthesis and conclusion

Mediator-Free Correlation Persistence identifies a distinction between two physical processes that are often treated as though they were the same:

- the preservation of a relational property of a joint quantum state;
- the continuous transmission of a new influence between subsystems.

The two are not equivalent.

A pair of quantum subsystems can be prepared in an entangled state, separated, and subsequently evolve under independent local operations. Their entanglement can remain constant under local unitary evolution or decay gradually under local environmental noise. Classical correlations can persist even when entanglement is absent. Bell nonlocality may persist over a different interval from either entanglement or total correlation.

These behaviors are not speculative. They follow from established quantum mechanics.

The contribution of MFCP is to formulate a common quantitative framework for investigating them. Its principal observable is a selected correlation witness:

\\[ W(t). \\]

Its principal temporal functional is:

\\[ \boxed{ \Pi_W[t_0,t_1] = \frac{1}{t_1-t_0} \int\_{t_0}^{t_1}W(t)\\,dt. } \\]

Its central dynamical model is:

\\[ \boxed{ \rho\_{AB}(t) = [ \mathcal E_A(t,t_0) \otimes \mathcal E_B(t,t_0) ]\rho\_{AB}(t_0), } \\]

for the ideal product-channel regime.

Its central operational distinction is:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Persistent correlation} \neq \text{continuous causal communication}. } \\]

The analysis establishes several results:

1. Local unitary evolution preserves bipartite entanglement.
2. Local CPTP evolution cannot increase standard bipartite entanglement monotones.
3. Quantum mutual information cannot increase under independent local channels.
4. Local trace-preserving operations cannot change the unconditional reduced state of the remote subsystem.
5. Exponential witness decay produces an analytically calculable persistence average.
6. Small deviations from product-channel evolution can be bounded by the accumulated norm of the residual generator.

These results supply a mathematical foundation for MFCP, but they do not establish a new universal persistence law.

The principal open problem is to derive a quantitative relationship connecting:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{initial state structure} + \text{environmental spectral properties} + \text{subsystem separation} + \text{local dynamics} \longrightarrow \text{correlation persistence}. } \\]

The most promising route is to begin with exactly solvable dephasing models, extend to non-Markovian environments, and then compare physically distinct implementations within a controlled scaling regime.

The strongest potential discovery would be a universal persistence function or sharp bound that predicts the survival of a specified correlation resource from independently characterized environmental and state parameters.

Such a result would distinguish the maintenance of an already-established relational state from the transmission of new causal influence, without requiring a new mechanism of superluminal communication or an unsupported ontological interpretation.

The final research proposition is therefore:

\\[ \boxed{ \textit{ A quantum correlation can persist during interaction-free evolution because the joint state retains relational structure under local dynamics; the quantitative survival of that structure is governed by the initial state and the subsequent dynamical environment, not necessarily by continuous communication between the subsystems. } } \\]

The first part is established quantum theory. The second part is the predictive programme of MFCP.
