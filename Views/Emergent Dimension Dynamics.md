## Abstract

Emergent Dimension Theory proposes that dimension is not a primitive attribute of a space but an invariant generated by structural complexity,

\[
\mathcal D=\Phi(\mathcal C),
\qquad 
\mathcal C=(C_s,C_r,C_g,C_c),
\]

where \(C_s\), \(C_r\), \(C_g\), and \(C_c\) encode symmetry, recursion, connectivity, and computational organization. Here this principle is promoted from a structural hypothesis to a dynamical physical theory. By treating the complexity coordinates \(C^A\) as fields on a relational substrate and coarse-graining over the fibers of the map \(\Phi\), one obtains a scalar dimension field \(D(x)=\Phi(C(x))\) with an effective kinetic coefficient determined by the complexity-space metric. The resulting effective action contains a dimension-dependent measure and a dimension-dependent gravitational coupling. Variation yields modified Einstein equations, a dynamical equation for \(D\), a universal fifth force proportional to \(\nabla D\), Yukawa corrections to Newtonian gravity, scale-dependent spectral dimension, modified gravitational-wave damping, cosmological dimension rolling, dimensional phase transitions, and ultraviolet dimensional reduction. Classical four-dimensional geometry appears as a low-energy fixed point of the complexity flow, while fractal, spectral, information, and data-manifold dimensions arise as projections of the same structural invariant. The theory predicts running dimension, critical dimensional susceptibility, black-hole entropy corrections, and possible deviations in gravitational-wave propagation.

## 2. Coarse Graining to a Dimension Field

The first nontrivial physical consequence of EDT is that the dimension field acquires a kinetic term determined by the geometry of complexity space.

For a fixed value of \(D\), define the fiber

\[
\mathcal F_D
=
\{C^A\mid \Phi(C)=D\}.
\]

The effective potential for \(D\) is obtained by minimizing the structural potential over the fiber:

\[
\boxed{
U(D)
=
\min_{C\in\mathcal F_D}V(C).
}
\]

The kinetic coefficient is obtained by asking: what is the least costly displacement in complexity space that changes \(D\) by one unit?

Let \(X^A\) be a vector in complexity space satisfying

\[
\Phi_A X^A=1.
\]

The minimal kinetic cost is found by minimizing

\[
G_{AB}X^AX^B
\]

subject to the constraint \(\Phi_A X^A=1\). Introducing a Lagrange multiplier \(\lambda\),

\[
\delta\left[
G_{AB}X^AX^B
-
\lambda(\Phi_A X^A-1)
\right]=0
\]

gives

\[
2G_{AB}X^B-\lambda\Phi_A=0.
\]

Thus

\[
X^A
=
\frac{\lambda}{2}G^{AB}\Phi_B.
\]

The constraint gives

\[
\frac{\lambda}{2}G^{AB}\Phi_A\Phi_B=1,
\]

so

\[
\lambda
=
\frac{2}{G^{AB}\Phi_A\Phi_B}.
\]

Therefore

\[
X^A
=
\frac{G^{AB}\Phi_B}{G^{CD}\Phi_C\Phi_D}.
\]

The minimal squared norm is

\[
G_{AB}X^AX^B
=
\frac{1}{G^{AB}\Phi_A\Phi_B}.
\]

Hence the effective dimension-field kinetic coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
Z(D)
=
\left[
G^{AB}(C_D)\Phi_A(C_D)\Phi_B(C_D)
\right]^{-1},
}
\]

where \(C_D\in\mathcal F_D\) is the minimizing configuration. If several minimizing branches exist, \(U(D)\) and \(Z(D)\) become multivalued, producing first-order dimensional phase transitions.

The Hessian of the EDT map,

\[
\boxed{
D_{AB}
=
\frac{\partial^2\Phi}{\partial C^A\partial C^B},
}
\]

controls nonlinear mixing among structural modes. Expanding around a background \(C^A_0\),

\[
\delta D
=
\Phi_A\delta C^A
+
\frac12 D_{AB}\delta C^A\delta C^B
+\cdots.
\]

Thus the scalar dimension is the linear projection of structural fluctuations, while \(D_{AB}\) governs their quadratic dimensional response.
