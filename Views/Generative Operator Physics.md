## Recursive Derivation of Quantum Dynamics, Causal Geometry, and Emergent Gravitation from Generative Operator Theory

## Abstract

We derive a physical theory from the axioms of Generative Operator Theory (GOT). The central GOT recursion

\[
\mathcal O_{n+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal O_n)
\]

is reinterpreted as a primitive law of physical evolution: operator objects generate the operator spaces, states, observables, spectra, and geometry of subsequent physical generations. We show that standard quantum mechanics, open quantum dynamics, causal propagation, quantum information geometry, gauge curvature, and spectral gravity arise as specializations of a single recursive operator formalism. The paper develops five principal results. First, completely positive trace-preserving generative maps yield Lindblad dynamics in the infinitesimal limit, with Hamiltonian and dissipative structure determined by the linearization of \(\Gamma\). Second, a generative Noether theorem relates symmetries of \(\Gamma\) to conserved charges and current laws. Third, tensorial GOT induces causal cones and quantum speed limits, giving an operator-theoretic origin to relativistic light-cone structure. Fourth, recursive commutator and tensor generation produce emergent spectral metrics, effective dimension, and holographic entropy bounds. Fifth, noncommuting local generative maps generate gauge curvature, and the spectral action of the generated Dirac operator yields Einstein–Yang–Mills-type field equations. The resulting framework, called Generative Operator Physics (GOP), treats spacetime, matter, and interactions as derived structures generated by recursive operator dynamics.

**Keywords:** generative operator theory, quantum dynamics, Lindblad equations, causal cones, spectral geometry, emergent gravity, gauge theory, tensor networks, operator algebras.

# 1. Physical Postulates from Generative Operator Theory

Generative Operator Theory begins with the recursion

\[
\mathcal O_{n+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal O_n),
\tag{1.1}
\]

where \(\mathcal O_n=(H_n,T_n)\) is an operator object and \(\Gamma\) is a generative transformation. To obtain physics, we interpret the operator object not as a passive mathematical datum but as the total physical state of a system at generation \(n\).

We introduce the following physical postulates.

## Postulate P1: Operatorial ontology

The physical content of a system is encoded by an operator object

\[
\mathcal O_n=(H_n,\rho_n,A_n),
\]

where \(H_n\) is a Hilbert space, \(\rho_n\) is a density operator on \(H_n\), and \(A_n\) is an observable algebra acting on \(H_n\).

Equivalently, the physical state is a pair

\[
(\rho_n,\Phi_n),
\]

where \(\rho_n\in \mathfrak T_1(H_n)\) is a trace-class state and \(\Phi_n:B(H_n)\to B(H_n)\) is the observable evolution map.

## Postulate P2: Generative dynamics

The physical evolution from generation \(n\) to generation \(n+1\) is given by

\[
\rho_{n+1}=\Psi(\rho_n),
\qquad
A_{n+1}=\Phi(A_n),
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(\Psi\) and \(\Phi\) are dual generative maps satisfying

\[
\operatorname{Tr}[\Phi(A)\rho]
=
\operatorname{Tr}[A\Psi(\rho)].
\tag{1.3}
\]

The elementary GOT recursion becomes

\[
\mathcal O_{n+1}=\Gamma(\mathcal O_n),
\qquad
\Gamma=(\Psi,\Phi).
\tag{1.4}
\]

## Postulate P3: Physicality constraints

A generative map is physical if it preserves positivity and normalization. Thus \(\Psi\) is completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP):

\[
\Psi(\rho)=\sum_{\alpha}K_\alpha\rho K_\alpha^\dagger,
\qquad
\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger K_\alpha=I.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The dual observable map \(\Phi\) is unital and completely positive:

\[
\Phi(A)=\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger A K_\alpha,
\qquad
\Phi(I)=I.
\tag{1.6}
\]

## Postulate P4: Generative covariance

If \(U:H\to H'\) is a unitary equivalence, then

\[
\Gamma(U\rho U^\dagger)=U_\Gamma\Psi(\rho)U_\Gamma^\dagger,
\tag{1.7}
\]

and similarly for observables. Localizing this covariance generates gauge connections.

## Postulate P5: Spectral action

The physical action associated with a generated operator \(D_\Gamma\) is spectral:

\[
S_\Gamma=\operatorname{Tr} f(D_\Gamma/\Lambda),
\tag{1.8}
\]

where \(f\) is a positive cutoff function and \(\Lambda\) is a spectral scale. Geometry, gravity, and gauge fields arise from the spectrum of recursively generated Dirac-type operators.

These five postulates convert GOT into a physical theory.

# 2. Quantum Dynamics as the Infinitesimal Limit of Generative Recursion

## 2.1 Unitary generation and the Heisenberg equation

Let

\[
\Gamma_U(A)=U^\dagger A U,
\qquad
U=e^{-i\tau H},
\tag{2.1}
\]

where \(H=H^\dagger\) and \(\tau>0\) is the elementary generative step. Then

\[
A_{n+1}=e^{i\tau H}A_n e^{-i\tau H}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Iterating gives

\[
A_n=e^{in\tau H}A_0 e^{-in\tau H}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Define physical time by

\[
t=n\tau.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Then

\[
\frac{A_{n+1}-A_n}{\tau}
=
\frac{e^{i\tau H}A_n e^{-i\tau H}-A_n}{\tau}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Taking \(\tau\to0\) yields

\[
\frac{dA}{dt}=i[H,A].
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus the Heisenberg equation is the infinitesimal form of unitary GOT recursion.

In tensor components,

\[
A_{n+1}{}^i{}_j
=
U^i{}_a A_n{}^a{}_b \overline{U^j{}_b},
\tag{2.7}
\]

and

\[
i[H,A]^i{}_j
=
i\left(H^i{}_a A^a{}_j-A^i{}_a H^a{}_j\right).
\tag{2.8}
\]

The commutator tensor of GOT,

\[
C(H)^i{}_j{}^a{}_b
=
H^i{}_b\delta^a{}_j-\delta^i{}_b H^a{}_j,
\tag{2.9}
\]

therefore encodes the infinitesimal generator of quantum time evolution.

## 2.2 Completely positive generation and the Lindblad equation

Let \(\Psi_\tau\) be a \(\tau\)-dependent CPTP generative map with \(\Psi_0=\operatorname{id}\). Assume differentiability at \(\tau=0\). Write its Kraus operators as

\[
K_0(\tau)=I+\tau A+O(\tau^2),
\qquad
K_\alpha(\tau)=\sqrt{\tau}\,L_\alpha+O(\tau),
\quad \alpha\ge1.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Trace preservation requires

\[
\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger K_\alpha=I.
\tag{2.11}
\]

Expanding to first order gives

\[
A^\dagger+A+\sum_{\alpha\ge1}L_\alpha^\dagger L_\alpha=0.
\tag{2.12}
\]

Hence there exists a self-adjoint operator \(H\) such that

\[
A=-iH-\frac12\sum_{\alpha\ge1}L_\alpha^\dagger L_\alpha.
\tag{2.13}
\]

Now expand

\[
\Psi_\tau(\rho)
=
K_0\rho K_0^\dagger
+
\sum_{\alpha\ge1}K_\alpha\rho K_\alpha^\dagger
+
O(\tau^2).
\tag{2.14}
\]

Using (2.10) and (2.13),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_\tau(\rho)
&=
\rho
+\tau\Bigg[
-iH\rho+i\rho H \\
&\qquad
-\frac12\sum_\alpha
\left(
L_\alpha^\dagger L_\alpha\rho+\rho L_\alpha^\dagger L_\alpha
\right)
+\sum_\alpha L_\alpha\rho L_\alpha^\dagger
\Bigg]
+O(\tau^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.15}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{\rho_{n+1}-\rho_n}{\tau}
\to
\mathcal L(\rho),
\tag{2.16}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L(\rho)
=
-i[H,\rho]
+
\sum_\alpha
\left(
L_\alpha\rho L_\alpha^\dagger
-
\frac12\{L_\alpha^\dagger L_\alpha,\rho\}
\right).
}
\tag{2.17}
\]

This is the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad generator. Hence Markovian open quantum dynamics is the continuum limit of completely positive GOT recursion.

## 2.3 Energy as the phase part of the generative linearization

Let \(\Gamma\) be differentiable at a fixed point \(\rho_*\). Its Fréchet derivative

\[
D\Gamma_{\rho_*}
\tag{2.18}
\]

is the linearized generative superoperator. Decompose its spectrum:

\[
\sigma(D\Gamma_{\rho_*})
=
\{e^{-i\tau E_j-\tau\gamma_j}\}.
\tag{2.19}
\]

The phases define physical energies,

\[
E_j=-\frac{1}{\tau}\arg\lambda_j,
\tag{2.20}
\]

while the moduli define decay rates,

\[
\gamma_j=-\frac{1}{\tau}\log|\lambda_j|.
\tag{2.21}
\]

Thus GOT gives an operator-theoretic definition of energy and dissipation:

> Energy is the angular velocity of the generative spectrum; decay is its radial contraction.

# 3. Generative Noether Theorem and Conserved Currents

## 3.1 Conserved observables

Let \(\Phi\) be the Heisenberg-picture generative map,

\[
A_{n+1}=\Phi(A_n).
\tag{3.1}
\]

An observable \(C\) is conserved if

\[
\Phi(C)=C.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then for any initial state \(\rho_0\),

\[
\langle C\rangle_n
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_0 A_n)
\]

satisfies

\[
\langle C\rangle_n
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_0 \Phi^n(C))
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_0 C),
\tag{3.3}
\]

so the expectation value is constant.

In Kraus form,

\[
\Phi(C)=\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger C K_\alpha.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus \(C\) is conserved iff

\[
\boxed{
\sum_\alpha K_\alpha^\dagger C K_\alpha=C.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

In components,

\[
\sum_\alpha
(K_\alpha^\dagger)^i{}_a
C^a{}_b
K_\alpha{}^b{}_j
=
C^i{}_j.
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the GOT form of a conservation law.

## 3.2 Symmetry implies conservation

Let \(U(\theta)=e^{-i\theta Q}\) be a one-parameter unitary group generated by a self-adjoint charge \(Q\). Suppose the generative map is covariant:

\[
\Phi\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{U(\theta)}
=
\operatorname{Ad}_{U(\theta)}\circ\Phi.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Differentiating at \(\theta=0\),

\[
\Phi([Q,A])=[Q,\Phi(A)].
\tag{3.8}
\]

If, in addition, the fixed-point algebra of \(\Phi\) contains \(Q\), i.e.

\[
\Phi(Q)=Q,
\tag{3.9}
\]

then \(Q\) is a conserved charge.

For Hamiltonian generation \(\Phi(A)=e^{i\tau H}Ae^{-i\tau H}\), the condition becomes

\[
[H,Q]=0.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Thus the usual quantum Noether theorem is recovered.

## 3.3 Local generative currents

Let the Hilbert space factor over a lattice,

\[
H=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}H_x,
\tag{3.11}
\]

and let \(\Phi\) be generated by local Kraus transitions \(K_\alpha(x,y)\) moving quantum information from site \(x\) to site \(y\). Define the local charge density

\[
q_x(n)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_n Q_x).
\tag{3.12}
\]

The discrete continuity equation is

\[
q_x(n+1)-q_x(n)
+
\sum_y J_{xy}(n)
=
0,
\tag{3.13}
\]

where the current is

\[
J_{xy}(n)
=
\sum_{\alpha:x\to y}
\operatorname{Tr}
\left[
\rho_n
\left(
K_\alpha^\dagger Q_y K_\alpha-Q_x
\right)
\right].
\tag{3.14}
\]

Thus GOT generalizes Noether’s theorem to recursive, possibly open and discrete, physical systems.

# 4. Causal Cones, Light Propagation, and Quantum Speed Limits

## 4.1 Locality from tensor generation

Let the generative map be local:

\[
\Phi=\exp(\tau\mathcal L),
\qquad
\mathcal L=\sum_X \mathcal L_X,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where each \(\mathcal L_X\) acts nontrivially only on a finite region \(X\). For an observable \(A\) supported in region \(X\), define

\[
A_n=\Phi^n(A).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The support of \(A_n\) grows with \(n\). The generative causal cone of \(X\) is

\[
\mathcal C_n(X)
=
\bigcup_{k=0}^n \operatorname{supp}(\Phi^k(A)).
\tag{4.3}
\]

If the local interaction range is \(R\) and the local generator norm is bounded by \(J\), then a Lieb–Robinson-type estimate holds:

\[
\boxed{
\|[A_n,B]\|
\le
C\|A\|\|B\|\,|X|
\exp\left[
-a\left(d(X,Y)-v_\Gamma n\tau\right)
\right],
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

where

\[
v_\Gamma \sim \frac{2eJR}{\tau}
\tag{4.5}
\]

is the generative velocity.

This gives an operator-theoretic origin to light cones. The speed of causality is not postulated but derived from the norm and locality of the generative map.

## 4.2 Quantum speed limit

For unitary generation with Hamiltonian \(H\), define the energy variance

\[
\Delta E_n^2
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_n H^2)
-
\left(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_n H)\right)^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Let \(F(\rho,\sigma)=\|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\|_1^2\) be the fidelity. The Bures angle is

\[
\mathcal L_B(\rho,\sigma)
=
\cos^{-1}\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then the discrete quantum speed limit is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_B(\rho_0,\rho_n)
\le
\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Delta E_k.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

For a CPTP generative map with spectral gap \(\gamma>0\),

\[
\|\rho_n-\rho_*\|_1
\le
C e^{-\gamma n\tau},
\tag{4.9}
\]

where \(\rho_*\) is the attractor state. Thus the approach to equilibrium is governed by the subdominant generative spectrum.

# 5. Emergent Geometry from Recursive Spectra

## 5.1 Generative Dirac operators

Let \(D_0\) be a self-adjoint seed operator. Define a recursively generated Dirac-type operator by

\[
D_{n+1}=\Gamma_D(D_n).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Natural choices include:

1. **Functional generation:**
   \[
   D_{n+1}=f(D_n).
   \tag{5.2}
   \]

2. **Commutator generation:**
   \[
   D_{n+1}=\operatorname{ad}_{D_n}.
   \tag{5.3}
   \]

3. **Additive tensor generation:**
   \[
   D_{n+1}=D_n\otimes I+I\otimes D_n.
   \tag{5.4}
   \]

Each recursion produces a spectral triple

\[
(A_n,H_n,D_n).
\tag{5.5}
\]

The geometry is not fixed; it is generated.

## 5.2 Spectral distance from commutators

For states \(\rho,\sigma\), define the generative spectral distance

\[
\boxed{
d_n(\rho,\sigma)
=
\sup_{\|[D_n,A]\|\le1}
\left|
\operatorname{Tr}[(\rho-\sigma)A]
\right|.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the Connes distance adapted to recursively generated Dirac operators.

In components,

\[
[D_n,A]^i{}_j
=
C(D_n)^i{}_j{}^a{}_b A^b{}_a,
\tag{5.7}
\]

with

\[
C(D_n)^i{}_j{}^a{}_b
=
D_n{}^i{}_b\delta^a{}_j
-
\delta^i{}_b D_n{}^a{}_j.
\tag{5.8}
\]

The commutator tensor therefore defines a metric structure on the space of observables and states.

In the semiclassical limit, if \(D_n\) behaves like a position or Dirac operator, then

\[
[D_n,A]\sim -i\nabla A,
\tag{5.9}
\]

and the condition \(\|[D_n,A]\|\le1\) becomes a Lipschitz condition. The distance (5.6) reduces to a geodesic distance.

Thus:

> Spacetime distance is dual to the norm of recursively generated commutators.

## 5.3 Tensor generation and effective dimension

Let tensor generation produce a sequence of Hilbert spaces

\[
H_{n+1}=H_n\otimes H_n.
\tag{5.10}
\]

If \(\dim H_0=d\), then

\[
\dim H_n=d^{2^n}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

The double-exponential growth of dimension defines a dimensional entropy

\[
h_{\dim}
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{1}{n}\log\log\dim H_n
=
\log 2.
\tag{5.12}
\]

To extract an effective spatial dimension, suppose the generated tensor network has volume \(V_n\) and boundary area \(A_n\). If

\[
A_n\sim V_n^{(d_{\rm eff}-1)/d_{\rm eff}},
\tag{5.13}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
d_{\rm eff}
=
\left(
1-
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\log A_n}{\log V_n}
\right)^{-1}.
}
\tag{5.14}
\]

Thus dimension is not assumed but computed from the asymptotic shape of the generative tensor network.

## 5.4 Entanglement and holographic bound

For a region \(R_n\) in the generated tensor network, let \(\partial R_n\) be its boundary. If each bond carries local dimension \(\chi\), then

\[
S(R_n)\le |\partial R_n|\log\chi.
\tag{5.15}
\]

This is an entanglement area law. In the GOT setting, it arises automatically from tensor generation. The holographic principle is therefore a consequence of finite generative branching.

# 6. Gauge Fields, Curvature, and Gravity from Noncommuting Generative Maps

## 6.1 Local generative covariance

Suppose the generative map varies locally:

\[
\mathcal O_{n+1}(x)=\Gamma_x(\mathcal O_n(x)).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let local unitary frames act by

\[
\mathcal O_n(x)\mapsto V(x)\mathcal O_n(x)V(x)^\dagger.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Covariance requires

\[
\Gamma_{V(x)}
=
\operatorname{Ad}_{V(x)}\circ\Gamma_x\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{V(x)^\dagger}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

To compare neighboring sites, introduce a parallel transporter

\[
U_{x,\mu}:H_x\to H_{x+\mu}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The transported local generator is

\[
\Gamma_{x+\mu}
=
\operatorname{Ad}_{U_{x,\mu}}\circ\Gamma_x.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The field \(U_{x,\mu}\) is a gauge connection.

## 6.2 Curvature from plaquette recursion

Let

\[
U_{x,\mu}=e^{-i\tau A_\mu(x)},
\tag{6.6}
\]

where \(A_\mu(x)\) is a self-adjoint gauge potential. The plaquette holonomy is

\[
W_{\mu\nu}(x)
=
U_{x,\mu}
U_{x+\mu,\nu}
U_{x+\nu,\mu}^\dagger
U_{x,\nu}^\dagger.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Using the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula,

\[
W_{\mu\nu}(x)
=
\exp\left(
-i\tau^2 F_{\mu\nu}(x)+O(\tau^3)
\right),
\tag{6.8}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
F_{\mu\nu}
=
\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu
-i[A_\mu,A_\nu].
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

In components,

\[
F_{\mu\nu}{}^i{}_j
=
\partial_\mu A_\nu{}^i{}_j
-
\partial_\nu A_\mu{}^i{}_j
+
A_\mu{}^i{}_k A_\nu{}^k{}_j
-
A_\nu{}^i{}_k A_\mu{}^k{}_j.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Thus gauge curvature is the failure of local generative maps to commute.

## 6.3 Spectral action and emergent Einstein–Yang–Mills equations

Let \(D_\Gamma\) be the recursively generated Dirac operator. Define the generative spectral action

\[
S_\Gamma
=
\operatorname{Tr} f(D_\Gamma/\Lambda).
\tag{6.11}
\]

Assume \(D_\Gamma\) defines a regular four-dimensional spectral triple. The heat-kernel expansion gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr} f(D_\Gamma/\Lambda)
\sim
f_4\Lambda^4\int d^4x\sqrt{g}
+
f_2\Lambda^2\int d^4x\sqrt{g}\,R
+
f_0\int d^4x\sqrt{g}
\left(
c_1 R^2+c_2 C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
+c_3 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
\right)
+\cdots.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Varying with respect to the metric yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1}{16\pi G}
G_{\mu\nu}
+
\alpha H_{\mu\nu}
+
\beta(g_{\mu\nu}\Box R-\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu R)
+
\frac{1}{4g^2}T^{\rm YM}_{\mu\nu}
+
\Lambda_{\rm cc}g_{\mu\nu}
=0,
}
\tag{6.13}
\]

where

\[
G^{-1}\propto f_2\Lambda^2,
\qquad
g^{-2}\propto f_0,
\qquad
\Lambda_{\rm cc}\propto f_4\Lambda^4.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Here \(H_{\mu\nu}\) denotes the higher-curvature contribution from \(R^2\) and Weyl-squared terms.

Thus gravity is not added by hand. It arises as the macroscopic variation of the spectrum of a recursively generated operator.

## 6.4 Generative Ricci flow

If the generative depth \(n\) is interpreted as a renormalization scale, the spectral action induces a flow of the effective metric:

\[
\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial n}
=
-\frac{1}{\Lambda^2}
\frac{\delta S_\Gamma}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\tag{6.15}
\]

To leading order,

\[
\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial n}
=

\tag{6.16}
\]

Thus the generative recursion naturally produces a Ricci-type flow of emergent geometry.

# 7. Particles as Eigenmodes of the Generative Transfer Operator

## 7.1 Linear generative spectra

Let \(\Gamma\) be a linear generative map on an operator space. Its eigenmodes satisfy

\[
\Gamma(X_\alpha)=\lambda_\alpha X_\alpha.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The general solution is

\[
X_n=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^n X_\alpha.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Write

\[
\lambda_\alpha=e^{-i\tau E_\alpha-\tau\gamma_\alpha}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then

\[
E_\alpha=-\frac{1}{\tau}\arg\lambda_\alpha,
\qquad
\gamma_\alpha=-\frac{1}{\tau}\log|\lambda_\alpha|.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Stable particles correspond to modes with \(|\lambda_\alpha|=1\). Unstable particles correspond to \(|\lambda_\alpha|<1\).

## 7.2 Dispersion relations from translation-covariant generation

Assume spatial translation covariance. Then generative eigenmodes are labeled by momentum \(k\):

\[
\Gamma(X_k)=\lambda(k)X_k.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Let

\[
\lambda(k)=e^{-i\tau\omega(k)}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

The function \(\omega(k)\) is the dispersion relation. If near \(k=0\),

\[
\omega(k)^2=m^2+c^2k^2+\alpha k^4+O(k^6),
\tag{7.7}
\]

then the continuum field \(\phi(t,x)\) satisfies

\[
\left(
\partial_t^2-c^2\nabla^2+m^2
\right)\phi
=
O(\tau^2\nabla^4\phi).
\tag{7.8}
\]

Thus the Klein–Gordon equation emerges as the infrared limit of a generative recursion.

The leading correction is a new physical prediction:

\[
\boxed{
\omega(k)^2
=
m^2+c^2k^2+\alpha\tau^2 k^4+\cdots.
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

## 7.3 Tensor generation and second quantization

Let \(T\) have eigenvectors \(\psi_i\) with eigenvalues \(\varepsilon_i\). The additive tensor generator

\[
T^{(2)}=T\otimes I+I\otimes T
\tag{7.10}
\]

has eigenvalues

\[
\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Iterating,

\[
T^{(n)}
=
\sum_{r=1}^n I^{\otimes(r-1)}\otimes T\otimes I^{\otimes(n-r)}

\[
E_{i_1,\dots,i_n}
=

\tag{7.13}
\]

This is precisely the additive energy structure of many-body quantum theory. Symmetric and antisymmetric subspaces yield bosonic and fermionic Fock spaces.

Therefore:

> Second quantization is the inductive-limit form of additive tensor generation.

# 8. Thermodynamics and the Arrow of Generative Time

For any CPTP generative map \(\Psi\), the quantum relative entropy satisfies data processing:

\[
S(\Psi(\rho)\|\Psi(\sigma))
\le
S(\rho\|\sigma).
\tag{8.1}
\]

If \(\rho_*\) is a fixed point,

\[
\Psi(\rho_*)=\rho_*,
\tag{8.2}
\]

then

\[
S(\rho_{n+1}\|\rho_*)
\le
S(\rho_n\|\rho_*).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Define the entropy production per step:

\[
\sigma_n
=
S(\rho_n\|\rho_*)-S(\rho_{n+1}\|\rho_*).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Then

\[
\sigma_n\ge0.
