## Abstract

We derive dynamical physical consequences from Observer Network Geometry (ONG). Starting from the ONG principle that distance is an observer consensus rather than a primitive background structure, we promote the consensus inverse problem to a variational principle on observation networks. Three new physical structures emerge. First, clock desynchronization is shown to define a gauge-invariant two-form on observer slices; its curvature is the obstruction to global consensus time, and its continuum dynamics obeys a Maxwell-type equation sourced by cycle inconsistencies. Second, null observation constraints imposed by light-signal records introduce Lagrange-multiplier stresses supported on null geodesics. Variation with respect to the spacetime metric yields a field equation of Einstein form,
\[
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=
\kappa\left(
T^{\rm mass}_{\mu\nu}
+
T^{\rm link}_{\mu\nu}
+
T^{\rm clock}_{\mu\nu}
\right),
\]
where \(T^{\rm link}_{\mu\nu}\) is a trace-free null information stress tensor constructed from observation tensions. Third, variation with respect to observer worldlines yields a relational force law: observers accelerate when observation tensions are unbalanced. In the weak-field limit, closed-loop timing observables are shown to be gauge-invariant curvature/vorticity fluxes. In cosmological symmetry, the observation network behaves as a radiation-like source. Quantum timing bounds enter through Fisher-information weights and induce quantum-limited corrections to the consensus stress. The result is a network-native physical theory in which geometry is not the stage on which observers act, but the dynamical consensus field generated by their observations.

## 1. Introduction: From Relational Geometry to Relational Physics

Observer Network Geometry establishes a kinematical inversion of classical geometry: observers, clocks, and signal records are primitive, while metric structure is reconstructed as an optimal compatibility class of observations. The Euclidean theory projects noisy pairwise ranging data onto metric or Euclidean-distance-matrix cones; the Lorentzian theory replaces pairwise distances by Synge world-function constraints,
\[
\sigma_g(x_e,x_r)=0,
\]
for null signal propagation.

The next step is to ask what physics follows when the consensus structure is not merely reconstructed but allowed to act back on observers and signals. We formulate this as a variational completion of ONG. The central claim is:

> **Observational compatibility constraints are physical constraints. Their Lagrange multipliers are stress-energy carriers.**

This produces a relational field theory with three coupled sectors:

1. **Clock gauge sector.**  
   One-way time-transfer residuals define a graph one-form. Its non-exact part is a synchronization gauge field \(A\). The curvature
   \[
   F=dA
   \]
   measures the failure of global clock consensus around closed loops. The continuum equation is Maxwell-like:
   \[
   \nabla_j F^{ji}=\gamma_{\rm clk} j^i,
   \]
   where \(j^i\) is a desynchronization current.

2. **Null information stress sector.**  
   Each observed light link imposes a null world-function constraint. Introducing Lagrange multipliers \(\lambda_a\) for these constraints produces a stress tensor
   \[
   T^{\rm link}_{\mu\nu}(x)
   =
   \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}
   \sum_a
   \lambda_a
   \int_{\gamma_a}
   k^{(a)}_\mu k^{(a)}_\nu\,
   \delta^{(4)}(x-\gamma_a(s))\,ds,
   \]
   where \(k^{(a)\mu}\) is the null tangent of the \(a\)-th signal. This tensor is trace-free and represents the gravitational charge of observation records.

3. **Observer force-balance sector.**  
   Variation with respect to observer worldlines gives
   \[
   m_i a_{i\mu}
   =
   \sum_{a\in i}
   \epsilon_{ia}\lambda_a k^{(a)}_\mu,
   \]
   where \(\epsilon_{ia}\) encodes incoming/outgoing orientation. Observers move until the net observation tension vanishes.

Together these give a new physical picture: **geometry is the stress field of observer consensus.**

## 2. Variational Observer Consensus

### 2.1 Observation links as constraints

Let \(M\) be a four-dimensional time-oriented Lorentzian manifold with metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\), signature \((-+++)\). Let observers follow timelike worldlines
\[
\gamma_i:I_i\to M,
\qquad
u_i^\mu=\frac{d\gamma_i^\mu}{d\tau_i},
\qquad
g_{\mu\nu}u_i^\mu u_i^\nu=-c^2.
\]
An observation link \(a\) is a null relation between emission and reception events
\[
x_a^-=\gamma_{i_a}(\tau^-_a),
\qquad
x_a^+=\gamma_{j_a}(\tau^+_a),
\]
idealized by the Synge constraint
\[
C_a[g]\equiv \sigma_g(x_a^-,x_a^+)=0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

In a noisy network the constraints are not exactly satisfiable. ONG then replaces exact constraints by optimal reconciliation. At the continuum level this is encoded by a constrained or penalized action.

### 2.2 Consensus action

We introduce the ONG dynamical action
\[
S[g,\{\gamma_i\},\{\lambda_a\},A]
=
S_{\rm EH}[g]
+
S_{\rm link}[g,\{\lambda_a\},\{\gamma_i\}]
+
S_{\rm mass}[\{\gamma_i\},g]
+
S_{\rm clock}[A,g,u],
\tag{2.2}
\]
with
\[
S_{\rm EH}[g]
=
\frac{1}{2\kappa}
\int_M
\sqrt{-g}\,(R-2\Lambda)\,d^4x,
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4},
\tag{2.3}
\]
\[
S_{\rm link}
=
\sum_a
\lambda_a\,\sigma_g(x_a^-,x_a^+),
\tag{2.4}
\]
\[
S_{\rm mass}
=
-\sum_i m_i c\int d\tau_i,
\tag{2.5}
\]
and \(S_{\rm clock}\) to be constructed below.

The multipliers \(\lambda_a\) are the dual variables of the ONG projection problem. In exact consensus they enforce null compatibility. In noisy consensus they become residual tensions:
\[
\lambda_a=\rho_a\,C_a
\]
for a quadratic penalty \(\frac12\rho_a C_a^2\). Thus \(\lambda_a\) measures how strongly the network insists on the \(a\)-th observation.

The use of the Einstein-Hilbert term is not an arbitrary imposition. If one demands a local, diffeomorphism-invariant, second-order continuum regularization of relational consensus, Lovelock uniqueness in four dimensions selects the Einstein-Hilbert density up to a cosmological constant and topological terms. Thus the gravitational sector is the natural smooth continuum completion of the ONG consensus problem.

## 3. Clock Gauge Field and Synchronization Curvature

### 3.1 Discrete clock residuals

In the Euclidean/static limit, ONG gives the one-way ranging law
\[
T_{ij}=d_{ij}+\beta_j-\beta_i.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Define the clock residual on oriented edge \(e=(i,j)\) by
\[
z_e=T_e-d_e.
\tag{3.2}
\]
If clocks differ only by offsets, then
\[
z_e=(B\beta)_e=\beta_j-\beta_i,
\]
where \(B\) is the oriented incidence operator.

With noisy or nonintegrable data one solves the weighted least-squares problem
\[
\beta^\star
=
\arg\min_\beta
\frac12
\sum_{e\in E}
w_e\left(z_e-(B\beta)_e\right)^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]
The normal equations are
\[
L\beta=B^\top W z,
\qquad
L=B^\top W B,
\tag{3.4}
\]
the weighted graph Laplacian system.

### 3.2 Gauge-invariant cycle holonomies

Let \(C\) be an oriented cycle. Since \(CB=0\),
\[
\sum_{e\in C} z_e
=
\sum_{e\in C}
\left(z_e-(B\beta)_e\right).
\tag{3.5}
\]
Thus the cycle sum is invariant under clock shifts \(\beta_i\mapsto\beta_i+\chi_i\). The physical observable is not the local offset but the holonomy
\[
H_C
=
\sum_{e\in C}z_e.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Nonzero \(H_C\) means that no global clock synchronization is compatible with the observations.

### 3.3 Continuum Hodge decomposition

In the continuum limit, edge residuals become a one-form \(\zeta\) on a consensus spatial slice \(\Sigma\). The least-squares decomposition becomes the Hodge decomposition
\[
\zeta=d\beta+A,
\qquad
\nabla^i A_i=0,
\tag{3.7}
\]
where \(A\) is the non-exact synchronization field. The exact part \(d\beta\) is gauge; the curvature
\[
F=dA=d\zeta
\tag{3.8}
\]
is gauge-invariant.

The cycle holonomy becomes
\[
\Delta\tau_C
=
\oint_C A
=
\int_S F,
\tag{3.9}
\]
where \(S\) is any surface spanning \(C\). This is the continuum desynchronization phase.

### 3.4 Maxwell-type consensus equation

The natural consensus energy for synchronization curvature is
\[
S_{\rm clock}
=
\int_\Sigma
\sqrt{h}
\left[
\frac{1}{4\gamma_{\rm clk}}
F_{ij}F^{ij}
-
A_i j^i
\right]d^3x,
\tag{3.10}
\]
where \(h_{ij}\) is the induced metric on \(\Sigma\), \(\gamma_{\rm clk}\) is a synchronization response constant, and \(j^i\) is a desynchronization current induced by non-closure of local observation plaquettes.

Varying \(A_i\) gives
\[
\nabla_j F^{ji}
=
\gamma_{\rm clk} j^i.
\tag{3.11}
\]
Gauge invariance requires
\[
\nabla_i j^i=0.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Equation (3.11) is the first new physical law of ONG: **clock frustration behaves as a gauge field sourced by desynchronization current.**

### 3.5 Relation to relativistic vorticity and Sagnac physics

For stationary observers in a spacetime with coordinates \((t,x^i)\), local Einstein synchronization gives the one-form
\[

=
-\frac{g_{0i}}{g_{00}}.
\tag{3.13}
\]
The failure of global simultaneity is

=

\tag{3.14}
\]
In terms of the observer four-velocity \(u^\mu\), one may write, in adapted coordinates,

=

\tag{3.15}

For slow motion,

\tag{3.16}
\]
where
\[
\omega_{\mu\nu}
=
h_\mu{}^\alpha h_\nu{}^\beta
\nabla_{[\alpha}u_{\beta]}
\tag{3.17}
\]
is the vorticity tensor of the observer congruence.

Therefore the desynchronization phase is
\[
\Delta\tau_C
=
\frac{2}{c^2}
\int_S
\omega_{ij}\,d\Sigma^{ij}
+
O(v^3/c^4).
\tag{3.18}
\]
For rigid rotation this reproduces the Sagnac effect, while in a gravitating stationary spacetime it encodes frame dragging. The ONG clock equation becomes, in the slow-motion limit,
\[
\nabla_j\omega^{ji}
=
\frac{\gamma_{\rm clk}c^2}{2}j^i.
\tag{3.19}
\]
Thus desynchronization current sources congruence vorticity. This is a genuinely relational gravitomagnetic law.
